REVIEW ASSESSMENT

STRENGTHS:
The story's strongest achievement is its embodied exhaustion — the ache in Liang's jaw, the smell of tank water on her hands, the three-point-two-second flicker of the terminal. These sensory anchors make the trap physical rather than conceptual. The bilingual code-switching performs real work: English as compliance-voice, Mandarin as the register where truth can almost be spoken. The daughter's reverse-engineering in her personal notebook is a precise image of knowledge transmission without liberation — she's learning to see the trap, not escape it.

BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The story flinches at its own invariant in the final third. Huìlín's question "Why is your work so hard?" receives a direct answer: "The algorithm has a bias. It underscores our platform." This frames the injustice as a correctable error ("They can fix it. They don't."), collapsing the missing-floor invariant into a story about institutional negligence rather than a founding choice with no neutral ground beneath it. The prose retreats from its own instrument.

INVARIANT FALSIFIER:
LOST. The passage "The algorithm has a bias. It underscores our platform" / "Can they fix it?" / "They can. They don't." explicitly frames the problem as a fixable error that better authorities could correct. This violates the missing-floor invariant. The story needed to hold that the algorithm's "bias" IS the floor — the interested partition treated as neutral ground — not a deviation from a recoverable fair baseline.

READINESS:
The prose is alive in its sensory particulars and its linguistic performance, but it resolves to a legible injustice (fixable bias, institutional negligence) rather than holding the untranslatable real. One more pass needed.
