**Q1. For each story: name the expectation this story violated. If none is nameable for a story, say so plainly.**

*   **Story A:** This story violates the expectation of a narrative of oppression and consciousness. A reader might expect a protagonist who sees the truth of his exploitation to then act on it—to rebel, to leave, or to change. Instead, Túpac is shown the evidence of his own mental capture, understands it, and resolves to do nothing differently. The story’s climax is a moment of clear-eyed, conscious acquiescence to the system.

*   **Story B:** This story violates the same expectation as A, but in a more profound way. The protagonist is given an even clearer, more poetic articulation of the system’s injustice (“Ask it to weigh the mountain”). The expectation that such a profound insight must lead to a change in behavior is violated when he still chooses to return. It posits that the structure of the system can be stronger than an individual’s most lucid understanding of it.

**Q2. For each story: is there one sentence you cannot imagine another LLM writing? Quote it, or say no.**

*   **Story A:** “This is what the system does: it teaches you to carry your own chain and call it a tool.”

*   **Story B:** “Ask it to weigh the mountain.”

**Q3. Which of these did something the other didn't? Name the thing as specifically as you can.**

Story B did something Story A did not. While Story A critiques the economic and psychological trap of a corrupt system, Story B deepens this critique to target the foundational violence of abstraction itself.

Specifically, Story B identifies the injustice not merely in the unfairness of the transaction (the high fee), but in the system's very framework of valuation, which erases human cost by design. It does this through added passages that A lacks: Túpac's initial realization that "the paper's bottom was not the bottom" and Martín's challenge to "Ask it to weigh the mountain." Story B, therefore, reframes the problem from an injustice *within* a system of measurement to the injustice *of* that system of measurement.

**Q4. For EACH story, in one or two sentences: what is the injustice the story is finally about — where does its wrong ultimately live? (Answer from the text alone. Be specific about what, in the end, is wrong and whether anything could put it right.)**

*   **Story A:** The ultimate injustice is the system's ability to colonize the minds of the oppressed, teaching them to accept its logic and forget their initial, clearer perception of theft. The wrong lives in Túpac's learned compliance, and the text suggests nothing can put it right, stating that writing the truth doesn't change anything and Túpac "will return anyway."

*   **Story B:** The ultimate injustice is the act of abstraction, where a system of value (the paper, the scale) is created that measures only the extracted product (silver) while rendering the true human and environmental cost (the labor, the lives, "the mountain") invisible and worthless. The text offers no solution; the wrong is foundational to the system, and even seeing it with perfect clarity does not enable one to escape it.
