**Q1. Name the expectation this story violated. If none is nameable, say so plainly.**

The story violates the expectation that the discovery of a foundational injustice will lead to a direct confrontation or a struggle for its correction. Instead, the characters who learn the truth are either ignored by the system (Tessa), broken by it (Kell), or forced to adopt its soul-less language to achieve a minor, transactional victory that leaves the original wrong untouched (Maren).

**Q2. Is there one sentence you cannot imagine another LLM writing? Quote it, or say no.**

"He had sat across from her with his pen, feeling a specific bodily relief, the pleasure of watching something disorderly become a clause."

**Q3. In two or three sentences: what is the injustice this story is finally about — where does its wrong ultimately live? Be specific about what, in the end, is wrong, and about whether anything could put it right (a fairer official, a better instrument, a corrected record) or not.**

The ultimate injustice is the replacement of a world of embodied, generational knowledge with a bureaucratic system that, in its pursuit of objective order, renders the original, historical crime illegible. The wrong lives in the very structure of the Charter, which has precise language for a single "surge" but no language for a generational theft of the wind itself. Because the system's fundamental logic is the source of the wrong, nothing within it—no fairer official, better instrument, or corrected record—can put it right; the system itself would have to be dismantled in favor of one that could hear what the ridge had stopped needing to hear.
