# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The floor invariant (primary).** The world contains a founding arrangement — who is permitted to hold the high offices, who may bind others by oath, who counts as first among the living — that is treated by every instrument of the world as simply *how things are*, when in fact it was *taken*, once, by force, and has never since been re-derived from anything but its own persistence. **Falsifier:** if the finished story frames the ruling order's authority as a legitimate inheritance that a bad ruler has merely corrupted — such that a good ruler could hold the same authority cleanly — the floor invariant was lost. The authority itself, not merely its current occupant, must be the seized thing.

**The grain (present, subordinate, non-primary).** There exists a capacity — once it is given to a person, it grows by its own logic, generates futures its giver cannot foresee or retract, and cannot be un-given without destroying the one who received it. **Falsifier:** if the story resolves by having this capacity revoked, contained, or brought back under predictable control, the grain was lost. It is present in this telling but subordinate: at least one beat must let the grain's question (can this growing thing be un-grown?) recede or resolve while the floor's question (was this authority ever anything but seized?) remains standing, unresolved, load-bearing to the end.

**The substrate.** Beneath the terraced city of Amrasal Height, the old aqueduct-god's Cistern-Oath is cut into the bedrock — a set of channel-locks that release the mountain's snowmelt into the city's rice-terraces only when the Wardens who hold the Bronze Keys speak the Oath's binding-clause aloud at the spring reckoning. Every surveyor, every engineer, every Warden's own apprentice can read the flow-gauges, the channel-pressure, the silt-load — the water is honestly measured, down to the finger-width. What no instrument in the city can read, because the Oath was never written to be read, only spoken and inherited, is *why the Height Wardens and no one else hold the Keys.* The gauges tell you how much water moves. Nothing tells you why the Wardens, and not the terrace-families who built the channels with their own hands three centuries ago, are the ones permitted to speak the clause that starts the flow.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence.** You kneel at the spring reckoning with your palm flat on channel-stone that will carry true water to true fields all season long, honestly, to the last measured finger-width — and no gauge in the Height, however finely made, has ever once been asked *who decided the Wardens' hands are the ones that open it.*

# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## Setting

Amrasal Height, a terraced highland city built into three vertical tiers of a single mountain flank, sometime after its ninth generation of continuous habitation — a period with no external dating system, reckoned locally only by "reckonings since the Sundering of the Lower Terrace," an event eight generations gone and half-legendary now. The city exists because of one thing: the Cistern-Oath, a channel-lock system cut into the mountain's bedrock that governs how snowmelt is released, tier by tier, into the rice-terraces below. Above the city, glacier melt collects in a stone cistern; below the cistern, a lattice of carved channels, sluice-stones, and bronze locks controls the water's descent. No one built the cistern. It was there, the founding chronicles say, when the first terrace-families arrived — a Mountain in the oldest sense, pre-existing, indifferent, load-bearing. What the first terrace-families *did* build, with their own hands and their children's hands, over two hundred years, was the channel-lattice itself: the sluices, the locks, the terraces that the water feeds.

The Bronze Keys — six forged keys, one per channel-lock — are held exclusively by the Height Wardens, a hereditary order occupying the city's uppermost tier, closest to the cistern, in stone halls with running water piped directly to them year-round. The terrace-families live on the lower two tiers, walking upward daily to petition, downward daily to work the fields the water reaches only when a Warden speaks the binding-clause. The founding chronicle states, in its oldest surviving fragment, that the first Warden "took up the Keys when the mountain's voice fell silent, that the people should not thirst without a hand to answer for the water." No chronicle says the people chose that hand. No chronicle says any other hand was offered the chance. The chronicles simply begin *after* the taking, and every subsequent generation of Wardens has treated the fragment as founding law rather than founding seizure.

Physically: the Warden tier is quiet, cool, unhurried — stone corridors, small gardens irrigated by leakage the Wardens don't have to petition for. The terrace-families' tiers are loud with labor, chronically thirsty in late season, organized entirely around the spring and autumn reckonings when the Keys turn. A terrace-family's whole year is planned around a moment they do not control and cannot verify except by its outcome: water came, or it didn't.

## Constraint Naturalizations

**C1 — Hierarchical cosmic order → The Warden Precedence.** The doctrine, taught to every child on both tiers, that Wardens alone may speak the binding-clause because the mountain's original voice chose them, and that this arrangement coordinates the whole city's survival — without it, the story goes, the terraces would flood or starve by turns, families fighting over sluice-order every season. This is not entirely false: the precedence *does* prevent six hundred families from converging on the cistern with six hundred competing claims each reckoning. It is also, invisibly, the mechanism by which the Wardens' tier eats first, bathes daily, and never once stands in a petition-line.

- *Warden Kessa Vandrel* (institutional, arbitrage exit, civilizational horizon) experiences the Precedence as pure coordination — the thing she was raised inside, that lets her plan sluice-timing five years out, that she can bend when convenient and always survive bending. She calls it *the Order,* without irony.
- *Halvor Threnn*, a mid-tier channel-foreman with standing enough to negotiate sluice-order for his hamlet but no Key of his own (powerful-but-subordinate, civilizational horizon, constrained exit) experiences the Precedence as a rope he is tangled in — it gives him real leverage over lesser terrace-families and real protection from Warden caprice, and it also costs him a season's grain in "Warden's portion" he cannot refuse.
- *Two terrace-families* — one generational-poor and trapped by land-debt (Mireth Osk), one recently ruined and civilizationally rooted with nowhere else to plant (the Calder line) — experience the Precedence as the wall of their whole lives. Mireth calls it, flatly, *the water's price.* The Calders, three generations settled, have no word for it at all; it is simply the shape the world has always had.

**C2 — Mandated mortal ignorance → The Unspoken Clause.** The binding-clause itself is never taught to anyone outside the Warden line, and worse, terrace-families are taught — gently, generationally, not by whip but by lullaby and proverb — that *wanting to know the clause* is itself the mark of a grasping, unlucky spirit, the kind the mountain refuses water to. This is enforced almost entirely by internalization: there is barely a guard on the cistern-stair. There doesn't need to be. Children are raised to flinch from the question.

- Mireth Osk (identity-locked into the terrace-family self-conception) doesn't merely lack the clause — she has been shaped to experience curiosity about it as a kind of moral failing in herself, a grasping she must suppress.
- Warden Vandrel (analytical exit, civilizational horizon) experiences the Unspoken Clause as clean administrative continuity: of course the clause is kept within the Order, that's what keeping an Order *means.*
- Halvor Threnn, sharp enough and senior enough among the terrace-foremen to think analytically about the whole arrangement without holding a Key, is the one person positioned to notice: the ignorance costs something real, in seasons lost to guesswork about timing, that a shared clause would not cost. He can see the price without yet having a way to refuse paying it.

**C3 — Punishment for divine transgression → The Silencing.** The chronicles record, and Wardens periodically enforce, a punishment for anyone outside the Order who learns or speaks the binding-clause: exile from the terraces, permanent, with no appeal — framed not as a Warden's sentence but as the mountain itself "refusing water to a thief of its voice." This framing is the entire point: it lets the Wardens execute the harshest sanction in the city while experiencing themselves as merely administering a natural consequence, not inflicting one.

- Halvor Threnn, should he act on what C2 lets him glimpse, falls instantly and completely from powerful-foreman to powerless-exile-in-waiting — the single steepest fall in status the city recognizes, worse than debt, worse than crop failure, because it is final and it is *civilizational*, meaning even his children's children could not undo it.
- Warden Vandrel experiences enforcing the Silencing as the least remarkable act she performs all year — less notable to her than adjusting a sluice-gate. That flatness of affect, more than any cruelty, is the tell.

## Linguistic Strategy — Direct Translation (source register: an invented highland liturgical dialect, rendered into English with retained syntax and untranslatable terms)

The Height's ritual language, *Old Sluice-tongue,* survives only in the binding-clause and reckoning-prayers; ordinary speech is a plain highland English inflected by its grammar. Key retained terms:

- ***vess*** — "owed-flow," the specific measured allotment a terrace-family is entitled to, distinct from *general water* (there is no single English word for water-you-are-owed vs. water-in-general in the Height's tongue; the distinction is load-bearing in every dispute)
- ***the mountain's answering*** — used only for the moment the Keys turn and water moves; never used for the Wardens' *decision* to turn them, which has no verb of its own — a grammatical gap that is itself the Unspoken Clause's fingerprint. You cannot say "the Wardens decided" in the old liturgical register; you can only say "the mountain answered." Terrace-families who slip and use a decision-verb about the Keys are gently corrected, as though they misspoke.
- ***grasp-thirst*** — the flinch-word for wanting to know the clause; carries the same shame-register as a word for theft or lust, taught to children before they're old enough to know why

Dialogue example:
> "Don't grasp-thirst at the stair, Sennet. It's not your *vess* to ask after — the mountain answers who it answers."
> "It didn't *decide* nothing. It's the Wardens' hands on the Keys."
> "Hush your mouth. That's not how the reckoning-tongue goes."

Halvor Threnn, moving between registers as a foreman must, code-switches: liturgical Sluice-tongue with Wardens ("the mountain's answering, may it favor the lower tier"), plain highland speech with his own hamlet ("I counted the channel-pressure myself, it's not what they're telling us"). The switch signals exactly how much he is performing deference versus speaking what he's actually measured.

## Naming
Highland patronymic-occupational blend: Warden line uses single ceremonial names passed clean through generations (*Vandrel*, *Ashcurrow*, *Petrenne* — old, untranslated, deliberately archaic-sounding even to in-world ears, marking lineage-continuity with the founding). Terrace-families use given name + trade-or-terrace epithet (*Halvor Threnn* — Threnn being his hamlet's terrace-row; *Mireth Osk* — Osk, an old debt-name inherited from a grandparent's default, worn like a scar). The Calder line is notably recent-settled, three generations only, and uses a flatter, newer naming convention (single surname, no epithet) — marking them as still slightly foreign to the terrace-culture's deeper self-understanding, which matters for how little language they have for their own condition.

## Inhabitation Sentences

**Warden Kessa Vandrel:** "You were born into stone halls with water piped in before you asked for it, and the hardest thing the Order has ever required of you is speaking six words at two reckonings a year — so you have never once had to ask what gives your mouth, and not another's, the right to speak them."

**Halvor Threnn:** "You can count the channel-pressure as well as any Warden alive, and you have built your whole standing on being trusted to negotiate the gap between what you know and what you're permitted to say — until the day the gap itself becomes unbearable."

**Mireth Osk:** "You have been taught since before you could walk that wanting to know why the water comes when it comes is a shameful thirst in yourself, so that the not-knowing feels like your own failing and not a wall someone else built."

**Sennet Calder:** "Your family came to this mountain three generations ago hoping distance from your old ruin would be enough, and you have inherited a life shaped entirely by a Precedence you have no grandmother's proverb to even name."

# SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

**RESOLVED**

- Ω_E01: Does the water-measurement system need to be genuinely accurate (not corrupt) for the floor-invariant to land correctly? → **Yes — resolved as fully honest.** The gauges, channel-pressure readings, and *vess* allotments are never falsified anywhere in the setting. → *Impact:* preserves the affordance gate; ensures the injustice cannot be summarized as "a miscalibrated instrument" — the instrument is honest, the *authority to operate it* is the theft. This is the load-bearing design choice of the whole naturalization.

- Ω_E02: Should the founding chronicle contain any hint of consent or election of the first Warden? → **No — resolved as silent, not merely ambiguous.** The chronicle begins after the taking and never addresses the question of alternative claimants. → *Impact:* prevents a "corrupted good origin" reading; the seizure is total and originary, satisfying the missing-floor falsifier.

- Ω_C03: Is the grain (self-directed growth capacity) attached to a specific character, or diffuse? → **Attached, lightly, to Halvor Threnn's analytical capacity itself** — his growing ability to read the system clearly, once he has it, cannot be un-given by Vandrel even if she exiles him; the exile removes his *standing*, not his *understanding*, which persists in whatever he's taught others before the Silencing falls. → *Impact:* keeps the grain present and subordinate per the dominance ordering — it is not the central drama, and it resolves/recedes (his understanding either transmits or doesn't) independent of whether the floor's question (was the Precedence ever legitimate?) is settled.

- Ω_C04: Does the setting foreclose the target break (rebellion assimilated into compromise, system preserved)? → **Checked, not foreclosed.** Halvor's TR4/TR5 arc — organizing terrace-foreman leverage, then a negotiated settlement that raises his standing without dismantling the Precedence — is fully buildable in this substrate: the Wardens can concede expanded *vess* transparency or foreman consultation without ever relinquishing the Keys or explaining why they alone hold them. → *Impact:* Section 1 is written to leave this executable downstream, not to stage it.

**UNRESOLVED**

- Ω_P05: Should the reckoning ceremony be seasonal-agricultural (spring/autumn) or tied to a lunar/astral calendar instead? → *Recommendation:* keep agricultural — ties the stakes directly to survival need rather than abstract cosmic timing, keeping the Mountain-substrate concrete rather than mystical. → Awaiting confirmation; low-impact, does not touch the invariant.

- Ω_P06: Should Sennet Calder's "newer, flatter naming" be played for pathos (rootlessness) or for clarity (an outsider lens on the Precedence for the reader)? → *Recommendation:* clarity-forward — she's the character with least inherited language for the injustice, useful as the entry point for a reader unfamiliar with the Height's assumptions. → Awaiting downstream (Stage 3/4) discretion; tonal only.
