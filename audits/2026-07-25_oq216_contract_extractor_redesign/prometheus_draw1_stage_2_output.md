# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant (floor, primary):** The world's governing order — the Vaultright hierarchy of the Cistern Courts — presents itself as inherited law, self-evidently legitimate because it has always been so. In fact it was founded on a seizure: three centuries ago the first Cistern-Warden took the aquifer rights of a conquered river-clan by force and then wrote the taking into the shape of sacred water-law, so that the seizure disappeared into the grammar of legitimacy itself. No instrument in this world — no court, no elder, no water-diviner — can audit this founding, because the very apparatus of judgment was built by the seizure to judge in its own favor. **Falsifier:** if the finished story frames Vaultright rule as a correctable abuse of an otherwise legitimate system — a bad Warden misapplying good law — the floor invariant was lost. The law itself has no floor beneath it; there is no fair Warden to imagine.

**Substrate:** The floor's native carrier is the **Deep Cisterns** — the ancient stone reservoirs beneath the city, cut before written memory, whose water level is measured *honestly* every dawn by the Ledger-Keepers using a bronze gauge-rod. The gauge reads true. It has always read true. What it cannot read, what no gauge could ever read, is *who the water belongs to* — that question was answered once, by force, and then sealed into the reading itself, so that "true measurement" and "Vaultright entitlement" became the same sentence in the city's mouth.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You lower the gauge-rod into water that answers you exactly as it should, to the finger-width, and you already know the number will be read tomorrow as proof that the Vaultright have always owned what they took.*

**Missing-floor invariant, stated:** Beneath the Cistern Courts there is no shared principle, only a first theft grown old enough to look like weather. **Falsifier:** if a wiser or more honest Warden could simply rule correctly and dissolve the injustice, the floor was never truly missing — it was only misplaced, and the naturalization failed.

**Break-affordance note:** The city's water-politics must leave room for a rebellion that is *absorbed* rather than victorious or crushed — a compromise that keeps the Cistern Courts standing in modified form. Nothing in the substrate (gauge-honesty, generational thirst, clan memory) forecloses this; the ending is not mine to write, only to leave open.

## Setting

The city of **Sorrahavet** sits inside a limestone caldera on a coastline of salt-marsh and dead volcanic vents, where the only fresh water for four hundred miles collects in a lattice of natural stone cisterns beneath the crater floor. Surface rain is brackish, poisoned by mineral leach; every drop of drinkable water is drawn up from the Deep Cisterns through eleven hand-cut wellheads, each older than any surviving record. The city is built in concentric terraces down into the caldera bowl, so that altitude is destiny: those who live on the crater rim inherit the driest, hardest ground and walk two miles down switchback stairs to draw water; those who live nearest the wellheads at the bowl's floor breathe cistern-damp year-round and pay for their nearness in fever season.

Three hundred years ago, the caldera belonged to the **Ashreth**, a river-clan who had cut the wellhead shafts across eleven generations and governed water-share by a spoken reckoning no outsider was taught. A mercenary captain named **Corvain Vault** broke the Ashreth's spring council by force, drowned their reckoning-keepers in their own cistern, and declared the wellheads sacred trust of his own bloodline, sealed by the gods of depth. His descendants, the **Vaultright**, have governed the water-law ever since through the **Cistern Courts** — a bureaucracy of gauge-readings, drought-writs, and inheritance-rites so thoroughly woven into the city's daily grammar that no living Sorrahavet-born person has ever known water governed any other way. The Ashreth survive as the **Undertow** — the caste of laborers, well-diggers, and gauge-carriers who do the physical work the Cistern Courts require and who alone still remember, in fragments, that the law was not always the law.

Each dawn, a Ledger-Keeper lowers a bronze gauge-rod into each of the eleven wellheads and calls the water-level aloud in the old counting-tongue. This reading is never falsified — the rod is honest, the Ledger-Keepers are bound by oath and by the simple fact that a false reading kills people in a drought city. But the reading's *meaning* — how much of tomorrow's draw belongs to Vaultright reserve, guild allotment, or Undertow ration — is fixed by writs three centuries deep, writs that treat Corvain Vault's conquest as the water's natural order rather than as an order imposed on the water.

## Constraint Naturalizations

**C1 (Hierarchical cosmic order) → The Vaultright Water-Law**, called by everyone simply *the Reckoning* — the total architecture of writs, gauge-rites, and inherited allotments that governs who draws what, when, and by whose leave. It is treated as sacred, ancient, self-evident.

- Agent_A (X1, institutional/arbitrage) → **Warden Iset Vault**, the current head of the Cistern Courts, ninth in the Vaultright line. She experiences the Reckoning as pure coordination — the thing that prevents Sorrahavet from tearing itself apart over water. She is not lying when she says this. From the Warden's terrace, with full access to every writ and every gauge-reading and the standing to reinterpret any of them, the Reckoning genuinely functions as a Rope: it resolves disputes, prevents violence, keeps the aqueduct guilds cooperating. She can always find an arbitrage — a reading of the old writs that serves her interest and looks, to her, exactly like justice.

- Agent_B (X2, powerful-but-subordinate) → **Master Ora Dellwick**, Guildmaster of the Aqueduct Engineers, whose guild physically maintains the wellhead machinery and therefore has real institutional standing — but whose guild charter exists entirely at Vaultright pleasure and can be revoked by writ. Ora experiences the Reckoning as Tangled Rope: it gives her guild real power, real income, real standing above the Undertow — and it also cages her, because the moment she moves against Vaultright interest, her charter is what gets pulled first.

- Agent_C (X3, powerless/generational/trapped) → **Nen Ashreth-Cray**, a gauge-carrier's daughter born into the Undertow, whose family has hauled water on Vaultright ration since before her grandmother's grandmother. Nen experiences the Reckoning as a Snare with no visible exit: her family's ration is generationally fixed, her caste-marking is load-bearing on her body (the cistern-damp scarring all gauge-carriers bear), and there is no imaginable future in which the Reckoning simply stops applying to her line.

- Agent_D (X4, powerless/civilizational/trapped) → **Old Pell**, the eldest living Undertow reckoning-keeper, who has memorized fragments of the pre-Vault spoken law and understands the Reckoning's full three-hundred-year arc — and whose extended historical horizon changes nothing about his exit. He knows exactly how the trap was built and exactly how long it has held, and this knowledge buys him no door out of it.

**C2 (Mandated mortal ignorance) → The Sealed Tongue** — the old counting-language in which the dawn gauge-reading is called aloud is deliberately never taught to the Undertow in full; they learn enough to perform the labor of gauge-carrying but not enough to independently verify what a reading *means* for allotment. Only Vaultright-trained Ledger-Keepers and guild-certified engineers learn the full counting-tongue.

- Agent_C (X3) experiences this as identity-lock: she has grown up inside the half-taught tongue, using it daily for labor, and it has fused with her sense of who she is — to fully learn the sealed portions would mean becoming something unrecognizable to her own family, a kind of caste-treason before it is ever a rebellion.
- Agent_A (X1) experiences this as pure coordination: standardized water-law requires a controlled technical vocabulary, the same way any specialist guild restricts its terms of art. She does not experience the restriction as concealment because, from her seat, there is nothing left to conceal — she already has the whole tongue.
- Agent_B (X2) experiences this analytically: Ora has full access to the counting-tongue by guild right, and from that vantage she can see precisely how the half-teaching functions as leverage — a genuine hybrid, because the guild's technical monopoly is real value *and* real complicity in keeping the Undertow dependent on translation they cannot perform themselves.

**C3 (Punishment for divine transgression) → The Drowning Writ** — the ancient and absolute penalty for anyone who draws water outside their allotment or teaches the sealed tongue to an unauthorized ear: forfeiture of standing, caste, and — in the oldest cases — literal drowning in the cistern one attempted to draw from. It is invoked exceedingly rarely, but its existence structures every risk calculation in the city.

- Agent_B (X2), once she acts (see below), falls instantly and totally from guild standing into Undertow status — the Drowning Writ does not merely punish an act, it retroactively erases the standing that made the act thinkable, which is why the punishment reads as the single highest-stakes exposure in the city's whole architecture.
- Agent_A (X1) experiences invoking the Drowning Writ as ordinary governance — the necessary, regrettable maintenance of order, no different in kind from a guild fining a late shipment. She does not perceive herself as wielding the city's most extreme instrument of extraction; she perceives herself as doing her job.

## The Inciting Action (mapped from TR1–TR5)

Ora Dellwick — Guildmaster, powerful-but-subordinate — obtains sealed-tongue water allotment records (the forbidden resource) and secretly teaches a working fragment of the true reckoning to Nen's kin-line, enabling the Undertow to draw an unsanctioned but survivable ration during drought season (TR1: transfer to restricted population). This is the act that collapses Ora's guild standing and activates the Drowning Writ against her (TR3). Nen's family, now holding real information, shifts from identity-locked to merely constrained (TR2) — they know they are trapped, which is not the same as being trapped without knowing it.

Warden Iset moves to enforce the Writ at full weight. But Ora does not go to the drowning-stone alone: she has spent fifteen years building quiet leverage among the aqueduct engineers whose cooperation Vaultright rule actually requires to keep the wellheads running (TR4) — a threatened engineers' strike during drought season is worth more to Iset than one Guildmaster's death. What follows is not liberation and not annihilation. It is a **negotiated settlement** (TR5): Ora's guild status is stripped but her life spared and her exile softened into internal demotion; the sealed tongue is partially, grudgingly opened to a licensed Undertow reckoning-clerk class — a narrow door, heavily supervised, revocable. The Reckoning survives, modified, still Vaultright, still unaudited at its root. Sorrahavet does not learn who Corvain Vault really was, or by what right his line still holds the wellheads. The floor is never examined. It only grows one visible crack, which the Cistern Courts immediately begin explaining as evidence of the law's flexibility and mercy.

## Power Differential Through Material Conditions

The crater's terraces make status literally vertical. The Vaultright compound sits on the caldera rim in dry stone halls with imported timber roofing, water arriving by gravity-fed private channel. The guild quarter occupies the middle terraces — solid stone housing, cistern access twice daily by right, but visible dependency: guild charters are Vaultright parchment, revocable at a stroke. The Undertow live at the bowl floor, in cistern-damp rock housing that never fully dries, where the young draw water on a fixed schedule that has not changed in three generations and the old bear pale mineral scarring on their forearms from decades of gauge-work. An Undertow child worries about tomorrow's ration; a guild engineer worries about next season's contract renewal; the Warden worries about the water table in fifty years, because fifty years is the only horizon that threatens her line's legitimacy.

## Linguistic Strategy: Code-Switching + Sealed Tongue as Direct Translation Hybrid

Sorrahavet runs on two linguistic registers. **Street-Sorrahavet** is the daily creole everyone speaks — market trade, family, insult, prayer. **The Counting-Tongue** is the old technical language of gauge-reading and water-law, structurally required for anything touching the Reckoning, and rationed by caste: guild members and Vaultright learn it fully; the Undertow learn only the operational fragments needed to carry gauges and haul water, not the fragments needed to *contest* a reading.

- Undertow speak Street-Sorrahavet for everything of the heart — grief, love, defiance, humor — and drop into broken Counting-Tongue only when forced to interface with officials, where their partial fluency marks them instantly as *laboring* rather than *judging* class.
- Guild and Vaultright speak Counting-Tongue fluently as a mark of standing, and use it *performatively* in Street-Sorrahavet conversation — dropping a technical term mid-sentence the way one might drop a title — to remind listeners of the gap.
- Example: Nen, furious, tells her mother in Street-Sorrahavet, *"They measured the water fair and still starved us,"* — a sentence the Counting-Tongue has no clean way to construct, because in the Counting-Tongue "measured fair" and "allotted rightly" are the same word, *dvashen*. This untranslatability is the whole point: the sealed tongue's grammar makes the floor's injustice literally unsayable inside it.
- Naming: Vaultright carry Old Vault surnames (Iset Vault, Corvain Vault) marking direct lineage-claim. Guild families carry occupational surnames fixed at guild founding (Dellwick, from an old wellhead-name). Undertow carry hyphenated clan-remnant names — *Ashreth-Cray*, *Ashreth-Mol* — preserving the drowned river-clan's name as a fossil fragment, a name that is simultaneously an identity and a caste-mark visible on every official document.
