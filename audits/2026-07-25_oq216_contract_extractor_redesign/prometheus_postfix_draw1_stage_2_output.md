# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The floor invariant (primary).**
The world contains a founding claim that everyone downstream treats as bedrock rather than as an event: that the current order over the settlement has always belonged to those who now hold it, and that this belonging is the natural shape of things rather than a seizure dressed in the clothes of time. **Falsifier:** if the finished story frames the ruling house's authority as a *correct allocation a fairer successor would restore* — a throne merely occupied by the wrong person — the floor invariant was lost. The floor is not about who should rule. It is that the very ground on which "who should rule" gets asked was poured by the people asking it.

**The grain (present, subordinate, non-primary).**
There is a second, smaller real in this world: a capacity, once given, that cannot be taken back by whoever gave it — a growing-toward-itself that the giver cannot steer once it starts. This is not the load-bearing invariant here. It appears, it matters in one register of the story, and then the floor question re-asserts itself and takes precedence. Per the dominance ordering, at least one beat must let the grain's question resolve or recede while the floor's question stands unresolved.

**The substrate that holds the floor.**
The settlement is built on terraced water-law: a system of channels, sluice-rights, and flood-easements cut into a river delta centuries before anyone alive can remember, administered now by a Court of Measures that reads the channels honestly, gauges flow honestly, and enforces allocations with real, uncorrupted instruments — and whose entire honest apparatus stands on a single unaudited fact: that the Court's founding family owns the river's source-lock by right of "first cutting," a claim no living person witnessed and no text but the Court's own predates. The instruments are not lying. The zero they are calibrated against was never chosen by anyone who had to live downstream of it.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence.**
You kneel at the sluice-gate with the water-warden's gauge in your hand, and the reading is true — the flow is exactly what the brass says it is — and the falseness you cannot gauge is further up, at the lock no one now living was present to see cut.

# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

The delta of the Kesh River fans into nine terraces before it reaches the sea, and every drop of water that reaches the lower terraces has passed through sluice-gates that the Vantar family has controlled for eleven generations. The Vantar claim is simple and total: their ancestor, First-Warden Ansa Vantar, cut the original lock into the river's single upstream gorge before any other family had settled the delta, and *first cutting* — under the Kesh Compact, the founding law no one alive has read in its original form, only in the Vantar family's copied and re-copied recensions — grants permanent title to whoever controls the source.

The Court of Measures administers this. Its water-wardens are meticulous, incorruptible in the narrow sense: their brass gauges are calibrated twice a year against a reference weir, cross-checked by rival wardens, and a warden caught falsifying a reading is drowned in the terrace they cheated. The gauges tell the truth. What they cannot do is gauge the lock itself — the wardens measure flow *downstream* of Vantar authority, never the legitimacy of the authority that set the flow's baseline in the first place. Everyone from the Warden-General to the lowest terrace farmer has only ever known a world where the Vantar lock is simply *there*, the way the gorge itself is there.

Four positions inhabit this world.

**The Sluice-Marshal** holds the Vantar seat itself, arbitrating disputes, dispatching wardens, adjusting terrace allocations in drought years — always, always, from behind the founding claim, never questioning it, because questioning it is the one motion the whole apparatus cannot survive. The Marshal's hands are clean. The system runs itself; the Marshal barely has to touch it. That ease is the tell.

**The Warden-General** commands the corps of gaugers and enforcers — powerful within the Court, decorated, feared by terrace-holders — but bound by oaths of service that make the Warden-General as answerable to Vantar recall as the lowest farmer, with none of the exit the Marshal enjoys. The General extracts real authority from the system and pays a real, asymmetric price for it: promotion, disgrace, and execution all run through the same gate.

**Imeh, a ninth-terrace flood-warden's daughter**, was born into the lowest allocation on the delta — the terrace that floods first and droughts first, whose water-right is calculated last in every dry season, whose family has petitioned the Court for three generations for a recount that never comes. She cannot leave; the ninth terrace is the only land her clan holds a name to, and leaving forfeits the name.

**Sohrab, once a mid-terrace grain-factor with standing enough to sit in Court sessions**, commits the act that turns the story: he obtains a sealed vial of *lock-water* — water drawn directly from above the Vantar gorge, forbidden to any hand but a Vantar warden's — and carries it down to the ninth terrace, to Imeh's family, whose fields have not tasted first-water in memory. The transfer is discovered. Sohrab's Court standing evaporates in a single hearing; he is remanded to the ninth terrace himself, stripped of factor's rank, made to work the flood-gates alongside the family he tried to help. Imeh's family, meanwhile, finds their allocation status shifted — not freed, but no longer *locked* to the lowest recount; they are folded instead into a contested, negotiable middle tier, watched now, disputed now, but no longer simply ignored.

The Sluice-Marshal's response to Sohrab's act is swift and, within the Compact's own terms, entirely lawful: Sohrab is not merely demoted but *unpersoned* from the Court roll, his name struck from the factor registry, his water-right reassigned. The Marshal experiences this as maintenance — a leak sealed, nothing more. Sohrab experiences it as his life ending while his body keeps working the gates.

What follows is not rupture. Sohrab and the ninth-terrace clans do not burn the sluice-gates down; they cannot, and burning them would drown three terraces below along with the Court above. Instead, over two dry seasons, Sohrab — stripped of rank but not of the grain-factor's ledger-knowledge he still carries in his head — helps the ninth and eighth terraces organize a joint claim: a bloc large enough in irrigated acreage that starving it costs the Marshal more in spoiled downstream tribute than tolerating it. The Marshal negotiates. The ninth terrace's allocation is raised — not to parity, not to first-water, but to a bracket where recount petitions are heard rather than filed and forgotten. Sohrab is not restored to factor's rank. He is granted a lesser, permanent seat: Terrace Advocate, a new office, invented for this settlement, that exists only so long as the Marshal finds it cheaper to include him than to exclude him.

Nowhere in any of this is the lock itself opened to question. The Compact's copied recension still names Ansa Vantar first-cutter. The Court of Measures still calibrates its gauges twice a year and hangs wardens who lie about flow. The water is, in every reading anyone takes, exactly what the brass says it is.

**Local terminology.** The founding claim is called *the First Cutting* or simply *the Cutting* — never "the law," never "policy," always the specific, singular, historical-sounding act. The daily grinding hierarchy of allocation is *the Reckoning*. Sohrab's punishment-that-is-also-Vantar's-ordinary-governance is called *striking from the roll* — a phrase used identically for a warden's routine retirement and for Sohrab's ruin, which is precisely the point: the language does not distinguish maintenance from punishment, because the Marshal's position does not experience a difference.

**Linguistic strategy: Code-switching (Register 4), blended with Naming (Register 5).**

Two registers of speech operate on the delta: **Compact-tongue**, the formal, archaic register used in Court, in the recensions, in any address to a Vantar official — full of passive constructions that erase agency ("it has been Reckoned," never "the Marshal decided") — and **terrace-cant**, the clipped, active, agricultural speech of daily terrace life, full of concrete verbs (*gauge, silt, breach, tithe*) and no passive voice at all.

Example:
> To the Court: *"It has been Reckoned that the ninth terrace's allocation stands at recount, pending review."*
> On the terrace, the same fact: *"They shorted us again. Third year running."*

The switch signals who is speaking to whom, and whether the speaker is performing legitimacy or naming a fact. When Imeh, late in the story, uses Compact-tongue in her own petition — deliberately, precisely, turning the Court's own passive grammar back on itself ("*it has been Reckoned* — by whom, and on what water-right was that Reckoning cut?") — the code-switch itself is the escalation.

**Naming.** Vantar family names are compound and possessive — *Vantar-of-the-Lock*, *Vantar-of-the-Roll* — asserting the Cutting in the name itself. Terrace families carry single working names tied to function and terrace number — *Imeh Ninth-Gate*, her father *Dosu Flood-Warden* — names that describe what you do for the delta, not what you own of it. Sohrab's stripped name, after judgment, becomes simply *the Struck* in Court records for one full season, before his invented office gives him back a name: *Sohrab Advocate*, a title with no Compact precedent, visibly newer and lesser than any Vantar compound.

**Inhabitation sentences.**

*Sluice-Marshal:* "You have never once had to ask whether the lock was yours to hold, because no gauge in the Court has ever been built to measure that question, only the water beneath it."

*Warden-General:* "You enforce a Reckoning you did not write, in a uniform that cannot be taken off, and every promotion you have earned has cost someone below you a season's water."

*Imeh:* "You are the ninth gate, which floods first and thirsts first, and the only water anyone ever gives you is the water someone else was willing to be ruined for."

*Sohrab:* "You carried one sealed vial past a lock you had spent your whole Court standing pretending not to see, and the roll struck your name before the water had even finished running downhill."
