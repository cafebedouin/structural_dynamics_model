# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The floor invariant:** Long before any living memory, the ruling order of the Sunken Court took the high seat by force — no different in method from any bandit-king's ambush — and then spent centuries writing that seizure backward into the shape of inheritance, until "the Court has always ruled" became indistinguishable from "the Court ought to rule." No tribunal in the Court's own halls, no genealogist in its own libraries, can find the seam where force became right, because the instruments that would look for the seam were built by the hand that made it. **Falsifier:** if the story resolves as a bad Regent corrupting a legitimate Court, the floor is lost — the Court was never legitimate; it was only first.

**Substrate that holds it:** The Old Ledger-Stone beneath the Court's foundations — a slab of black basalt, older than the Court's own founding myths, on which is carved (in a script no living scholar can fully read) the record of *every* claimant who has ever taken the high seat, going back further than the Court's own histories admit. The Court reads the Stone as its charter. The Stone does not say what the Court says it says.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You kneel to press your ear to stone older than the order that rules you, listening for the name of the first king, and the stone gives you only silence exactly where the Court's histories are loudest.*

**The grain (subordinate):** A vine — the Kindling-Vine — grafted by the Court's own gardeners onto condemned prisoners as a punishment-graft meant to slowly consume and silence them; but the graft, once rooted, sometimes *teaches itself* to its host, granting a wild, ungovernable fluency in growing-things that no gardener can then retract. **Falsifier:** if the story ends with the graft's gift fully stripped back under Court control, the grain is lost — but this grain is allowed to recede unresolved; only the floor must stand at the end.

## Setting (the affordance test, applied)

**The Sunken Court of Ammerlin**, a terraced city built downward into a drained inland sea-basin, its seven tiers descending from the Rim (where the ruling Court holds session in the old sea-cliffs) to the Silt (the reclaimed lakebed where the landless work the drained soil). Era: an invented late-medieval agrarian polity, roughly analogous to no single documented culture — Rim architecture draws on carved-cliff dwelling, Silt agriculture on terraced wetland reclamation, neither tied to a real region. This displaces the source across **era** (invented pre-industrial secondary world, not the source's likely setting) and **governing institution** (a basin-court of cliff-nobility legitimated by a stone charter, not the source's institution).

The Court claims descent from the First Regent, who "raised the waters back" and gave the basin to the people in trust — a founding myth carved everywhere in Rim stone. The Old Ledger-Stone, predating the Court's own cliffs, is the one surviving artifact from *before* the First Regent, and the Court has spent generations ensuring it is read only by Court-trained Stonewardens, whose training consists mostly of learning which passages not to translate.

**Affordance test applied:** Could a better instrument read the Ledger-Stone's "true" verdict on the Court's legitimacy? No — the Stone's script is not a code with a correct decryption; it is a record laid down by every claimant who ever stood over it, including the ones the Court erased, and reading it *for* a verdict is a category error the Stone does not support. It has no true value under any instrument. It is Tier-1 ground, not a mismeasured number. This passes the gate: the injustice cannot be reduced to "the Court misread its own charter" — the Court's charter, honestly read by its own honest tools, still cannot produce a legitimate origin, because there is no legitimate origin *to* produce.

**Scored-Snare gate:** Since `inherent_instrument = no`, the central Snare (C3, punishment for defying the Court) is **not** naturalized as a sentence-tariff, a crime-severity index, or any tallied system. Punishment in Ammerlin is the Graft — physical, sensory, administered by hand, never numbered.

## Character Positions

**Regent-Warden Ossaine** (X1) — Institutional, arbitrage exit, civilizational time horizon. Head of the seated Court, sole living authority permitted final translation of the Ledger-Stone's ambiguous passages. Occupies the Rim's oldest cliff-house, dry, warm, private, with a personal Stonewarden on retainer. Speaks last in Court, never first — deference flows *to* Ossaine, a material fact registered in the room before any word is spoken.

**Marshal Tevrin Cael** (X2) — Powerful-but-subordinate, civilizational-then-biographical time collapse across the arc, constrained-then-trapped exit. Commands the Silt-wardens (the Court's enforcers on the lower tiers) but answers to Ossaine and cannot read the Stone himself. Lives on the Third Tier — better than the Silt, worse than the Rim, a house with one dry room and one that floods every spring, which he has stopped mentioning to Rim visitors.

**Silt-tenant Merred** (X3) — Powerless, generational time horizon, trapped-then-identity-locked exit. Works reclaimed lakebed soil that floods on the Court's schedule, not the weather's — the Basin-Right's water-management is itself an instrument of timed extraction dressed as natural drainage. Cannot read. Does not know that not-reading is a choice anyone ever made on her behalf.

**Ovett** (X4) — Merred's son, powerless, civilizational time horizon (he thinks in terms of what will still be true for *his* son), trapped exit. Younger, angrier, the first Silt-born in three generations to ask aloud why the water only runs one way — the character whose civilizational horizon cannot buy him a single degree of actual mobility, per the spec's TR-independent Snare confirmation.

## Linguistic Strategy: **Code-Switching + Naming (combined)**

**Register split:** Rim-speech (formal, Court-Latinate, full of water-descent metaphor: "as the rain finds the river") versus Silt-cant (clipped, agricultural, oath-heavy, superstitious about the Stone). Tevrin Cael code-switches constantly and audibly — Rim-formal in Court, Silt-cant with his own wardens — and the switch is the tell of his hybrid position: neither register is fully his.

- *Rim-speech, Ossaine, in Court:* "The Basin-Right does not command. It describes. Water has always found the Silt; we merely keep the channels clear."
- *Silt-cant, Merred, at the threshing floor:* "Don't go asking the stone questions, girl. Stone don't answer. Stone just sits there and lets the Wardens do the answering *for* it."
- *Tevrin, switching mid-sentence, to a Silt-warden after a Court session:* "The Regent-Warden finds the petition... —look, just tell them no. Tell them no and don't say Ossaine's name doing it."

**The substrate's name, non-quantitative:** Locals call the Old Ledger-Stone simply **the Undersong** — because Stonewardens claim that if you press your ear to it long enough in silence, you hear something like singing beneath the carved lines, a sound that is never the same twice and that no Warden has ever transcribed. It is a hum, not a reading. This is deliberate: nothing in Ammerlin's language permits the Undersong to be quoted as a verdict.

**Naming tradition:** Rim names (Ossaine, Tevrin Cael) follow a clipped double-syllable-plus-clan-suffix pattern (-aine, -in, Cael as an old cliff-clan name), aspirational and vaguely liturgical. Silt names (Merred, Ovett) are single-syllable-rooted, agricultural, often literally descriptive in the old Silt-tongue (Merred: "furrow-kept"; Ovett: "late-sown") — the naming split itself encodes C2's Unlettered Covenant, since Silt names were never meant to be written down at all, only spoken.

## Break-Affordance Confirmation

The setting leaves TR4→TR5's arc fully executable: Tevrin's graft-punishment (C3, felt not tallied), his organized leverage (discovering *what the Stone actually contains* — or credibly threatens to reveal it), and the negotiated settlement (Ossaine cannot afford exposure of the floor invariant, Tevrin cannot afford total ruin — both settle, χ rises but stays sub-Snare, the Court persists in modified form) — this is a rebellion **assimilated into compromise**, not rupture. The Undersong itself never delivers a verdict on-screen; it recedes (the grain subordinates) at the exact moment the compromise is struck, while the floor's question — *was this ever legitimate?* — is left standing, unanswered, un-audited, permanent. That is the Seeded Possibility ending the spec requires.
