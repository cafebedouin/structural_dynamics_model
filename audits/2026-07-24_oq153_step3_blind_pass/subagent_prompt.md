- **Q0. Is there an identifiable kernel at all** — a foundational content that could *have* an owner

- **Q1. Is there a party who owns/controls the kernel** — a body that could change it at all? NO (a
  kernel exists but authority is distributed/emergent; no one can decide a change) → **`absent_diffuse`**.
  YES → Q2.
- **Q2. Is there a defined, recognized procedure by which that party amends THE KERNEL ITSELF** — the

The Q2 cut is kernel-amendment legitimacy, NOT activity or application-revisability. A system may
develop its *application* vigorously while its *kernel* is closed (→ frozen). Application-revisability
is shared by frozen and licensed_revisable and is NOT the test.

## Values
- **licensed_revisable** — recognized authority + a defined procedure to amend the kernel itself.
- **frozen** — recognized owner but NO legitimate procedure to amend the kernel itself.
- **absent_diffuse** — NO single party owns the kernel; change comes from uncoordinated adoption.
- **unauthored** — cannot be assigned from who-may-amend-the-kernel-and-how alone (no identifiable
  kernel, or the text won't decide). Always give the reason.

## Boundary cases
1. A procedure that exists but is rarely used → licensed_revisable (existence, not frequency).
2. Declared non-amendment: available-but-unchosen (licensed_revisable) vs illegitimate/foreclosed
   (frozen). "We do not intend to revise" vs "shall not be altered." Text won't decide → unauthored.
3. Emergent standard, no governing body → absent_diffuse; same function owned by a body with a
   revision process → licensed_revisable.
4. Charter with an amendment article → licensed_revisable; one declaring itself unalterable → frozen.
5. A formal multi-party governing process (RFC track, consortium vote) is NOT absent_diffuse — a
   procedure exists → licensed_revisable. absent_diffuse requires the ABSENCE of a governing process.
6. A canon whose text is closed but whose application is developed → frozen. A tradition that revises

   precedent) → licensed_revisable.
