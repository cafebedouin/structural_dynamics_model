Q1.

**Story A:** The expectation violated is narrative resolution/catharsis for a dystopian-conversion plot. The protagonist (Mina) is not redeemed and does not return to resisting the system; the antagonist (Ahmad) is never confronted or corrected — his one moment of doubt ("What did the system miss when it measured only what was logged?") is explicitly talked back down through repetition until "the discomfort faded." The story substitutes plot resolution with an elegy: it closes not on a turn of events but on two powerless characters (Ibu Salmah, Pak Rashid) affirming that what was lost is still "real" even though it no longer matters.

**Story B:** Same base violation, taken further. It removes even the metaphysical consolation A supplies — no "we remember" exchange, no line naming the unwritten care as persisting ("ontologically real"). It then violates a second, narrower expectation: that the story's own opening example of unmeasured care (Mina sitting through Aisyah's fever, fed and watched by neighbors, "nobody wrote it down") would stand as an untouched reference point. Instead the story ends with Mina teaching that exact scenario back to new villagers as three loggable categories — even the founding instance of *timbangan* gets retroactively absorbed.

Q2.

**Story A:** "The substrate—the unwriteable care, the direct reciprocal keeping between people who knew each other's names—would remain ontologically real."

**Story B:** "Otherwise it reads as taking without giving, and your score falls."

Q4.

**Story B** does something **Story A** doesn't: it adds an entire closing scene — a training workshop — absent from A. In it, Mina teaches new registrants to convert the story's own opening act of unmeasured care (a child's fever, tended by neighbors who "kept her" without logging anything) into three transaction entries ("*Nursing care, received. Meal support, received. Childcare, received.*"), and states a rule that appears nowhere in A: "whoever helps you should log the giving too. Otherwise it reads as taking without giving, and your score falls" — a new bilateral-logging requirement that closes off even one-sided, unreciprocated witnessing.

**Story A** does something **Story B** doesn't: it contains material B omits entirely — the night Mina logs helping the widow Fatimah's son's infected cut; Ibu Salmah's monologue defining fairness ("Fairness is not a number. Fairness is knowing who kept you when you needed keeping..."); the closing exchange "At least we remember" / "We remember"; and the full dialogue between Pak Rashid and Ibu Salmah about whether the ledger's spread constitutes "winning" ("The ledger can spread without being right... I think the difference between *timbangan* and debt remains real even when nobody recognizes it" / "True but irrelevant"), including the line asserting the unmeasured substrate remains "ontologically real."
