**Q1. For each story: name the expectation this story violated. If none is nameable for a story, say so plainly.**

*   **Story A:** This story violated the expectation that knowledge is power. The narrative builds a clear injustice and then introduces a protagonist, Kiran, who scientifically proves the injustice, its mechanisms, and its origins. The expectation is that this knowledge, once revealed to those in power, will lead to reform. The story violates this by showing the system is not broken but is functioning as intended, and the knowledge is ultimately powerless to change the material outcome for the people being harmed.

*   **Story B:** This story violated the same expectation as Story A: that knowledge is power. It follows the identical plot where the discovery and documentation of a systemic flaw fails to produce any change, as the system's administrators do not see it as a flaw.

**Q4. Which of these did something the other didn't? Name the thing.**

Story A used a formal device that Story B did not: it interspersed the narrative with formatted text blocks representing the official, bureaucratic records of the Equilibrium Registry. These records provided a cold, quantitative counterpoint to the human story being told.
