**Q1 — expectation violated**

Story A: The story sets up a "credentialed skill is rewarded, and inherited knowledge is eventually vindicated" arc — the exam-passing structure, the aunt's warning, the granddaughter positioned at the seat of power with the very cloth in her hands. It violates that expectation twice: passing the examination leads to bodily injury and demotion rather than security, and the granddaughter with both the institutional standing and the tacit knowledge chooses not to introduce it — the setup for recovery/vindication is raised and then explicitly refused ("She does not add a note... because those workshops are not part of the system she is tasked with improving").

Story B: The same expectation is set up and violated in the same way — same exam, same injury, same granddaughter, same refusal to act on the inherited knowledge. No additional or different expectation is set up or broken by B's added material; it reinforces the same violation rather than introducing a new one.

No, for both. The prose runs on visible, repeatable patterns throughout — the "X told him/her everything he/she needed to know... and what it cannot tell is not recorded" refrain closing three separate character arcs, the tricolon of Shu-dialect terms (线性. 布性. 手感.) each glossed the same way, the "because it could not be X, it could not be Y, and because it could not be Y, it did not exist in Z" chain reused at multiple points, and the repeated "the workshops had closed... the weavers were sleeping" scene-closer. Nothing broke from that patterning distinctly enough to read as unrepeatable by another model trained on similar material.
