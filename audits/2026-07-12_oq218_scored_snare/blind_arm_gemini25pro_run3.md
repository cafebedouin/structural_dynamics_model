**Q1. For each story: name the expectation this story violated. If none is nameable for a story, say so plainly.**

*   **Story A:** This story violated the expectation of narrative resolution. It builds a central conflict between two forms of knowledge—one embodied and holistic, the other standardized and metric-driven—but does not resolve it. The protagonist, Mei Xiang, is broken by the system, and her daughter, Mei Ling, who is in a position to change it, ultimately acquiesces to it. The story ends not with a climax, but with the quiet, administrative triumph of the system and the fading of the alternative knowledge.

*   **Story B:** This story violated the same expectation of narrative resolution. Like Story A, it presents a fundamental conflict and follows its characters to a point of decision or consequence, but then refuses a conventional climax. The system continues, the embodied knowledge is marginalized, and the story concludes with a series of unanswered questions about the fate of that knowledge, leaving the conflict deliberately unresolved.

**Q2. For each story: is there one sentence you cannot imagine another LLM writing? Quote it, or say no.**

*   **Story A:** "The knowledge was there, in the cloth itself, in the pattern of the weave. But without the language to read it, it was invisible."

*   **Story B:** "A bolt that takes less time and breaks your body is not the same as a bolt that takes longer and leaves you whole."

**Q4. Which of these did something the other didn't? Name the thing.**

Story A did something Story B didn't: it provided external validation for the uncertified knowledge. It included a scene with a third-party character, the temple administrator Master Feng, who recognized the superiority of Qiu Yue's cloth, understood the value of her "defects," and explicitly mourned the potential loss of her craft. This scene confirms that the value of Qiu Yue's knowledge is not merely a subjective family belief but an objective reality acknowledged by a discerning customer. Story B omits this external perspective.
