# The Incomplete Derivation Problem: When Theoretical Physics Meets Structural Uncertainty

## I. The Pattern That Demands Explanation

For three years, theoretical physicists have proposed that magnetic monopoles—hypothetical particles carrying isolated magnetic charge—might exist not as the equal-mass north/south pole pairs predicted by every prior model, but as a complete magnetic sector mirroring the electric sector's lepton-hadron hierarchy. The Non-Minimal Composite Charge Conjecture (NMCC) predicts magnetic particles at 2.4 GeV and 4.4 TeV, iron-shell electromagnetic shielding below 580,000 K, and applications to early supermassive black hole formation, dark matter, and planetary magnetic anomalies.

The framework makes testable predictions. NASA's Psyche mission will arrive at metallic asteroid 16 Psyche in 2029, where NMCC predicts diamagnetic element depletion contradicting standard siderophile chemistry. BepiColombo is currently acquiring high-precision magnetometry data at Mercury, where NMCC predicts dipole offset scaling with solar proximity. These are clean binary tests with no overlapping parameter space.

Yet the framework rests on five explicitly incomplete derivations flagged by the authors themselves: magnetic QCD confinement dynamics (Ω:MassRatio), cosmological abundance calculations (Ω:Abundance), magnetic weak boson spectrum (Ω:Chirality), dark matter halo density profiles (Ω:HaloProfile), and relationship to inflationary cosmology (Ω:Inflation). These are not peripheral technical details—they are load-bearing structural elements determining whether the 2.4 GeV and 4.4 TeV mass predictions represent physics or numerology.

This creates an unusual situation: a theoretical framework transparent about its incompleteness, making falsifiable predictions, while the mathematical foundations required to evaluate those predictions remain uncomputed. The question is not whether NMCC is correct—that awaits empirical data—but what this pattern reveals about how theoretical physics handles structured uncertainty.

## II. Three Structural Tensions

### A. Dimensional Analogy vs. Lagrangian Derivation

**Documented Pattern:**

The NMCC mass predictions derive from dimensional scaling rather than magnetic sector Lagrangian first principles. The magnetic lepton mass (~2.4 GeV) is calculated by scaling the electron mass by the ratio of magnetic to electric fine structure constants (α_m/α_e ≈ 34/0.0073 ≈ 4658), yielding m_e × 4658 ≈ 2.38 GeV. The magnetic hadron mass (~4.4 TeV) applies the proton/electron mass ratio (1836) to the magnetic lepton: 2.38 GeV × 1836 ≈ 4.37 TeV.

This dimensional analysis assumes the 1836 proton/electron mass ratio—determined by QCD confinement dynamics in the electric sector where the strong coupling α_s ≈ 0.1—transfers directly to the magnetic sector where α_m ≈ 34. However, QCD confinement is a non-perturbative phenomenon where binding energy scales depend critically on the coupling constant. Whether confinement dynamics at α_m ≈ 34 produce the same mass ratios as at α_s ≈ 0.1 requires computing the magnetic QCD scale Λ_QCD^(m), which remains undone (Ω:MassRatio).

**Why Simpler Explanations Are Insufficient:**

One might argue this is standard practice—dimensional analysis often guides theoretical development before rigorous derivations. However, three elements distinguish NMCC from typical dimensional scaling:

1. **Coupling regime divergence**: The electric sector operates in perturbative QCD (α_s ≈ 0.1); the magnetic sector operates at α_m ≈ 34, well into strong coupling where perturbative methods fail and lattice QCD becomes necessary. The mass ratio could plausibly differ by factors of 2-10.

2. **Empirical testability**: Unlike dimensional estimates for phenomena currently beyond experimental reach, NMCC makes predictions testable by 2029 (Psyche mission) and ongoing BepiColombo measurements. The dimensional analysis is not a placeholder awaiting better technology—it's the foundation for near-term empirical claims.

3. **Downstream dependencies**: The 4.4 TeV mass prediction feeds directly into the early SMBH formation mechanism (mass-ordered precipitation requires the magnetic hadron to freeze out earlier than baryons) and the dark matter interpretation (shell shielding temperature depends on binding energies derived from these masses). If the mass ratio calculation fails, multiple applications collapse simultaneously.

**What Distinguishes This Case:**

Standard dimensional analysis in physics typically operates in one of two regimes: (a) order-of-magnitude estimates where factors of 2-10 don't matter, or (b) scaling relations where the functional form matters but the coefficient is fitted to data. NMCC operates in neither regime—it makes specific numerical predictions (2.4 GeV, 4.4 TeV) that determine falsifiability while acknowledging the derivational foundation is incomplete.

The framework is structurally unusual: transparent about incompleteness, empirically testable, and dependent on whether α_m ≈ 34 produces qualitatively similar or qualitatively different confinement dynamics than α_s ≈ 0.1. This is not a minor technical uncertainty—it's the difference between physics and numerology.

### B. CPT Discriminator vs. Conventional Models

**Documented Pattern:**

Every prior magnetic monopole model—from Dirac (1931) through 't Hooft-Polyakov (1974) to modern ATLAS searches—assumes CPT symmetry requires north and south magnetic poles to have identical masses as charge conjugates. NMCC predicts instead that magnetic charge comes in a mass hierarchy (magnetic lepton/magnetic hadron) analogous to electric charge (electron/proton), with north and south poles as distinct particle species rather than CPT conjugates.

This is NMCC's most structurally novel claim. Current LHC searches at ATLAS look for monopole pairs produced via Drell-Yan or photon-fusion processes, assuming equal-mass north/south poles. ATLAS reported null results for monopole masses up to ~4 TeV using 34.4 fb⁻¹ of 13 TeV collision data (2019). However, these searches assume:

1. Equal-mass pole pairs (CPT conjugacy)
2. Electromagnetic interaction cross-sections
3. Highly ionizing signatures in the detector

NMCC violates all three assumptions: it predicts a 2.4 GeV / 4.4 TeV mass hierarchy, electromagnetic shielding below T_shell ≈ 580,000 K (far below LHC collision energies but relevant for terrestrial detection), and GeV-scale nuclear transition signatures rather than ionization tracks.

**Why This Matters:**

The CPT discriminator provides a clean falsification pathway independent of completing the five derivations. If monopoles are detected and have:

- **Equal masses**: NMCC is falsified; conventional models are vindicated
- **Hierarchical masses matching 1836 ratio**: NMCC gains support; conventional models require revision
- **Hierarchical masses with different ratio**: Both frameworks require revision; new physics indicated

This is a genuine structural distinction, not a parameter variation. The mass hierarchy claim does not depend on whether the magnetic Λ_QCD calculation succeeds—it depends on whether magnetic charge behaves like a conserved quantum number (admitting mass hierarchies like electric charge) or like a CPT-conjugate pair (requiring mass degeneracy).

**Current Observational Status:**

No monopoles have been detected. The ATLAS null results do not discriminate between NMCC and conventional models because:

1. If monopoles don't exist, both models are wrong
2. If monopoles exist but are electromagnetically shielded (NMCC claim), ATLAS wouldn't detect them
3. If monopoles exist at masses > 4 TeV, ATLAS sensitivity drops rapidly above this threshold

The Cabrera event (February 14, 1982)—a single candidate monopole detection never reproduced—remains unexplained by any model. NMCC does not explain this event either, but it suggests searches have targeted the wrong mass range, interaction channels, and temperature regimes.

**Institutional Implications:**

The CPT discriminator reveals a coordination pattern in experimental monopole searches. All major searches (ATLAS, MoEDAL, IceCube) assume equal-mass poles, not because this is empirically established, but because it follows from CPT symmetry—a theoretical prior. If that prior is wrong, decades of null results may reflect search strategy rather than monopole absence.

This is not conspiracy—it's standard scientific practice. Theoretical priors guide experimental design. However, when those priors remain untested assumptions rather than empirically validated constraints, null results become less informative. NMCC's mass hierarchy prediction, if correct, would mean existing searches were well-designed for detecting something that doesn't exist.

### C. Incomplete Derivations as Epistemic Load

**Documented Pattern:**

The NMCC preprint explicitly identifies five incomplete derivations:

1. **Ω:MassRatio**: Magnetic Λ_QCD computation using α_m ≈ 34
2. **Ω:Abundance**: NMCC cosmological abundance vs. observed dark matter density Ω_dm ≈ 0.27
3. **Ω:Chirality**: Magnetic weak boson masses and couplings in right-handed SU(2)_R sector
4. **Ω:HaloProfile**: Predicted dark matter halo density profile vs. rotation curves and gravitational lensing
5. **Ω:Inflation**: Relationship to inflationary cosmology and monopole dilution

These are flagged with omega notation, explicitly marked as unresolved. The authors do not claim these derivations are straightforward or that completion is imminent—they acknowledge these as open problems requiring substantial theoretical work.

**This creates an unusual epistemic structure:**

From the perspective of **theoretical physics community standards**, the incomplete derivations represent responsible scientific practice. The framework is presented as a conjecture, not a complete theory. The gaps are disclosed, not hidden. Empirical predictions are made despite incompleteness, allowing the framework to be tested before full mathematical closure.

From the perspective of **empirical research investment**, the incomplete derivations create risk. The Psyche mission (arrival 2029) could test NMCC's diamagnetic element depletion prediction, but if Ω:MassRatio resolves inconsistently—showing that α_m ≈ 34 produces qualitatively different confinement dynamics—the prediction's theoretical foundation collapses. BepiColombo magnetometry data are being acquired now, but without Ω:HaloProfile, the dark matter interpretation cannot be rigorously evaluated.

**Why This Tension Matters:**

The incomplete derivations are not independent. Consider the dependency chain:

- **Ω:MassRatio** determines whether 4.4 TeV is justified → feeds into **Ω:Abundance** (relic density depends on freeze-out temperature, which depends on mass)
- **Ω:Abundance** determines whether NMCC particles constitute dark matter → feeds into **Ω:HaloProfile** (halo structure depends on particle properties)
- **Ω:Chirality** determines magnetic weak sector → affects **Ω:Inflation** (weak interactions during reheating influence monopole production)

If any one derivation fails, downstream applications become suspect. This is not a gracefully degrading argument structure—it's a cascade dependency where one failure propagates through the network.

**Comparison to Alternative Frameworks:**

The early SMBH formation problem now has multiple competing explanations:

1. **Primordial black holes**: Form from density fluctuations in the early universe; require specific inflationary power spectrum
2. **Dark stars**: Powered by dark matter annihilation; require high dark matter densities in first halos
3. **Direct collapse**: Gas clouds collapse directly to 10⁴-10⁶ M_☉ black holes; require suppression of star formation
4. **NMCC mass-ordered precipitation**: 4.4 TeV magnetic hadrons freeze out earlier than 938 MeV baryons; requires completing Ω:MassRatio and Ω:Abundance

NMCC is the only framework where the seeding mechanism depends on incomplete derivations. Primordial black holes, dark stars, and direct collapse all have fully specified (though not necessarily correct) theoretical foundations. This creates asymmetric epistemic risk: if NMCC's derivations fail, it loses explanatory power; if competing frameworks fail, they fail on empirical grounds, not mathematical incompleteness.

**What Makes This Structural Rather Than Individual:**

The incomplete derivation pattern is not unique to NMCC. Theoretical physics regularly proposes frameworks before full mathematical closure—string theory operated for decades with incomplete non-perturbative formulations; loop quantum gravity still lacks a complete semiclassical limit; supersymmetry makes predictions despite unresolved hierarchy and cosmological constant problems.

What distinguishes NMCC is the **temporal proximity of empirical tests to derivational gaps**. Psyche arrives in 2029; BepiColombo data are being acquired now; JWST SMBH observations are already published. The framework is not proposing tests for future generations—it's making predictions testable within 5 years while acknowledging the mathematical foundations remain incomplete.

This creates a coordination pattern across theoretical physics: frameworks compete for empirical validation before achieving mathematical closure. The incentive structure rewards testable predictions over derivational completeness. This is not necessarily wrong—empirical constraints can guide theoretical development more effectively than pure mathematical exploration. However, it creates a structural tension between **transparent incompleteness** (scientific integrity) and **premature empirical investment** (resource allocation).

## IV. Alternative Explanations Considered

### A. Standard Elite Privilege Explanation (For Institutional Coordination)

**Simpler Explanation:**
The coordination pattern in experimental monopole searches (all assuming equal-mass poles, all targeting electromagnetic signatures) reflects standard scientific practice guided by theoretical priors, not deliberate exclusion of alternative models.

**Why Insufficient:**
Three elements distinguish this case from standard theoretical guidance:

1. **CPT symmetry is an assumption, not an empirical constraint**: No experiment has ever detected magnetic monopoles, so the equal-mass pole requirement comes from theoretical symmetry arguments, not from measurement. NMCC questions whether CPT applies to magnetic charge the same way it applies to electric charge.

2. **Alternative mass hierarchies were not systematically excluded**: The ATLAS search design could have included hierarchical mass scenarios without additional cost—the detector sensitivity and analysis pipeline would work for both equal-mass and hierarchical-mass cases. The choice to optimize for equal-mass poles reflects theoretical consensus, not experimental necessity.

3. **Null results are interpreted as monopole absence rather than search strategy limitation**: When ATLAS finds no monopoles up to 4 TeV assuming equal masses, the conclusion is "monopoles don't exist in this mass range," not "monopoles with these specific properties don't exist in this mass range." This conflates model-dependent search strategy with model-independent empirical constraint.

**Key Differentiator:**
Standard theoretical guidance becomes coordination when alternative hypotheses are systematically excluded from experimental design despite being testable with existing technology. NMCC's mass hierarchy prediction could be tested by ATLAS—it simply hasn't been, because the theoretical prior (CPT symmetry) seemed more compelling than the alternative (mass hierarchy analogous to leptons/hadrons).

### B. Standard Dimensional Analysis Explanation (For Mass Predictions)

**Simpler Explanation:**
Using dimensional scaling to estimate particle masses before rigorous derivation is common practice in theoretical physics—it provides order-of-magnitude guidance for experimental searches and identifies promising research directions.

**Why Insufficient:**
Three elements distinguish NMCC's dimensional analysis from typical scaling arguments:

1. **Testability timeline**: Most dimensional estimates in theoretical physics guide searches for phenomena decades away from experimental reach (e.g., GUT-scale monopoles at 10¹⁷ GeV, proton decay lifetimes > 10³⁴ years). NMCC makes predictions testable by 2029 (Psyche) and with ongoing data acquisition (BepiColombo). The dimensional analysis is not a placeholder—it's the foundation for imminent empirical tests.

2. **Coupling regime extrapolation**: Standard dimensional analysis operates within a single coupling regime (perturbative or non-perturbative) or interpolates between well-understood limits. NMCC extrapolates from perturbative QCD (α_s ≈ 0.1) to strong coupling (α_m ≈ 34), crossing a regime boundary where perturbative methods fail and non-perturbative dynamics dominate. This is not interpolation—it's extrapolation across a phase transition.

3. **Cascade dependencies**: Typical dimensional estimates stand alone—if the estimate is wrong by a factor of 10, the error is localized. NMCC's 4.4 TeV prediction feeds into early SMBH formation (requires mass-ordered precipitation), dark matter abundance (requires specific freeze-out temperature), and planetary magnetism (requires specific binding energies). If the mass ratio calculation fails, multiple applications collapse simultaneously.

**Key Differentiator:**
Dimensional analysis becomes problematic when it substitutes for rigorous derivation in empirically testable regimes where the physics is qualitatively different from the regime being scaled. NMCC's α_m ≈ 34 coupling is not a small perturbation around α_s ≈ 0.1—it's a qualitatively different regime where confinement dynamics could plausibly produce different mass ratios.

### C. Multiple Competing Mechanisms Explanation (For Early SMBH Formation)

**Simpler Explanation:**
The JWST early SMBH problem has multiple proposed solutions (primordial black holes, dark stars, direct collapse, NMCC precipitation). This is normal scientific practice—competing hypotheses coexist until empirical data discriminate between them.

**Why Insufficient:**
One element distinguishes NMCC from competing mechanisms:

**Derivational completeness asymmetry**: Primordial black holes, dark stars, and direct collapse all have fully specified theoretical foundations. Their predictions may be wrong, but the mathematical frameworks are complete. NMCC's early SMBH mechanism depends on Ω:MassRatio (determines 4.4 TeV mass scale) and Ω:Abundance (determines relic density available for precipitation)—both explicitly incomplete.

This creates asymmetric epistemic risk: competing mechanisms can be falsified on empirical grounds (e.g., if JWST observations rule out the required initial conditions), but NMCC can be falsified on *derivational* grounds (e.g., if Ω:MassRatio shows α_m ≈ 34 produces different mass ratios than assumed). The framework could fail before empirical testing even begins.

**Key Differentiator:**
When one competing mechanism depends on incomplete derivations while others have complete theoretical foundations, the incompleteness becomes structural rather than incidental. NMCC is not proposing an alternative mechanism within the same mathematical framework—it's proposing a mechanism contingent on whether the mathematical framework itself closes consistently.

Regardless of whether NMCC's incomplete derivations resolve consistently, the structural pattern reveals gaps in how theoretical physics handles structured uncertainty. The following actions address these gaps without requiring acceptance of NMCC's specific claims:

**Justification**: Current searches assume CPT-conjugate equal-mass poles based on theoretical symmetry arguments, not empirical constraints.
