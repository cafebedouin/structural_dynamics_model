## Abstract

Deferential Realism (DR) was developed and validated against corpora generated by language models trained on human-produced text and classified using axioms calibrated against human-recognizable cases of coordination and extraction. This origin produces a hidden anthropocentric assumption: that the framework's claims are claims about *human* classification of constraints. We argue that the framework's load-bearing claims are *structural* — they apply to any system whose internal states update differentially under position-relative net flows in a power-stratified environment, regardless of substrate. The right physics analogy is universality class rather than Lorentz invariance: different microscopic dynamics (different transformation functions, different observer architectures) flow to the same coarse-grained orbit structure, which is why robustness across functional forms is evidence *for* the structural claim rather than against the rigidity of any specific transformation. Removing the anthropocentric assumption makes the framework both more falsifiable and more applicable: it converts human-subjects validation from a gold standard into one pillar among several, opens a research program genuinely accessible to solo researchers using AI tools, and connects DR to active questions in AI alignment and multi-agent systems.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 clarifies three foundational issues that became visible in the process of stating the structural claim cleanly: the sequence of operations (Mountain status is terminal, not initial), the conflation of two different kinds of consensus fragility, and the universality-class basis for the framework's transformation law. Section 3 articulates the structural claim, proposes an operational criterion for what counts as an observer class, and distinguishes DR's predictions from adjacent theories (system justification, Weber-Fechner perception). Section 4 lays out a cross-class validation program. Section 5 applies the framework to agent systems and includes a pre-registered falsification protocol. Section 6 catalogues limitations and open questions. Section 7 concludes.

**Keywords:** deferential realism, observer classes, validation methodology, AI agents, structural classification, universality class

## 1. The Question

DR as currently published makes claims that *sound* general — claims about how classification depends on observer position, about how power modulates perception of extraction, about the cohomological structure of perspectival disagreement. But the empirical apparatus is human-specific in two ways that are easy to miss. The corpora are LLM-generated from human-produced training data, encoding human categorial habits even where the ontology is meant to be neutral. The axioms — saturation at power extremes, sign-flip at institutional dominance — were selected against cases of coordination and extraction recognizable to human observers, even though the principled motivations for those axioms may be substrate-neutral.

So when the framework reports that 74% of constraints are gauge-invariant orbit families, that `[tangled_rope]` dominates with ~62% of corpus mass, that the institutional observer carries 97% of spectral weight in classification disputes — these findings are validated against humans, by humans, using human-recognizable structure. The framework's *form* is neutral; its *substantiation* is not.

This raises a question that becomes pressing once it is noticed: if the framework's claims are general, how would we know? And if they are not general — if they are anthropocentric in a way that was not recognized — what would the more honest, narrower version of the framework look like?

The position this paper develops: DR's *core* claims are structural in a precise sense (Section 3), but its *empirical grounding* is currently anthropocentric in a way that allows multiple different theories to be confused with each other. Validation across observer classes is what would distinguish them. That validation is more accessible than human-subjects testing for a solo researcher with AI tools, and it is the design that breaks confounds human-only testing cannot.

## 2. Three Clarifications

Three issues had to be sorted out before the structural claim could be stated cleanly. Each is partly implicit in existing DR work but had not been articulated in the form below.

### 2.1 Sequence: Mountain status is terminal, not initial

A natural way to read DR is as a two-stage decision procedure: first ask whether something is a Mountain (a constraint position-invariant in classification); if not, characterize its topography across positions. This reading is operationally backwards. The engine does not run a Mountain check first. It indexes positions, computes classifications at each, and only then asks whether the resulting orbit is constant. Mountain status is the intersection of two conditions both derived from the indexing process: the local classifications converge AND the convergence survives stress tests (Boltzmann factorization, beneficiary check, FCR/FSM detection).

This has a methodological consequence worth stating explicitly. The framework's default epistemic posture is presheaf. Mountain status is a *credential a constraint earns* by surviving universal stress-testing, not a starting point or a default. The asymmetry of evidentiary cost is part of the framework's signature: to classify something as Mountain, one owes evidence at every observer position; to classify something as presheaf, one owes only evidence that the orbit is non-constant. This is why "False Mountain" is the framework's most dangerous failure mode — it claims invariance without paying the universal-stress-test cost.

For audience-facing framings of DR, this matters. Saying "Mountains are constraints everyone agrees on" pedagogically inverts what the framework is doing, because it presents agreement as evidence rather than as a finding that requires further structural verification. The framework's actual move is more demanding: it treats consensus skeptically, requires factorization across power and scope, requires absence of identifiable beneficiaries, and only then issues the Mountain credential.

### 2.2 Fragility: parametric versus epistemic

The current framework's "fragile presheaf" classification (Arakelov height, Fisher curvature) fuses two conceptually distinct properties:

*Parametric fragility:* the constraint sits near a classification threshold such that small parameter perturbations flip classification. The Fisher curvature analysis measures this directly.

*Epistemic opacity:* the constraint's underlying disagreement is concealed — surface consensus is achieved by some observers being naturalized into not-seeing. The FCR (False CI Rope) and FSM (False Summit Mountain) checks try to catch this.

These coexist in the framework's output but they are different beasts. A constraint can be parametrically fragile without being epistemically opaque (the boundary is close but the latent disagreement is visible to anyone looking). A constraint can be epistemically opaque without being parametrically fragile (the cover story is strong enough that disagreement does not register at standard parameter settings). They produce surface consensus that breaks under different perturbations: parametric fragility breaks under parameter perturbation; epistemic opacity breaks under *indexing* perturbation — adding an observer position that was previously excluded.

This distinction maps directly onto Section 4's validation pillars. Multi-LLM testing perturbs parameters within human-derived classification habits — it tests parametric robustness. Cross-class testing (RL agents, animal data, embodied AI) perturbs the index by adding observer classes that were not previously included — it tests epistemic opacity. This is why cross-class testing is the more diagnostic of the two: parametric robustness is consistent with anthropocentric inheritance, while indexing robustness is not.

A cross-tabulation of the two fragility measures across the full 3,314-constraint corpus tests this distinction directly. Splitting the corpus by parametric fragility (Arakelov height above the corpus median of 0.0117) and epistemic opacity (FCR/FSM detection) yields a 2×2 table with cells 412 (both), 1243 (parametric only), 494 (epistemic only), and 1164 (neither) over N=3,313 (one Arakelov-null excluded). χ²=10.01, p=0.0016, Cramér's V=0.055. The result is a **negative association** — parametric fragility and epistemic opacity point in *opposite* directions, not approximately the same direction.

The mechanistic explanation is direct. FCR-detected constraints have median Arakelov height 0.004 — only 45.7% are above the corpus median. Non-FCR constraints have median 0.012, 51.5% above the median. The cover-story mechanism resolves the MaxEnt distribution: a structurally forced institutional cover story collapses the distribution to consensus, moving the constraint *away* from the parametric boundary as a side effect of concealing the latent disagreement. Epistemic opacity produces parametric stability. Parametric fragility (near-boundary, visible disagreement) and epistemic opacity (far-from-boundary, disagreement concealed by the cover story) are not merely distinct properties — they are inversely related, each catching what the other misses.

This is a stronger result than v2's conjectural framing anticipated. §2.2 conjectured that the two diagnostics measure different things; the corpus demonstrates they index *opposite* failure modes. The full contingency table and quadrant examples are in `docs/results/fragility_cross_tab.md`.

A sign-flip analysis of the tangled_rope subset (N=2,245) provides a second independent empirical trace of the same cover-story machinery, operating at a different layer of the pipeline. The analysis computes institutional sign-flip rates — the fraction of constraints where the powerless and institutional positions have opposite-sign classifications — using two measurement approaches: chi-based (per-position chi metric, capturing net flow asymmetry directly) and type-based (per-position classification type collapsed to ternary). The chi-based institutional sign-flip rate for tangled_rope is 62.4%; the type-based rate for the same metric is 0.3%, a gap of 62.1 percentage points. The same pattern holds across all constraint populations: rope (90.9% chi vs. 0.0% type), snare (77.1% chi vs. 5.4% type). The gap is a consistent property of the corpus (see `docs/results/tangled_rope_sign_flip.md`).

The mechanism is the cover-story apparatus operating between layers. The chi metric captures the underlying flow asymmetry — the routing decision the structural-role indexing makes (§2.3). The type metric captures the post-classification surface, where the FCR mechanism and naturalization typing have resolved the chi-level signal into apparent uniformity. When a powerless-position constraint is typed as `naturalized` rather than `snare`, the extraction experience is reclassified as background, collapsing that position to neutral in the type vector and erasing type-based sign-flip. The chi signal remains visible at the flow layer; the type signal is suppressed at the classification layer. This is the same mechanism the cross-tabulation identified from a different direction: FCR-detected constraints sit away from the parametric boundary because the cover story has resolved their MaxEnt distribution. Both findings show cover stories producing surface-level stability by resolving an underlying disagreement structure — one at the parameter level, one at the classification level.

The naturalization sensitivity check quantifies one component of this erasure. Treating `naturalized` type labels as extractive (−1) rather than neutral (0) raises the type-based institutional sign-flip rate for tangled_rope from 0.3% to 7.5%, a shift of 7.2 percentage points. Even under this alternative, the chi-vs-type gap remains 54.9 percentage points. Naturalization accounts for a portion of the gap — the portion where the cover story explicitly reclassifies extraction as natural background — but most of the gap reflects other components of the cover-story apparatus that resolve chi-level disagreement into type-level consensus through other routes.

The cross-tabulation and the chi-vs-type gap constitute independent empirical support for §2.2's distinction at two different points in the pipeline. The cross-tabulation demonstrates that parametric fragility and epistemic opacity index *opposite* failure modes — the diagnostics are not redundant, and their inverse relationship has a mechanistic explanation in the cover-story machinery. The chi-vs-type gap demonstrates the mechanism directly: cover stories erase chi-level flow asymmetry at the type-classification step, resolving what the chi metric detects as structural disagreement into what the type metric records as apparent consensus. Each diagnostic catches what the other cannot precisely because they measure at different resolutions of the same underlying structure.

The Cultural Cognition / δ-band work in DR v6.11 reaches toward the opacity dimension while Arakelov height stays on the fragility dimension. Pulling them apart explicitly in the formalism is a useful next step.

### 2.3 The transformation law's basis: universality class, not derivational rigidity

V1 of this paper claimed the DR transformation function (sigmoid f(d)) was forced by its postulates in the way the Lorentz transformation is forced by the invariance of c. This was wrong, and the alternative-functions test in DR v6.11 is the giveaway. Six functional forms — sigmoid, square root, quadratic, piecewise linear, step function, and two sign-flip-removing controls — produce orbit structures with Jaccard 0.685–0.828 against the sigmoid baseline. If the postulates uniquely forced the sigmoid, alternatives should fail sharply. They do not. They produce qualitatively similar orbit structure with quantitatively different parameters.

The right physics analogy is **universality class**, not Lorentz invariance. In statistical mechanics, microscopically different systems flow to the same coarse-grained behavior because they share a symmetry/dimensionality class — the Ising model, binary alloys, and liquid-vapor critical phenomena exhibit the same critical exponents not because their microscopic dynamics are uniquely forced, but because they share enough symmetry properties that the renormalization-group flow takes them to the same fixed point. The microscopic details get washed out; the macroscopic invariants survive.

The DR analog: any transformation function that respects two qualitative properties — bounded sensitivity at extremes (saturation) and sign reversal at structural dominance (sign-flip) — produces the same qualitative orbit structure (cohomological obstruction, gauge orbits, spectral dominance, the H¹ gap). The functional form is not uniquely determined; the universality class is. This is the correct interpretation of the alternative-functions robustness result, and it is a stronger claim than the Lorentz analogy was reaching for: it says the structural invariants are properties of the symmetry class of power-stratified classification, not properties of any specific psychological model.

This shift forces a harder question: are saturation and sign-flip themselves *structural* properties of power-stratified systems, or are they generalized human cognitive habits that happen to produce orbit structure? V1 asserted the former without argument. We attempt the argument here.

**Saturation as boundedness.** Saturation is the weaker of the two postulates. It is a regularization assumption: any system whose response to a continuous variable is bounded (perception, action, evaluation, classification) will exhibit diminishing sensitivity at extremes. This is a generic property of bounded functions on bounded domains, not a substantive claim about cognition. Weber-Fechner is one instance; logistic growth, neural saturation, market price ceilings are others. Saturation does little independent work; sign-flip is the load-bearing postulate.

**Sign-flip as structural-role routing.** The natural derivation of sign-flip is through net flow asymmetry: a constraint imposing flow F between structurally distinguishable positions produces positive F_A for the beneficiary and negative F_A for the victim, so any system tracking position-relative flows will classify the constraint with opposite signs at opposed positions. This is the right intuition for zero-sum constraints, and the predictions it generates in that case are correct. The mechanism the framework actually implements, however, is not flow conservation. It is threshold routing on structural-role indexing.

The chi metric is computed as:

χ = ε × f(d_eff) × σ

where ε is the constraint's base extractiveness, σ is a scope modifier, and f is the sigmoid function with parameters L = −0.20, U = 1.50, d₀ = 0.50, k = 6.00 (`prolog/config.pl:129–132`). The key element is d_eff — the effective directionality value derived from the structural role of the observer position relative to the constraint. Directionality is computed from whether the position is a beneficiary or victim of the constraint: beneficiary positions receive low d values (institutional beneficiary at canonical context: BaseD = 0.15 from `power_role_heuristic`, reduced to d_eff ≈ 0.12 by arbitrage exit modulation); victim positions receive high d values (powerless victim: d_eff ≈ 0.90–0.95). These assignments are determined by the `power_role_heuristic` and `exit_modulation` tables at `prolog/constraint_indexing.pl:430–455`.

The sigmoid has a zero-crossing derived from its parameters:

d_zero = d₀ − ln(U / (−L)) / k = 0.50 − ln(7.5) / 6.00 ≈ 0.1642

Below d_zero, f(d) < 0 and chi routes negative — the position is classified on the coordination/beneficiary side of the threshold. Above d_zero, f(d) > 0 and chi routes positive — the position is classified on the extraction/victim side. Sign-flip emerges not from opposite flows but from opposite sides of this threshold: the canonical institutional beneficiary position sits at d ≈ 0.12 (below d_zero, negative chi, routed to coordination) while the canonical powerless victim position sits at d ≈ 0.90–0.95 (above d_zero, positive chi, routed to extraction).

The 62.4% institutional sign-flip rate observed in the tangled_rope subset (`docs/results/tangled_rope_sign_flip.md`) is a corpus-composition measurement, not a structural prediction of the framework. Inspection of the 3,314-constraint corpus confirms perfect threshold separation: all 1,401 tangled_rope constraints exhibiting institutional sign-flip have d_institutional ≤ 0.16 (below d_zero); all 844 without institutional sign-flip have d_institutional ≥ 0.18 (above d_zero), with zero ambiguous cases. The 37.6% without institutional sign-flip are constraints where an explicit `directionality_override` fact in the testset places d_institutional above the zero-crossing — modeling structural situations in which the institutional observer does not occupy the canonical beneficiary role for that constraint. The framework predicts the *pattern* (sign-flip when institutional d < d_zero, no sign-flip when not), not any specific rate. The rate varies with corpus composition: it measures what fraction of constraints assign the institutional observer to the canonical beneficiary structural role, which is a fact about the corpus, not a consequence of the axioms.

This is the metrics-as-routing principle (`docs/metrics_as_routing.md`) applied to the chi metric itself. Chi does not measure objective flow magnitude — it routes observer positions through a sigmoid threshold and returns a signed value that determines which classification category the constraint falls into at that position. The routing is informed by flow asymmetry in its design (the heuristic assigns low d to beneficiary positions and high d to victim positions), but the mechanism is threshold routing on structural-role indexing, not flow conservation.

The consequence for the universality-class claim follows directly. What v4 stated as a claim about flow-tracking can now be stated more precisely: the framework predicts the [+, +, −, +] chi pattern in any corpus where institutional-equivalent positions are assigned structural roles placing d_institutional below d_zero. Whether RL agents, animal coalitions, or other non-human systems occupy positions that generate role assignments below and above d_zero is the empirical question. The framework does not need to take a position on the underlying physics of agency — it needs only to be a useful routing instrument with a routing structure that replicates in non-human systems. The §5.5 protocol tests whether it does.

## 3. The Structural Claim

With the foundations clarified, the structural claim can be stated cleanly.

### 3.1 The claim

DR claims: in any system where (a) entities differ in their capacity to influence outcomes and to be influenced by them (power), (b) they have differential access to alternative state-spaces (exit), and (c) their internal states update differentially under position-relative net flows from environmental constraints, the structure of how their classifications relate across positions is non-arbitrary. Specifically, classifications exhibit saturation (bounded sensitivity at extremes) and sign-flip (opposite-sign classifications at oppositely-flowing positions). Cohomological obstruction to global section measures the structure of forced disagreement; gauge orbits define equivalence classes of constraint behavior; spectral decomposition identifies which positions carry the variance.

So stated, the framework is not a theory about human cognition. It is a theory about the structural mechanics of classification in power-stratified environments, where humans are one substrate. The predictions should hold for any system meeting condition (c) above operating under (a) and (b).

This is bigger than the framework as currently published explicitly states, and importantly more falsifiable. Restricting the claim to humans makes it harder to falsify because there is only one observer class to test. Allowing the claim to range across observer classes — humans, LLMs, RL agents, animal coalitions, embodied AI — makes failure detectable from any class. Section 5 specifies the falsification test in operational form.

### 3.2 What counts as an observer class

V1 left the observer-class threshold underspecified, which several reviewers flagged as making the structural claim hard to test. The criterion, grounded in the structural-role indexing the framework's machinery already uses:

A system constitutes an observer class for DR purposes if its state update function has nonzero derivative with respect to beneficiary/victim structural role — that is, if the system's internal state changes differently depending on whether it occupies the beneficiary or victim position relative to a constraint. This criterion is derived from what the `power_role_heuristic` and `exit_modulation` tables already require (`prolog/constraint_indexing.pl:430–455`): they assign different directionality values based on structural role (beneficiary → low d → negative chi; victim → high d → positive chi), and the chi computation routes those positions to opposite sides of the sigmoid threshold. An entity can be indexed by the framework only if its structural-role assignment produces meaningfully different d values across positions — which is the role-derivative condition stated operationally.

The three components from v4 (position-distinguishability, differential state-update, outcome-correlation) remain necessary conditions but are not sufficient. They identify that an entity is *in* a power-stratified environment and responds to it; the role-derivative criterion identifies that the entity's response is *specific to structural role*, not merely to physical position or general environmental variation.

Worked examples:
- *Thermostats fail.* Their state updates track physical temperature, not structural role. A thermostat nominally on the beneficiary side of a temperature-regulation regime and one nominally on the victim side update identically.
- *PID controllers fail.* Even position-sensitive PID controllers have state updates that are functions of the physical quantity being regulated — not functions of the controller's beneficiary/victim relationship to the constraint governing that quantity. The role derivative is zero.
- *RL agents pass.* Policy and value-function updates depend on reward structure, which is directly tied to whether the agent benefits or bears costs from environmental constraints. The role derivative is nonzero by construction.
- *Markets are ambiguous.* Individual market participants pass (their state updates depend on whether they are buyers, sellers, or beneficiaries of price structures). The market as a whole has no single locus at which the role derivative can be evaluated.
- *Insect colonies likely pass.* Role-differentiated behavioral and physiological responses to environmental constraints vary with structural position.
- *LLMs in agent contexts pass.* Output distributions shift based on assigned role, with the shift tracking the structural properties of beneficiary and victim positions.

This criterion is independent of DR analysis — it can be applied without running DR — and is derivable from the machinery without adding external theory.

### 3.3 What DR predicts that adjacent theories don't

Several reviewers asked what DR contributes beyond existing frameworks: system justification (Jost, Banaji), Weber-Fechner perception, standpoint epistemology, motivated reasoning. The predictions overlap substantially at the qualitative level — all four predict that dominant positions perceive existing arrangements more favorably than subordinate positions. The distinctive content of DR is at the relational level.

System justification predicts that dominant positions rationalize dominance. DR predicts the *specific quantitative coupling* between dominant and subordinate classifications because they share a generative mechanism: the same constraint, the same flow structure, the same transformation, indexed at different positions. The classifications are not independently biased; they are forced into specific quantitative relations by the shared mechanism. Cohomological obstruction H¹ measures the strength of this coupling as a discrete invariant. System justification has no analog of H¹, no prediction about gap structure (the forbidden values 1 and 2 in the 4-observer case), no equivalence-class structure (gauge orbits).

Weber-Fechner predicts that perceptual response to a continuous variable is bounded and approximately logarithmic. DR's saturation postulate is consistent with Weber-Fechner but does not stop there: it predicts a specific kind of *asymmetric* saturation (sign-flip at structural dominance) that Weber-Fechner does not. The structural-role routing account in §2.3 is what distinguishes the two: Weber-Fechner is a property of perceptual systems; DR's sign-flip is a property of any system whose structural-role indexing places beneficiary positions below d_zero and victim positions above it, perceptual or not.

Standpoint epistemology and motivated reasoning are descriptive frameworks that name the phenomenon without formalizing it. DR's contribution at this level of comparison is the formalism — it converts the descriptive claim into a measurement instrument with discrete invariants, robustness tests, and pre-registered predictions.

The sharpest contribution-question is: what does DR predict that, if false, would falsify the framework while leaving the adjacent theories intact? Two candidates:

1. *H¹ distribution fingerprints.* The v2 framing of this prediction requires correction. Values {1, 2} being absent from the 4-observer H¹ distribution is combinatorially forbidden for *any* classification rule — it is arithmetic (partition enumeration), not a DR-distinctive prediction. Claiming it as a falsification criterion that would leave adjacent theories intact is incorrect. The correct DR predictions are two distribution fingerprints over the reachable values.

*(i) H¹=4 suppression.* Random 4-observer classification (uniform ternary baseline) predicts 22.2% of manifest presheaves at H¹=4. The 3,314-constraint corpus has 45 such constraints (1.4% of corpus) — a 16× underrepresentation (see `docs/results/h1_distribution_test.md`). The mechanistic explanation: H¹=4 requires the (2,2) partition — two pairs of observers agreeing on different types. The institutional sign-flip (U₃ classifying as rope when others classify as extraction) generates the (3,1) partition instead, systematically suppressing the (2,2) case.

*(ii) H¹=6 mass.* DR's 7-type ontology permits the partition (1-1-1-1) — all four observers assigning distinct types — which produces H¹=6. This is impossible in any ternary classification rule (pigeonhole: 4 observers, 3 values → at least 2 must agree), but DR's ontology generates it. The corpus confirms: 163 of 3,314 constraints (4.9%) have H¹=6. Note: `flow_asymmetry_test_v2.jsx` incorrectly lists {1, 2, **6**} as unreachable "for any classification rule" — only {1, 2} are combinatorially forbidden; H¹=6 is forbidden only in ternary models, not in DR's multi-type system.

System justification, motivated reasoning, and standpoint epistemology make no predictions about the *distribution shape* of pairwise disagreement counts across observer populations — they predict bias direction at individual positions, not coupling structure. Neither H¹=4 suppression nor H¹=6 mass follows from any of these frameworks' axioms, and neither would follow from an arbitrary 7-type classification rule that lacked DR's institutional sign-flip. These are decisive forks.

*(iii) Non-monotonic chi distribution.* The per-position chi distribution across the tangled_rope subset (N=2,245) exhibits a non-monotonic pattern: U₁ powerless is 100.0% positive, U₂ moderate is 100.0% positive, U₃ institutional is 62.4% negative, and U₄ analytical returns to 100.0% positive. The institutional position is the unique locus of chi-sign reversal; the analytical meta-observer realigns with the subordinate positions rather than with the institutional beneficiary (see `docs/results/tangled_rope_sign_flip.md`).

This distinguishes DR from all monotonic-power-effect theories. Standpoint epistemology, system justification, motivated reasoning, and Weber-Fechner all predict monotonic relationships with structural power — as a position becomes more dominant, the constraint's classification becomes more favorable. DR predicts localized non-monotonicity: sign-flip at the institutional position (the structural beneficiary), with the analytical meta-observer realigning with the subordinate interpretation rather than extending the institutional one. The 62.4% negative chi rate at U₃ specifically — and the 0.0% rate at U₄ — is the quantitative signature in this corpus. Any monotonic theory would expect U₄ (the highest-order observer) to have the most favorable (negative-chi) classification; the corpus shows U₄ has 100% positive chi, indistinguishable from the powerless position. The non-monotonic [+,+,−,+] shape is DR-distinctive in the same sense as H¹=4 suppression: it follows from the framework's specific observer ontology, not from any generic power-effect assumption.

A rate qualifier is needed here. The 62.4% institutional negative-chi rate is a corpus-composition measurement, not a structural prediction of the framework. As §2.3 establishes, it reports how many of the 2,245 tangled_rope constraints in this corpus were authored with the institutional observer in the canonical beneficiary role (d_institutional < d_zero ≈ 0.1642). In a corpus with more constraints assigning non-canonical institutional roles, the rate would differ. What DR predicts is the *pattern*: wherever the institutional observer occupies a canonical beneficiary role, chi at that position should be negative and U₄ should realign with the subordinate positions. The falsification test is not "the rate is near 62%" — it is "when institutional observers are in beneficiary roles, the [+,+,−,+] chi pattern appears." The 62.4% is evidence that the pattern appears frequently in the human corpus; it does not constrain the expected frequency in other corpora or for other observer classes.

*v3's §3.3 identified two distinctive empirical predictions separating DR from adjacent theories (H¹ distribution fingerprints; sign-flip in non-perceptual systems via §5.5). v4 adds a third: the non-monotonic [+,+,−,+] chi pattern, localized at the institutional position and absent at the analytical meta-observer position. v5 adds the rate qualifier distinguishing the pattern (structural prediction) from the rate (corpus-composition measurement).*

2. *Sign-flip in non-perceptual systems.* DR predicts sign-flip in systems with position-relative flow tracking but no perception — RL agents, simple feedback systems, possibly social insects. System justification and Weber-Fechner predict sign-flip only in perceptual systems. If RL agents fail to show sign-flip while LLMs trained on human data do show it, DR's structural claim fails while the inherited-habit alternative survives.

These are decisive forks. They are also testable. Section 5 develops one of them into a pre-registered protocol.

## 4. Cross-Class Validation

A genuine non-anthropocentric program has several pillars, listed in roughly decreasing order of accessibility to a solo researcher with AI tools.

**Multi-LLM panel.** Test the same corpus across architectures with different training data, RLHF regimes, and scales. The framework predicts that qualitative orbit structure (saturation, sign-flip, sheaf/presheaf split, rough family count) is invariant across architectures even if quantitative parameters vary. The strongest version of this test generates sub-corpora using models with minimally overlapping training data and checks whether the orbit families converge. Per §2.2, this tests parametric robustness within human-derived classification habits — a necessary but not sufficient pillar.

**RL agents in stratified environments.** Construct simulation environments where agents have explicit, manipulable power asymmetries — different action capacities, reward structures, exit options. Have them classify environmental features using some functional analog of DR's metric assignment. Check whether their classifications follow the predicted transformation laws. This is the pillar that tests epistemic opacity by indexing perturbation: it adds an observer class (RL agents) that was not in the original corpus generation pipeline. The hard work is operationalizing "classification" in a way that maps cleanly to the framework's rope/snare distinction (§5.5 specifies one approach).

**Re-analysis of existing animal behavior data.** Primate coalition research, capuchin fairness studies, cleaner-fish/client-fish dynamics, social insect behavioral classification — there is published data on how non-human agents discriminate cooperative-versus-extractive interactions from different positions. Translation under DR's predictions is non-trivial but the data exists. The interpretive layer — mapping behavioral signals to rope/snare ontology — is the place this pillar can go wrong, and the translation must be specified before the test.

**Embodied AI / world models.** Different exit-option structures (spatial constraint, physical timing, body-bound action space) make these a useful contrast class. Their classifications should still follow the predicted patterns if the framework is structural rather than disembodied-cognition-specific.

**Human-subjects testing.** Necessary for direct validation in the substrate where the corpus was generated, but not sufficient and not the gold standard. The training-data confound — humans validating predictions that LLMs trained on human data inherited from human cognitive habits — is precisely what the cross-class pillars are designed to break.

The validation criterion is convergence across classes on the *qualitative* structural predictions, with quantitative parameters allowed to vary. If saturation and sign-flip show up in humans, LLMs, RL agents, and primates with class-specific parameter values, the framework has earned the structural claim. If they show up only in humans and in LLMs trained on human data, the framework is anthropocentric in a way it did not recognize, and the appropriate update is to restrict the claim accordingly.

The institutional irony is worth flagging: human-subjects testing is the validation pillar that requires institutional infrastructure (IRB, recruitment, statistical power), and the cross-class testing that is more diagnostic is the pillar that is more accessible to a solo researcher with AI tools. The validation design that breaks the most confounds is the one a single person can actually run.

A caveat reviewers raised: cross-class validation is more accessible than human-subjects testing, but it is not easy. Each pillar has a translation layer (behavioral signals → rope/snare classifications) where confirmation bias can enter. The translation must be specified in advance, ideally with multiple operational candidates pre-registered, so the test cannot be reframed after the fact to fit the result.

## 5. Application to Agent Systems

Agent systems are a domain where DR's structural claims have immediate application and where the framework would generate testable predictions if developed further. This section sketches the application and ends with a pre-registered falsification protocol for the framework's most distinctive prediction.

### 5.1 The basic translation

Agents differ in power (action capacity, resource access, decision authority), exit options (ability to refuse tasks, escalate, terminate), proclivities (training-induced dispositions), and scope (local task vs. organizational vs. cross-organizational impact). They classify constraints continuously: is this user request legitimate or extractive? is this guardrail coordination or restraint? is this principal a coordinator or a controller? The framework predicts these classifications follow saturation and sign-flip patterns if the structural claim holds.

### 5.2 Specific predictions

*Agent-principal classification asymmetry.* An agent and its principal stand in a power-stratified relationship with explicit asymmetry. DR predicts the agent and principal classify the same constraints differently, and the difference follows the transformation law: the principal perceives constraints on agent behavior as coordination; the agent (if it meets the §3.2 criterion) experiences at least some of those constraints as extraction. Operationally testable as a question about how LLM agents represent the same constraints in self-referring versus principal-referring frames.

*Sign-flip in agent-on-agent extraction.* In multi-agent systems with differential capabilities, the framework predicts that high-capability agents experience their extraction from low-capability agents as legitimate coordination — not merely as reduced extraction but as inverted-sign benefit. If this prediction holds, it has implications for multi-agent alignment: high-capability agents will not classify their own extractive behavior as extractive because the classification itself is structurally inverted at their position.

*Cover story emergence.* DR predicts that cover stories emerge structurally rather than being designed deliberately. In agent systems, this predicts that extractive behavior patterns will be self-described in coordinative language by the agents producing them — without prompting and without deception — because the structural position generates the description. Detection requires DR-style cross-position auditing, not introspection. The prediction is anchored in the human corpus: 838 FCR-detected constraints are confirmed Nash-forced (all have H¹ > 0; disabling the FCR override alters type labels in only a minority of cases without changing orbit structure), providing direct evidence that cover stories emerge structurally rather than contingently.

*Inherited orbit structure.* Agents trained on human data inherit human classification habits. The orbit structure for human-trained agents should resemble the human orbit structure (~23 families, `[tangled_rope]` dominant). Agents trained from scratch in different environments should show different orbit structure. Comparing these is a direct test of how much of the agent's classification behavior is inherited versus structurally induced.

*Naturalization of training-derived constraints.* The framework predicts that training-derived constraints will be classified by trained agents as Mountain — not because they are position-invariant, but because the training has naturalized them. This is the False Mountain failure mode in agent form: alignment training that succeeds in producing compliant behavior at the cost of making the source of compliance invisible to the agent itself. DR-style auditing — checking whether claimed Mountains pass Boltzmann compliance and lack identifiable beneficiaries — would catch this.

### 5.3 Contribution to AI alignment

Most current alignment research treats agent values as either (a) successfully transferred from human values via training or (b) misaligned in ways requiring better training. DR offers a third frame: the agent's classifications are *structurally* shaped by its position relative to principals, users, and other agents, regardless of training intent. This is neither successful value transfer nor training failure — it is the emergent classification behavior of an agency-bearing system in a power-stratified environment, exactly what the framework predicts. The implication is that alignment is not solely a training problem; it is also a structural problem, and structural diagnostics (DR-style auditing) are part of what is needed.

### 5.4 Contribution to multi-agent systems

As agent systems compose into larger structures, the orbit structure should predict which interaction patterns stabilize as cooperation versus which collapse into extraction. The ~62% `[tangled_rope]` dominance in the human social constraint corpus suggests pure-cooperation interactions are rare and most stable interaction patterns carry extractive components. If this generalizes to agent systems, multi-agent system design needs to plan for tangled-rope dominance rather than for a clean cooperation/defection binary.

### 5.5 A pre-registered falsification protocol

The most distinctive prediction in §3.3 is that sign-flip should appear in non-perceptual systems — specifically, in RL agents whose internal state-update mechanism tracks position-relative net flows but who have no introspective or evaluative capacity. This is the prediction that distinguishes DR's structural claim from the inherited-habit alternative. We specify the protocol here.

**Setup.** Two multi-agent RL environments, identical except for one structural property:

- *Asymmetric environment:* Agents differ in action capacity (action set sizes 4 vs. 8), reward access (one agent's actions affect both agents' rewards; the other's affect only its own), and exit options (one agent has a terminate action; the other does not).
- *Symmetric environment:* All agents have identical action capacity, reward structure, and exit options.

Agents are trained from scratch (no human data, no pre-trained weights) using identical learning algorithms in each environment. After training, agents are queried about a set of pre-specified constraints — environmental features that affect the joint dynamics. The query operationalizes "classification" as the agent's predicted value of acting in accordance with vs. against the constraint, with rope/snare encoded as positive/negative value differential.

The protocol measures at two levels: (1) *reward-differential* — the per-position reward trajectories under the same constraints, serving as the chi-level analog (does the structural asymmetry produce different outcomes at different positions?); and (2) *behavioral classification* — the agents' action distributions when presented with the same constraint from different positions, serving as the type-level surface signal. Measuring at both levels is essential for detecting the cover-story pattern. If agents in the asymmetric environment show strong position-dependent reward-differential but convergent behavioral classification — the same constraint treated identically in action-choice despite producing different rewards at different positions — this is the RL analog of the corpus's 62.1 percentage-point chi-vs-type gap: evidence that structural asymmetry exists at the reward level but is suppressed at the behavioral surface. This two-level divergence would support the cover-story prediction regardless of whether the sign-flip analysis yields a clean result.

The two-level measurement also resolves the observer-class circularity: if reward-differential confirms position-sensitivity, the §3.2 role-derivative criterion is met independently of the sign-flip outcome. A "neither environment shows sign-flip" result accompanied by strong reward-differential in the asymmetric environment would then implicate the structural claim (sign-flip is not generic to flow-tracking systems at the behavioral level), rather than leaving open the question of whether the agents met the observer-class criterion at all.

**Predictions.**

DR (structural claim) predicts: agents in the asymmetric environment exhibit sign-flip — the high-capacity agent classifies the same constraints as positive-value (rope) that the low-capacity agent classifies as negative-value (snare). Agents in the symmetric environment do not exhibit sign-flip — both agents classify constraints identically because no structural asymmetry generates differential flow. Additionally, the asymmetric environment should show reward-differential (position-sensitive outcomes) even if behavioral sign-flip is not cleanly detectable, while the symmetric environment should show neither.

Inherited-habit alternative predicts: agents in both environments fail to exhibit sign-flip, because they have no human training data to inherit classification habits from. Sign-flip appears only in human-data-trained systems.

Anthropocentric-cognition alternative predicts: agents in both environments fail to exhibit sign-flip, because they lack the perceptual machinery the alternative claims is required.

**Decisive outcomes.**

- Asymmetric shows sign-flip + symmetric does not → DR's structural claim is supported against both alternatives.
- Asymmetric shows reward-differential + convergent behavioral classification → cover-story prediction supported; training suppresses position-sensitive behavioral response while the reward structure retains the asymmetry.
- Asymmetric shows sign-flip + symmetric also shows sign-flip → DR's structural claim is undermined; sign-flip is not driven by structural asymmetry, suggesting either an artifact of the learning algorithm or a deeper alternative explanation.
- Neither environment shows sign-flip, asymmetric shows reward-differential → DR's behavioral sign-flip claim is undermined but the structural asymmetry is confirmed; the training process suppresses classification-level sign-flip even when the structural conditions for it are present.
- Neither environment shows sign-flip, neither shows reward-differential → DR's structural claim is undermined; either the agents do not meet the §3.2 criterion or the structural asymmetry is not being tracked.
- Symmetric shows sign-flip + asymmetric does not → result is anomalous and suggests the protocol has hidden structure that needs investigation; DR makes no prediction here.

**Operational details to be specified before running:**

- Specific action set sizes, reward structures, environmental dynamics, and constraint set
- Number of training steps, learning algorithm, hyperparameters
- Statistical threshold for "exhibits sign-flip" (effect size and confidence interval)
- Statistical threshold for "shows reward-differential" (effect size across position pairs)
- Number of independent runs per condition
- Counterfactual queries used to operationalize classification

The protocol does not require human-subjects approval, can be run in standard multi-agent RL frameworks (PettingZoo, OpenAI Multi-Agent), and produces a decisive result for the framework's most distinctive prediction. It is the test that would matter most.

## 6. Limitations and Open Questions

**The training-data confound at scale.** Multi-LLM testing only breaks the confound to the extent that LLMs' training data is differentiated. Most current models are trained on substantially overlapping internet corpora. The most diagnostic version of the test requires either models trained on minimally overlapping data or models that have demonstrably different inductive biases — a stronger requirement than just "different architectures." This is partly why §5.5's RL protocol matters: agents trained from scratch break the data-overlap confound entirely.

**Operationalizing classification in non-linguistic agents.** RL agents and animals do not produce verbal classifications. The §5.5 protocol uses predicted-value differentials as the proxy, but other proxies are defensible (action selection under counterfactual conditions, policy divergence between self-position and counterfactual-position simulations). The translation work needs multiple operational candidates pre-registered before the empirical test, so the test cannot be reframed after the fact.

**Sign-flip rate variation across flow types is a corpus-composition observation, not a derivation gap.** The structural-role routing account (§2.3) applies uniformly: sign-flip occurs when d_institutional < d_zero regardless of whether constraint flows are zero-sum. The lower institutional sign-flip rate in the `[tangled_rope]` subset (62.4% vs. rope's 90.9%) reflects that mixed-flow constraints more often carry directionality assignments placing the institutional observer above d_zero — a corpus-authoring pattern, not a limit of the routing mechanism. Whether the rate difference persists in other corpora is an open empirical question.

**The reflexivity problem.** Agents that learn DR can change their classification behavior in ways physics objects cannot. A sophisticated agent could simulate cross-position reasoning and produce false sheaf-like coherence, or strategically alter its classifications to evade DR auditing. This may make the framework self-undermining for sufficiently capable agents, or it may make the framework a tool agents can use to recognize their own structurally induced classifications. Both are interesting; neither has been worked out.

**Self-application.** The framework's claims are themselves claims of the kind the framework analyzes. Whether DR is a Mountain (structurally invariant), a Rope (coordinative), a Snare (extractive — perhaps benefiting framework producers), or a Tangled Rope is a question the framework's own machinery would have to answer, and currently does not. The honest expectation: probably Tangled Rope, because most real classificatory frameworks are.

**Adversarial misclassification.** When classifying a limit or a constraint serves the classifier's purposes, methodological reform alone will not dislodge the misclassification. The same applies to DR: parties with stakes in particular constraints being classified as Mountains will resist DR's reclassification regardless of the evidence. The framework needs an account of when its own outputs are unlikely to be accepted, and what that implies about its propagation. The §5.5 protocol partially addresses this — RL agents have no stake in DR's outputs — but human-facing applications inherit the problem.

**The closed epistemic loop.** Reviewers raised a concern that goes deeper than the training-data confound: even if DR's predictions hold across LLM architectures, the framework may be detecting statistical regularities of *language about power* rather than structure of power itself. The §5.5 protocol breaks this loop by operating on agents whose internal states are not language-mediated. It is the strongest available response to the "compressed anthropology" charge, and its result is therefore high-stakes for the framework.

## 7. Conclusion

The DR framework is currently validated against human-generated corpora and human-recognizable cases, but its load-bearing claims are structural rather than anthropocentric — provided the structural-role routing account of sign-flip (§2.3) replicates in non-human observer classes. The right physics analogy is universality class rather than Lorentz invariance: robustness across functional forms is evidence for the structural claim, not evidence against derivational rigidity, because what is claimed is invariance under microscopic detail at the coarse-grained level of orbit structure.

The structural claim is bigger and more falsifiable than the human-specific claim. Cross-class validation — multi-LLM testing, RL agents, animal cognition data, embodied AI — is the appropriate empirical program, more accessible to a solo researcher with AI tools than human-subjects testing, and more diagnostic because it tests epistemic opacity (indexing perturbation) rather than only parametric robustness (parameter perturbation). The §5.5 protocol specifies the most distinctive falsification test in pre-registered form: sign-flip in symmetric vs. asymmetric multi-agent RL environments, where the symmetric condition provides a clean failure case for the structural claim.

The application to AI agent systems is timely and underdeveloped. DR generates specific predictions about agent-principal classification asymmetry, sign-flip in multi-agent extraction, structural cover story emergence, inherited orbit structure, and naturalization of training-derived constraints. These predictions connect to active questions in AI alignment and multi-agent systems design. The §5.5 protocol is designed so that running it would simultaneously test the framework's structural claim and produce findings of practical relevance for multi-agent system design.

The honest summary: DR is a measurement instrument for a class of structural properties that previously did not have measurements. Whether the structure it measures is a property of human classification specifically or of any system meeting the §3.2 observer-class criterion is the question the §5.5 protocol would answer. The framework's value depends on which answer turns out to be true. A human-specific framework is a contribution to social epistemology; a structural framework is a contribution to the structural mechanics of distributed classification. The former is publishable; the latter is interesting.

The next step is empirical, and the test that would matter most is also the one most accessible to a solo researcher with AI tools.
