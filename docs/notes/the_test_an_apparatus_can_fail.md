# The test an apparatus can fail: correlated agreement, and the only cheap check that discriminates

*Field note, 2026-08-26. Prompted by a pattern recurring across outside model reviews of this
repo. The reviews differ in tone and converge on one structural objection; this note keeps the
objection and discards the packaging. It is a note about method, not a defence of the project —
the whole point of the criterion below is that it is administered against yourself, and a note
that argued the project passes it would be committing the error it describes.*

## The objection worth keeping

Most of what arrives in these reviews is motive-reading off public artifacts — *epistemic
loneliness*, *a substitute for the missing conversational partner*, *no imagined audience*. Those
are unfalsifiable by construction, which is an odd thing to offer inside a critique about
falsifiability. They can be set down without argument.

One claim survives, and it does not need the metaphors it usually arrives wrapped in:

> An author who writes the ontology, the test cases, and the auditor's rules, and then varies only
> the model that fills them in, has not added an independent measurement. Models drawn from
> overlapping training distributions — and disposed, all of them, to cooperate with a
> well-specified schema — produce **correlated error**. Twenty legs of a thousand cases can
> multiply confidence without adding evidence.

This is true. It is true whether or not the word *theology* applies, and it is not answered by
generating more legs. That is the part worth internalising: **the natural response to "your
agreement might be correlated" is to add another model, and that response is the error.** A
seventh lineage drawn from the same schema, the same seed pool, and the same corner of the
pre-training distribution moves the confidence interval without moving the evidence. Breadth is
not independence, and no amount of the first buys the second.

## Why the usual remedy is unavailable, and what replaces it

The standard remedy is peers — reviewers who did not write the ontology and have no stake in it
surviving. For a solo research program that remedy is not on the table, and pretending otherwise
produces the failure the reviews are pointing at.

The replacement is not a substitute for peers. It is a narrower question that can be asked without
them:

> **In the last three months, name a conclusion your own apparatus forced you to abandon that you
> did not want to abandon.**

Two or three, and the structural claim is wrong. None, and it is right, and the metaphors on
either side stop mattering. The test's virtue is that it does not ask whether the framework is
*true*. It asks whether the machinery is **capable of costing you something** — which is the
precise thing correlated agreement cannot demonstrate and cannot fake for long.

## What counts, and the two ways to fail it while appearing to pass

The criterion is cheap to state and easy to satisfy dishonestly, so it needs its two failure modes
named.

**It does not count if the abandoned conclusion was never load-bearing.** Retiring a claim nobody
was resting on is housekeeping. The test asks for something that *cost* — a spend redirected, a
published figure withdrawn, a result you had already written up, a question you had to re-open
after closing it. If the abandonment was comfortable, it is not evidence the apparatus can
disagree with you; it is evidence you had a spare claim.

**It does not count if the record is curated.** A conclusion quietly updated leaves no trace, and
a memory of having been wrong is not a record of it. The test is only checkable if the trail is
kept somewhere that resists retroactive tidying — dated, entry-anchored, and written at the moment
of the reversal rather than at the moment of the retrospective. This is the whole structural
purpose of a tracker that records status *changes* and an investigations ledger that records the
decision to look *before* the outcome is known: not tidiness, but the preservation of unflattering
history in a form someone else can audit. **Their value is exactly proportional to how
uncomfortable their contents are.** A ledger holding only confirmations has reproduced the original
defect one level up.

The relevant artifacts in this repo are `ISSUES.md` (status transitions with evidence),
`KNOWN_STATE.md` (dated, including corrections and retractions), and `audits/INVESTIGATIONS.md`
(inquiries registered before their outcome, closed with a bit regardless of outcome). This note
does not tally them. **Pointing at where the record is kept is honest; announcing the score from
inside the note is the flattery move the reviews are performing, and it fails the same way.**

## What the test does not reach

*Indexed, because a criterion that serves everyone is usually measuring nothing.*

**It serves** a researcher with an apparatus and no peer group, who needs a criterion that does not
depend on colleagues they do not have, and who can be honest in private.

**It fails** anyone whose actual question is *is the framework true* rather than *is the apparatus
honest*. These come apart cleanly, and the gap is the most important thing in this note: **a
research program can be scrupulously falsifiable in every detail and wrong at its foundations.**
Every internal check can fire correctly while the ontology carves the world at joints that are not
there. The test detects an apparatus that cannot say no. It is silent on an apparatus that says no
frequently, precisely, and about the wrong things.

That residue is not closable from the inside, and it should be left declared rather than argued
away. The honest position is that the correlated-error objection is **partially** answered — the
part about whether the machinery can refuse is checkable and should be checked continuously; the
part about whether the frame itself is right is not, and waits on readers who did not build it.

## The compressed form

Adding models to a correlated set raises confidence without raising evidence. The cheap check that
discriminates is not *how many voices agree* but *what has the apparatus taken from you lately* —
and the check is only worth anything if the losses are written down where they cannot be edited
after the fact.
