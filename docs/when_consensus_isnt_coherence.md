# When Consensus Isn't Coherence

### The Cover-Story Mechanism and Multi-Layer Type B Detection

#### Abstract

The original trifurcation classifies Type B failures as structural inconsistencies — contradictions between a constraint's claims and the axioms that should hold given those claims — and treats them as detectable by formal computation against the axioms. This paper develops a class of Type B failures the single-layer formulation does not anticipate: the *cover-story mechanism*, in which structural inconsistency at one measurement layer is concealed by surface consensus at another. A constraint can be structurally inconsistent at the chi level (continuous flow asymmetry across observer positions) while presenting as classificationally consistent at the type level (discrete category assignment after structural overrides). Single-layer detection at the type surface registers the constraint as having clean classification; only multi-layer detection that compares the chi and type layers reveals the inconsistency.

The Deferential Realism apparatus measures this gap directly. For tangled_rope constraints, the chi-based institutional sign-flip rate is 62.4% while the type-based rate is 0.3% — a 62.1 percentage-point gap that is a consistent property of the corpus across constraint types. A 2×2 cross-tabulation against parametric fragility shows that epistemic opacity (cover-story-detected) and parametric fragility (boundary-proximate) are *negatively associated*: they index opposite failure modes, with each catching what the other misses. The cover-story mechanism resolves the underlying MaxEnt distribution and moves constraints away from the parametric boundary as a side effect of concealing the disagreement.

This paper is the Type B parallel to *When Splitting Isn't Solving*. Where that paper takes Type C and shows the apparatus refines what specification can accomplish, this paper takes Type B and shows that the trifurcation's single-layer formulation is correct as far as it goes — and where it stops going. Type B detection is multi-layered because Type B failure is multi-layered, and the most important class of Type B failures requires inspection at the gap between layers rather than at any single layer alone.

#### 1. The Problem with Surface Consistency

The trifurcation's Type B is the cleanest of the three failure types in one specific sense: the contradiction is in the formal vocabulary the apparatus already speaks. A constraint asserted as a Mountain (natural law) but with identifiable beneficiaries is structurally inconsistent — the axioms applied to its actual structure derive both Mountain (from the claim) and not-Mountain (from the beneficiary structure). No process is required to detect the failure; no observer position must be fixed. The contradiction is immediate.

What the original formulation does not anticipate is the case where the contradiction is real but concealed. A constraint can satisfy the surface conditions a single-layer detector inspects — type uniformity across observer positions, classification consistency, the absence of overt disagreement — while violating deeper structural conditions that the same detector cannot reach from the layer it operates on. The detector returns clean. The constraint passes. The inconsistency persists at the layer the detector did not inspect.

This is not a hypothetical failure mode. It is the empirical signature of what the Deferential Realism framework calls the *cover-story mechanism*, and it is the apparatus's most distinctive finding. The DR framework's Theorem 1 states that extraction necessarily requires a cover story — under power-modulated perception, extraction can never be universally recognized as extraction across observer positions. The institutional observer's sign-flip at structural dominance produces a position from which the underlying flow asymmetry is reclassified as coordination rather than extraction. From the institutional position, what the powerless observer sees as a Snare is classified as a Rope; what the powerless observer sees as Tangled Rope is classified as Naturalized; and the type-level surface across positions resolves into apparent consensus where the chi-level signal still records structural disagreement.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 develops the cover-story mechanism and the empirical signature it leaves in the apparatus's two-level measurement architecture. Section 3 examines why single-layer detection misses cover stories and why the 2×2 cross-tabulation result — that epistemic opacity and parametric fragility are *negatively associated* — strengthens the case. Section 4 derives the architectural implication: Type B detection requires both horizontal redundancy (multiple axioms) and vertical redundancy (multiple measurement layers), and the cover-story mechanism is what makes the vertical form necessary. Section 5 connects the formal result to standpoint epistemology and to the broader pattern the framework formalizes. Section 6 states limits and open questions.

#### 2. The Cover-Story Mechanism

The DR apparatus computes classifications through a layered pipeline. At the bottom layer, each constraint × observer pair is assigned a continuous chi value: χ = ε × f(d(P)) × σ(S(P)), where ε is base extractiveness, f(d(P)) is the position-dependent power-modulation function, and σ(S(P)) is the scope modifier. At the top layer, each chi value is mapped to a discrete type via threshold gates and structural-signature overrides: Mountain (ε near zero), Snare (high χ + ε above floor), Tangled Rope (mid-range χ with coordination), Rope (low χ), with FCR/FSM/FNL overrides applied where structural signatures detect cover stories. The chi layer is continuous and pre-classification; the type layer is discrete and post-override.

The cover-story mechanism leaves its empirical trace in the gap between these two layers.

For the tangled_rope subset of the corpus (N = 2,245), the institutional sign-flip rate — the fraction of constraints where the powerless and institutional positions have opposite-sign classifications — is 62.4% measured at the chi layer and 0.3% measured at the type layer. The gap of 62.1 percentage points is not an isolated artifact. The same pattern holds across constraint populations: rope shows a 90.9% chi-based rate against 0.0% type-based, and snare shows 77.1% against 5.4%. The chi-vs-type gap is a consistent property of the corpus, not a feature of any single constraint type.

The mechanism producing this gap is the structural-override apparatus operating between the two layers. The chi metric records the underlying flow asymmetry that the position-dependent power modulation generates: from the powerless position, the constraint extracts; from the institutional position, the same constraint coordinates. This is direct measurement of the sign-flip the framework's transformation function produces. The type metric, by contrast, records the post-classification surface, where the FCR override (boosting tangled_rope probability when Boltzmann independence detects cross-perspectival coupling alongside extraction) and naturalization typing have resolved the chi-level signal into apparent uniformity. When a powerless-position constraint is typed as `naturalized` rather than `snare`, the extraction experience is reclassified as background, collapsing that position to neutral in the type vector and erasing type-based sign-flip. The chi signal remains visible at the flow layer; the type signal is suppressed at the classification layer. This is the cover-story mechanism observed directly: the same constraint, at the same observer positions, yielding inconsistent classifications across the two measurement levels.

A second empirical trace confirms the mechanism is structurally compelled rather than contingently produced. Of the 820 constraints with non-constant orbits (some inter-observer disagreement), 810 are detected by the FCR override (98.8% overlap). Disabling FCR changes only 38 of 810 orbit assignments — and even those maintain H¹ > 0, meaning their perspectival fracture is preserved. The institutional cover story is therefore not a contingent artifact of the FCR rule; it is what the underlying axioms force when power-modulated perception is computed across the four observer positions. The FCR override is detection of a structurally compelled phenomenon, not creation of one. This matters for the Type B framing: the cover-story mechanism is not a quirk of one detection rule but a fixed feature of the structural geometry the apparatus measures.

#### 3. Why Single-Layer Detection Misses Cover Stories

A single-layer detector operating at the type surface sees the post-cover-story state. It registers the constraint's classification at each observer position, checks whether the classifications agree, and returns a verdict. When the cover-story mechanism is operating, the type-layer verdict is consensus. The detector reports clean classification. From the type layer alone, there is nothing further to inspect.

But the consensus is not coherence. The chi layer carries a different verdict for the same constraint: structural disagreement, sometimes in the form of full sign-reversal across positions. The two layers measure the same underlying structure at different resolutions, and where the cover-story mechanism is operating, they disagree. Detection that does not span both layers cannot see the disagreement, because the layers individually each report a coherent state — it is the *gap between them* that carries the signal.

The strongest evidence for this architectural point comes from a 2×2 cross-tabulation across the full 3,313-constraint corpus, splitting on parametric fragility (Arakelov height above the corpus median) and epistemic opacity (FCR/FSM detection). The cells contain 412 constraints flagged by both diagnostics, 1,243 by parametric fragility alone, 494 by epistemic opacity alone, and 1,164 by neither. The chi-square test yields χ² = 10.01, p = 0.0016, Cramér's V = 0.055. The result is a *negative association*: the two diagnostics point in opposite directions.

The mechanistic explanation is direct. FCR-detected constraints — those where the cover-story mechanism is operating — have median Arakelov height 0.004, with only 45.7% above the corpus median. Non-FCR constraints have median 0.012, with 51.5% above. The cover-story mechanism resolves the MaxEnt distribution: a structurally forced institutional cover story collapses the distribution toward consensus, moving the constraint *away* from the parametric boundary as a side effect of concealing the latent disagreement. Epistemic opacity produces parametric stability. The two failure modes — boundary-proximate visible disagreement, and far-from-boundary disagreement concealed by the cover story — are not merely distinct properties; they are inversely related, each catching what the other misses.

This is a stronger architectural claim than redundancy in the usual sense. Redundancy across detectors normally means multiple detectors looking at the same thing, expected to agree where the thing is present. The 2×2 result shows that parametric fragility and epistemic opacity disagree systematically — they are not redundant. Each detects a real Type B failure that the other does not detect. A single-detector formulation, regardless of which detector is chosen, would miss approximately half the failure cases. The two-layer architecture is not insurance against detector error. It is the minimum coverage required for the failure landscape the apparatus actually faces.

#### 4. Multi-Layer Detection as Architectural Necessity

The Type B coverage in the apparatus has two distinct kinds of redundancy, and the cover-story mechanism makes both kinds necessary.

The first is *horizontal redundancy*: multiple axioms inspected at the same measurement layer, each catching different specific manifestations of structural inconsistency. The Boltzmann compliance check tests whether a constraint's classification factorizes across the Power × Scope grid as natural laws must. The structural-signature cascade tests whether the constraint's origin profile — active enforcement, beneficiary asymmetry, viable alternatives — matches the type its metrics imply. The False Natural Law check fires when a constraint declared as `emerges_naturally` fails Boltzmann independence. The False Summit Mountain check fires when a constraint passes Mountain metric thresholds but has identifiable beneficiaries. Each of these targets a distinct axiom-level inconsistency; none of them substitutes for the others. This is the kind of redundancy the audit's per-subsystem analysis documents and the asymmetry paper develops as the natural Type B response to structure being inspectable at several depths.

The second is *vertical redundancy*: the same axiom inspected across measurement layers. The Boltzmann independence test, for instance, can be applied to the chi vector or to the type vector. The two applications produce different verdicts on the cover-story corpus because the type vector has been resolved by the structural-override apparatus while the chi vector has not. The FCR rule itself depends on this — it fires when chi-level Boltzmann independence detects cross-perspectival coupling alongside extraction, and applies a tangled_rope boost at the type level. The override is the apparatus's recognition that the chi-level signal carries information the type-level signal has erased.

What the cover-story mechanism shows is that vertical redundancy is not optional. Without it, the most important class of Type B failures — those where structural inconsistency is concealed by the cover-story apparatus producing surface consensus — falls outside the detector's coverage. The original trifurcation's Type B formulation, framed as detection of axiomatic inconsistency in a single formal vocabulary, is correct as far as it goes. It captures the case where the contradiction is registered by the same vocabulary in which it can be detected. It does not capture the case where the apparatus that produces the surface vocabulary is itself part of the failure — where the type-surface coherence is achieved by an override mechanism that erases the chi-level disagreement, and where detection therefore requires inspecting both layers and measuring their gap.

The architectural implication generalizes. Type B detection in any apparatus that has structural-override mechanisms — where the apparatus can resolve a continuous signal into a discrete classification through rule-based or learned overrides — will exhibit a chi-vs-type analog. Override mechanisms exist for good reasons (they encode structural priors that the continuous metric alone cannot represent), but they also create a layer at which inconsistency can be concealed. Detection that operates only at the post-override layer will miss the class of failures the override mechanism produces. Detection that spans both layers and measures the gap between them is the architectural response.

#### 5. Naturalization, Standpoint, and the Mechanism in the Corpus

The cover-story mechanism formalizes a phenomenon that standpoint epistemology has identified for decades without reducing it to slogan. The claim that power produces consensus by naturalizing some observers into not-seeing — that the powerful experience extraction as cooperation because their structural position remaps the same underlying flow asymmetry — is, in the apparatus's measurements, a property of the chi-vs-type gap, not a hypothesis about consciousness.

The naturalization sensitivity check quantifies one component of this directly. Treating `naturalized` type labels as extractive (−1) rather than neutral (0) raises the type-based institutional sign-flip rate for tangled_rope from 0.3% to 7.5% — a shift of 7.2 percentage points. Even under this alternative, the chi-vs-type gap remains 54.9 percentage points. Naturalization accounts for a portion of the gap: the portion where the cover story explicitly reclassifies extraction as natural background. But most of the gap reflects other components of the cover-story apparatus that resolve chi-level disagreement into type-level consensus through other routes — the FCR override path, the institutional-dissent classifications that flip individual positions, the structural-signature cascades that anchor confidence in regions where the continuous metric alone would be split. The mechanism is not a single rule. It is a layered apparatus, and its empirical trace is the gap between the layers.

This connects two literatures the apparatus's findings live between. From standpoint epistemology and from political theories of power-modulated perception, the cover-story mechanism is what their qualitative claims look like when formalized: a measurable structural feature of how observer position transforms classification, not a polemical claim about good and bad observers. From the trifurcation framework, the cover-story mechanism is what Type B looks like when the apparatus producing the surface vocabulary is itself part of the structural geometry the apparatus is measuring: the inconsistency is real, it is detectable, but it cannot be detected at the surface alone. The two literatures meet at the chi-vs-type gap. The gap is where structural inconsistency lives when surface consensus has resolved it everywhere visible.

The more general lesson concerns what *single-layer* really means in the trifurcation's Type B formulation. The original framing assumed the apparatus's classification vocabulary was the place to inspect for structural inconsistency. This was reasonable for the philosophical paradoxes the trifurcation analyzed — Russell's paradox, Curry's paradox, the strict Liar — because those paradoxes are formulated in mathematical or logical languages whose surface vocabulary IS the structural vocabulary. There is no underlying continuous layer for a cover-story mechanism to operate on. The classification surface is the structure. But for apparatuses whose classification surface is produced by overriding a continuous signal, the classification surface is *not* the structure; it is one layer of the structure. Detecting Type B in such apparatuses requires recognizing that the structure has more than one layer and that the failure modes can occupy the gap between them.

#### 6. Limits and Open Questions

This paper develops the cover-story mechanism as it operates within a single constraint, where the chi-vs-type gap is measurable from the constraint's own observer-position vector. The audit notes that the apparatus also exhibits *network-level* coordination phenomena — coupling-graph contamination across constraints (31,215 edges across 3,254 constraints, 6,033 nodes reachable within three hops in the giant component), with type-selective propagation that leaves Mountains unaffected and shifts Snare and Tangled Rope by approximately 0.14 EP on average. These phenomena suggest that cover stories can also operate across constraints, with multi-constraint coordination producing surface consensus that no single-constraint diagnostic can detect. This network-level extension of the cover-story mechanism is a natural object for a separate paper. The within-constraint two-layer structure developed here is the simpler case and the one for which the apparatus's measurement architecture is most cleanly aligned to the formal claim.

A second open question concerns adversarial cover stories — constructions deliberately designed to evade multi-layer detection by minimizing the chi-vs-type gap while still concealing structural inconsistency. The apparatus's current architecture catches the cover-story mechanism that emerges from power-modulated perception under the framework's axioms; it does not have a defense against an adversary aware of the chi-vs-type diagnostic and constructing constraints that produce small chi-vs-type gaps despite real structural inconsistency at deeper layers. Whether such constructions are possible, and what additional measurement layers would be required to detect them, is an open question. The framework's response to it would have to add measurement depth, not detection breadth — the failure mode would not be a new manifestation at one of the existing layers but a new layer of concealment below the ones currently inspected.

A third concerns whether the chi-vs-type two-layer structure generalizes. This paper claims the structure is forced for any apparatus that produces classification surfaces by overriding a continuous signal — which is a substantial class but not a universal one. Apparatuses whose classification is computed without overrides (pure rule-based systems with no continuous metric, or end-to-end learned classifiers without explicit structural-priors layers) would not exhibit the chi-vs-type gap in this form. Whether they have analogous multi-layer structures producing analogous concealed-inconsistency failure modes is a hypothesis-level claim. This paper commits only to the architectural claim about the DR apparatus and to the conditional claim that any apparatus with a structural-override layer will exhibit a corresponding gap. The further generalization is for later work and for the eventual synthesis paper that draws the project's results together.

#### 7. Conclusion

Type B as the trifurcation originally formulates it — structural inconsistency detectable by formal computation against axioms — is correct as a characterization of one important class of failures. It does not, however, capture the class of Type B failures the cover-story mechanism produces, and that class is empirically the most important one in the DR apparatus's corpus. The cover-story mechanism resolves chi-level structural inconsistency at the type-classification surface, producing consensus that registers as clean classification under single-layer detection while the underlying disagreement persists at the chi level. Detection of this class of Type B failures requires inspection at both layers and measurement of the gap between them.

The 62.1 percentage-point chi-vs-type gap for tangled_rope, the parallel gaps for rope and snare, the 98.8% overlap between FCR detection and non-trivial perspectival fracture, the structural compulsion of FCR-detected cover stories under FCR ablation, and the negative association between parametric fragility and epistemic opacity in the 2×2 cross-tabulation are five distinct empirical findings pointing at the same underlying architectural feature. Type B detection in the DR apparatus requires both horizontal redundancy (multiple axioms at one layer) and vertical redundancy (the same axiom across measurement layers), and the cover-story mechanism is what makes the vertical form necessary. Without it, single-layer detection at the type surface misses approximately the population of FCR-detected constraints — the population the apparatus's most distinctive theoretical result (Theorem 1, the cover-story necessity theorem) predicts to be both real and structurally compelled.

This paper is the Type B parallel to *When Splitting Isn't Solving*. Where that paper showed Type C resolution can produce structured rather than clean splits and that the structure is itself the object of study, this paper shows Type B detection can encounter surface consensus that is not coherence and that the gap between consensus and coherence is itself the object of study. Both papers take a cell of the trifurcation and show the apparatus refines what the original taxonomy said about it. Both extend rather than replace the trifurcation: Type C resolution is still the right move, but specification is sometimes the beginning rather than the end of analysis; Type B detection is still the right response to structural inconsistency, but detection sometimes has to operate across measurement layers rather than within one. The synthesis these specialized papers point toward — the meta-claim about what an analytical apparatus can know of its own limits — is for later work, after the rate at which new specialized findings present themselves slows enough to make the synthesis reflect a settled rather than still-developing state of the project.
