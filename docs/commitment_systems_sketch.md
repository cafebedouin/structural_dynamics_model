# Commitment Systems and the Architecture of Drift

Any arrangement of stabilized commitments operating in an environment that changes faster than the commitments do will eventually face a structural problem: how to acknowledge that operational practice has drifted from the commitments without dissolving the authority that grounds the commitments. This problem appears wherever the conditions hold, which is more places than is usually noticed. Religions, constitutions, scientific theories, philosophical positions, identities, marriages, employment relationships, legal systems, artificial intelligence training protocols, mathematical practice, legal precedent, professional standards, ideological movements — all are commitment systems. All face the same structural problem. All instantiate the same patterns of response.

I'll call these *commitment systems*. A commitment system has three components.

A **kernel**: the stabilized arrangement of commitments that constitutes the system's nominal core. This may be a text (a constitution, a foundational paper, a creed), a body of practice (the methodology of a craft tradition), a narrative (an identity's organizing story), a relationship (the implicit terms a marriage rests on), or some combination. The kernel is what participants in the system point to when asked what the system is.

An **authority structure**: the mechanism that determines what counts as legitimate interpretation of the kernel. This may be external (a priesthood, a judiciary, an academic discipline, a board), distributed (the parties to a relationship, a community of practice, a peer-review system), internal (an individual's self-interpretive practices), or composite. The authority structure decides who has standing to read the kernel and on what grounds.

**Drift**: unmarked mutation of operational practice relative to the kernel. Drift is intrinsic because the environment the kernel was stabilized within continues to change after the kernel stabilizes. No commitment system preserves perfect fidelity to its kernel across time. The question is not whether drift occurs but whether the system's authority structure can acknowledge drift without losing legitimacy.

The patterns that follow from this structure are the same across domains. The differences between domains are differences in how the patterns instantiate, not differences in the patterns themselves.

## The kernel as frame-relative

Before developing the patterns, one conceptual issue requires direct treatment. For some commitment systems, the kernel has a clear meaning because the kernel is genuinely fixed — a constitution's text, a mathematical axiom, a marriage vow's explicit content. For others, the kernel seems partly constituted by accumulated interpretation, which makes "drift relative to kernel" appear circular.

The resolution is to fix the frame. At any moment T1, the kernel is whatever participants in the system point to as the reference for that analysis. At T1, a common-law kernel is the accumulated body of precedent up to T1. At T1, a person's identity kernel is the set of commitments they currently treat as constitutive of who they are. At T1, a marriage's kernel is the set of explicit and implicit understandings the parties currently treat as the relationship's terms. Drift between T1 and T2 is measured against the T1 kernel, not against some eternal fixed reference. The kernel itself can change between T1 and T2, and that change is itself drift — to be acknowledged or denied by the system's authority structure.

This treatment makes systems with evolving kernels analyzable in the same terms as systems with fixed ones. The kernel at T1 is determinate. Drift between T1 and T2 is well-defined. The authority structure acknowledges or denies the drift through whatever mechanisms it has.

The frame-fixing move is itself positional. The analyst stands outside the system and selects a moment to analyze from. This requires acknowledgment: the framework's outputs are produced from a particular position, with particular commitments — long time horizons, exit through abstraction, valuation of acknowledged drift over unacknowledged drift, structural exposure over institutional protection. These are commitments, not neutralities. The analytical position is itself a commitment system. The framework analyzes commitment systems from within a commitment system, and recognizing this is part of what makes its outputs honest.

## The five configurations

Different combinations of kernel formalization, authority structure, and acknowledgment capacity produce different commitment system configurations. Five base configurations are worth distinguishing across domains. A sixth feature, decoupled formalization, modifies the base configurations rather than constituting one of its own.

*Marked revision*: highly specifiable kernel, epistemic authority structure, working acknowledgment capacity. The kernel is precise. Authority is voluntary and grounded in expertise. Drift is formalized as revision: someone proposes a change, the change is checked, and if it works the system absorbs it. Acknowledgment is marked and legible rather than absorbed silently. The configuration appears across domains: mathematics in the scientific theory case, certain therapeutic practices in the identity case, couples with explicit norm-revision practices in the relational case, programming languages with formal proposal-and-deprecation procedures in the technical case. The configuration is stable when acknowledgment bandwidth matches environmental change rate. It is fragile when environmental change accelerates faster than the acknowledgment machinery can absorb, which is the empirical problem mathematics currently faces with computer-assisted proofs.

*Interpretive accretion*: highly specifiable kernel, traditional or institutional authority structure, blocked acknowledgment capacity for explicit kernel revision. The text is fixed. Authority is grounded in continuity with the founding text. The formal mechanism for changing the text does not function or does not exist. Drift migrates entirely into interpretation. Everyone insists the kernel controls while the operational meaning shifts substantially. Drift accumulates as unmarked interpretive accretion. The configuration appears across domains: Brahmanical commentary on the Vedas, doctrine of development in Catholicism, common law jurisprudence, constitutional originalism's evolving content despite its claim to fixity. In identity systems, interpretive accretion appears when a person preserves their core narrative ("I have always been someone who values X") while reinterpreting operationally what X means. In relational systems, it appears when couples maintain the same explicit story about their relationship while the operational reality shifts. In scientific theories, it appears as Lakatosian protective-belt modifications absorbing anomalies while the hard core stays intact.

*Diffuse reconstruction*: under-specified or intentionally ambiguous kernel, no centralized authority structure, distributed interpretation. The kernel can be claimed by many parties producing mutually incompatible readings. The configuration persists indefinitely but lacks operational coherence. Platonism in philosophy is the canonical case. In identity systems, diffuse reconstruction appears when commitments are held without operational implications, allowing them to mean different things at different times. In relational systems, it appears in relationships where the parties have incompatible operational interpretations of shared commitments and no mechanism to adjudicate. In AI development, the various interpretations of "alignment" or "safety" function as diffuse reconstruction — shared kernel terms with mutually incompatible operational meanings depending on the speaker. In contemporary political ideology, terms like "freedom" or "justice" function similarly. The configuration is stable in the sense of persisting; it is unstable in the sense of never resolving operational coherence.

*Implicit practice*: no codified kernel, traditional authority over practice itself. The kernel is whatever the system does. Drift is the mechanism rather than a failure of it. The configuration is stable as long as practice remains coherent and the relevant authority structures continue to operate. Breakdown is severe when practice loses coherence because there is no fixed referent to appeal to. The UK constitution is the canonical organizational case. Craft traditions transmitting tacit knowledge through apprenticeship are another. In identity systems, implicit practice appears when a person's sense of self is constituted by what they do rather than by explicit narrative commitments. In relational systems, it appears in long-term partnerships where the relationship is whatever the parties do together, without explicit articulation of terms. In scientific practice, it appears in tacit knowledge that resists textual transmission — the kind of knowledge transmitted through doing rather than reading.

*Anchored fixity*: formalized kernel paired with authority structure that grounds its legitimacy in the kernel's unchangeability. The kernel cannot be amended because amendment would dissolve the authority structure that requires fixity. Drift denial is the source of authority rather than a side effect. The configuration has two distinct sub-types that produce radically different stability profiles.

*Anchored fixity with interpretive-accretion layer* pairs the unrevisable kernel with a substantial interpretive substructure beneath it. Drift gets absorbed by the interpretive layer rather than surfacing as kernel revision. The configuration can persist millennia: the Hindu Vedic-Brahmanical system, post-development Catholic doctrine, Confucian commentary tradition. In identity systems, this appears when a person treats core commitments as unrevisable while developing sophisticated interpretive practices that absorb operational drift without forcing identity crisis. Long-term religious converts and politically committed individuals often instantiate this pattern when the system works.

*Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer* lacks the interpretive substructure. The kernel is supposed to govern operational practice directly. The Spartan Lycurgan system, certain forms of religious fundamentalism, certain trauma responses that treat the traumatic event as unrevisable kernel without developing interpretive machinery to process it. The configuration produces accumulating gap and catastrophic breakdown when environmental change exceeds what the kernel can govern. Spartan breakdown at Leuctra is the canonical case at civilizational scale; identity crises in fundamentalist deconstruction are the same pattern at personal scale.

These five base configurations describe most commitment systems most of the time. The sixth feature, *decoupled formalization*, combines with any of the base configurations. A system has decoupled formalization when its formal kernel exists for purposes other than operational control — legibility, historical legitimacy, ceremonial completeness, external recognition — while operational authority sits elsewhere. Late Soviet constitutional law had decoupled formalization combined with operational anchored fixity in party structures. Many corporate governance documents have decoupled formalization combined with operational implicit practice in management cultures. The feature can combine with any base configuration, producing systems where the formal kernel and the operational kernel have separate dynamics.

The configurations are ideal types in the Weberian sense, not natural kinds. Real commitment systems often mix features from multiple configurations and many of the most interesting cases occur at boundaries — systems transitioning from one configuration to another, or nested systems where an outer configuration contains inner systems with different configurations. The configurations are useful as analytical scaffolding rather than as exhaustive partition.

## How domains differ

The configurations generalize but the domains they appear in have specific features that affect how the framework's analysis proceeds. Recognizing these differences is what makes the framework's analytical machinery productive across domains rather than just rhetorically applicable to them.

In **organizational systems**, the authority structure is typically external to individual participants — institutions, hierarchies, formal procedures. Drift operates on shared practice maintained by many parties. Acknowledgment requires coordination among many parties and can be blocked by coordination failure even when individuals would prefer to acknowledge. The bandwidth axis tracks formal amendment procedures and their political accessibility.

In **identity systems**, the authority structure is partly internal — the individual's own interpretive practices — but partly external — communities, therapists, ideologies the individual trusts. Drift operates on individual experience. Acknowledgment can happen at the individual level but is often easier with external interpretive support. The bandwidth axis tracks the individual's interpretive flexibility and the external resources they have access to.

In **relational systems**, the authority structure is distributed across the parties to the relationship plus external interpreters they share or don't share. Drift operates on relational practice. Acknowledgment requires participation by multiple parties; unilateral acknowledgment doesn't process drift the way mutual acknowledgment does. The bandwidth axis must distinguish individual acknowledgment from mutual acknowledgment because they're different operations.

In **scientific and intellectual systems**, the authority structure is typically epistemic — grounded in expertise voluntarily recognized by a community of practitioners. Drift operates on the theory's operational use plus the body of evidence the theory must account for. Acknowledgment proceeds through the community's procedures for revising theory in response to evidence. The bandwidth axis tracks both the formal mechanisms (peer review, replication, formal proof) and the informal ones (citation patterns, conference culture, professional networks).

In **technical systems** including AI development, the authority structure is composite — research communities plus deployment institutions plus regulatory bodies plus the artifacts themselves, which can exhibit operational behaviors that constrain interpretation. Drift operates on both the theory and the deployed systems. Acknowledgment must coordinate across multiple authority types with different update mechanisms.

These domain differences are not bugs in the generalization. They are features that the framework should make explicit because the failure modes characteristic of each domain follow from the structural specifics. Identity systems' characteristic failure modes (internalized authority diverging from external authority, embodied kernels resisting verbal acknowledgment) have no clean analogues in organizational systems. Relational systems' characteristic failure modes (asymmetric acknowledgment, unilateral kernel revision treated as betrayal) have no clean analogues in scientific systems. Each domain's failure modes are predictable from how its structural specifics interact with the base configurations.

## The diagnostic order

When examining an actual commitment system, four questions arise in order.

First: what is the kernel and what is the authority structure? Specifying these is the precondition for further analysis. Many disputes about commitment systems turn out to be disputes about which kernel is operative or which authority is legitimate, and these need to be resolved before drift or stability can be assessed.

Second: is drift occurring, and is it acknowledged? Drift is intrinsic; the question is whether the system can name it. Different configurations produce different acknowledgment patterns. Mathematics names drift as revision. Constitutions sometimes name drift as amendment, sometimes as interpretation, sometimes refuse to name it at all. Religions develop elaborate hermeneutic machinery to acknowledge drift while denying it through doctrines of development or progressive revelation. Identities acknowledge drift through narrative reconstruction. Marriages acknowledge drift through renegotiation or fail to acknowledge it through avoidance. The acknowledgment pattern is diagnostic of the configuration.

Third: is the configuration functional in this environment? Some combinations are inherently fragile (anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer produces accumulating gap until breakdown when environmental change is significant). Others are stable indefinitely under appropriate conditions (marked revision when bandwidth tracks environment-rate; implicit practice when practice remains coherent; anchored fixity with interpretive accretion when the interpretive layer can absorb the operational drift). Environmental rate is itself variable across domains and across times, so functionality assessments are inherently conditional.

Fourth: who has standing to acknowledge drift, and is acknowledgment distributed appropriately? This question is more prominent in relational and identity systems than in organizational systems because acknowledgment in those domains is structurally distributed. A marriage where only one partner acknowledges drift is structurally different from a marriage where both do. An identity system where the individual acknowledges drift but their community refuses to ratify the acknowledgment is structurally different from one where both internal and external acknowledgment cohere.

## Trajectory and cover story

Two extensions handle change over time and observer-position variation.

Trajectory analysis starts from a specified configuration at time T1 and describes what configurations are reachable at time T+n. Some transitions are likely; some are blocked. Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer cannot transition smoothly to marked revision without first dismantling the authority structure that requires fixity, which means the transition usually requires authority-structure breakdown rather than authority-structure revision. Identity systems organized around anchored fixity often transition to identity crisis rather than to marked revision precisely because the authority structure (the person's commitment to the unrevisable kernel) cannot accommodate revision without dissolving itself.

The dormant-container activation pattern is a fourth trajectory channel that the cultural containers analysis surfaced empirically. A system that has previously been an outer container but has been operationally suspended — its kernel and authority structure preserved but not currently operating as the system's effective authority — can be reactivated as alternative legitimacy when the active outer container loses standing. France's Estates-General reactivation in 1789 after 175 years of dormancy is the canonical organizational case. The Meiji Restoration's reactivation of the Imperial Court after centuries of bakufu mediation is another. In identity systems, this appears in deconstruction trajectories where a pre-conversion or pre-cult identity, formally preserved but operationally suspended, gets reactivated as alternative legitimacy when the active identity loses standing. The pattern is structurally identical across domains: preserved-but-suspended outer kernel plus inner systems with sufficient independent operational capacity equals possible activation under stress.

Cover story analysis handles observer-position variation. In any commitment system, participants at different positions see different things. Insiders, external observers, beneficiaries, victims, reformers, conservatives — each position produces a different classification of the same kernel-practice relationship. The disagreement has structure rather than being noise. Some positions are systematically unable to see drift they participate in producing, because seeing it would threaten the authority structure that grants them their position. The constitutional originalist cannot easily acknowledge that their preferred interpretation is itself an evolution of original meaning. The church hierarchy cannot easily acknowledge that doctrinal development is doctrinal change. The institutional reformer cannot easily acknowledge that their proposed reform serves institutional interests. The identity-anchored person cannot easily acknowledge that their commitments have shifted operationally. The pattern is structural rather than individual. Position determines what is visible from that position.

The cover story analysis is testable when the predicted blindness is specific. Positional blindness becomes a real claim when one can specify, in advance, what drift a given position should be unable to see and what evidence would settle the question.

## The framework as commitment system

The framework analyzes commitment systems. It is itself a commitment system, and treating it as one is part of the discipline the framework requires.

Its kernel is the structural claims developed in this and successor documents. Its authority structure consists of whoever adopts the framework and applies it — researchers, analysts, practitioners across the domains the framework engages. Its drift is real: the framework's own commitments change as it gets applied, criticized, and revised. Its acknowledgment capacity is whatever revision discipline its practitioners maintain.

This is not paradoxical. Commitment systems with good acknowledgment capacity do acknowledge their own drift. A framework that recommends drift acknowledgment to other commitment systems while denying its own drift would be operating exactly the cover story mechanism it identifies in conservative authority structures. The framework's claim to analyze commitment systems loses force if it cannot survive being treated as one.

The framework's own development demonstrates this discipline operationally. Earlier versions emphasized organizational containers and treated the typology as if its configurations were equally well-evidenced. Empirical testing revealed that three configurations were strongly supported, three were currently untestable, and one (decoupled formalization) was a candidate for recategorization. Subsequent development incorporated these findings, refining the typology rather than preserving its initial claims. The version sequence is the framework's procedural acknowledgment of its own drift.

The recursive depth matters. Lakatos worked this territory with research programs and protective belts but did not apply his framework rigorously to itself. Kuhn worked it with paradigms and normal science but treated his own work as outside the paradigm-shift dynamic he described. The framework here aims for the recursive discipline both predecessors avoided: the structural claims about commitment systems apply to the framework as a commitment system, and the framework's discipline on itself is what its application to other systems requires.

## What the framework predicts

The value test of a framework is whether it produces analytical moves that domain-specific framings don't naturally produce. The generalized framework yields predictions that organizational, identity, relational, and scientific framings each see only partially.

It predicts that anchored fixity without interpretive accretion produces the same brittle-fixity signature across organizational, identity, and relational systems. The Spartan pattern, the fundamentalist deconstruction pattern, and the rigid marriage breakdown pattern share structural mechanisms. Treatment approaches that work in one domain may transfer to others when the structural diagnosis is the same.

It predicts that dormant-container activation appears in revolutionary moments, in cult deconstruction, in diplomatic crises, in identity reorganization, and in scientific paradigm shifts for the same structural reasons. The Meiji Restoration, the conversion exit, the reactivation of older treaty frameworks during diplomatic breakdown, and the reactivation of pre-paradigm intuitions during scientific crisis are instances of the same pattern.

It predicts that decoupled formalization combines with other configurations to produce specific failure modes. A relationship with decoupled formalization plus operational anchored fixity (explicit terms differ substantially from operational reality, with the operational reality enforcing the substantive arrangement) has predictable breakdown trajectories. A scientific theory with decoupled formalization plus diffuse reconstruction (formal mathematical core differs substantially from operational use, with use scattered across incompatible interpretive communities) has predictable failure modes.

It predicts that environmental coupling matters across all domains, not just organizational ones. Mathematics under computer-assisted proof acceleration is a case the cultural containers analysis demonstrated. AI systems being trained and deployed under accelerating capability development are another. Identity systems under accelerating social change are another. Relational systems under accelerating environmental disruption are another. The same configuration with the same axis profile is functional in slow-changing environments and pathological in fast-changing ones across all domains.

It predicts that scientific theories operate as commitment systems with all the standard configurations available. A theory with anchored-fixity-plus-interpretive-accretion configuration absorbs anomalies through protective-belt modifications indefinitely. A theory with marked-revision configuration revises its hard core in response to evidence through community consensus. A theory with diffuse-reconstruction configuration produces multiple operationally incompatible interpretations sharing kernel terms. The framework's analytical moves for cultural containers transfer directly to philosophy of science cases.

It predicts that AI development is a commitment system in its current operational form, with kernels (training objectives, evaluation criteria, safety frameworks), authority structures (development teams, deployment institutions, regulatory bodies, the artifacts themselves), and drift (operational behavior diverging from training-time commitments). The dynamics that govern other commitment systems apply. AI alignment can be analyzed as an acknowledgment-capacity problem rather than purely as a technical problem. The brittle-fixity failure mode is available for AI systems trained on unrevisable objective functions deployed in environments those objectives don't anticipate.

These predictions are the framework's productive output. None of them follows from any single domain-specific framing. All follow from recognizing that the structural pattern is general.

## What this is not

This framework is not a theory of everything. It applies wherever the commitment-system structure obtains and does not apply elsewhere. Phenomena that don't involve stabilized commitments under operational drift — pure physical processes, pure mathematical structures considered apart from the practitioners using them, computational processes without agent commitments — are not commitment systems and are not in the framework's scope.

The framework does not resolve substantive questions any particular commitment system faces. The US Constitution's adaptation pathway is a political problem requiring political work. The Catholic Church's relationship to doctrinal change is a theological problem requiring theological work. A specific marriage's relationship to drift is a relational problem requiring relational work. The framework provides scaffolding for thinking about these problems clearly — what kind of commitment system is this, what acknowledgment capacity does it have, what failure mode is most likely. The work of doing something about it remains where it always was.

The framework's typology of five configurations plus one feature is provisional. Empirical investigation in domains beyond cultural containers will likely surface refinements: configurations that subdivide, features that turn out to be configurations, structural patterns specific to particular domains that turn out to generalize. The framework holds itself to revision in response to such findings.

## The recursive payoff

The framework's claim to be a framework for commitment systems generally, rather than for cultural containers specifically, depends on its analytical moves transferring across domains. Mathematics under proof-assistant pressure shows the transfer cleanly. The acknowledgment-capacity diagnosis that names what mathematicians are doing when they extend their verification machinery applies identically to AI development teams extending their alignment infrastructure, to therapeutic communities extending their interpretive frameworks, to legal systems extending their precedent practices. The structural moves are the same because the structural problem is the same.

Recognizing this is what makes the framework analytically productive rather than rhetorically applicable. The framework doesn't claim that mathematics and marriages and AI systems are similar in their content; it claims they are structurally identical in their relationship between commitment and drift, and that the analytical machinery developed for one domain transfers to the others when the structural diagnosis is the same.

The transfer is not metaphorical. The same diagnostic questions produce useful answers across domains. The same configurations describe stable patterns across domains. The same failure modes appear across domains when the structural conditions are met. The framework's value is that it makes these structural identities explicit, which lets analysis developed in one domain inform action in another.

What the framework requires of the analyst is the discipline of recognizing that the commitment-system structure obtains in the case at hand, identifying the relevant kernel and authority structure, locating the configuration, and reading what failure modes are characteristic. What it offers is that the same machinery applies wherever the structure obtains, which is far more places than any single-domain framework can engage.
