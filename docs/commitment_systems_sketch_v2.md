# Commitment Systems and the Architecture of Drift

Any arrangement of stabilized commitments operating in an environment that changes faster than the commitments do will eventually face a structural problem: how to acknowledge that operational practice has drifted from the commitments without dissolving the authority that grounds the commitments. This problem appears wherever the conditions hold, which is more places than is usually noticed. Religions, constitutions, scientific theories, philosophical positions, identities, marriages, employment relationships, legal systems, artificial intelligence training protocols, mathematical practice, professional standards, ideological movements — all are commitment systems. All face the same structural problem. All instantiate the same patterns of response.

This is a theoretical claim, not an empirical survey. The argument is that the structural pattern is real and the framework that analyzes it is portable across the domains where the pattern obtains. The empirical work of testing the framework in specific domains is the research program this paper aims to enable, not the work this paper performs.

I'll call these *commitment systems*. A commitment system has three components.

A **kernel**: the stabilized arrangement of commitments that constitutes the system's nominal core. This may be a text (a constitution, a foundational paper, a creed), a body of practice (the methodology of a craft tradition), a narrative (an identity's organizing story), a relationship (the implicit terms a marriage rests on), or some combination. The kernel is what participants in the system point to when asked what the system is.

An **authority structure**: the mechanism that determines what counts as legitimate interpretation of the kernel, and which has its own incentives, resource flows, and stake in preserving its standing. Authority structures may be external (a priesthood, a judiciary, an academic discipline, a board), distributed (the parties to a relationship, a community of practice, a peer-review system), internal (an individual's self-interpretive practices), or composite. The authority structure decides who has standing to read the kernel and on what grounds; it also benefits from interpretations that preserve its standing and pays costs for interpretations that erode it. Treating authority structures as politically neutral interpretive machinery misses what they are. They have skin in the game.

**Drift**: unmarked mutation of operational practice relative to the kernel. Drift is intrinsic because the environment the kernel was stabilized within continues to change after the kernel stabilizes. No commitment system preserves perfect fidelity to its kernel across time. The question is not whether drift occurs but whether the system's authority structure can and will acknowledge drift without losing legitimacy, and whether the costs of acknowledgment are distributed in ways that allow it to happen.

The framework's central claim is that systems that maintain commitments under environmental change face this structural problem, that the responses available to them form a small finite set, and that the diagnostic moves and failure mode predictions that follow are the same across domains because the structural problem is the same. The framework is bold rather than cautious because boldness is what makes it useful. A hedged framework that included sufficient caveats to be immune to falsification would also be immune to predictive bite.

## What kind of drift

Drift is not a single phenomenon. The single concept bundles distinct dynamics with different causes, different rates, and different responses appropriate to them. The framework treats five drift types as load-bearing distinctions.

*Environmental drift* is operational practice changing in response to environmental change that the kernel did not anticipate. Computational tools change what mathematicians do. Demographic change changes what marriage means as institution. Technological capability change changes what AI systems are actually doing. The kernel was stabilized in one environment; the environment moves; practice follows the environment; the gap between kernel and practice opens. This is the drift type most commitment systems are designed to handle through whatever acknowledgment capacity they have.

*Authority drift* is the interpretive structure itself changing — through personnel turnover, through institutional evolution, through the gradual replacement of one generation of interpreters by another with different commitments. The kernel may stay textually fixed while what counts as legitimate reading of it shifts. This drift type often runs faster than environmental drift and produces gaps the framework's standard configurations don't always anticipate, because the framework assumes authority is the stable element processing change in practice.

*Endogenous drift* is the authority structure's own incentives producing reinterpretation independent of environmental pressure. The structure benefits from reading the kernel in particular ways; over time the readings drift toward what benefits the structure. This is the drift type that authority structures actively suppress acknowledgment of, because acknowledging it would expose that the readings serve the readers. The cover story analysis below is the framework's primary tool for handling endogenous drift.

*Recognition drift* is the gap between drift that has occurred and drift that has been recognized as having occurred. A commitment system can be operating substantially distant from its kernel while participants honestly believe the system is operating in fidelity to the kernel. Recognition drift accumulates separately from operational drift and can exceed it substantially before any acknowledgment moment. The 50-pound weight gain example is precise: gradual increments accumulate below the threshold of recognition; at some point the gap becomes visible.

*Sabotage drift* is participants who recognize the gap deliberately exploiting it rather than acknowledging it. They use the cover story as a crowbar. This is rarer than the other drift types but it produces distinctive trajectories — failure mode patterns where the system collapses faster than its structural configuration would predict because actors are working to collapse it.

These five drift types are not equivalent. They have different causes, different rates, different observation requirements, and different appropriate responses. The framework that treats drift as a single phenomenon loses the analytical traction the decomposition provides. The configurations and failure modes below operate over these distinct drift types, and which drift type dominates in a particular system shapes which response is appropriate.

## The kernel as frame-relative

Before developing the configurations, one conceptual move requires explicit treatment.

For some commitment systems, the kernel has a clear meaning because the kernel is genuinely fixed — a constitution's text, a mathematical axiom, a marriage vow's explicit content. For others, the kernel seems partly constituted by accumulated interpretation, which makes "drift relative to kernel" appear circular.

The resolution is to fix the frame. At any moment T1, the kernel is whatever participants in the system point to as the reference for that analysis. At T1, a common-law kernel is the accumulated body of precedent up to T1. At T1, a person's identity kernel is the set of commitments they currently treat as constitutive of who they are. At T1, a marriage's kernel is the set of explicit and implicit understandings the parties currently treat as the relationship's terms. Drift between T1 and T2 is measured against the T1 kernel, not against some eternal fixed reference. The kernel itself can change between T1 and T2, and that change is itself drift to be acknowledged or denied.

This treatment makes systems with evolving kernels analyzable in the same terms as systems with fixed ones. The kernel at T1 is determinate. Drift between T1 and T2 is well-defined. The authority structure acknowledges or denies the drift through whatever mechanisms it has, with whatever incentives it has, in whatever environment it operates within.

The frame-fixing move has a philosophical commitment built into it that should be made explicit. The framework privileges the analyst's position over participant positions for the specific purpose of making drift visible. Participants embedded in a commitment system often cannot see the drift they're participating in producing, because seeing it would threaten the authority structure that grants them their position. The analyst's exterior position is what makes the analysis possible. This is not a claim that the analyst sees the truth and participants see illusion. It is a claim that the analyst sees something specific — the gap between kernel at T1 and practice at T2 — that participant positions are structurally unable to see clearly. Critics who object that this privileges the analyst's position are correct that it does so. The framework's response is that the privilege is constitutive of the analytical move rather than incidental to it. A framework that didn't privilege exterior observation could not perform the analysis.

The analytical position is itself a commitment system. Its kernel is the framework's structural claims. Its authority structure is whoever applies the framework. Its drift is real and the framework holds itself to the same standards it applies to others. Recognition that the framework is subject to its own analysis is what distinguishes the framework from Lakatos's research programs (which Lakatos did not rigorously apply to his own work) and Kuhn's paradigms (which Kuhn treated as something happening to others while writing from outside).

## The five configurations

Different combinations of kernel formalization, authority structure type, acknowledgment capacity, and coupling between formal and operational kernels produce different commitment system configurations. Five base configurations are worth distinguishing across domains. The configurations are claims about attractor patterns in the configuration space, not partitions of all possible systems. Mixed and transitional configurations are common; the configurations name the points systems tend to cluster around.

*Marked revision*: highly specifiable kernel, epistemic authority structure with limited extraction stake in kernel preservation, working acknowledgment capacity. The kernel is precise. Authority is voluntary and grounded in expertise. Drift is formalized as revision: someone proposes a change, the change is checked, and if it works the system absorbs it. Acknowledgment is marked and legible rather than absorbed silently. The configuration appears across domains: mathematics in the scientific theory case, certain therapeutic practices in the identity case, couples with explicit norm-revision practices in the relational case, programming languages with formal proposal-and-deprecation procedures in the technical case. Stability requires that acknowledgment bandwidth matches environmental change rate and that the authority structure does not develop strong extraction incentives in kernel preservation. The configuration is fragile when environmental change accelerates beyond acknowledgment capacity (mathematics under computer-assisted proof pressure) or when authority structures develop extraction stakes (epistemic communities turning into rent-seeking guilds).

*Interpretive accretion*: highly specifiable kernel, traditional or institutional authority structure with substantial stake in kernel preservation, blocked acknowledgment capacity for explicit kernel revision. The text is fixed. Authority is grounded in continuity with the founding text. The formal mechanism for changing the text does not function or does not exist. Drift migrates entirely into interpretation. Everyone insists the kernel controls while the operational meaning shifts substantially. Drift accumulates as unmarked interpretive accretion. The configuration appears across domains: Brahmanical commentary on the Vedas, doctrine of development in Catholicism, common law jurisprudence, constitutional originalism's evolving content despite its claim to fixity. In identity systems, interpretive accretion appears when a person preserves their core narrative ("I have always been someone who values X") while reinterpreting operationally what X means. In relational systems, it appears when couples maintain the same explicit story about their relationship while the operational reality shifts. In scientific theories, it appears as protective-belt modifications absorbing anomalies while the hard core stays intact. The configuration is durable across millennia in the right environments and can produce massive operational change without ever surfacing kernel revision.

*Diffuse reconstruction*: under-specified or intentionally ambiguous kernel, no centralized authority structure, distributed interpretation with mutually incompatible readings claiming the same source. The kernel can be claimed by many parties producing operationally incompatible interpretations. The configuration persists indefinitely but lacks operational coherence. Platonism in philosophy is the canonical case. In identity systems, diffuse reconstruction appears when commitments are held without operational implications, allowing them to mean different things at different times. In relational systems, it appears in relationships where the parties have incompatible operational interpretations of shared commitments and no mechanism to adjudicate. In AI development, the various interpretations of "alignment" or "safety" function as diffuse reconstruction — shared kernel terms with mutually incompatible operational meanings depending on the speaker. In contemporary political ideology, terms like "freedom" or "justice" function similarly. The configuration is stable in the sense of persisting; it is unstable in the sense of never resolving operational coherence. It often serves strategic purposes for parties who benefit from operational ambiguity.

*Implicit practice*: no codified kernel, traditional authority over practice itself. The kernel is whatever the system does. Drift is the mechanism rather than a failure of it. The configuration is stable as long as practice remains coherent and the relevant authority structures continue to operate. Breakdown is severe when practice loses coherence because there is no fixed referent to appeal to. The UK constitution is the canonical organizational case. Craft traditions transmitting tacit knowledge through apprenticeship are another. In identity systems, implicit practice appears when a person's sense of self is constituted by what they do rather than by explicit narrative commitments. In relational systems, it appears in long-term partnerships where the relationship is whatever the parties do together, without explicit articulation of terms. In scientific practice, it appears in tacit knowledge that resists textual transmission. The configuration's failure mode is loss of practice coherence (often through generational discontinuity, environmental disruption, or authority structure breakdown) without any fixed referent to reconstruct from.

*Anchored fixity*: formalized kernel paired with authority structure that grounds its legitimacy in the kernel's unchangeability and extracts substantial benefit from preventing kernel revision. The kernel cannot be amended because amendment would dissolve the authority structure that requires fixity. Drift denial is the source of authority rather than a side effect. The configuration has two sub-types that produce radically different stability profiles.

*Anchored fixity with interpretive-accretion layer* pairs the unrevisable kernel with a substantial interpretive substructure beneath it. Drift gets absorbed by the interpretive layer rather than surfacing as kernel revision. The configuration can persist millennia: the Hindu Vedic-Brahmanical system, post-development Catholic doctrine, Confucian commentary tradition. In identity systems, this appears when a person treats core commitments as unrevisable while developing sophisticated interpretive practices that absorb operational drift without forcing identity crisis.

*Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer* lacks the interpretive substructure. The kernel is supposed to govern operational practice directly. The Spartan Lycurgan system, certain forms of religious fundamentalism, certain trauma responses that treat the traumatic event as unrevisable kernel without developing interpretive machinery to process it. The configuration produces accumulating gap and catastrophic breakdown when environmental change exceeds what the kernel can govern. Spartan breakdown at Leuctra is the canonical case at civilizational scale; identity crises in fundamentalist deconstruction are the same pattern at personal scale.

The framework predicts that anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer produces brittle-fixity failure across organizational, identity, and relational systems. The Spartan pattern, the fundamentalist deconstruction pattern, and the rigid-relationship breakdown pattern share structural mechanisms. Treatment approaches that work in one domain may transfer to others when the structural diagnosis is the same. This is a falsifiable prediction. If brittle-fixity signatures do not appear similarly across these domains when the structural conditions are met, the framework's generalization claim fails.

A sixth feature, *decoupled formalization*, combines with any of the base configurations rather than constituting one of its own. A system has decoupled formalization when its formal kernel exists for purposes other than operational control — legibility, historical legitimacy, ceremonial completeness, external recognition — while operational authority sits elsewhere. Late Soviet constitutional law had decoupled formalization combined with operational anchored fixity in party structures. Many corporate governance documents have decoupled formalization combined with operational implicit practice in management cultures. The feature can combine with any base configuration, producing systems where the formal kernel and the operational kernel have separate dynamics.

## How domains differ

The configurations generalize but the domains they appear in have specific features that affect how the framework's analysis proceeds. The differences are not bugs in the generalization; they are features that the framework should make explicit because the failure modes characteristic of each domain follow from how the structural specifics interact with the base configurations.

In **organizational systems**, the authority structure is typically external to individual participants. Drift operates on shared practice maintained by many parties. Acknowledgment requires coordination among many parties and can be blocked by coordination failure even when individuals would prefer to acknowledge. Authority structures have institutional incentives and resource flows; the cover story dynamics are typically about preserving institutional standing.

In **identity systems**, the authority structure is partly internal — the individual's own interpretive practices — but partly external, through communities, therapists, ideologies the individual trusts. Drift operates on individual experience. Acknowledgment can happen at the individual level but is often easier with external interpretive support. The authority structure's incentives operate through identity coherence — acknowledging certain drift would dissolve the self the structure is interpreting. Embodied kernels (held in the body's responses rather than in explicit beliefs, often the case for trauma-organized identities) resist interpretive accretion in domain-specific ways that the framework's standard configurations don't fully anticipate.

In **relational systems**, the authority structure is distributed across the parties to the relationship plus external interpreters they share or don't share. Drift operates on relational practice. Acknowledgment requires participation by multiple parties; unilateral acknowledgment doesn't process drift the way mutual acknowledgment does. This produces a structural feature unique to relational systems: asymmetric acknowledgment, where one party recognizes drift the other refuses to recognize. Power asymmetries within the relationship determine which interpretations prevail when acknowledgment is contested. The framework's authority structure analysis must handle distributed authority with asymmetric standing.

In **scientific and intellectual systems**, the authority structure is typically epistemic — grounded in expertise voluntarily recognized by a community of practitioners. Drift operates on the theory's operational use and on the body of evidence the theory must account for. Authority structures can shift from epistemic to traditional over time as fields mature; this transition is itself a configuration change the framework can track.

In **technical systems** including AI development, the authority structure is composite — research communities plus deployment institutions plus regulatory bodies plus the artifacts themselves, which can exhibit operational behaviors that constrain interpretation. Drift operates on theory, on deployed systems, and on the authority structure's own definitions. The current state of AI alignment is a useful diagnostic case: the kernel is something like training objectives and safety specifications; the authority structure includes labs, deployers, regulators, and feedback loops; drift includes distribution shift, reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, and emergent behaviors. The framework's analysis treats AI alignment as an acknowledgment-capacity problem with the structural difficulty that the authority structure (lab safety teams plus deployment institutions plus regulators) operates on faster cycles than the technical artifacts, producing the inverted configuration where authority drifts faster than practice rather than the standard configuration where practice drifts and authority lags. This is a specific structural prediction: AI development is currently operating in an unusual configuration where the framework's standard failure mode analyses need adjustment.

## The diagnostic order

When examining an actual commitment system, four questions arise in order.

First: what is the kernel and what is the authority structure? Specifying these is the precondition for further analysis. Many disputes about commitment systems turn out to be disputes about which kernel is operative or which authority is legitimate, and these need to be resolved before drift or stability can be assessed.

Second: which drift types are operative, and is any drift acknowledged? Different drift types require different responses. Environmental drift requires bandwidth that tracks environmental rate. Authority drift requires recognition that the interpretive structure itself is changing. Endogenous drift requires cover story analysis to see what the authority structure has an interest in not seeing. Recognition drift requires checking whether participants can see the drift that has occurred. Sabotage drift requires identifying actors who are exploiting the gap deliberately. The acknowledgment pattern across these drift types is diagnostic of the configuration and the authority structure's incentives.

Third: is the configuration functional in this environment? Some combinations are inherently fragile (anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer produces accumulating gap until breakdown when environmental change is significant). Others are stable indefinitely under appropriate conditions (marked revision when bandwidth tracks environment-rate and authority structures don't develop extraction stakes; implicit practice when practice remains coherent; anchored fixity with interpretive accretion when the interpretive layer can absorb operational drift). Environmental rate is itself variable across domains and across times, so functionality assessments are inherently conditional.

Fourth: who has standing to acknowledge drift, and is acknowledgment distributed appropriately? This question is more prominent in relational and identity systems than in organizational systems because acknowledgment in those domains is structurally distributed and often asymmetric. The question generalizes to organizational systems through the cover story analysis: even where formal acknowledgment authority exists, the structural distribution of who benefits from acknowledgment and who pays for it determines whether acknowledgment can actually happen.

## Trajectory and cover story

Two extensions handle change over time and observer-position variation.

Trajectory analysis starts from a specified configuration at time T1 and describes what configurations are reachable at time T+n. Some transitions are likely; some are blocked. Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer cannot transition smoothly to marked revision without first dismantling the authority structure that requires fixity, which means the transition usually requires authority-structure breakdown rather than authority-structure revision. Identity systems organized around anchored fixity often transition to identity crisis rather than to marked revision precisely because the authority structure (the person's commitment to the unrevisable kernel) cannot accommodate revision without dissolving itself.

The dormant-container activation pattern is a fourth trajectory channel beyond absorption, rejection, and operational separation. A system that has previously been an outer container but has been operationally suspended — its kernel and authority structure preserved but not currently operating as the system's effective authority — can be reactivated as alternative legitimacy when the active outer container loses standing. France's Estates-General reactivation in 1789 after 175 years of dormancy is the canonical organizational case. The Meiji Restoration's reactivation of the Imperial Court after centuries of bakufu mediation is another. In identity systems, this appears in deconstruction trajectories where a pre-conversion or pre-cult identity, formally preserved but operationally suspended, gets reactivated as alternative legitimacy when the active identity loses standing. The pattern is structurally identical across domains: preserved-but-suspended outer kernel plus inner systems with sufficient independent operational capacity equals possible activation under stress.

Cover story analysis handles observer-position variation and the political dimension of authority structure. In any commitment system, participants at different positions see different things. Insiders, external observers, beneficiaries, victims, reformers, conservatives — each position produces a different classification of the same kernel-practice relationship. The disagreement has structure rather than being noise. Some positions are systematically unable to see drift they participate in producing, because seeing it would threaten the authority structure that grants them their position. The constitutional originalist cannot easily acknowledge that their preferred interpretation is itself an evolution of original meaning. The church hierarchy cannot easily acknowledge that doctrinal development is doctrinal change. The institutional reformer cannot easily acknowledge that their proposed reform serves institutional interests. The identity-anchored person cannot easily acknowledge that their commitments have shifted operationally.

The pattern is structural rather than individual. Position determines what is visible from that position. Where authority structures have substantial extraction stakes in kernel preservation, endogenous drift produces cover stories that serve the structure's interests; the structure is structurally unable to see what would threaten its position. This is not a moral claim about institutional actors; it is a structural claim about how authority structures with stake in kernel preservation generate predictable blindness. The blindness is testable in specific cases: for a given configuration and position, the framework can predict what drift the position should be unable to see and what cover story will replace it.

## The framework as commitment system

The framework analyzes commitment systems. It is itself a commitment system, and treating it as one is part of the discipline the framework requires.

Its kernel is the structural claims developed in this and successor documents. Its authority structure consists of whoever adopts the framework and applies it. Its drift is real: the framework's own commitments change as it gets applied, criticized, and revised. Its acknowledgment capacity is whatever revision discipline its practitioners maintain.

This is not paradoxical. Commitment systems with good acknowledgment capacity do acknowledge their own drift. A framework that recommends drift acknowledgment to other commitment systems while denying its own drift would be operating exactly the cover story mechanism it identifies in conservative authority structures.

The framework's own preference for marked revision over interpretive accretion is not a bias to be apologized for. The framework's purpose is making drift visible. Marked revision is what drift becoming visible consists of. Interpretive accretion is adaptive in many environments — the Brahmanical commentary tradition's millennia of operation is empirical evidence of this — but it is adaptive specifically by absorbing drift below the threshold of acknowledgment. The framework that did not prefer marked over absorbed would be a framework that could not make the distinction it exists to make. The preference is constitutive rather than incidental.

Critics objecting that this smuggles in Enlightenment values are correct that the framework values marked acknowledgment. The framework's response is that valuing marked acknowledgment is what analyzing commitment systems requires. A 50-pound weight gain accumulates through increments below the threshold of recognition; the analytical move that makes the gain visible is necessarily a move that prefers marked recognition over absorbed change. The same applies to commitment systems generally. The framework's diagnostic moves require an analytical position that prefers acknowledged drift over absorbed drift; without this preference the framework's distinctions could not be drawn.

What would falsify the framework. The framework rests on load-bearing claims: that the kernel/authority/drift structure obtains across the domains specified, that the configurations describe real attractors rather than analytical artifacts, that the failure mode predictions transfer across domains because the structural mechanisms are the same, that environmental coupling is operative, and that the recursive self-application produces real constraint on the framework's development rather than epistemic immunity. If any of these load-bearing claims fail, the framework fails. Specifically: if commitment systems across the named domains do not exhibit kernel/authority/drift structure under examination, the generalization claim fails. If the configurations do not predict failure modes across domains, the typology fails. If environmental coupling does not produce the predicted stability/fragility patterns, the calibration claim fails. If the framework cannot revise itself in response to empirical findings (as the cultural containers investigation forced), the recursive discipline claim fails. The framework is bold rather than hedged because boldness is what makes these failure modes detectable. A framework that included sufficient qualifications to be immune to falsification would be immune to predictive bite.

## A demonstration: the environment-conditional stability of mathematics

The framework's value test is whether it yields analytical moves that domain-specific framings don't naturally produce. One short demonstration.

Mathematics is conventionally treated as the paragon of stable knowledge — the canonical example of a tradition that has functioned for thousands of years without crisis. The framework's prediction about marked-revision configurations (specifiable kernel, epistemic authority, working bandwidth) supports this characterization. But the framework adds a calibration condition: stability is environment-relative. The same configuration that produces stability in one environment can produce stress in another.

Mathematics' acknowledgment machinery is calibrated for a specific environment: proofs that can be checked by human readers within reasonable time, building on a literature small enough that experts can maintain working knowledge of their subfields. The environment is slow-changing — mathematical objects don't change, foundational results stay valid, and amendment can proceed at the pace of human verification.

This environment has been changing. Computer-assisted proofs introduce results that no human can verify directly. The 1976 four-color theorem began the controversy; Hales' 1998 proof of the Kepler conjecture made it acute when journal referees spent four years unable to verify the computer code, and the Annals of Mathematics ultimately published an abridged version while explicitly refusing to vouch for the computational part. The Coq formalization in 2005 settled the case but established that human verification was no longer sufficient for an emerging class of results. Subfields proliferate faster than experts can track them; Kevin Buzzard has argued in public talks that no single person understands the complete proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, and that the mathematical literature may contain accepted results that are in fact incorrect — a claim he raises not as scandal but as motivation for formalization. Lean and other proof assistants are now being adopted not as supplements to human verification but as the only feasible way to verify some contemporary work; Peter Scholze's perfectoid spaces work was verified through the Liquid Tensor Experiment.

The framework's diagnosis: mathematics' acknowledgment machinery is being stressed by an environment that now produces drift faster than human-paced verification can track. The proof assistant movement is bandwidth extension in response to environmental acceleration. The conventional view treats mathematics as proof that stable knowledge is possible; the framework's reading is that mathematics demonstrates a particular configuration that produces stability under particular conditions, and that the work being done now is the work of recalibrating the configuration to a changed environment.

The result is intuitive once stated. Mathematicians extending their verification machinery in response to computational complexity are not abandoning the architecture; they are recalibrating it. But the conventional framing — mathematics as paragon of stability — obscures what they are doing. The framework names it precisely: environmental acceleration is producing drift faster than the previous bandwidth could acknowledge, so the bandwidth is being extended to maintain the acknowledgment-rate match. The same analysis would apply to any commitment system facing environmental acceleration. AI development is the contemporary case where the analysis matters most.

## Why this matters

The standard debates about cultural change collapse into moralized disputes — tradition versus progress, fidelity versus innovation, original meaning versus living interpretation. These debates persist because they cannot be settled in their own terms. Whichever side wins one round, the other side returns with the same arguments restated.

The commitment systems framework reframes these debates as questions about architecture rather than values. The question is not whether drift is good or bad but whether the system's authority structure can acknowledge the drift its domain actually produces, given the rate at which that domain changes and the incentives the authority structure has. Systems whose acknowledgment capacity matches their environment's drift rate adapt without crisis. Systems whose acknowledgment capacity falls behind accumulate gap until something breaks.

This reframe has practical consequences across domains. It identifies which commitment systems are stable and which are unstable as structural rather than political questions. It distinguishes legitimate reform from rationalized continuity by asking whether reform changes the authority structure's incentives or just relabels the kernel. It locates the source of recurring crises in architecture rather than personalities. And it provides a vocabulary for analyzing systems across domains that previously had no shared analytical framework.

The framework does not resolve the substantive questions any particular commitment system faces. The US Constitution's adaptation pathway is a political problem requiring political work. The Catholic Church's relationship to doctrinal change is a theological problem requiring theological work. A specific marriage's relationship to drift is a relational problem requiring relational work. The framework provides scaffolding for thinking about these problems clearly — what kind of system is this, what acknowledgment capacity does it have, what failure mode is most likely given the configuration and the environment. The work of doing something about it remains where it always was.

## What this enables

This sketch establishes the theoretical foundation for a research program. The framework's claims are bold and load-bearing; their value is in driving subsequent operational work rather than in being finished theory.

The next phase of work is operationalization. Acknowledgment capacity needs formal measurement primitives — candidate indicators include bandwidth (acknowledgment events per unit time), latency (delay between drift and acknowledgment), resolution (granularity of distinctions the structure can register), cost (social/cognitive/institutional cost of acknowledgment), and authority coupling (whether acknowledgment requires consensus, elite approval, or individual action). Each of these is a separate engine extension. Drift decomposition needs corpus testing in domains beyond organizational containers. The five configurations need empirical verification in identity, relational, scientific, and AI domains. The cover story analysis needs application to specific cases with predefined positional blindness predictions.

The framework's contribution is to specify what this operational work should target. The kernel/authority/drift structure tells the engine what objects to represent. The five configurations tell it what attractors to look for. The diagnostic order tells it what queries to support. The trajectory and cover story analyses tell it what dynamic patterns to track. The framework-as-commitment-system recursion tells it that the engine's outputs are themselves subject to drift and require their own acknowledgment discipline.

What the framework predicts, taken together, is that commitment systems are a distinct analytical object that has been hiding in plain sight across multiple disciplines, that the same machinery analyzes them across domains, that the failure modes are predictable from structural configuration plus environmental rate plus authority structure incentives, and that the operational work of making this analytically tractable is the next several years of the research program. The sketch is bold rather than hedged because boldness is what makes the predictions worth testing. The framework either does this work or it doesn't; the next phase finds out.
