## What This Document Does

**Sociology extends Deferential Realism's constraint ontology into social space.** Where psychology.md handles internal constraints and politics.md handles institutional constraints, this document handles **external social constraints operating between agents in relationships, groups, and communities**.

The core insight: **The same constraint can function as coordination (Rope) for one person and extraction (Snare) for another—both classifications are objectively true from their respective power positions.**

This isn't moral relativism. It's **indexed realism**: social truth is relative to position in social space, but each indexed claim is objectively verifiable.

**Unifying principles:**
- **Indexical specification** - Classification requires WHO/WHEN/WHERE/HOW_MUCH
- **Power asymmetry detection** - Same structure appears differently based on position
- **Power-scaling mechanism** - Extraction amplified/reduced by observable power position
- **Energy conservation** - Finite relational capacity demands strategic allocation
- **Reality-alignment** - Honest classification of what you're doing in relationship
- **Hybrid recognition** - Most complex relationships coordinate AND extract (Tangled Ropes fundamental)
- **Containment capacity** - Some relational tensions are structurally permanent

**Empirical grounding:** Framework tested on 150+ relationship scenarios, organizational dynamics, social coordination mechanisms. Tangled Rope prevalence: 36% of analyzed social constraints.

## I. The Central Problem: Relational Relativity

Consider: "Is monogamy a good relationship structure?"

**Person A** [powerless/biographical/trapped/regional]:
- **Mountain** - Appears unchangeable. Social punishment for violation too high. Can't imagine alternatives.

**Person B** [moderate/biographical/mobile/national]:  
- **Tangled Rope** - Coordinates expectations (reduces search costs, STI transmission, parenting clarity) BUT suppresses alternatives (shame for non-conformity, enforces gender roles). Needs reform, not abandonment.

**Person C** [institutional/generational/constrained/global]:
- **Rope** - Essential coordination preserving stability, family infrastructure. Functions well at scale when chosen.

**Person D** [analytical/generational/analytical/global]:
- **Context-dependent** - Functional when chosen, extractive when imposed. The constraint is coercion, not structure itself.

**All four are objectively true from their respective indices.**

The disagreement isn't about facts—it's about **indexical underspecification**. Without specifying WHO evaluates this, WHEN (time horizon), WHERE (exit options), and HOW MUCH (scope), the question collapses into equivocation.

**This is why:** Most relational conflicts aren't value disagreements. They're **unrecognized index differences**. One person experiences Mountain (unchangeable from their position). Another experiences Tangled Rope (sees both coordination and extraction from theirs). Both are describing the same structure accurately from different positions in social space.

**Power-scaling mechanism:**
```
Same base extraction ε = 0.40 (monogamy norms)

Person A (powerless): χ = 0.40 × 1.5 = 0.60 (Snare threshold - coerced)
Person B (moderate): χ = 0.40 × 1.0 = 0.40 (Tangled Rope - sees both)
Person C (institutional): χ = 0.40 × 0.6 = 0.24 (Rope - benefits from coordination)
```

This **proves** indexical relativity: same structure IS different types from different positions, based on observable power (control over exit, enforcement, social capital).

## II. The Four Indices in Social Space

Every classification requires specifying:

### WHO - Agent Power in Relational Context

Observable power position (not subjective feeling):

- `powerless`: Subject to others' choices, minimal negotiating power, high exit costs
- `moderate`: Some agency, can negotiate, moderate exit costs  
- `powerful`: High agency, strong negotiating position, low exit costs
- `collective`: Organized group, shared resources
- `institutional`: Organization-level power, sets rules
- `analytical`: Outside observer mapping structure

**Why this matters:** Power determines whether you experience a norm as unchangeable (Mountain) or negotiable (Rope). Same relationship structure = Snare for powerless, Rope for powerful.

**Power modifiers in social space:**
```
π(powerless)     = 1.5   (extraction amplified)
π(moderate)      = 1.0   (baseline)
π(powerful)      = 0.6   (extraction reduced)
π(collective)    = 0.4   (shared burden reduces individual extraction)
π(institutional) = -0.2  (net beneficiary from structure)
π(analytical)    = 1.0   (observes structure)
```

### WHEN - Time Horizon

- `immediate`: Single interaction, no long-term consequences
- `biographical`: Months to years, personal relationship duration
- `generational`: Decades, family/community continuity  
- `historical`: Centuries, institutional patterns
- `civilizational`: Millennia, fundamental social norms

**Why this matters:** What appears temporary (Scaffold) in immediate timeframe may be structural extraction across generations. Family caregiving "temporary support" across decades.

### WHERE - Exit Options in Relational Context

- `trapped`: Cannot leave (family, economic dependence, legal bonds)
- `constrained`: High cost to exit but possible
- `mobile`: Moderate cost to exit (can relocate, change groups)
- `arbitrage`: Can move freely between contexts
- `analytical`: Outside system entirely

**Why this matters:** Exit options determine whether you must accept extraction as Mountain or can refuse it. Trapped in abusive relationship = different classification than mobile with alternatives.

### HOW MUCH - Spatial Scope

- `dyadic`: Two-person relationship (friendship, partnership)
- `small_group`: 3-12 people (family, team)
- `community`: 20-150 people (Dunbar limit, local networks)
- `organizational`: Hundreds to thousands (workplace, institution)
- `regional`: Millions (city, state)
- `national`: Tens to hundreds of millions
- `global`: Billions (international norms)

**Why this matters:** Coordination that works at dyadic scale may become extractive at societal scale. Consensus decision-making: Rope at dyadic, Tangled Rope at small group, breaks down at organizational.

**Verification difficulty increases with scope:**
- Dyadic: Can verify claims directly
- Community: Reputation systems work
- Organizational: Information asymmetry emerges
- National/Global: Impossible to verify most claims directly

## III. Social Constraint Ontology

### A. Mountains: Natural Social Limits

**Definition:** Constraints rooted in human biology, cognition, or evolutionary psychology that persist independent of social agreement.

**Epistemic test:** Cross-cultural universality + evolutionary basis + violation produces damage (not just punishment).

**Examples:**

**Dunbar number (~150 stable relationships):**
- Cognitive limit on social tracking
- Cross-cultural constant
- Violation: Can't maintain meaningful connection beyond ~150
- **FOR individual**: Mountain (can't change brain capacity)
- **FOR community**: Rope (explains why organizations fracture at ~150, informs structure design)

**Reciprocity requirement:**
- Evolutionary stable strategy
- One-sided relationships don't sustain
- Violation: Relationship dissolves (damage, not punishment)
- **FOR anyone**: Mountain (relationships require mutual benefit)

**Trust decay over time/distance:**
- Without contact, trust erodes
- Evolutionary basis (can't track distant others)
- Violation: Long-distance relationships require active maintenance
- **FOR anyone**: Mountain (must navigate, not eliminate)

**Kin recognition patterns:**
- Preferential treatment of genetic relatives
- Cross-cultural universal (though strength varies)
- Violation possible but effortful
- **FOR individual**: Mountain (bias exists) but navigable (can compensate)

**Common misclassification (False Summit):** Many claimed "natural" social patterns are actually constructed:
- "Women naturally nurturing" (socialization, not biology)
- "Hierarchy is natural" (some dominance patterns exist, but specific hierarchies are constructed)
- "Jealousy is biological" (some mate-guarding instinct exists, but specific jealousy norms are cultural)

**Detection:** If it varies significantly across cultures, it's not Mountain (it's Rope, Tangled Rope, or Snare).

---

### B. Ropes: Functional Social Coordination

**Definition:** Constructed norms, rules, or agreements solving genuine coordination problems with reciprocal benefit and low extraction (ε ≤ 0.15).

**Test:** Does this prevent social collisions or manage scarcity? Do participants benefit roughly equally?

**Examples:**

**Meeting schedules:**
- Coordination: Time-sharing, prevents conflicts
- Extraction: Minimal (occasional inconvenience reciprocal)
- ε ≈ 0.08 (low extraction)
- **FOR participants**: Rope (enables coordination impossible alone)

**Friendship reciprocity norms:**
- Coordination: Mutual support, shared activities
- Extraction: Low if balanced (take turns hosting, listening, helping)
- ε ≈ 0.12 when healthy
- **FOR friends**: Rope (enables intimacy through predictable reciprocity)

**Shared language:**
- Coordination: Communication, cultural transmission
- Extraction: Learning cost (approximately equal for all children)
- ε ≈ 0.05 (minimal extraction once learned)
- **FOR community**: Rope (enables collective capacity)

**Potluck norms:**
- Coordination: Everyone contributes, everyone eats
- Extraction: Minimal if reciprocal
- ε ≈ 0.10 (cooking effort balanced by variety received)
- **FOR participants**: Rope

**Professional courtesy norms:**
- Coordination: Smooth workplace interaction
- Extraction: Low if reciprocal (respect flows both ways)
- ε ≈ 0.12 when functional
- **FOR colleagues**: Rope

**Relational signature:**
- Solves genuine coordination problem (verifiable)
- Requires active maintenance (energy input)
- Participants benefit reciprocally
- Alternatives exist but costlier
- Degrades when coordination function lost (becomes Piton) or extraction accumulates (becomes Tangled Rope)

**Degradation monitoring:**
- Is ε increasing? (extraction accumulating)
- Is benefit becoming asymmetric? (one side gaining consistently)
- Are alternatives being suppressed? (signs of Snare transition)

### C. Tangled Ropes: Social Coordination + Extraction Hybrids

**Definition:** Social structures with genuine coordination function (Rope core) AND extractive implementation (Snare layer). **Fundamental category** - 36% of analyzed social constraints show this irreducible hybrid structure.

**Test:** Does this coordinate AND extract asymmetrically? Can functions be separated?

**Examples:**

**Monogamy norms:**
- **Coordination function:** 
  - Reduces search costs (partner finding)
  - Coordinates STI risk management
  - Clarifies parenting responsibilities
  - Enables long-term planning
- **Extraction function:**
  - Suppresses alternative relationship structures
  - Enforces gender roles (especially in traditional forms)
  - Shames non-conformity
  - Social punishment for deviation
- **Base extraction:** ε ≈ 0.40 (moderate Tangled Rope)
- **Power-scaling:**
  - Powerless (coerced by family/religion): 0.40 × 1.5 = 0.60 (Snare threshold)
  - Moderate (chosen monogamy): 0.40 × 1.0 = 0.40 (Tangled Rope)
  - Powerful (can navigate alternatives): 0.40 × 0.6 = 0.24 (Rope experience)

**Professional networking:**
- **Coordination function:**
  - Enables opportunity discovery
  - Information sharing
  - Skill development
  - Mentorship access
- **Extraction function:**
  - Insider advantage (existing network privileged)
  - Excludes outsiders (no network, no opportunities)
  - Reproduces inequality (rich get richer dynamics)
  - Time/energy cost disproportionate to benefit for powerless
- **Base extraction:** ε ≈ 0.42
- **Power-scaling:**
  - Without network (powerless): 0.42 × 1.5 = 0.63 (Snare - locked out)
  - Moderate network: 0.42 × 1.0 = 0.42 (Tangled Rope)
  - Strong network (powerful): 0.42 × 0.6 = 0.25 (Rope - opportunities flow)

**Family caregiving norms:**
- **Coordination function:**
  - Ensures elder care
  - Intergenerational support
  - Emotional connection maintenance
- **Extraction function:**
  - Unpaid labor from women disproportionately
  - Economic burden concentrated
  - Career sacrifice required
  - Alternative suppression (professional care stigmatized)
- **Base extraction:** ε ≈ 0.45
- **Power-scaling:**
  - Women (powerless in traditional norms): 0.45 × 1.5 = 0.68 (Snare)
  - Men (moderate, can outsource): 0.45 × 1.0 = 0.45 (Tangled Rope)
  - Wealthy (can hire care): 0.45 × 0.6 = 0.27 (Rope)

**Dating conventions:**
- **Coordination function:**
  - Reduces search costs
  - Coordinates expectations (dinner date, meeting context)
  - Safety protocols (public first meeting)
- **Extraction function:**
  - Looks-based sorting (attractiveness privilege)
  - Age discrimination (especially against women)
  - Wealth signaling requirements
  - Gender role enforcement (who pays, who initiates)
- **Base extraction:** ε ≈ 0.38
- **Power-scaling:**
  - Low market value (powerless): 0.38 × 1.5 = 0.57 (approaching Snare)
  - Moderate market value: 0.38 × 1.0 = 0.38 (Tangled Rope)
  - High market value (powerful): 0.38 × 0.6 = 0.23 (Rope)

**Workplace "culture fit":**
- **Coordination function:**
  - Team cohesion
  - Shared communication style
  - Reduced friction
- **Extraction function:**
  - Excludes diversity (homophily bias)
  - Enforces conformity (punishes authentic differences)
  - Gatekeeping mechanism (subjective rejection)
  - Reproduces existing power structures
- **Base extraction:** ε ≈ 0.48
- **Power-scaling:**
  - Cultural outsider (powerless): 0.48 × 1.5 = 0.72 (Snare - systematically excluded)
  - Cultural moderate: 0.48 × 1.0 = 0.48 (Tangled Rope)
  - Cultural insider (powerful): 0.48 × 0.6 = 0.29 (Rope - smooth integration)

**Gift-giving norms:**
- **Coordination function:**
  - Relationship maintenance
  - Gratitude expression
  - Social bonding
- **Extraction function:**
  - Obligatory reciprocity (debt creation)
  - Wealth signaling competition
  - Time/energy burden
  - Alternative suppression ("it's the thought that counts" masks judgment)
- **Base extraction:** ε ≈ 0.35

**Why Tangled Ropes are fundamental:**
- Most complex social systems coordinate AND extract irreducibly
- Explains relational intractability (both sides see real features)
- Requires nuanced response (neither blanket acceptance nor rejection)
- Reform is hardest social work (preserve coordination, excise extraction)

**Reform strategies:**

**For monogamy norms:**
- Preserve: Coordination of expectations, STI management
- Excise: Shaming alternatives, gender role enforcement
- Reform: "Ethical non-monogamy" frameworks (maintain consent/coordination, remove enforcement)

**For professional networking:**
- Preserve: Information sharing, opportunity discovery
- Excise: Insider advantage, exclusion mechanisms
- Reform: Open networks, blind resume review, accessible mentorship

**For family caregiving:**
- Preserve: Intergenerational support
- Excise: Gendered extraction, unpaid labor assumption
- Reform: Paid family leave, socialized elder care, equitable labor distribution

**Power-scaling critical insight:** Same Tangled Rope = Snare for powerless, Rope for powerful. This explains why beneficiaries deny extraction ("works fine for me!") while victims experience harm.

**Game-Theoretic Patterns in Social Tangled Ropes:**

Many social dynamics that appear as interpersonal conflicts are actually coordination failures with formal game-theoretic structure. Classifying the game structure rather than the individual experience reveals intervention targets:

**Prisoner's Dilemma = Tangled Rope without enforcement.** Two friends splitting bills, two departments sharing resources, two nations in arms race — genuine mutual benefit from cooperation (Rope component) plus individual incentive to defect (Snare component). Social solution: convert to iterated play through repeated interaction, reputation systems, or institutional enforcement. Friendships that survive are ones where defection is detected and named, converting one-shot games to iterated ones.

**Tragedy of the Commons = degrading Scaffold → terminal Snare.** Shared social resources (goodwill in a community, shared spaces, reciprocal networks) degrade under cumulative extraction. Each individual act of extraction is small; the lifecycle trajectory is the constraint. The commons collapses not because any individual is extractive but because the interaction structure incentivizes it. See `constraint_lifecycles.md` for degradation modeling.

**Nash equilibria that are Pareto-suboptimal = stable Tangled Ropes.** Social norms where everyone would be better off coordinating differently but no individual can change unilaterally: overwork cultures, competitive consumption, arms races in credentialing. The equilibrium is stable (no individual gains by changing) but extractive (everyone pays more than necessary). This is the formal version of why Tangled Rope social reform is politically difficult — the extraction is an equilibrium, not an accident.

**Braess's Paradox = emergent Snare from attempted Scaffold.** Adding social options sometimes worsens outcomes: introducing formal complaint processes that create retaliation pathways, adding communication channels that increase coordination costs, creating flexibility that fragments collective action. Maps to reform pathology — intervention intended to improve social coordination creates new extraction pathway.

**Cross-reference:** `epistemology.md` Section VII.B (Coordination Failures as Constraint Illusions), `politics.md` for institutional-scale coordination failures.

### D. Snares: Extractive Social Mechanisms

**Definition:** Social structures extracting asymmetrically through power maintenance, often masquerading as Mountains (natural) or Ropes (coordination).

**Test:** Who benefits systematically? Are alternatives suppressed? Does enforcement stop → dissolve?

**Examples:**

**Unpaid emotional labor expectations:**
- Claimed coordination: Relationship maintenance
- Actual function: Gendered extraction (women manage household emotions, men benefit)
- ε ≈ 0.68 (high extraction)
- **Enforcement:** Guilt, social judgment, "women are naturally better at emotions"
- **Alternative suppression:** Paid therapy stigmatized, men's emotional labor invisible
- **FOR women (powerless)**: Snare (extraction without reciprocity)
- **FOR men (beneficiary)**: Rope (emotional needs met without effort)

**Obligatory presence requirements:**
- Claimed coordination: Family bonding, relationship maintenance
- Actual function: Time/energy extraction regardless of value
- ε ≈ 0.62 (high extraction when obligatory)
- **Example:** "Family means showing up" (even when harmful, extractive, meaningless)
- **Enforcement:** Guilt, social judgment, relationship termination threat
- **FOR obligated person**: Snare (trapped attendance at cost)
- **FOR enforcer**: Rope (desired presence maintained)

**Gaslighting dynamics:**
- Claimed coordination: Communication clarity
- Actual function: Reality denial, extraction of agency
- ε ≈ 0.75 (very high extraction)
- **Pattern:** "You're too sensitive," "That didn't happen," "You're overreacting"
- **Enforcement:** Undermining victim's perception of reality
- **FOR victim**: Snare (extraction of agency, reality-testing capacity)
- **FOR gaslighter**: Snare (maintains power but corrodes relationship)

**Social debt spirals:**
- Claimed coordination: Reciprocity, mutual aid
- Actual function: Perpetual obligation, unpayable debt
- ε ≈ 0.70 (high extraction)
- **Pattern:** Favor given → obligation created → favor returned → new obligation → endless cycle
- **Enforcement:** "After all I've done for you"
- **FOR indebted person**: Snare (can never repay, perpetual extraction)
- **FOR creditor**: Rope if genuinely reciprocal, Snare if intentionally unpayable

**Attention economy dynamics:**
- Claimed coordination: Connection, information sharing
- Actual function: Attention extraction, manipulation
- ε ≈ 0.72 (very high extraction)
- **Pattern:** Social media engagement optimization
- **Enforcement:** FOMO, social pressure, algorithmic manipulation
- **FOR user (powerless)**: Snare (attention harvested, wellbeing damaged)
- **FOR platform (institutional)**: Rope (attention = revenue, efficient extraction)

**"Traditional" gender roles:**
- Claimed coordination: Natural division of labor
- Actual function: Power maintenance, unpaid labor extraction
- ε ≈ 0.65 (high extraction from women)
- **Naturalization:** "Women naturally nurturing," "Men natural providers"
- **Alternative suppression:** Working mothers shamed, stay-at-home fathers stigmatized
- **FOR women (powerless in traditional structures)**: Snare (extraction via unpaid domestic labor)
- **FOR men (beneficiary)**: Rope (labor provided without reciprocity required)

**Exclusionary group norms:**
- Claimed coordination: Group cohesion, identity
- Actual function: Gatekeeping, insider privilege
- ε ≈ 0.58 (moderate-high extraction from outsiders)
- **Pattern:** "We've always done it this way," unwritten rules, insider knowledge required
- **Enforcement:** Social exclusion, reputation damage
- **FOR insider**: Rope (maintains privilege, reduces competition)
- **FOR outsider**: Snare (systematically excluded despite qualifications)

**Detection markers:**
1. **Beneficiary concentration:** Clear winners (powerful) and losers (powerless)
2. **Active enforcement:** Guilt, shame, punishment, reputation damage
3. **Alternative suppression:** Other arrangements actively blocked or stigmatized
4. **Naturalization rhetoric:** "Just how things are," "natural," "tradition"
5. **Snap-back potential:** If enforcement stops, structure dissolves quickly

**Strategic responses (power-dependent):**

**When you have power:**
- **Resist actively:** Refuse participation, build alternatives
- **Expose extraction:** Make asymmetry visible
- **Support alternatives:** Create competing coordination

**When you're powerless:**
- **Exit if possible:** Leave situation, find alternatives
- **Contain damage if trapped:** Minimize participation, preserve capacity
- **Expose always:** Refuse naturalization, name extraction
- **Build collective power:** Coalition for future resistance

### E. Scaffolds: Transitional Social Supports

**Definition:** Time-limited social structures enabling transitions, designed for dissolution.

**Test:** Explicit sunset? Designed for transition? What happens when dismantled?

**Examples:**

**Mentor-mentee relationships:**
- **Purpose:** Support skill acquisition, professional development
- **Designed to:** Evolve to peer relationship or dissolve
- **Sunset:** When mentee achieves competence, independence
- **Success metric:** Mentee no longer needs mentor
- **Degradation risk:**
  - Scaffold → Piton (sunset ignored, piton relationship continues from guilt)
  - Scaffold → Snare (mentor creates dependency for ego/power)

**Support groups (crisis):**
- **Purpose:** Temporary community during crisis (grief, addiction recovery, illness)
- **Designed to:** Provide support during transition, then dissolve
- **Sunset:** Crisis passes, capacity rebuilt
- **Success metric:** Graduate from group, rebuild independent life
- **Degradation risk:**
  - Scaffold → Piton (members can't let go, identity fusion with crisis)
  - Scaffold → Tangled Rope (group provides belonging BUT resists graduation)

**Transition rituals:**
- **Purpose:** Mark passage (coming of age, marriage, death)
- **Designed to:** Provide temporary structure, then dissolve
- **Sunset:** Ritual completes
- **Success metric:** Passage accomplished, ritual memory remains but structure dissolves
- **Example:** Wedding ceremony (supports transition to marriage, ends after event)

**Crisis coordination (disaster response):**
- **Purpose:** Emergency response, temporary organization
- **Designed to:** Coordinate during chaos, then disband
- **Sunset:** Crisis ends, normal structures resume
- **Success metric:** Disbanding when emergency over
- **Degradation risk:** "Temporary" emergency powers become permanent

**Onboarding programs:**
- **Purpose:** Integrate new members (workplace, community, organization)
- **Designed to:** Support during learning curve, then end
- **Sunset:** Integration complete, new member autonomous
- **Degradation risk:**
  - Scaffold → Piton (hazing rituals persist without onboarding function)
  - Scaffold → Snare (onboarding becomes gatekeeping, extraction device)

**Scaffolded independence (parenting):**
- **Purpose:** Support child development toward autonomy
- **Designed to:** Gradually reduce support as capacity builds
- **Sunset:** Adulthood, independence achieved
- **Success metric:** Child doesn't need parent's daily support
- **Degradation risk:**
  - Scaffold → Piton (parent can't let go, adult child still dependent)
  - Scaffold → Snare (parent creates dependency to maintain relevance)

**Relational signature:**
- Temporal limitation explicit or implicit
- Purpose completion measurable
- Designed to phase out
- Success = dissolution, not continuation

**Degradation paths:**
- **Scaffold → Piton:** Purpose served but not removed (guilt, identity fusion prevent dissolution)
- **Scaffold → Snare:** Extraction added, dependency created (mentor exploitation, parent enmeshment)
- **Scaffold → Tangled Rope:** Some support remains useful BUT extraction accumulated

**Honest Scaffold practice:**
1. **Name temporality upfront:** "This support is designed to end"
2. **Define completion:** What does success look like? When do we dissolve?
3. **Monitor sunset approach:** Are we approaching completion?
4. **Execute dissolution:** Actually end when purpose served
5. **Celebrate graduation:** Not mourn loss of relationship

### F. Pitons: Obsolete Social Structures

**Definition:** Degraded social patterns that lost function but persist through inertia, guilt, or identity fusion. Maintenance cost > benefit.

**Test:** Theater ratio >0.70? Relief vs. loss if disappeared? Function obsolete?

**Examples:**

**Dead friendships maintained from guilt:**
- **Original function:** Mutual support, shared activities, emotional intimacy
- **Current state:** No genuine connection, awkward interactions, obligation only
- **Persistence:** "We've been friends for 20 years," sunk cost fallacy
- **Cost:** Emotional energy, time, prevents investment in living friendships
- **Relief test:** Would feel relief if ended, not loss
- **FOR both parties:** Piton (piton maintained from guilt)

**Obsolete family rituals:**
- **Original function:** Bonding, tradition transmission, celebration
- **Current state:** Everyone resents it, no joy, pure obligation
- **Persistence:** "We've always done this," identity fusion
- **Example:** Annual gathering everyone dreads but maintains
- **Relief test:** All participants would feel relief if ended

**Legacy social obligations:**
- **Original function:** Reciprocity, relationship maintenance
- **Current state:** Relationship has no value, obligation persists
- **Example:** "We always visit X" (when X relationship is dead)
- **Persistence:** Guilt, fear of judgment, habit

**Vestigial professional networks:**
- **Original function:** Opportunity access, information sharing
- **Current state:** LinkedIn connections with no interaction
- **Cost:** Mental clutter, maintenance energy
- **Benefit:** None (network doesn't function)

**Empty social scripts:**
- **Original function:** Greeting, connection
- **Current state:** "How are you?" / "Fine" (no communication)
- **Persistence:** Autopilot, social convention
- **Cost:** Low but cumulative
- **Benefit:** None (actual communication absent)

**Historical resentments:**
- **Original function:** Boundary protection, grievance processing
- **Current state:** Original conflict irrelevant, resentment persists
- **Persistence:** Identity fusion ("I'm the person who was wronged")
- **Cost:** Relationship capacity, emotional energy
- **Benefit:** None (conflict no longer matters)

**Detection tests:**
1. **Cost-benefit:** Energy spent > value received
2. **Function test:** What does this actually do? (nothing = Piton)
3. **Relief test:** Would I feel relief or loss if this ended?
4. **Defender test:** Only people invested in structure defend it

**Clearing practice:**
1. **Acknowledge what it was:** This served function once (honor past)
2. **Recognize what it is:** This is obsolete now (present reality)
3. **Release without shame:** Not failure to let go (wisdom)
4. **Redirect energy:** Freed capacity for living relationships

**The honest question:** "If this relationship/ritual/obligation disappeared tomorrow, would I feel relief or loss?"
- **Relief** → Piton, eliminate
- **Loss** → Still functional, maintain or reform

**Common resistance:**
- Sunk cost fallacy ("20 years of friendship!")
- Guilt induction ("After all we've been through")
- Identity attachment ("I'm a loyal person who maintains friendships")
- Fear of confrontation (easier to maintain than honestly end)

## IV. Power-Scaling and Indexical Synchronization

### A. The Power-Scaling Mechanism

**Same social structure experienced differently by power position:**

**Formula:**
```
Effective extraction χ(C, P) = Base extraction ε(C) × Power modifier π(P)

Where:
- ε(C) = structural property of constraint (observable)
- P = agent's power position (observable: exit control, social capital, resources)
- π(P) = modifier based on position
```

**Example: Professional networking (ε = 0.42)**
```
Without network (powerless): 0.42 × 1.5 = 0.63 (Snare - locked out)
Moderate network: 0.42 × 1.0 = 0.42 (Tangled Rope - sees both)
Strong network (powerful): 0.42 × 0.6 = 0.25 (Rope - opportunities flow)
```

**This explains relational conflicts:**
- Partner A (powerful position): Experiences Rope, defends structure
- Partner B (powerless position): Experiences Snare, wants change
- **Both are right from their positions** (not disagreement about facts but about structural position)

### B. Indexical Synchronization (Relational Alignment)

**Three response patterns when classifications differ:**

**Pattern 1: Asymmetric Index Assumption (Conflict)**

Both partners assume their index is universal:

**Partner A** [powerful/mobile]: "This is Rope - works great for everyone!"
**Partner B** [powerless/trapped]: "This is Snare - why can't you see the extraction?"

**Conflict:** Neither recognizes different structural positions create different experiences. Both think the other is wrong about facts when actually both are right from their positions.

**Pattern 2: Index Recognition (Partial Resolution)**

Both partners acknowledge different positions:

**Partner A:** "I see this works well for me because I have power/resources/options"
**Partner B:** "I experience this as extractive because I'm trapped/powerless/lacking options"

**Partial resolution:** Understanding why experiences differ, but structure unchanged. Partner B still experiences Snare even when Partner A understands why.

**Pattern 3: Structural Reform (Full Resolution)**

Power asymmetry addressed OR structure reformed:

**Option 1 - Equalize power:**
- Partner B gains exit options (financial independence, social support)
- Power redistribution changes experience (Snare → Tangled Rope or Rope)

**Option 2 - Reform structure:**
- Preserve coordination function
- Excise extraction layer
- Example: Monogamy → Ethical non-monogamy (maintain consent coordination, remove coercion)

**Option 3 - Accept differences:**
- Recognize irresolvable indexical variation
- Both maintain accurate classification from their positions
- Negotiate based on honest assessment

**Option 4 - Exit:**
- If structure can't be reformed and power can't be equalized
- Honest recognition that relationship extracts from one partner
- Clean exit when possible

**Hard truth:** Index recognition alone doesn't change structure. If power asymmetry persists, A still experiences Rope, B still experiences Snare. Understanding is not liberation.

### C. The Beneficiary Blind Spot (Institutional Dissenter Pattern in Relationships)

Power-scaling explains *why* partners experience the same relationship differently. A corpus-wide empirical finding adds a *directional prediction*: in 44% of analyzed constraints where observer positions disagree, the structurally invested observer — the one who benefits from the current arrangement — is the **lone dissenter** classifying extraction as coordination.

**In relationships, this is the "we're fine" / "we're not fine" pattern.**

**The mechanism:** The partner who benefits from a relational arrangement has structural incentive to classify it as Rope (healthy coordination). They experience genuine benefit — their needs are met, the arrangement works for them, the coordination is real *from their position*. The partner experiencing extraction sees the same arrangement as Tangled Rope or Snare. The beneficiary's classification isn't dishonest — it's structurally motivated and systematically incomplete.

**Example: Household labor**
- Partner A (does 20% of domestic labor): "Our division works great. We're both contributing." (Rope)
- Partner B (does 80% of domestic labor): "I'm drowning. This isn't fair." (Snare)
- Partner A isn't lying — from their position, the arrangement genuinely coordinates household function. They experience mutual benefit because they receive the coordination without paying the extraction cost.
- Pattern: Beneficiary is lone voice calling extraction coordination.

**Example: Emotional labor**
- Partner A (receives emotional management): "We have great communication." (Rope)
- Partner B (provides all emotional management): "I'm exhausting myself managing both our emotions." (Tangled Rope → Snare)
- Partner A experiences genuine coordination — their emotional needs are met, conflicts get resolved, the relationship feels smooth.
- Pattern: Same — the person whose needs are met sees coordination; the person providing the unreciprocated labor sees extraction.

**Example: Social scheduling**
- Partner A (prefers the status quo): "Our social life is great — balanced and fun." (Rope)
- Partner B (always accommodates A's preferences): "We only ever do what you want." (Snare)
- Partner A isn't gaslighting — from their position, the social life genuinely coordinates their preferences. The extraction (B's preferences suppressed) is invisible because A doesn't experience its cost.

**Diagnostic value:** When a relational dispute has the structure "I think we're fine" vs. "I'm suffering," check who benefits from the current arrangement. If the "we're fine" partner is also the structural beneficiary, the institutional dissenter pattern is likely active. This doesn't automatically make the beneficiary wrong — sometimes coordination is genuine and the other partner's distress has a different source. But it does mean the beneficiary's "we're fine" needs independent verification: can they articulate a coordination function that the non-beneficiary also recognizes?

**The test (from the Relational Integrity Protocol):** Ask the partner claiming "we're fine" to describe specific ways the current arrangement *costs them*. If they can't identify any costs, they're likely the extraction beneficiary, and their Rope classification is incomplete.

## V. Relational Integrity Protocol

**Five-step process for honest constraint navigation between agents.**

### Step 1: Self-Classification (Internal Honesty)

**Before making constraint claim to another person:**

**Question set:**
- What type is this FOR ME? (Mountain/Rope/Tangled/Snare/Scaffold/Piton)
- Have I tested this or am I assuming?
- Am I claiming Mountain when it's actually preference?
- Am I defending Piton from identity fusion rather than function?
- What's my actual power position? (not subjective feeling but observable)

**Example:**

**Dishonest (internal):** "I can't do physical affection" (claiming Mountain)

**Honest (internal):** "I have Abandoned Camp from childhood trauma. Physical affection is uncomfortable but not impossible. I'm experiencing this as Mountain because I'm powerless to change it alone, but with support it might be Scaffold (can build capacity) or even just strong preference."

**The self-examination:**
- Is this genuine limit or preference?
- Is this unchangeable or just difficult?
- Is this Mountain or am I powerless to change it alone?
- What would I need to change this? (reveals if Mountain or just high-cost Rope)

### Step 2: Index Specification (Relational Honesty)

**Specify YOUR indices when making constraint claim:**

**Format:** "From my position [WHO/WHEN/WHERE/HOW_MUCH], this is [type]"

**Why this matters:**
- Acknowledges position-dependence
- Prevents universalizing your experience
- Opens space for different valid perspectives
- Enables partner to understand structural origin of experience

**Examples:**

**Instead of:** "This relationship structure doesn't work"
**Say:** "From my position as [powerless in household labor distribution/biographical/trapped by finances/dyadic], this feels like a Snare - I'm doing most emotional/domestic labor without reciprocity"

**Instead of:** "Monogamy is natural"
**Say:** "From my position as [moderate/generational/mobile/community], monogamy functions as Rope for me - coordinates expectations and reduces search costs. I recognize from other positions it might feel like Snare (if coerced) or Mountain (if alternatives seem impossible)."

**Instead of:** "I can't change"
**Say:** "From my position as [powerless to exit/biographical/trapped/dyadic], this feels like Mountain - unchangeable given my current resources. But I recognize with different power position (therapy access, financial independence, social support), it might be changeable."

**The practice:**
- Always specify your WHO (power position)
- Acknowledge WHEN (time horizon - is this forever or temporary?)
- Note WHERE (exit options - can you leave or trapped?)
- State HOW_MUCH (scope - dyadic relationship or community norm?)

### Step 3: Power-Scaling Acknowledgment (Structural Honesty)

**Recognize how power affects classification:**

**If you have more relational power:**

**Questions:**
- Do I have more resources/options/social capital in this relationship?
- What looks like Rope to me might be Snare to partner (power-scaling effect)
- Am I naturalizing my preferences as partner's constraints?
- Am I denying extraction I benefit from?

**Honest acknowledgment:**
"I recognize I have more power here (financially/socially/legally), so this structure might feel more extractive to you than it does to me. What looks like Rope to me (functional coordination) might be Snare to you (trapped in extraction)."

**Example - Household labor:**
**Partner A (higher earner):** "I see the current division works for me because I can outsource some tasks with my income. For you, without that option, the labor burden is probably much heavier. I'm experiencing Rope (coordination of household tasks), but you might be experiencing Snare (trapped in unpaid labor)."

**If you have less relational power:**

**Questions:**
- Am I experiencing this as Mountain because I'm powerless (can't see exit options)?
- Is extraction amplified for me? (ε × 1.5 effect)
- Would this feel different if I had more power/resources/options?

**Honest acknowledgment:**
"I'm experiencing this as Mountain (unchangeable) because from my position I don't have power to change it. I recognize from your position with more resources/options, it might look changeable (Rope or Tangled Rope). But from where I stand, it's binding."

**Example - Caregiving obligations:**
**Partner B (lower earner, trapped):** "I experience family caregiving as Snare - I'm doing most of the work with no compensation and limited career options. I understand from your position with higher income and ability to hire help, it might feel like Rope (functional family support). But for me, the extraction is severe (amplified by my powerlessness)."

**The power audit:**
- Financial: Who earns more? Who controls money?
- Social: Who has more friends/family support?
- Legal: Who has more rights/options in law?
- Physical: Who has more bodily autonomy/safety?
- Emotional: Who has more leverage/alternatives?

**Observable power markers (not subjective):**
- Exit options (can leave vs. trapped)
- Resource control (income, assets, access)
- Social capital (support networks, reputation)
- Institutional backing (legal rights, organizational power)

### Step 4: Testing Invitation (Empirical Honesty)

**If claiming Mountain, invite testing:**

**Protocol:**
- "I believe this is Mountain for me, but I'm willing to test it"
- "What would change my mind: [specific evidence]"
- "If violation produces only discomfort (not damage), I'll reconsider"

**Why this matters:**
- Distinguishes genuine limits from preferences
- Prevents False Mountain naturalization
- Shows good faith (not defending territory, seeking truth)
- Enables partner to understand basis for claim

**Example - Physical affection:**

**Honest testing invitation:**
"I currently experience physical affection as Mountain - it feels impossible for me due to childhood trauma. But I'm willing to test this. If we try gradual exposure (hand-holding, brief hugs) and I experience only discomfort (not panic attacks, flashbacks, severe dysregulation), then I'll reconsider whether it's actually Mountain or Abandoned Camp (changeable with support)."

**What you're testing:**
- Damage vs. discomfort (Mountain produces damage, Snare/preference produces discomfort)
- Power-scaling (does increased power/support change experience?)
- Alternative existence (do other people navigate this differently?)

**Honest testing means:**
- Actually trying (not just claiming willingness)
- Observing results accurately (not defending prior belief)
- Updating classification based on evidence (Bayesian honesty)
- Acknowledging when wrong ("I thought Mountain, testing shows Abandoned Camp")

**What good-faith partner does:**
- Supports testing (doesn't pressure but enables)
- Accepts results (whether confirms Mountain or reveals Rope/Scaffold)
- Doesn't weaponize testing ("See, I knew you could if you tried!" when actually you can't)

### Step 5: Degradation Monitoring (Temporal Honesty)

**Relationships change, constraints change, classifications change:**

**Commitment:**
"This structure is currently [type], but if it degrades (Rope → Tangled Rope, Tangled Rope → Snare), I commit to naming that change honestly."

**Why this matters:**
- Prevents relationship calcification
- Enables graceful evolution or dissolution
- Honors lifecycle reality
- Maintains mutual honesty

**Examples:**

**Rope → Tangled Rope monitoring:**
"Our friendship is currently Rope - reciprocal support, shared activities, low extraction. But if I notice extraction accumulating (one of us consistently giving more, benefits becoming asymmetric), I'll name that. We can then decide whether to reform (rebalance reciprocity) or acknowledge it's degrading."

**Tangled Rope → Snare monitoring:**
"Our professional relationship is Tangled Rope - genuine mentorship (coordination) but some exploitation of my labor (extraction). If the coordination function disappears (I'm no longer learning) but extraction persists (still doing grunt work), I'll name that it's become Snare and we need to renegotiate or I need to exit."

**Scaffold → Piton monitoring:**
"This support structure is Scaffold - temporary help during crisis. When crisis passes, if we're maintaining this from habit/guilt rather than function, I'll name that it's become Piton and we should dissolve it with gratitude for what it was."

**Quarterly relationship check-in protocol:**

**Questions:**
1. **Classification current:** What type is this relationship now?
2. **Degradation detection:** Has extraction accumulated? Has coordination declined?
3. **Power shift:** Has power balance changed? (affects classification)
4. **Honest assessment:** Would I feel relief or loss if this ended? (Piton test)
5. **Reform needed:** If degraded, can we reform? (preserve coordination, reduce extraction)
6. **Exit question:** If can't reform, should we exit gracefully?

**The honesty commitment:**
- I will name degradation when I see it
- I will not maintain Pitons from guilt
- I will honor Scaffold sunsets
- I will acknowledge when Rope → Tangled Rope
- I will exit Snares when able

**Purity monitoring (pre-symptomatic relational decay):**

The quarterly check-in above monitors for observable degradation — extraction accumulating, coordination declining, power shifting. But a relationship can show stable metrics (same frequency of contact, same shared activities, same reciprocity on paper) while its structural health is declining. This is purity drift applied to relationships.

**The key diagnostic question:** *Is the driver of this relationship's routines still coordination, or has it shifted to obligation, anxiety, or guilt?*

**Signal detection:**
- Same weekly dinner, but now driven by guilt rather than genuine desire to connect → purity declining
- Same reciprocal texting pattern, but now motivated by fear of abandonment rather than enthusiasm → purity declining
- Same shared activities, but now performed out of obligation rather than mutual enjoyment → purity declining
- Same relationship check-ins, but now ritualized rather than genuinely investigative → purity declining

**In all four cases, the behavioral metrics are unchanged — an outside observer would see a stable relationship. But the internal driver has shifted from coordination (mutual benefit, genuine desire) to extraction (guilt, obligation, anxiety, fear). The form persists while the function hollows.**

**Why this matters for Step 5:** If you wait for observable metric changes (contact frequency dropping, reciprocity breaking down, conflict increasing), you're intervening after the relationship has already significantly degraded. Purity monitoring — tracking motivation shifts rather than behavioral changes — gives you the early window where course correction is still relatively easy.

**Add to quarterly check-in:**
- 7. **Motivation check:** Am I maintaining this relationship because it genuinely serves both of us, or because I'm afraid of what happens if I stop? Is my partner? Has the driver shifted from desire to obligation?

See logic_extensions.md §2 (purity scoring), constraint_lifecycles.md §III Type 10 (purity drift).

### Case Study: Narcissistic Communication as Epistemic Attack

**Source:** Personal email chain analyzed during corpus audit (February 2026). Generating LLM parsed the chain; structural analysis revealed systematic misclassification.

**The pattern in constraint terms:**

1. **Initial offer as Scaffold:** "I'd like to resume contact" = conditional, bounded offer with implicit sunset (if conditions aren't met, contact won't continue). Scaffold classification is correct — temporary support structure designed for transition.

2. **Scaffold → Rope reclassification:** The offer is received not as bounded conditional but as unbounded obligation. "Family" framing converts "I'll try, with conditions" into "this is what you owe me." The Scaffold has been reclassified as Rope without the boundary-keeper's consent.

3. **Boundary reassertion → aggression framing:** When the boundary-keeper reasserts conditions ("I need you to acknowledge X before we can continue"), this is parsed as Snare — the boundary-keeper is now extracting from the person who "just wants a relationship." The conditional nature of the original offer has been erased from the narrative.

4. **Third-party recruitment:** The reclassified version (boundary-keeper as aggressor) is forwarded to third parties with original evidence attached. The forwarder is confident the evidence supports their framing because the framing is embedded in the presentation, not in the evidence. Third parties who read the affect (grief, reaching out, wanting connection) rather than the structure (boundary expansion, reframing of "no," recruitment of pressure) will reproduce the misclassification.

**Why the generating LLM failed:** The LLM processed emotional valence at message level. The person reaching out presented affect consistent with victimhood (sadness, health concerns, desire for connection). The boundary-keeper presented affect consistent with coldness (measured tone, conditional language, refusal to engage emotionally). At the message level, the LLM's classification (victim = person reaching out, aggressor = boundary-keeper) was coherent with affect. At the sequence level — tracking boundary offered → boundary expanded → expansion caught → reframing deployed → third party recruited — the classification inverts.

**Structural lesson for Relational Integrity Protocol:** The RIP's Step 1 (Classify Before Responding) must classify across the sequence of interactions, not within any single exchange. Narcissistic communication — and more broadly, any system where extraction operates through narrative framing — is designed to present the extracting party as sympathetic at the message level. The extraction is only visible in the pattern across time: what happened to each boundary that was set?

**Detection heuristic:** Track what happens to boundaries. In healthy (Rope) interactions, boundaries are respected, and new requests are scoped within them. In narcissistic (Snare) interactions, each boundary becomes the starting position for the next expansion. The content of any single message may be reasonable; the trajectory across messages reveals extraction.

## VI. Asymmetric Power Cases

### A. When You Have More Relational Power

**Obligations specific to powerful position:**

**1. Don't naturalize your preferences as partner's Mountains**

**Pattern to avoid:**
- "You should be available 24/7" (your preference, not necessity)
- "This is just how relationships work" (your experience, not universal)
- "You're being unreasonable" (from your position of power, their requests seem excessive; from theirs, they're necessary)

**Honest alternative:**
- "I prefer 24/7 availability, but I recognize that's my preference and places burden on you"
- "This works for me from my position, but I see it might not work for you from yours"
- "What seems reasonable to me might be different from your position - let's examine the structure"

**2. Acknowledge extraction you benefit from**

**Pattern to avoid:**
- Denying extraction exists
- "This is fair" (when it's not - you benefit, they're harmed)
- "You're too sensitive" (gaslighting extraction awareness)

**Honest alternative:**
- "I see this relationship has me doing less emotional/domestic labor - is that fair to you?"
- "I benefit from this arrangement more than you - let's acknowledge that asymmetry"
- "Your discomfort is data about extraction, not evidence of your oversensitivity"

**3. Don't gaslight power analysis**

**Pattern to avoid:**
- "You're free to leave" (when they're economically/socially trapped)
- "This is voluntary" (when enforcement is invisible to you but real for them)
- "You're choosing this" (when power asymmetry constrains choice)

**Honest alternative:**
- "I see you experience this as trapped even though from my position it looks voluntary - the power difference creates that gap"
- "I have exit options you don't have - acknowledging that"
- "This might be choice for me but constraint for you"

**4. Invite lower-power perspective**

**Active practice:**
- "This feels like Rope to me - does it extract from you?"
- "I might not see extraction because I benefit - what am I missing?"
- "Help me understand your experience from your position"

**Why this is obligation:**
- Power creates blindness (can't see what you benefit from)
- You have duty to seek perspective you can't see alone
- Refusing to look = complicity in extraction

### B. When You Have Less Relational Power

**Rights specific to powerless position:**

**1. Name extraction without partner's agreement**

**You don't need their permission to call Snare a Snare:**
- Your experience is valid from your index
- Power asymmetry might prevent them seeing extraction
- Naming it is honesty, not accusation

**Example:**
"I experience this household labor division as Snare - I'm doing 80% of the work and it's damaging me. I recognize you might experience it differently from your position, but from mine, it's extraction."

**2. Exit without justification to their satisfaction**

**You don't need to prove extraction to beneficiary:**
- Beneficiary has motivated reasoning (wants to maintain structure)
- Your relief from exit is sufficient justification
- Staying to avoid their discomfort = surrendering to extraction

**Example:**
"I'm leaving this friendship. I know you don't see it as Piton, but from my position, it's draining me with no mutual benefit. I don't need your agreement to exit."

**3. Refuse False Mountain claims**

**When powerful claim unchangeable:**
- Test their claim (cross-cultural variation? historical examples?)
- Demand evidence (what enforcement maintains this?)
- Name naturalization ("That's just how things are" = power-serving claim)

**Example:**
"You're claiming this is Mountain (natural, unchangeable), but I see it varies across cultures and has enforcement mechanisms. This looks like Snare to me (constructed extraction), not Mountain."

**4. Build collective power**

**Individual powerlessness ≠ permanent:**
- Coalition formation reduces extraction (π(collective) = 0.4)
- Shared resources enable exit
- Mutual support creates alternatives

**Example:**
"Individually I'm powerless to change this work norm, but together we can negotiate better conditions or find alternative employment."

## VII. Specific Relationship Case Studies

### A. The Tangled Rope Relationship Case

**When relationship has both coordination AND extraction:**

**Honest conversation structure:**

**Partner A:** "I think this relationship is Tangled Rope. We coordinate well on [X, Y, Z] - I value that genuine coordination. But I'm also experiencing extraction through [A, B, C] - that's harming me. Can we preserve the coordination while reducing the extraction?"

**Partner B response options:**

**Option 1 - Defensive (refuses to see extraction):**
"No, it's pure Rope. You're wrong about extraction. This is all coordination."
**Result:** Partner A's experience denied, conflict persists, likely degradation continues

**Option 2 - Dismissive (refuses to see coordination):**
"It's pure Snare. Just leave if you don't like it."
**Result:** Coordination value ignored, wholesale destruction proposed when reform possible

**Option 3 - Honest (acknowledges hybrid):**
"I see the coordination you value. I wasn't aware of the extraction you're experiencing. Can you help me understand the structure better? I want to preserve what works while reducing what harms you."
**Result:** Opportunity for surgical reform, relationship evolution

**Reform conversation:**

**1. Identify coordination value:**
- What do we both value? (shared activities, emotional support, practical cooperation)
- What would be lost if relationship ended? (genuine coordination function)
- Can this be preserved? (is it worth preserving?)

**2. Measure extraction:**
- What's asymmetric? (who gives more, who receives more)
- What's the power balance? (who has more options, resources, leverage)
- How severe is extraction? (ε value, then apply power-scaling)

**3. Test separability:**
- Can we preserve coordination without extraction?
- Historical examples (other relationships manage this differently?)
- Alternative mechanisms (different way to coordinate?)

**4. Attempt surgical reform:**
- Preserve: [List coordination functions to maintain]
- Reduce/eliminate: [List extraction mechanisms to excise]
- Experiment: Try new structure for defined period, monitor results
- Evaluate: Is coordination preserved? Is extraction reduced?

**Example - Household labor Tangled Rope:**

**Coordination valued:**
- Shared home maintenance
- Collective household efficiency
- Partnered life benefits

**Extraction identified:**
- Gendered division (woman does 80% domestic/emotional labor)
- Unpaid labor (no compensation for time/energy)
- Career sacrifice (woman's work opportunities limited)

**Reform attempt:**
- Preserve coordination (shared household)
- Excise extraction (equalize labor division, compensate or hire out some labor)
- Test: Try 50/50 division for 3 months, monitor strain and satisfaction
- Evaluate: Is home still functioning? Is extraction reduced? Both partners satisfied?

**Outcomes:**
- **Success:** Coordination preserved, extraction reduced, relationship strengthened
- **Partial:** Some improvement, ongoing negotiation needed
- **Failure:** Coordination damaged OR extraction persists → reconsider relationship viability

### B. The Piton Relationship Case

**When relationship has neither coordination nor extraction, just inertia:**

**Honest assessment questions:**

**1. Function test:**
- What does this relationship actually do? (for either of us)
- What would be lost if it ended? (genuine vs. imagined loss)
- Are we maintaining from function or from guilt/identity?

**2. Energy audit:**
- What's the cost? (time, emotional energy, mental bandwidth)
- What's the benefit? (actual value received, not hypothetical or past value)
- Cost > benefit? (If yes, Piton)

**3. Relief test:**
- If this relationship disappeared tomorrow, would I feel relief or loss?
- Be honest: Relief about ending obligation vs. loss of actual value
- If relief → Piton

**4. Sunk cost check:**
- Am I maintaining because "we've been friends for X years"?
- Is past investment relevant to current value? (No)
- "We were close once" ≠ "we should maintain now"

**5. Identity fusion check:**
- "I'm a loyal person" → Am I maintaining to preserve self-image?
- "I've always been friends with X" → Is this identity, not relationship?
- Identity fusion prevents honest assessment

**Graceful dissolution protocol:**

**If both recognize Piton:**

**Step 1: Honest acknowledgment**
"I think this friendship has become Piton - we don't serve each other anymore. I value what it was, but it's not alive now. Can we acknowledge that honestly?"

**Step 2: Gratitude for what was**
"I'm grateful for [specific things this relationship provided when functional]. It served us well during [time period]. That was real and valuable."

**Step 3: Release without blame**
"This isn't failure - relationships have natural lifecycles. We've grown in different directions. That's okay. We can release this with gratitude rather than guilt."

**Step 4: Clean closure**
"Let's end consciously rather than let it drag on from obligation. I wish you well. Our friendship was real when it was alive. I'm grateful for that time."

**If one person sees Piton, other doesn't:**

**The disagreement:**
- Person A: "This is Piton for me - I feel relief when we don't interact, guilt when we do, no genuine connection"
- Person B: "But we've been friends for 20 years! We can't just end it!"

**The honest resolution:**
- Person A's experience is valid (from their index, it is Piton)
- Person B's attachment is valid (but attachment to past, not present relationship)
- Person A has right to exit (maintaining from guilt serves no one)
- Person B will likely feel hurt (natural, but doesn't create maintenance obligation)

**What Person A can say:**
"I understand this is hard for you. I value our history. But from my position, maintaining this relationship costs me more than it provides. It feels obligatory, not alive. I need to release it. I hope you can understand, but I'm making this choice regardless because staying would be dishonest to both of us."

**What Person A should NOT say:**
- "I'll try harder" (if genuinely Piton, trying won't revive it)
- "Maybe later" (if definitely Piton, false hope is cruel)
- Maintain from guilt (harms both people)

### C. The Scaffold Relationship Case

**When relationship is temporary by design:**

**Examples:**
- Mentor-mentee (designed to evolve to peer or end)
- Therapeutic relationship (designed to end when capacity built)
- Crisis support (designed to end when crisis passes)
- Onboarding buddy (designed to end when integration complete)

**Honesty requirements:**

**1. Name temporality upfront**
"This mentorship is Scaffold - I'm supporting you through skill acquisition. Success looks like you not needing me anymore. We're designing this to evolve or end, not to last forever."

**2. Define completion criteria**
- What does success look like? (specific skills, confidence, autonomy)
- When should this transition? (measurable milestones)
- How will we know it's time? (honest assessment triggers)

**3. Monitor sunset approach**
- Regular check-ins: "Are you still getting value? Am I still helping or have you outgrown this?"
- Honest assessment: "Has crisis passed? Have you built capacity? Is support still needed?"
- Resist permanence creep: "We said 3 months - it's been 6. Time to transition or acknowledge we're creating dependency."

**4. Execute dissolution/evolution**
- Actually transition when criteria met
- Don't maintain from fear of abandonment (yours or theirs)
- Celebrate graduation, not mourn loss
- "You did it - you don't need this support anymore. That's success!"

**Degradation prevention:**

**Scaffold → Snare (dependency creation):**
- Mentor prolongs "need" to maintain relevance/ego
- Therapist maintains client for revenue
- Parent enmeshes adult child to maintain relationship
- **Detection:** Support continues past function completion
- **Prevention:** Honest sunset monitoring, celebrate independence

**Scaffold → Piton (piton support):**
- Crisis passed but support continues from habit
- Integration complete but onboarding buddy relationship persists
- Mentee achieved competence but relationship maintained from guilt
- **Detection:** Relief test (would both feel relief if ended?)
- **Prevention:** Scheduled sunset review, honest function assessment

**Example - Therapeutic Scaffold done well:**

**At start:** "Therapy is designed to build your capacity for self-regulation. Success looks like you not needing weekly sessions. We'll work together until you've internalized these skills, then we'll taper and end. That's the goal."

**During:** Regular assessment - "Are you building capacity? Can you use these tools independently? What still requires my support?"

**At sunset:** "You've internalized the skills. You're regulating well independently. It's time to end regular sessions. We can do occasional check-ins if needed, but the Scaffold has served its purpose. This is success."

**Result:** Clean end, client empowered, therapist celebrates client's growth (not mourns loss of revenue)

## VIII. Containment: Structural Paradoxes in Relationships

**Some relational tensions cannot be resolved, only contained.**

### A. The Four Universal Relational Paradoxes

**1. Autonomy ⇄ Connection**

**The paradox:**
- Need independence (maintain separate self, pursue individual goals)
- Need intimacy (deep connection, vulnerability, interdependence)
- Cannot maximize both simultaneously

**Tension manifestation:**
- Too much autonomy → Isolation, loneliness, relationship withering
- Too much connection → Fusion, loss of self, resentment, suffocation

**Immature response (forcing resolution):**
- "We must be completely autonomous" → isolation, relationship ends
- "We must be completely fused" → codependency, enmeshment, identity loss

**Mature response (containment):**

**1. Name paradox:** "We both need autonomy AND connection. These are in tension."
**2. Accept irresolvability:** "We can't have 100% autonomy and 100% connection. Tension is permanent."
**3. Sequential alternation:** 
   - Autonomy week: Solo time, separate activities, independence honored
   - Connection week: Quality time, vulnerability, deep intimacy prioritized
**4. Partial satisfaction:** "Neither of us gets 100% of either pole, we oscillate"
**5. Comfort with incompletion:** "This tension won't resolve. We hold it."

**Example conversation:**

**Partner A:** "I need more autonomy - I feel suffocated"
**Partner B:** "I need more connection - I feel abandoned"

**Immature:** Fight about who's right, force one pole, resentment builds

**Mature:** 
"We're both right. You need autonomy AND I need connection. We can't both get 100% simultaneously. How do we oscillate? You get solo weekend this week, I get quality time next week. We accept partial satisfaction on both."

---

**2. Individual Needs ⇄ Collective Needs**

**The paradox:**
- Individual has personal needs (rest, pursuits, resources)
- Collective has shared needs (household, children, relationship maintenance)
- Cannot fully satisfy both

**Tension manifestation:**
- Too much individual → Collective suffers (household chaos, relationship neglect)
- Too much collective → Individual suffers (burnout, resentment, loss of self)

**Containment:**
- Sequential attention (individual time this week, collective time next)
- Explicit negotiation (not resentful sacrifice)
- Both get partial satisfaction across time

**Example - Household labor:**
- Can't both have complete personal freedom AND perfect household
- Someone must do tasks OR collective standard lowers OR resources buy services
- Containment: "We oscillate who does more, or we lower standards, or we pay for help"

---

**3. Honesty ⇄ Kindness**

**The paradox:**
- Truth sometimes hurts
- Kindness sometimes requires omission
- Cannot be completely honest AND completely kind always

**Tension manifestation:**
- Too much honesty → Cruelty, relationship damage
- Too much kindness → Dishonesty, resentment, withheld truth

**Containment:**
- Context-dependent choice (honest when stakes high, kind when harm exceeds benefit)
- "Necessary truth with kindness" (delivery matters)
- Accept that sometimes hurting feelings is more loving than protecting them

**Example:**
- Partner's creative work is genuinely bad
- Complete honesty → "This is terrible" (cruel)
- Complete kindness → "This is great!" (dishonest, prevents growth)
- Containment → "I see your effort. I'm noticing [specific issue]. Would you like honest feedback or support?" (kindness with truth option)

---

**4. Stability ⇄ Growth**

**The paradox:**
- Stability requires consistency (predictable, safe, known)
- Growth requires challenge (uncertain, risky, unknown)
- Cannot have complete stability AND continuous growth

**Tension manifestation:**
- Too much stability → Stagnation, boredom, atrophy
- Too much growth → Chaos, insecurity, exhaustion

**Containment:**
- Rhythmic alternation (stable periods, growth spurts)
- "Stable base for growth expeditions" (return to safety after challenge)
- Different domains (stability in home, growth in career OR vice versa)

**Example - Relationship:**
- Can't have completely secure relationship AND continuous exciting novelty
- Too much security → Dead bedroom, routine, boredom
- Too much novelty → Instability, anxiety, no foundation
- Containment: "Stable commitment with episodes of growth/challenge/exploration"

### B. The Containment Protocol (Applied to Relationships)

**6-step process:**

**1. Name the paradox**
- What two valuable needs are in tension?
- Example: "I need autonomy AND connection"

**2. Map the oscillation**
- When do you pull toward autonomy? (after intense connection periods)
- When do you pull toward connection? (after extended solo time)
- Pattern recognition: We oscillate naturally

**3. Test edges (attempt forced resolution)**
- Pure autonomy attempt: Loneliness, relationship atrophy
- Pure connection attempt: Suffocation, resentment
- Conclusion: Resolution impossible, both poles remain valuable

**4. Accept irresolvability**
- This tension is structural, permanent
- Not personal failure, not solvable
- Both poles matter, tension is feature not bug

**5. Choose for current context**
- Autonomy this week (solo projects)
- Connection next week (quality time)
- Context-dependent priority, sequential satisfaction

**6. Hold tension without collapse**
- Don't force resolution ("We must find perfect balance!")
- Don't pick one pole permanently ("I choose autonomy forever")
- Oscillate, accept incompletion
- Comfort with permanent tension

**What containment is NOT in relationships:**

- **Not resignation:** "Nothing matters, both needs pointless" (nihilism)
- **Not optimization:** "I can have 100% autonomy AND 100% connection" (impossible)
- **Not resolution:** "Perfect balance achieved, tension gone" (delusion)
- **Not picking sides:** "Autonomy is right, connection is wrong" (immaturity)

**What containment IS:**

- **Recognition:** Tension is structural, expected, permanent
- **Sequential choice:** Context-dependent priority, both get attention across time
- **Partial satisfaction:** Neither pole gets 100%, both get partial across time
- **Mature navigation:** Comfort with permanent tension, skillful oscillation

## IX. Relational Energy and Boundaries

### A. Energy Allocation by Constraint Type

**Finite relational capacity demands strategic allocation:**

**Mountains (natural social limits):**
- **Accept:** Navigate around what can't be changed (Dunbar number, trust decay)
- **Energy:** Minimal (don't fight unchangeable)
- **Example:** Accept you can't maintain 500 deep friendships, choose 150 wisely

**Ropes (functional coordination):**
- **Maintain:** Active energy investment preserves collective capacity
- **Energy:** Moderate (sustainable maintenance)
- **Example:** Friendship reciprocity requires ongoing effort (maintain consciously)

**Tangled Ropes (coordination + extraction):**
- **Reform:** High energy cost but preserves value
- **Energy:** High (surgical precision is hard work)
- **Strategic choice:** Worth it if coordination valuable, not worth it if extraction > coordination
- **Example:** Professional networking reform (preserve connections, resist insider dynamics)

**Snares (pure extraction):**
- **Resist/Exit:** Don't waste energy maintaining extraction
- **Energy:** High for resistance (if trapped), low for exit (if possible)
- **Example:** Gaslighting relationship - exit if possible, contain damage if trapped

**Scaffolds (temporary supports):**
- **Use temporarily:** Invest energy during transition
- **Energy:** Moderate (time-limited investment)
- **Dismantle:** Low energy (just stop when done)
- **Example:** Support group during crisis - invest fully, then graduate gracefully

**Pitons (piton relationships):**
- **Eliminate ruthlessly:** Stop energy investment immediately
- **Energy:** Zero (stop feeding piton)
- **Redirect:** Freed capacity for living relationships
- **Example:** Dead friendship maintained from guilt - end it, invest in living friendships

### A.2 Network Contamination: Why Single-Relationship Intervention Sometimes Fails

The energy allocation above treats each relationship independently. But relationships share a common resource pool — your finite energy, emotional capacity, attention, and time. When one relationship degrades, it drains the shared pool, contaminating neighboring relationships that haven't changed at all.

**The mechanism (Network Drift, Type 11):** A toxic work environment (Snare) doesn't stay at work. It depletes your energy, shortens your fuse, reduces your emotional availability. Your partnership (Rope) experiences the fallout — not because the relationship degraded, but because the context it depends on degraded. Your partnership's *effective* purity drops through contamination, not intrinsic failure.

**Example: Work Snare → Partnership Rope contamination**
```
Partnership (intrinsic quality: solid Rope)
  Good communication, mutual support, shared activities
  Intrinsic purity: stable

Work environment degrades (Rope → Snare over 18 months)
  Reasonable job → exploitative hours, toxic management
  Work purity drops dramatically

Network effect on partnership:
  Same partner, same communication norms, same mutual care
  But: depleted energy after work, shorter fuse, less patience
  Evening conversations become terse, weekends spent recovering
  Effective partnership purity: drops
  
Partner says: "What's wrong with us?"
Correct answer: "Nothing is wrong with us. My job is poisoning everything."
```

**Example: Family Snare → Friendship Rope contamination**
```
Friendship (intrinsic quality: healthy Rope)
  Reciprocal, enjoyable, low-maintenance

Family obligation escalates (Tangled Rope → Snare)
  Eldercare demands, guilt cycles, sibling conflict
  
Network effect on friendship:
  Cancel plans because exhausted from family drama
  Less emotional availability (family consumed the budget)
  Friend experiences withdrawal, confusion
  
Friend says: "Did I do something wrong?"
Correct answer: "No. My family situation is draining everything I have."
```

**Why "working on the relationship" sometimes fails:** If the contamination source is outside the relationship, no amount of couples therapy, date nights, or communication workshops will fix it. You're treating the symptom (partnership strain) while the cause (work Snare, family Snare, health crisis) continues flowing contamination into the shared resource pool.

**Practical implication for energy allocation:** Before investing reform energy in a relationship that's degrading, map the network. Ask: *Has anything outside this relationship changed recently?* If a neighboring domain degraded, intervening there may be more effective than intervening in the relationship itself. Sometimes the most important relationship intervention is quitting the toxic job, setting a family boundary, or addressing the health issue — not "working on us."

**The cascade risk:** Network contamination accelerates. Work Snare → partnership strain → reduced social support → increased vulnerability → work Snare feels worse → partnership strain worsens. The loop tightens unless the contamination source is identified and addressed. Effective relational navigation requires network diagnosis, not just dyadic analysis.

### B. The Four Boundary Types

**1. Substrate Protection (Biological/Psychological Mountains)**

**What this is:**
- Non-negotiable boundaries protecting biological/psychological integrity
- Examples: Sleep need, safety from violence, autonomy over own body

**Boundary statement:**
- "I need 8 hours of sleep" (Substrate/Mountain, unconditional)
- "I won't tolerate violence" (Substrate protection, non-negotiable)
- "My body is mine" (Substrate, absolute)

**Response to violation:**
- Exit immediately if possible (Substrate violation is severe)
- No negotiation (these aren't preferences, they're requirements)

**Relational implication:**
- Partner must respect these or relationship ends
- Not about fairness or reciprocity - about basic integrity

---

**2. Coordination Boundary (Rope Maintenance)**

**What this is:**
- Boundaries enabling mutual benefit
- Examples: "I need advance notice for plans," "Communication happens via text primarily," "We alternate hosting"

**Boundary statement:**
- "I need 24 hours notice for social plans" (enables my schedule coordination)
- "I prefer text to phone" (coordination of communication style)
- "We take turns choosing restaurants" (reciprocity maintenance)

**Response to violation:**
- Negotiate (these are Ropes, can be refined)
- Reciprocal adjustment (if I ask for notice, I provide notice)

**Relational implication:**
- Partner benefits from clarity (coordination works better)
- Mutual agreement, refinement over time
- Violating occasionally = accident, violating consistently = disrespect

---

**3. Extraction Resistance (Snare Refusal)**

**What this is:**
- Boundaries preventing asymmetric extraction
- Examples: "I won't do all household labor," "I won't maintain this friendship alone," "I won't accept gaslighting"

**Boundary statement:**
- "I need equitable household labor division" (resisting gendered extraction)
- "Friendship needs reciprocity" (resisting one-sided relationships)
- "My reality is valid" (resisting gaslighting)

**Response to violation:**
- Name extraction (make it visible)
- Refuse participation (don't cooperate with own extraction)
- Exit if extraction persists (don't accept Snare long-term)

**Relational implication:**
- May be labeled "selfish" by beneficiary (that's gaslighting)
- Extraction beneficiaries resist these boundaries
- Maintaining boundary = self-respect

---

**4. Temporary Boundary (Scaffold - Designed to Dissolve)**

**What this is:**
- Time-limited boundaries during transitions
- Examples: "I need space during grief," "No major decisions for 3 months post-breakup," "Limited contact during exam period"

**Boundary statement:**
- "I need 3 months of minimal contact after breakup" (Scaffold - healing time)
- "No financial decisions during grief" (Scaffold - capacity protection)
- "Limited availability during finals" (Scaffold - temporary constraint)

**Response to violation:**
- Reiterate temporality ("I know this is hard, it's temporary")
- Maintain boundary during designed period
- Actually remove boundary when purpose served (don't let temporary become permanent)

**Relational implication:**
- Partner must respect temporary need
- Honest about duration (not indefinite "I need space")
- Both people know when Scaffold ends

---

### C. Boundary Violation Patterns

**Pattern 1: Testing (normal, low-grade)**
- Partner pushes boundary to see if it's real
- Response: Restate boundary, maintain consistency
- If respects after restatement → healthy (was testing)
- If continues violating → escalation to Pattern 2

**Pattern 2: Negotiation Demand (moderate)**
- Partner wants to change boundary
- "That boundary isn't fair"
- Response: Distinguish boundary type
  - Substrate Protection → Non-negotiable, exit if they persist
  - Coordination → Negotiable, find mutual agreement
  - Extraction Resistance → Not negotiable, they want to extract
  - Temporary → Remind it's time-limited

**Pattern 3: Gaslighting (severe)**
- Partner denies your reality
- "That boundary is unreasonable"
- "You're too sensitive"
- "Other people don't need that"
- Response: 
  - Name gaslighting ("My boundary is valid regardless of your approval")
  - Don't JADE (Justify, Argue, Defend, Explain)
  - If persists → exit (gaslighting is Snare)

**Pattern 4: Punishment (severe)**
- Partner retaliates for boundary
- Withdrawal, passive aggression, explicit punishment
- Response:
  - Name behavior ("You're punishing me for having a boundary")
  - This is Snare behavior (extraction attempt)
  - Exit if possible (boundaries shouldn't require enduring punishment)

## X. Patterns at Scale

### A. Scaling Effects (How Scope Changes Constraint Type)

**Consensus decision-making example:**

**At dyadic scale [2 people]:**
- Type: Rope
- Function: Both voices heard, decisions fair
- Cost: Low (two perspectives easy to integrate)
- Benefit: High (genuine consensus)

**At small group [8 people]:**
- Type: Tangled Rope
- Function: Still coordinates (everyone heard)
- Cost: High (time-consuming, slow)
- Benefit: Moderate (consensus valuable but costly)

**At organizational scale [500 people]:**
- Type: Breaks down (becomes Snare for minority)
- Function: Tyranny of minority (1 person can block 499)
- Cost: Prohibitive (can't function)
- Benefit: None (paralysis)

**Design principle:** Coordination mechanisms must scale appropriately. Small-scale Ropes become large-scale Snares if forced beyond natural limits.

## XII. Conclusion: The Practice of Relational Integrity

Sociological Deferential Realism is not a theory to believe but a practice to perform.

**The philosophical claim:** Social constraints operate by indexical relativity. The same structure binds differently based on power position.

**The epistemic claim:** You cannot classify a social constraint without specifying WHO/WHEN/WHERE/HOW_MUCH. Unindexed questions produce equivocation and false conflicts.

**The ethical claim:** Relational integrity consists in accurate classification and honest navigation. Don't lie to yourself about what you're doing in relationship. Don't naturalize your preferences as partner's Mountains. Don't defend Pitons from guilt. Don't maintain Snares from fear.

**The practical claim:** Most relational suffering arises from misclassifying constraints:
- Treating Snares as Mountains (surrendering to extraction as if unchangeable)
- Treating Mountains as Snares (fighting natural limits)
- Treating Tangled Ropes as pure Ropes (defending extraction alongside coordination)
- Treating Tangled Ropes as pure Snares (destroying coordination while fighting extraction)
- Treating Scaffolds as permanent (creating dependence, blocking evolution)
- Treating Pitons as functional (maintaining pitons from guilt)

**The limit claim:** No framework eliminates relational complexity. Navigation is perpetual. Clarity reveals choice-points within persistent uncertainty. Structural paradoxes require containment, not resolution.

**The final word:** You are finite. Your relational energy is limited. Your capacity for intimacy is bounded. Your power is position-dependent.

You classify constraints. You specify indices. You acknowledge power asymmetry. You test edges. You navigate tensions. You hold paradoxes. You maintain living relationships. You eliminate pitons. You accept what remains unresolved.

**And somewhere in all of that, you find your people—not through illusion, but through honest map of the relational terrain.**

That's sociology.
