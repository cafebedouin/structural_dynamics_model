**[THE ONE-INCH FRAME]**

In a dry dock in the Piraeus, a shipwright stacks the planks he has been quietly saving — every timber pulled from Theseus's ship as it rotted, one per voyage, for ten years. Out in the Aegean, the ship he pulled them from is still sailing, sound, every board new. Two hulls. One name. He has not yet decided which is lying.

---

## 1. The example that was supposed to be easy

Plutarch records the puzzle as a settled fixture of Athenian philosophical life: the ship preserved down to the time of Demetrius Phalereus, its decayed planks swapped one at a time for new timber, until the question forces itself — is it the same ship? One school said it remained the same; another said it did not. The puzzle survives because the disagreement does.

The modern diagnostic treatment has a clean answer, and the answer is correct as far as it reaches. The contradiction is generated by *drift*: the predicate "the same ship" is reapplied at each replacement while the criterion for sameness silently mutates. Fix the criterion at the start — declare that "the ship" means its matter, or declare that it means its functional form — hold that criterion across every plank, and the paradox dissolves. No mystery survives strict frame discipline. In the trifurcation of *Debugging Philosophy*, Theseus is the textbook Type A case, the exemplar of dissolution-by-frame-fixing.

That account is right about one layer of the object and silently wrong about the object. This essay argues three things. First, frame-fixing does not resolve the Ship of Theseus; it *suppresses* the part of it that is not drift. Second, once you run the puzzle through an instrument that separates perspective from commitment-structure — the two-axis framework — the ship resolves into a contested commitment with two contradictory readings, and the famous gap between Plutarch's version and Hobbes's reassembly version turns out to be a change in the *relation between the readings*, not a change in the metaphysics — a change that, on inspection, is itself relative to how the name is made to refer. Third — the reflexive payoff — the founding exemplar of dissolution turns out to be the compact demonstration of why dissolution was the wrong stance.

The scope is narrow on purpose. This is an account of the puzzle's structure, not a theory of personal identity and not a ruling on which ship is the real one. The framework it imports is the two-axis Deferential Realism program; readers without that program will find the claims legible but unmotivated, which is itself a fact the essay has to own (§6).

## 2. What frame-fixing actually does

Watch the dissolution move in slow motion. You are handed a flickering object and told to stop the flicker by choosing a criterion. You choose matter: "the ship" is the timber it is made of. Now the answer is determinate — the all-new ship in the Aegean is a different ship, cleanly, no paradox. Or you choose form: "the ship" is the continuous functional organization. Again determinate — same ship, cleanly, no paradox. The flicker stops.

But notice *what* stopped it. There were two criteria in the room — matter-continuity and functional-form — and they rest on contradictory foundational commitments. Matter-continuity grounds identity in persistence of constituents; functional-form grounds it in persistence of organization through replacement of constituents. No single coherent account of identity holds both: one says the stuff is what matters and the other says the stuff is precisely what does not. Frame-fixing did not reconcile these. It *selected one and silenced the other*. The contradiction is still there; you have agreed not to look at it.

This is the difference between dissolving a paradox and choosing a side in one. The plank replacement — the genuine drift — is the *occasion* that surfaces the contradiction. It is not the *generator*. The generator is the pair of incompatible identity-axioms, and that pair survives any amount of frame discipline, because frame discipline operates on the drift and the contradiction is not made of drift. A drift remedy applied to a structural conflict produces the appearance of resolution and the persistence of the problem. The puzzle keeps coming back for two and a half millennia not because nobody thought to fix the frame, but because fixing the frame was never addressed to the thing keeping it alive.

The simpler explanation deserves its hearing: perhaps the contradiction *is* just vagueness about a threshold — at what percentage of replaced planks does identity lapse? — and the whole thing is Sorites in a hull. That account works for the question "*when* did it stop being the same?" It does not work for "*in virtue of what* is it the same or not?" The threshold question is drift; the in-virtue-of question is a clash of foundations. Theseus contains both, layered, and the tradition's error is to treat the visible layer as the whole.

## 3. The ship as a contested commitment

Decompose it with the framework's own primitives. "The ship" is a **kernel** — a contested commitment that participants point to when asked what is at stake. Matter-continuity and functional-form are two **readings** of that kernel, each resting on a contradictory **foundational axiom**. The object is no longer one thing oscillating between two answers. It is one disputed commitment carrying two ratified interpretations, each internally coherent, jointly incompatible.

This reframing earns its keep immediately, because it changes the question the puzzle is asking. The naive question — *which reading is correct?* — is a foundational-axiom dispute with no index that dissolves it; it is held for containment, not resolved (see Ω_P). The productive question is the one the kernel decomposition makes available: *what is the structural relation between the two readings, and does it change across the variants of the puzzle?* It does. That is the next section, and it is where the contribution lives.

## 4. The edge-flip: why Hobbes's ship is a different *kind* of problem

Here is the move the older treatments cannot make, because they lack the instrument for it.

The two-axis framework's conflict-signature result (Theorem 8) says: when two readings of one kernel hold contradictory foundational axioms, the contradiction *alone* does not fix the structure. A second, independent fact does — the typed relation between the readings. Two contradictory readings that *coexist* (each live, neither making the other impossible) are **licensed plurality**. The same two contradictory readings joined by a *forecloses* relation (one making the other structurally impossible) are **real closure**. Same contradiction; different structural object; the edge decides.

Apply it to the two famous versions of the ship. In Plutarch's version — gradual replacement, nothing reassembled — the matter-reading and the form-reading *coexist*. You can hold either; if you ground identity in matter the renewed ship is new, if you ground it in form it is the same, and neither verdict forecloses the other's possibility. They are two standing positions a philosopher can occupy, which is exactly how Plutarch reports it: one side held one thing, the other side the other, and the ship sailed on under both descriptions at once. Contradiction with coexistence — licensed plurality.

In Hobbes's version — the discarded planks saved and reassembled into a second hull — something changes, and it is not the criteria. The same two readings are in play. What changes is that the readings now have **rival referents competing for one designation** — the matter-reading points at the Piraean hull, the form-reading at the Aegean hull, and "the Ship of Theseus" is a single name. But whether that competition is *foreclosure* or merely *contest* turns on a premise the older treatments leave silent: how the name refers. State the criterion plainly — two readings foreclose iff asserting one makes the other unsatisfiable *under the same naming function*. Under rigid single-reference naming, two hulls cannot both bear the name, the assignments are jointly unsatisfiable, and the edge is `forecloses` — real closure. Under contextual naming — registry, salvage law, museum, sentiment each indexing differently — both hulls can bear the name in different frames, and the edge reverts to `coexists_with` with ambiguity. So the reassembly does not *produce* foreclosure; it produces foreclosure *given a single-reference gauge*. The reviewer who notes that Hobbes himself still holds the form-reading is correct, and it is not an objection: Hobbes selects a reading *and* a rigid naming gauge, and the closure is downstream of the gauge.

**This is the new joint, and v2 cuts it cleaner than v1 did.** The difference between Plutarch's puzzle and Hobbes's puzzle is not that Hobbes's is "harder," or "has an extra ship." The difference is a change in the typed relation between two otherwise-identical readings of one kernel — and that relation is *itself index-relative*. The edge-flip is not a fact about the wood; it is a fact about the wood plus a naming gauge. To the dissolution view both versions are "the identity paradox," one merely more vexing; it cannot carve the joint because it has collapsed the readings into a single flicker to be stopped, and it has no place at all for the gauge that decides the edge. The two-axis view names the joint *and* names what the edge is relative to — which is the framework's native register, gauge-variance, not a defect in the claim.

A detail worth keeping, because it demonstrates the framework rather than decorating it: Hobbes favored the continuously-repaired ship, on grounds of spatial and temporal continuity. In the framework's terms that is not a *solution* — it is Hobbes selecting a reading and a gauge and declaring them the reference frame. His preference is a t0 declaration, position-dependent, no more privileged than the Athenians who took the other side. The framework predicts that a resolution will always turn out, on inspection, to be a reading-selection (and a gauge-selection) wearing the clothes of a discovery. Hobbes's does both.

## 5. What the kernel does not carry: a retracted conjecture

The first version of this essay conjectured a fourth thing — that the matter-continuity reading instantiates the framework's *detection independence* (perspectival coherence at H¹ = 0 paired with axiom foreclosure on the commitment axis), by analogy to a corpus case in Islamic jurisprudence. Checked against the corpus, the analogy fails, and the failure is worth more to the essay than the conjecture would have been.

Axiom foreclosure, under the framework's own routing, fires only on a foundational axiom whose grounding is *empirically contingent* — a premise that evidence can override. The clean corpus instance is a prohibition reading whose axiom (that the harm of a prohibited act is intrinsic and primary) was collapsed by accumulated evidence while the authority structure declined to acknowledge it; there the two axes are co-measurable and non-coinciding — H¹ = 0 across all four canonical positions, `cs_axiom_foreclosed` firing, the drift trajectory terminating at `axiom_foreclosure`. The matter-continuity criterion has no such premise. "Identity consists in constituent matter" is a metaphysical ground, not an empirical claim that evidence falsifies. (This follows directly from the foreclosure predicate's precondition: an axiom with non-empirical grounding is *ineligible* to route to foreclosure, regardless of how practice drifts around it.) So the matter-reading is not "probably not foreclosed" — it is disqualified at the grounding gate.

This is exactly the joint the strongest reviewers found independently: the move from "practice individuates the ship by its form" to "the matter-axiom is foreclosed" conflates pragmatic individuation with metaphysical ground. The framework agrees with them, and encodes the agreement as the empirical-grounding precondition. The Ship of Theseus carries perspectival structure and axiom *conflict* (§4); it does not carry axiom *foreclosure*. Detection independence is real, and anchored elsewhere — on a kernel whose foreclosed axiom is empirical and whose H¹ = 0 was measured at the same state as its foreclosure. It simply is not what this kernel exhibits, and the essay now claims exactly that and no more.

One methodological note, because it is the same principle the essay is about, applied to the essay's own evidence. The misattribution was caught by insisting that both axes' numbers come from a single measurement state — that a perspectival value and a commitment value cited side by side were computed at the same point, not joined across drift. That discipline is the separate-mechanisms principle turned inward: a claim that lives at the seam between two independent measurements is only trustworthy when the seam is checked. Retracting the conjecture is a net gain, and so is the gauge-variant edge in §4 — both make the essay claim what the structure supports and stop there.

## 6. The objection that has to land: "this is old philosophy in private clothes"

The strongest move against this essay is not a rebuttal of any claim. It is a reclassification of the whole enterprise — and a competent, charitable critic makes it, because from where they stand it is true.

The move: *None of this is new. The matter/form distinction is Aristotle. Aristotle already privileged the formal cause and concluded the repaired ship persists. Hobbes already distinguished the two variants and already preferred continuity. Locke, Hume, and two thousand years of commentary already mapped this terrain. You have taken known philosophy, renamed its parts in a private vocabulary, and presented the relabeling as discovery. This is concept inflation: complexity mistaken for depth. The existing framework needs no second axis; it needs you to use its existing words.*

That blow should land before the response arrives, because it is mostly right about what it claims. The criteria are ancient. The variants are Hobbes's. The preference for form is Aristotle's. If the contribution were "there are two criteria for identity," the critic would win outright and the essay would be decoration.

The contribution is elsewhere, and the reclassification fails at exactly one point. The tradition names the two criteria and *argues for one of them*. What it does not supply is an account of *why Plutarch's puzzle and Hobbes's puzzle are different kinds of problem rather than the same problem at two difficulty settings.* Run the test: both variants have the identical two criteria, matter and form. If the criteria were the whole story, the two variants would be structurally identical and merely differ in how hard they feel. They are not structurally identical, and the difference is not in the criteria — it is in the relation between them, which the reassembly changes from coexistence to foreclosure *under a single-reference gauge*. "Form versus matter" cannot express that difference because it lives one level below it, and it has no term at all for the gauge the edge is relative to. The edge does the work, and the tradition has no handle for the edge.

Put to the plain-language test, the claim survives without the vocabulary: *the two-ships puzzle is a different kind of problem from the one-ship puzzle, because now the two answers cannot both stand — and whether they can both stand depends not on the criteria, which are the same in both, but on whether one name is forced to pick one ship.* That sentence needs no framework. What the framework adds is that "cannot both stand" is a *general, measurable, index-relative* property — the same property that distinguishes a living doctrinal pluralism from a doctrine that has killed its rival — and not a Theseus-specific intuition. The jargon is compression of a general result, not inflation of a local one.

Where the critic wins, and the essay must concede it: for anyone who wants to *answer the puzzle* — to be told which ship is really Theseus's — this contributes nothing, by design. It refuses the verdict. The deferential stance does not adjudicate which reading is right; it maps the structure of the disagreement and stops. A reader seeking resolution should take Hobbes's answer or Aristotle's and go; this essay is not for that purpose and does not improve on them for it. The instrument also costs something to hold: it requires the two-axis program, which is not free to acquire, so the reframing is available to the analytically equipped and opaque to everyone else. That is a real limit on who the contribution serves, and it is named here rather than buried.

What remains, after the concession, is narrow and intact: an account of the puzzle's *structure* that distinguishes its variants by a property — and a relativity of that property — the older accounts could state the symptoms of but not the mechanism.

## 7. What the founding example says about where we started

The reflexive turn is the reason this was worth writing down.

*Debugging Philosophy* offered the Ship of Theseus as its clean Type A case — the exemplar of a paradox that is really an engineering bug, dissolved by fixing the frame. Take the instrument that program eventually built, the two-axis framework, and turn it back on the example that launched it. The founding exemplar was misclassified. Theseus is Type A on its surface (the plank drift, the threshold vagueness), Type B underneath (the contradictory identity-axioms that frame-fixing can only suppress), and Type C across its variants and observer positions (which ship is "real" dissolves into *real for what, from where* — and, we can now add, *under which naming gauge*). It was never the easy case. It was all three mechanisms wearing one hull, and calling it Type A was the move that hid the other two.

And the dissolution stance itself — *fix the frame and the paradox goes away* — turns out, under the framework, to be a foreclosed axiom. It presupposed that there is a privileged frame to converge on, a place where the flicker stops and the answer is simply true. The deferential program forecloses exactly that premise: which state counts as the reference frame is position-dependent, a declaration and not a discovery, and the disagreement that remains after you declare it is a real structural feature to be measured, not a residue to be cleaned. The tool built to debug philosophy, run over its own origin story, reclassifies the origin and retires the stance that motivated it.

Theseus's ship was never the puzzle that dissolves when you fix the frame. It was the puzzle that shows fixing the frame is how you hide which axiom you killed.

### Open Questions (Ω)

**Ω_E — Is the gauge-variant edge computable on a Theseus kernel? (empirically resolvable).** §4 claims the edge between the matter- and form-readings flips between `coexists_with` and `forecloses` as the naming function moves from contextual to single-reference. Resolution: instantiate a Theseus kernel (two readings, their foundational axioms, the typed edge) and the two variants (Plutarch: no rival referent; Hobbes: rival referent), then vary the naming-function parameter and confirm the typed edge tracks it. A measured flip would upgrade §4 from argued to demonstrated. (This replaces v1's Ω_E, which conjectured matter-reading foreclosure; that question was resolved negatively in §5 — the matter-axiom is ineligible at the grounding gate.)

**Ω_C — "Which is the real ship?" (conceptually underspecified).** The question carries no single answer because it carries no index. Real for legal ownership, real for sentimental continuity, real for museum authenticity, real for the registry — these specify different objects (plausibly the Aegean hull for continuity-of-use, the Piraean hull for material provenance). The puzzle's felt depth at this point is underspecification, and it dissolves on indexing — which is the same index that fixes the naming gauge in §4.

**Ω_P — Matter versus form as the ground of identity (structurally irresolvable / preference-dependent).** Whether identity is grounded in constituent matter or in continuous organization is a foundational-axiom conflict. No index dissolves it; it is held by containment, not resolved by analysis. Decision authority rests with whoever is declaring the reference frame for a given purpose — which is to say, it is a commitment, not a fact, and the framework declines to adjudicate it.
