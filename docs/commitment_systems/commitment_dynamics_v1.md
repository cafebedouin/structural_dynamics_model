# The Decay and Transmission of Commitments

*A theory of cultural dynamics, v1. This document is the re-carved successor to "Commitment Systems and Possibility Space" (v0.1). It is a substantial restructuring, not a revision: the predecessor bundled a theory of cultural **dynamics** (how commitments decay, transmit, and are lost) with a **kinematics** (a five-motion taxonomy of how commitments move through a "possibility space") and a **formalism** (a sheaf over kernel-space, a reachable window, the Dirac sea). An adversarial review — eight independent models red-teaming the predecessor's joints, instructed to break rather than illustrate — found the dynamics largely sound in mechanism, the kinematics under-carved and non-exhaustive, and the formalism fitted to the wrong genre of object. This version keeps the dynamics, demotes the kinematics to informal description, and removes the formalism. What was the document's proudest material is gone; what was its quietest material is the spine.*

---

## Status

This is a theory of cultural transmission and decay, developed by reasoning and stress-tested against documented cases by adversarial review, not validated against a purpose-built corpus. It is offered as a mechanism-level account with stated failure modes, not as a finding. Two things are true at once and the document is built to hold both: the surviving mechanisms are sharper and better-supported than the predecessor's, *and* the predecessor's most beautiful apparatus — the possibility-space geometry — did not survive contact with cases and is not here. The discipline that the predecessor's coda named is the one this version tries to honor in its bones rather than its closing paragraph: an attractive structure is not evidence for itself, and the way you find out whether a beautiful idea is true is to keep looking for the place it leaves a mark. The geometry never left a mark. The mechanisms did.

A note on what changed and why, because the genealogy matters for reading it. The predecessor recorded a single null against the Deferential Realism (DR) constraint corpus and bundled its whole apparatus under that null. A later predicate-level audit of the DR engine showed that one narrow synchronic component — the beneficiary/victim distinction — does carry classification weight, and that component graduated into the commitment-systems framework proper (see the v5.1 framework revision). That graduation is **synchronic**: it concerns how a fixed constraint classifies. It is not in this document. This document is the **diachronic** remainder — how commitments change over time — and the audit that graduated the synchronic bit said nothing for or against it. The two were entangled in the predecessor and are now separated: the synchronic committer signal lives in the framework; the diachronic dynamics live here, on their own evidence.

## 1. The claim, reduced to its mechanisms

Culture is the set of commitments a population currently exercises. A commitment not transmitted is not held; a commitment held by no living practitioner is a dead kernel. The dynamics of culture are therefore the dynamics of *what stays exercised and what lapses*, and the predecessor's durable contribution was a small set of mechanisms governing that. Stripped of the geometry, they are:

1. **Decay-by-disuse.** A commitment stays available only by being exercised. Unexercised commitments lapse — not by anyone deciding against them, simply by no one keeping them live.
2. **The reservoir structure.** "Exercised" does not require everyone; a commitment survives as long as *someone* in the population still practices it. A specialist subculture is a reservoir that keeps a commitment available to the wider population. When the last practitioner stops, it is gone.
3. **The symbol/commitment split.** Two things decay at very different rates. The *symbol* — the word, the written rule, the codified form — is slow to decay and archive-protectable. The *commitment* — the lived, tacit practice — is fast to decay and survives only by exercise. A surviving symbol over a dead commitment is a **husk** that feels like retention and is not.
4. **Reconstruction-from-symbol.** A husk is not nothing. A high-symbol commitment can be reconstructed from its surviving symbol after the lived practice has entirely lapsed — but the reconstruction inherits only the symbolic fraction and must re-improvise the tacit fraction. This is why revived practices feel like reconstructions rather than continuations.

These four are the spine. Everything else in this document is either a consequence of them, a bounded claim about their limits, or a piece of the predecessor that did not survive and is marked as such.

The single most important structural change from the predecessor: it treated the **possibility-space geometry** as the load-bearing core and the mechanisms as its consequence. The adversarial review inverts this. The mechanisms are the real thing. The geometry was scaffolding the mechanisms turned out not to require — and, where it made specific structural claims, the claims were wrong. The mechanisms need no notion of "adjacent kernels," no metric on a kernel-space, no sheaf, no window. They are statements about exercise and decay, and they are portable to any domain where transmission can be observed (linguistic, diachronic, institutional) without carrying any geometry along.

## 2. Decay-by-disuse and the reservoir

A commitment is held in a population's repertoire by being exercised, and atrophies when it is not — the same logic as a capability maintained by use and lost by neglect. A population commits to one kernel; the alternatives go unexercised; they lapse. This collapses what look like three separate drivers of cultural change — environmental pressure, endogenous foreclosure (committing to one thing forecloses others), and recombination — into one channel: in every case the unexercised alternative starves.

The reservoir qualification is what keeps this from being too strong, and it is load-bearing for everything downstream. A commitment does not require universal practice to survive; it requires *one live reservoir.* Craft vocabularies survive in specialists, not in the general lexicon. Liturgical languages survive in clergy. The reservoir is why a lapsed-seeming commitment can sometimes climb back to wide practice: as long as one reservoir holds it, it is recoverable; when the last reservoir empties, the door closes.

**What this gets right and what the review confirmed.** The reservoir mechanism is robust. The reviewers' attempts to break decay-by-disuse mostly failed against it — and the cases that *did* break a stronger version of the claim are exactly the ones the reservoir plus the symbol/commitment split (below) absorb correctly. Decay-by-disuse, qualified by the reservoir, survives.

**Where the predecessor over-claimed: strict reachability.** The predecessor stated that "reachability reduces to extant practice somewhere in the stack" — that a commitment with zero living practitioners is, flatly, gone. This is false, and it is the cleanest break the review produced. Modern Hebrew and Classical Latin were both reconstructed into living practice from a state of zero native/spoken exercise, using the surviving symbolic record. Rabbinic study preserved the detailed form of Temple sacrifice for nineteen centuries with zero performance. The strict claim — gone means unrecoverable — is wrong, and the correct mechanism is the merger described in §4: reachability is extant practice *or* symbolic reconstructability, where the reconstructed thing inherits only its symbolic fraction. The predecessor's §6 already half-stated this as a footnote about dormancy; the review forces it to the center.

## 3. Finite capacity, reduced to where it survives

The predecessor claimed culture has a finite carrying capacity for live commitments at three nested levels — individual (a cognitive ceiling), cultural (users × domains), and ecological (the shrinking number of distinct languages) — and that commitment is therefore zero-sum: holding one kernel occupies capacity its alternatives would need to stay live.

The adversarial review broke the **middle, culture-level** claim decisively and convergently. Six of eight reviewers independently produced the same counterexample structure: cultures *accumulate* live commitments without shedding, by compartmentalization and layering. Plural legal systems (multiple personal-law regimes coexisting under one state), syncretic religious practice (two full ontologies held at once), parallel interpretive schools — all are cases where the population's set of live commitments grows, for centuries, with no observable zero-sum shedding. The defender's only rescue — redraw the boundary of "a commitment" wider, or partition into sub-domains so capacity is finite "within" each — was independently identified as circular by four reviewers: if "finite" can expand by re-bounding whenever it is violated, it predicts nothing.

This is also, notably, the level the predecessor *itself* flagged as most likely to fail, and the reservoir mechanism of §2 is what defeats it: if a commitment survives in a specialist reservoir, the culture-level set does not shed by capacity pressure — it sheds, when it sheds at all, by reservoir extinction, which is a weaker and more contingent process than a zero-sum capacity bound.

**What survives.** The individual-cognitive bound (a person holds only so many live distinctions) and the ecological bound (the number of distinct languages is shrinking) were not broken — neither was attacked successfully, and both are independently attested. So finite capacity survives at the extremes and fails in the middle. The honest claim is: capacity is bounded per individual and per ecology; at the culture level, commitments are governed by reservoir survival, not by a zero-sum ceiling. The zero-sum framing is retired as a general law and kept only as the individual-level mechanism it actually describes.

## 4. The symbol/commitment split and reconstruction

This is the strongest single idea in the predecessor and the one the review rated most robust. Two decay rates: the symbol slow and archivable, the lived commitment fast and exercise-dependent. A husk is a surviving symbol over a dead commitment, and the quiet danger is that the husk *feels like* retention — you know the word, so you feel you have access, but the access is to the form, not the practice.

Dormancy-survivability tracks the symbolic fraction of a commitment. High-symbol kernels (legal codes, mathematical results, written doctrine) sleep and reactivate because most of their content lives in the symbol; Justinian's code sat dormant and reachable for centuries. High-tacit kernels (craft knowledge, the felt interior of an observance, survival-under-catastrophe competence) die when they go dormant, leaving only a husk.

The review forces one correction and one merger, both of which strengthen the idea:

**Correction — the husk is recoverable, lossily.** The predecessor treated the husk as essentially dead. Hebrew and Latin show a husk can seed a full reconstruction. So the mechanism is not "tacit content dies, end of story" but "tacit content lapses and can be re-improvised from the symbol at a loss." The reconstruction is real practice, but it is *new* practice fitted to an old symbol, not the original lived content recovered. This is why revivals feel reconstructed: they are.

**Merger — decay-by-disuse and the symbol/commitment split are one mechanism, not two.** One reviewer attacked the split by arguing it never makes a prediction distinct from decay-by-disuse — that wherever a symbol survives without practice, disuse already explains the loss, so the split is redundant. The attack fails, and its failure is instructive: the two *do* diverge in prediction, on exactly the reconstruction cases. Strict decay-by-disuse predicts "Hebrew is gone, unrecoverable." The symbol/commitment split predicts "the symbol survived, so reconstruction is possible." They diverged; reconstruction happened; the split was right where bare disuse was wrong. So the correct object is a single mechanism with two coupled rates: a commitment has a symbolic fraction (slow decay, archivable, reconstructable) and a tacit fraction (fast decay, exercise-dependent, re-improvised at a loss on reconstruction), and "reachability" is the union of live practice and symbolic reconstructability. This merged mechanism is the spine's load-bearing center.

## 5. Two roads to a false "natural"

The predecessor's most genuinely original move, and it survives — but its sharp edge has to be re-filed, because the review caught the edge over-stated.

The DR engine's False Natural Law machinery detects **beneficiary-naturalization**: a constraint presents as a mountain (natural, unauthored, just how things are) but has a beneficiary structure inconsistent with that presentation, so it is reclassified. The other road is **path-naturalization**: a commitment looks natural not because a beneficiary hides behind a cover story, but because *its alternatives lapsed through disuse.* "Moral standing begins at birth" presents to a modern observer as obvious; the alternatives that were once practiced have simply left the live set. History did the naturalizing.

The distinction is real and worth keeping: a beneficiary-hunting instrument is blind by construction to a constraint naturalized by disuse rather than extraction, because it asks "who benefits" and the honest answer is "no one in particular — the alternative starved." This is genuinely diagnostic and it exports cleanly to any critical method built on beneficiary-hunting.

**The over-claim the review broke: "no beneficiary."** The predecessor stated path-naturalization flatly *has no beneficiary*. The review could not find a single uncontaminated case. Every candidate — QWERTY (four reviewers), the naturalized free market, printing-press-era doctrine — turned out to have identifiable beneficiaries on inspection (incumbents, training institutions, network holders). The reviewers' near-unanimity here is partly a measurement artifact (they hunted beneficiaries, so they found them — the same blindness, inverted), but the conclusion stands: a strictly beneficiary-*less* naturalized commitment is empirically homeless. There are zero validated clean cases.

So the corrected claim is weaker and truer. The clean distinction is not *beneficiary versus none*; it is **engineered closure versus lapsed closure that later accretes defenders.** A commitment can be naturalized by lapse (no one engineered the alternatives' death) and then, once it is the only live option, acquire beneficiaries who profit from and defend the status quo. The naturalizing mechanism (lapse) and the maintaining mechanism (post-hoc beneficiaries) are distinct, and conflating them is what made "no beneficiary" both attractive and false. Path-naturalization is retained as a mechanism — lapse-naturalization is structurally distinct from extraction-naturalization — but on probation, with zero clean instances and a sharper definition that does not require the absence of beneficiaries, only the absence of *engineered closure* at the moment of naturalization.

## 6. The catastrophe claim, corrected from theorem to gradient

The predecessor's bleakest and most striking conclusion: a population *structurally cannot* retain hard-won tacit catastrophe-knowledge (war, famine, atrocity) across generations of non-catastrophe, because retaining tacit content requires exercising it and exercising it requires the catastrophe. The forgetting is adaptive — peace needs the capacity that wartime competence would occupy.

This was the predecessor's "carrying-capacity theorem," and it was the single most-attacked joint in the review: seven of eight reviewers targeted it, and it was the most common choice for strongest breaker. It does not survive as a theorem. The decisive case was the Aneyoshi tsunami stones — a village that held an *actionable* land-use commitment (build above the marker, refuse development below it) across 78 years of non-catastrophe by exercising it in settlement patterns, and survived the 2011 tsunami while neighbors who relied on engineered seawalls did not. The theorem says this retention is structurally impossible and that forgetting is adaptive. Here retention happened without the catastrophe, and forgetting would have been *maladaptive*. The Dutch flood-control culture and aviation/nuclear safety training are the same shape: tacit catastrophe-competence retained for generations through deliberate simulation, drill, and ritualized exercise, without re-experiencing the catastrophe.

The corrected claim is a gradient, not a theorem, and it is sharper for being weaker:

> Tacit catastrophe-knowledge decays **by default**, because peace removes the conditions that exercise it. It can be **rescued by deliberate, costly simulation** — drills, ritualized practice, architectural commitment, professional training that substitutes for the conditions. The rescue is **lossy and incomplete**, and it is **more complete for proceduralizable knowledge** (land-use rules, checklists, evacuation routes) **than for deep embodied competence** (combat reflex, the full social reality of famine). And simulation **competes for finite attention**, so it is the first thing deprioritized as peace lengthens.

This relocates the forgetting from *structural necessity* to a *budget decision under lengthening peace*, which is both truer and more predictive: it picks out *which* societies relearn catastrophe — the ones that let their simulation lapse — rather than condemning all of them equally. The predecessor's "adaptive forgetting" insight survives intact (simulation does compete for finite capacity, and letting it lapse is often locally rational), but it no longer rests on an impossibility claim that documented cases falsify. Note that what the review confirmed *does* decay reliably is the deep embodied fraction; what survives via husk-plus-simulation is the proceduralizable fraction — which is exactly the symbol/commitment split of §4 applied to catastrophe knowledge. The catastrophe claim is not a separate theorem; it is §2 and §4 specialized to a domain where the tacit fraction is unusually large and the exercise conditions are unusually terrible to maintain.

## 7. The kinematics: demoted to description

The predecessor's §5 proposed that cultural change *decomposes into exactly five motions* of a window over possibility space: climb (fringe to frame), drop (frame to fringe), same-level swap, nascent (new commitment forming at the bottom), and site-expansion (the possibility space gaining a point that did not exist). This was presented as a taxonomy — a carving of cultural change at its joints.

The adversarial review found the five-motion taxonomy is **not a carving**, and this is the largest single structural casualty. The findings, in order of severity:

- **It is not exhaustive.** Three reviewers independently surfaced a missing motion: *top-down imposition* — a commitment installed at the top of the population stack and forced downward into dominant practice (the Meiji calendar, the Turkish alphabet reform, the Mormon manifesto under federal coercion). The five motions assume change is *endogenous* (climbs from a fringe, swaps internally). They have no cell for a commitment imposed from above or swapped under external coercion. A second missing motion is *contraction* of the possibility space — the post-1945 disappearance of "winnable great-power total war" as a thinkable doctrine, a commitment becoming not merely fringe but unthinkable, removed from the space. The predecessor has site-*expansion* (the space gains a point) but no inverse, even though its own mechanisms (decay, the window shedding states) describe contraction. The kinematics claim contraction; the motion list has no cell for it.

- **Two of the five collapse into each other.** Nascent (a new commitment forming at the bottom) and site-expansion (the possibility space acquiring a new point) cannot be distinguished on real cases — intellectual property, electronic money, zero-as-a-number were each offered as a case where there is no criterion separating "this became *thinkable*" (site-expansion) from "this was first *held*" (nascent). This is the same seam that runs through the whole investigation: the boundary between "the space changed" and "the occupied set changed" is exactly the synchronic/diachronic seam, and the taxonomy has no principled cut at it.

- **Same-level swap is a junk drawer.** Two reviewers independently noted that swap — "the held commitment changes content without changing level" — is so unconstrained it absorbs nearly any horizontal change that is not obviously a climb or drop. It is robust the way a residual category is robust: by being defined as whatever-else.

- **Only drop survives clean.** A dominant commitment falling to contested fringe is descriptively robust across cases. (The repeated attack that "drop just *is* decay" is a category error — it conflates the *kinematic* description with the *dynamic* mechanism, which the rebuild below separates — so it is not a successful break, but it is the only motion that survives, and it survives as description, not as a joint in a taxonomy.)

The honest verdict: the five motions are not five mechanisms, and they are not jointly exhaustive or mutually exclusive. They are an informal vocabulary for *describing* trajectories, and they are useful as such, but they were over-promoted to a taxonomy in the predecessor. **This document keeps climb, drop, swap, and the imposed and contraction motions the review added as informal descriptive terms, and explicitly disclaims them as a carving.** The reason cultural change does not decompose into a clean motion-taxonomy is the same reason the geometry failed: change is a *flow driven by the mechanisms of §§2–6*, not a set of discrete labeled moves, and chopping the flow into named motions is exactly the over-discretization the review exposed. The dynamics are real; the kinematic carving was false precision.

## 8. The formalism, removed

The predecessor reached for three formal pictures: the **Dirac sea** (a filled background of unoccupied possible kernels, detected only as absence), the **reachable window** as a region of a structured possibility space, and a **sheaf** over kernel-space with adjacency and morphisms. The case for keeping any of them rested entirely on whether the space of kernels has *structure* — whether "adjacent" and "reachable-from" mean anything — which the predecessor honestly flagged as unverified (its "morphism question").

This document removes the formalism, for a reason stronger than "unverified." The synchronic audit that graduated the beneficiary/victim bit (v5.1) did so through a classifier that routes kernels by a *flat lookup* over (power × bit) with **no adjacency relation whatsoever** — the working committer instrument never needed one kernel to be "near" another. That is empirical evidence for the deflationary horn of the predecessor's own fork: where a committer instrument actually works, it behaves as an unstructured set with a classification grid, not as a site with morphisms. The sheaf-and-window language is not merely unverified; the one place a committer mechanism became load-bearing, the geometry was absent and unmissed.

There is also a genre error the predecessor half-saw and §7 now makes precise. A sheaf lives over a *fixed* base. The diachronic phenomena — decay, the window translating, site-expansion — are the *base itself changing* (kernels entering and leaving the set of existables), which is not a section shifting on a fixed site but a moving base, a different and harder kind of object. The predecessor flagged site-expansion as "the one that strains the whole apparatus"; the sharper statement is that site-expansion is not one motion among five but evidence that the object was never a presheaf in the first place. The diachronic dynamics are a *flow on which commitments are live*, governed by §§2–6. They are not a cohomology, and forcing them into sheaf language was fitting the right tool to the wrong genre of object.

What is kept from the formal gestures, demoted to metaphor with no inferential load: the *image* of an unseen background of unchosen alternatives, made briefly visible as absence, remains a useful way to ask "who is not in the conversation, what was not committed to." It is a framing device, not a model. Nothing in this document's claims depends on it, and that independence is deliberate — a formalism you can delete without losing a prediction was never doing inferential work.

## 9. What this is good for, and where to look for a mark

Reduced to mechanisms, the theory is portable in a way the geometry-laden version was not, and that portability is where it could earn the belief it has not yet earned. The mechanisms — decay-by-disuse-with-reservoirs, the symbol/commitment split with lossy reconstruction, lapse-versus-engineered naturalization, and the catastrophe gradient — make claims about *transmission and decay* that should leave marks in domains where transmission is directly observable:

- **Linguistic / lexical data.** The reservoir mechanism and the symbol/commitment split predict specific patterns in vocabulary survival: which terms persist in specialist reservoirs after leaving common use, which lapse entirely, and which are reconstructed (with shifted meaning) from textual record. This is measurable diachronically and is the most plausible near-term test bed.

- **Revival cases as a natural experiment.** The reconstruction-from-symbol claim predicts that revived practices recover the symbolic fraction and re-improvise the tacit fraction. Hebrew, Latin, reconstructed liturgies, and revived crafts are a comparative set where the symbolic/tacit ratio varies and the prediction (high-symbol revivals are more continuous, high-tacit revivals more reconstructive) is checkable.

- **Catastrophe-knowledge retention.** The gradient predicts that societies retaining actionable disaster competence are those maintaining costly simulation, and that the proceduralizable fraction survives where the embodied fraction does not. Tsunami-stone communities, flood cultures, and safety-critical professions are a comparative set.

What this document explicitly does **not** claim, so the next reader is not misled by the predecessor's ambition: it does not claim a structured possibility space, it does not claim a five-motion taxonomy, it does not claim a beneficiary-less naturalization, and it does not claim the catastrophe impossibility. Each of those was in the predecessor, each was attractive, and each either failed against cases or was never load-bearing where a related mechanism worked. The seed the predecessor preserved is still here — but it has been cut back to the part that the stress test left standing, and the cuttings are recorded above so they are not replanted without their failure attached.

## 10. The adversarial review, as method

One note on how this version was produced, because it is the part most worth carrying to the next investigation. The predecessor was tested the way the founding work in this lineage was tested: by running its joints against cases that could break them. But the predecessor tested *itself*, and a theory testing itself selects confirming cases. This version was re-carved by handing the predecessor's joints to eight independent models with a single instruction — break these, do not illustrate them, a confirming case is worthless — and adjudicating which breaks were real (survived a defender's absorption) versus absorbable (the defender escapes cleanly).

The result was asymmetric in a way no single mind, including the original author's, had produced: the mechanisms survived, the kinematics and the formalism did not, and the specific breaks (Aneyoshi against the catastrophe theorem, Hebrew against strict reachability, plural-legal-systems against culture-level capacity, the missing top-down and contraction motions) were convergent across independent reviewers, which is the signal that they are properties of the theory rather than of one clever attack. The discipline that distinguishes this from the predecessor is not that this version is more cautious — it is that its claims survived being attacked by minds that were not trying to protect them. That is the standard the next revision should be held to as well: not whether the structure is beautiful or internally coherent, but whether it survives an adversary who is paid to break it and finds he cannot.
