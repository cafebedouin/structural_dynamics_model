# Commitment Systems and the Architecture of Drift

Any arrangement of stabilized commitments operating in an environment that changes faster than the commitments do will eventually face a structural problem: how to acknowledge that operational practice has drifted from the commitments without dissolving the authority that grounds the commitments. This problem appears wherever the conditions hold, which is more places than is usually noticed. Religions, constitutions, scientific theories, philosophical positions, identities, marriages, employment relationships, legal systems, artificial intelligence training protocols, mathematical practice, professional standards, ideological movements — all are commitment systems. All face the same structural problem. All instantiate the same patterns of response.

This is a theoretical claim, not an empirical survey. The argument is that the structural pattern is real and the framework that analyzes it is portable across the domains where the pattern obtains. The empirical work of testing the framework in specific domains is the research program this paper aims to enable, not the work this paper performs.

The framework treats commitment systems as a distinct analytical object that has been hiding in plain sight across multiple disciplines, that the same machinery analyzes them across domains, that failure modes are predictable from structural configuration plus environmental rate plus authority structure incentives plus several cross-cutting structural features. The sketch is bold rather than hedged because boldness is what makes the predictions worth testing. The framework either does this work or it doesn't; the next phase finds out.

## What this paper does

This sketch establishes the theoretical foundation for a research program by specifying the framework at five structural levels.

**Principles** are structural facts that operate across all commitment systems and constrain what configurations are possible. They are not features systems have or don't have; they are conditions that always operate. Three principles are load-bearing: authority extends only as far as enforcement, drift is intrinsic, and acknowledgment requires standing within the system being acknowledged.

**Configurations** are attractor patterns in the space of possible commitment system arrangements. Five base configurations are identified: marked revision, interpretive accretion, diffuse reconstruction, implicit practice, and anchored fixity (with two sub-types based on the presence of an interpretive-accretion layer). The configurations describe how systems handle the relationship between kernel and operational practice.

**Lifecycle phases** are temporal states systems pass through. Formation, stabilization, operation, atrophy, and renewal-or-dissolution. The configurations describe phase characteristics rather than timeless identities; the same system in different phases may operate in different configurations, and the lifecycle dimension is critical to understanding which failure modes are likely when.

**Features** are cross-cutting structural dimensions that modify configurations. Five features are identified: decoupled formalization, ritualized renewal, velocity mismatch, distribution of acknowledgment authority, and kernel encoding substrate. Each feature can combine with any base configuration to produce specific failure modes.

**Default conditions** are structural facts that obtain in most actual cases rather than features systems have or don't have. Two defaults are central: nesting (almost every commitment system is nested in larger commitment systems and contains smaller commitment systems) and coupling to other commitment systems (most systems are coupled to other systems whose dynamics affect their own). The framework's analytical machinery must handle nested-and-coupled systems as the typical case rather than as exceptions.

**Parameters** are variables the framework tracks at observer positions: exit options, power, time horizons, and spatial-temporal scope. These parameters determine what any particular position within a commitment system sees and what consequences any particular position can produce. The framework's positional analysis is parameterized through these variables.

These five levels together produce a framework with substantial analytical reach. Principles constrain. Configurations describe. Lifecycle phases situate. Features modify. Defaults condition. Parameters position. Each level does different work; together they handle the complexity that commitment systems actually exhibit.

## Three primitives

Before developing the levels, the framework's three primitives need explicit statement.

A **kernel** is the stabilized arrangement of commitments that constitutes the system's nominal core. This may be a text (a constitution, a foundational paper, a creed), a body of practice (the methodology of a craft tradition), a narrative (an identity's organizing story), a relationship (the implicit terms a marriage rests on), or some combination. The kernel is what participants in the system point to when asked what the system is.

An **authority structure** is the mechanism that determines what counts as legitimate interpretation of the kernel, with its own incentives, resource flows, and stake in preserving standing. Authority structures may be external (a priesthood, a judiciary, an academic discipline, a board), distributed (the parties to a relationship, a community of practice, a peer-review system), internal (an individual's self-interpretive practices), or composite. The authority structure has skin in the game; treating it as politically neutral interpretive machinery misses what it is.

**Drift** is unmarked mutation of operational practice relative to the kernel. Drift is intrinsic because the environment the kernel was stabilized within continues to change after the kernel stabilizes. No commitment system preserves perfect fidelity to its kernel across time. The question is not whether drift occurs but whether the system's authority structure can and will acknowledge drift without losing legitimacy.

The kernel is frame-relative. At any moment T1, the kernel is whatever participants in the system point to as the reference for that analysis. Drift between T1 and T2 is measured against the T1 kernel, not against some eternal fixed reference. The kernel itself can change between T1 and T2, and that change is itself drift to be acknowledged or denied. This treatment makes systems with evolving kernels analyzable in the same terms as systems with fixed ones.

Drift is not a single phenomenon. The single concept bundles distinct dynamics with different causes, different rates, and different responses appropriate to them. The framework treats five drift types as load-bearing distinctions: *environmental drift* (operational practice changing in response to environmental change), *authority drift* (the interpretive structure itself changing through personnel turnover or institutional evolution), *endogenous drift* (the authority structure's incentives producing reinterpretation independent of environmental pressure), *recognition drift* (the gap between drift that has occurred and drift that has been recognized as having occurred), and *sabotage drift* (participants who recognize the gap deliberately exploiting it). These drift types co-occur and interact; the diagnostic order specified below handles their sequential identification.

## Principles

Three structural facts operate across all commitment systems. They are not features systems may have or lack; they are conditions that constrain what configurations are possible.

**Authority extends only as far as enforcement.** An authority structure's nominal power is bounded by its operational enforcement capacity. Enforcement can be coercive (state power, institutional sanction), normative (social standing, exclusion from community), or self-imposed (the individual's commitment to their own kernel). When nominal authority outruns enforcement capacity, the gap between formal claims and operational reality opens, producing predictable failure modes. The principle bears on every configuration: marked revision requires enforcement of acknowledgment events; interpretive accretion requires enforcement of who has commentary standing; anchored fixity requires enforcement of kernel preservation; diffuse reconstruction's instability follows from enforcement absence; implicit practice depends on enforcement through practice continuity. The principle is also the structural basis for decoupled formalization as a feature — decoupled formalization occurs precisely when the formal kernel claims authority the enforcement infrastructure doesn't support.

**Drift is intrinsic.** Every commitment system experiences drift because no kernel can fully anticipate the environment its operational practice will encounter. The question for any commitment system is not whether drift occurs but how the system's authority structure handles it. Systems that deny drift accumulate gap until breakdown. Systems that acknowledge drift through whatever mechanism is appropriate to their configuration can maintain stability indefinitely under the right conditions. The principle bears on the framework's analytical position: the framework's purpose is making drift visible, and the framework's preference for acknowledged drift over absorbed drift is constitutive of what the framework is for rather than a bias to be apologized for.

**Acknowledgment requires standing.** Drift acknowledgment is not a free intellectual move; it is an authority-structure operation that requires the actor performing it to have standing within the system being acknowledged. Mathematicians can acknowledge mathematical drift; outsiders cannot, because the acknowledgment requires participation in mathematical practice. Priests can acknowledge doctrinal drift within their tradition; the same observation from outsiders has different status. Citizens can participate in constitutional acknowledgment; foreign observers cannot, regardless of how clearly they see the drift. This principle has substantial consequences. It bears on cover story analysis (authority structures with extraction stakes have standing but limited willingness to acknowledge), on relational systems (acknowledgment in distributed-authority systems requires participation by multiple parties with standing), and on the framework's recursive self-application (the framework can acknowledge its own drift only because it operates within a community of analysts and practitioners who have standing within it).

These three principles are not configurations and not features. They are conditions under which all commitment systems operate, and they shape what configurations are possible, what failure modes are likely, and what acknowledgment operations are available.

## Configurations

Different combinations of kernel formalization, authority structure type, acknowledgment capacity, and the principles above produce different commitment system configurations. Five base configurations are worth distinguishing across domains. The configurations are claims about attractor patterns in configuration space, not partitions of all possible systems.

*Marked revision*: highly specifiable kernel, epistemic authority structure with limited extraction stake in kernel preservation, working acknowledgment capacity. The kernel is precise. Authority is voluntary and grounded in expertise. Drift is formalized as revision: someone proposes a change, the change is checked, and if it works the system absorbs it. Acknowledgment is marked and legible rather than absorbed silently. The configuration appears across domains: mathematics in the scientific theory case, certain therapeutic practices in the identity case, couples with explicit norm-revision practices in the relational case, programming languages with formal proposal-and-deprecation procedures in the technical case. Stability requires that acknowledgment bandwidth matches environmental change rate and that the authority structure does not develop strong extraction incentives in kernel preservation.

*Interpretive accretion*: highly specifiable kernel, traditional or institutional authority structure with substantial stake in kernel preservation, blocked acknowledgment capacity for explicit kernel revision. The text is fixed. Authority is grounded in continuity with the founding text. Drift migrates entirely into interpretation. Everyone insists the kernel controls while the operational meaning shifts substantially. Drift accumulates as unmarked interpretive accretion. The configuration appears across domains: Brahmanical commentary on the Vedas, doctrine of development in Catholicism, common law jurisprudence, constitutional originalism's evolving content despite its claim to fixity. The configuration is durable across millennia in the right environments. A distinction within this configuration matters: interpretive accretion can either be failure-to-acknowledge (the kernel is treated as fixed and drift is hidden) or acknowledgment-through-different-mode (the kernel explicitly anticipates interpretive development as part of how it works). The Quaker Faith and Practice tradition, which rewrites the document every generation, operates as acknowledgment-through-different-mode; constitutional originalism that denies its own evolution operates as failure-to-acknowledge. The framework should distinguish these.

*Diffuse reconstruction*: under-specified or intentionally ambiguous kernel, no centralized authority structure, distributed interpretation with mutually incompatible readings claiming the same source. The kernel can be claimed by many parties producing operationally incompatible interpretations. The configuration persists indefinitely but lacks operational coherence. Platonism in philosophy is the canonical case. In identity systems, diffuse reconstruction appears when commitments are held without operational implications. In AI development, the various interpretations of "alignment" or "safety" function as diffuse reconstruction. In contemporary political ideology, terms like "freedom" or "justice" function similarly. The configuration is stable in the sense of persisting; it is unstable in the sense of never resolving operational coherence. It often serves strategic purposes for parties who benefit from operational ambiguity.

*Implicit practice*: no codified kernel, traditional authority over practice itself. The kernel is whatever the system does. Drift is the mechanism rather than a failure of it. The configuration is stable as long as practice remains coherent and the relevant authority structures continue to operate. Breakdown is severe when practice loses coherence because there is no fixed referent to appeal to. The UK constitution is the canonical organizational case. Craft traditions transmitting tacit knowledge through apprenticeship are another. In identity systems, implicit practice appears when a person's sense of self is constituted by what they do rather than by explicit narrative commitments.

*Anchored fixity*: formalized kernel paired with authority structure that grounds its legitimacy in the kernel's unchangeability and extracts substantial benefit from preventing kernel revision. Drift denial is the source of authority rather than a side effect. Two sub-types produce radically different stability profiles. *Anchored fixity with interpretive-accretion layer* pairs the unrevisable kernel with a substantial interpretive substructure beneath it; drift gets absorbed by the interpretive layer rather than surfacing as kernel revision; the configuration can persist millennia. *Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer* lacks the interpretive substructure; the kernel is supposed to govern operational practice directly; the configuration produces accumulating gap and catastrophic breakdown when environmental change exceeds what the kernel can govern. Spartan breakdown at Leuctra is the canonical case at civilizational scale; identity crises in fundamentalist deconstruction are the same pattern at personal scale.

The framework predicts that anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer produces brittle-fixity failure across organizational, identity, and relational systems. This is a falsifiable prediction. If brittle-fixity signatures do not appear similarly across these domains when the structural conditions are met, the framework's generalization claim fails.

## Lifecycle phases

Commitment systems are temporal entities. The configurations describe states systems are in at moments; the lifecycle describes how systems move through states over time. Five phases are worth distinguishing.

*Formation*: a kernel is being stabilized, an authority structure is being constituted, the commitment system is becoming distinct as an object. Drift is irrelevant because there is not yet a stable enough kernel to drift from.

*Stabilization*: the kernel and authority structure have been established. The system has working acknowledgment mechanisms but they have not yet been tested by significant environmental change. Drift exists but has not yet accumulated.

*Operation*: the system is functioning as designed. Drift accumulates and is processed through whatever mechanisms the configuration provides. This phase can persist indefinitely under stable environmental conditions and intact authority structures.

*Atrophy*: the system's acknowledgment mechanisms degrade through non-exercise, authority structure capture, or accumulated unrecognized drift. The system continues to operate but with declining match between formal kernel and operational practice. Recognition drift becomes substantial.

*Renewal-or-dissolution*: the system either reactivates its acknowledgment capacity (through external pressure, internal reform, dormant-container activation, or other mechanisms) or dissolves into different commitment systems.

The lifecycle dimension is the analyst's longer time horizon. It is not a property participants can typically observe from within. Participants experience their commitment system as continuous present; the lifecycle pattern becomes visible from positions with longer time horizons. The framework does not claim that participants should know which phase their system is in. The framework claims that the analyst, taking the longer view, can identify patterns that distinguish phases and predict which failure modes are likely in which phase.

There is a critical methodological constraint here. You cannot know where a system is in its lifecycle from inside it. The US constitutional system might be in late operation, atrophy, or pre-renewal — the determination depends on which phase ultimately obtains. The same applies to China after Xi, to AI development trajectories, to long-term marriages, to scientific paradigms. The framework's value is not deterministic prediction. It is shape recognition that aids probabilistic prediction. The framework identifies which trajectories are structurally available from any given phase and what conditions would shift probabilities toward each.

This is what "outcomes are a lattice and probabilities" means concretely. Each phase has a set of possible next phases. Renewal can lead back to operation or to a new stabilization with modified kernel. Atrophy can lead to renewal or to dissolution. The transitions are not predetermined; they depend on conditions the framework can identify. The framework's contribution is making the lattice explicit and the probability-shaping conditions specifiable, not closing the lattice down to deterministic prediction.

The configurations interact with the lifecycle in important ways. Marked revision is typically operational-phase configuration with high acknowledgment capacity. Interpretive accretion can be stable in operation phase but is also characteristic of late operation or early atrophy as kernel revision becomes increasingly costly. Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer is characteristic of late atrophy when kernel preservation becomes the system's primary operation. Diffuse reconstruction can be persistent across phases or characteristic of formation phases that never fully stabilize. Implicit practice is characteristic of long-operation systems where formal kernel has eroded but practice continues. These are tendencies, not necessities — the configurations can appear in various phases, but certain pairings are more common than others.

## Features

Five cross-cutting structural features modify base configurations. Each feature can combine with any configuration to produce specific failure modes. The features are not configurations themselves; they describe dimensions along which configurations can vary.

**Decoupled formalization.** A system has decoupled formalization when its formal kernel exists for purposes other than operational control — legibility, historical legitimacy, ceremonial completeness, external recognition — while operational authority sits elsewhere. Late Soviet constitutional law had decoupled formalization combined with operational anchored fixity in party structures. Many corporate governance documents have decoupled formalization combined with operational implicit practice in management cultures. The feature's characteristic failure mode is mismatch between symbolic and actual authority producing predictable breakdown when external pressure forces alignment between formal claims and operational reality. The feature is structurally consequential because it makes commitment systems harder to analyze — the visible kernel and the operative kernel are different.

**Ritualized renewal.** A system has ritualized renewal when it includes scheduled obligatory moments where the relationship between kernel and operational practice must be actively reconstituted. Four mechanism subtypes appear across domains. *Electoral renewal* replaces interpreters at scheduled intervals (parliamentary elections, term limits, periodic leadership selection); the kernel stays formally intact while operational meaning shifts because interpretation has changed hands. *Documentary renewal* rewrites the kernel itself at scheduled intervals (Quaker Faith and Practice generational revision, certain constitutional review processes); the new document is held continuous with the old but its content has changed. *Physical renewal* rebuilds material kernel through scheduled reconstruction (Ise Grand Shrine every twenty years, certain ritual rebuilding traditions); the new structure is held identical to the old through ritual identity. *Sunset renewal* requires specific provisions to be explicitly renewed to remain in force (legislative sunset clauses, regulatory review requirements); the provision lapses unless authority-structure action renews it. The feature's characteristic failure mode is the distinction between exercised renewal and performative renewal — a renewal mechanism can continue operating on schedule while becoming procedural rather than substantive. Elections that become procedural rather than substantive (uncontested, captured electorates), document revisions that become elite-managed locks on drift rather than acknowledgments of it, physical reconstructions where the rebuilding tradition has lost continuity, sunset clauses renewed reflexively without substantive review — all are performative-renewal failures. The framework predicts that performative renewal is more dangerous than failed renewal because it produces apparent acknowledgment without actual drift processing.

**Velocity mismatch.** A system has velocity mismatch when operational drift and authority drift operate at substantially different rates. The standard direction has operational practice outpacing authority structure: technology outrunning regulation, capability development outrunning safety frameworks, financial innovation outrunning compliance infrastructure, germline editing outrunning ethical-legal consensus. The inverted direction has authority outpacing operational practice: AI safety frameworks updating faster than the systems they govern, regulatory panic generating rules that anticipate capabilities not yet built, governance bodies revising standards faster than implementers can adapt. Both directions produce failure modes. The standard direction produces governance gaps where capability outruns rules. The inverted direction produces meta-misalignment where rules update so fast that operational systems cannot track them — systems optimize against moving goalposts, alignment targets become unstable, the very meaning of compliance shifts mid-optimization. The feature is particularly consequential in contemporary technical systems where capital, computational, and institutional pressures push authority structures and operational practices to different velocities.

**Distribution of acknowledgment authority.** A system has distributed acknowledgment authority when multiple parties have standing to acknowledge drift. The dimension along which this varies is concentration: some systems have concentrated authority (one party or small group can acknowledge), some have distributed authority (many parties can acknowledge), and the distribution can be hierarchical (cathedral-like with central acknowledgment) or peer-network (bazaar-like with distributed acknowledgment). Cathedral systems' characteristic failure mode is concentration of acknowledgment in positions that develop extraction stakes — the central acknowledger can no longer acknowledge what would threaten their position, and the system's acknowledgment capacity collapses to whatever the central position can tolerate. Bazaar systems' characteristic failure mode is coordination of acknowledgment — many parties can acknowledge but the system needs some convergence on what has been acknowledged, and coordination failure produces effective non-acknowledgment despite distributed acknowledgment capacity. Asymmetric acknowledgment, where some authority positions acknowledge while others refuse, is a specific case of distribution-of-acknowledgment-authority dynamics. In relational systems with two parties, asymmetric acknowledgment is the central failure mode. In larger distributed-authority systems, the asymmetry generalizes to factional disputes about what has been acknowledged.

**Kernel encoding substrate.** A system's kernel can be encoded in multiple ways with different implications for acknowledgment. Some kernels are encoded in ways that admit precise interpretation and revision (mathematical notation, formal law, software specifications). Others are encoded in ways that resist precise operations (cultural practice, embodied knowledge, narrative identity, religious experience, somatic responses). The encoding substrate determines what kinds of acknowledgment operations are operationally available. A kernel encoded as mathematical formula can be acknowledged through formal revision because the formula can be precisely modified. A kernel encoded as bodily response cannot be acknowledged through verbal revision because the encoding does not support that operation. Trauma-organized identity has an embodied kernel substrate that resists abstract interpretive acknowledgment; couples therapy that operates only at the verbal level often fails because the operative kernel is somatic rather than verbal. Caste and outcaste distinctions, digital versus analog representations, abstraction versus embodiment — these are all variations along the encoding substrate dimension. The feature's characteristic failure mode is mismatch between acknowledgment mechanism and encoding substrate: applying verbal acknowledgment to an embodied kernel, applying formal revision to a kernel held in tacit practice, applying explicit deliberation to a kernel encoded in collective ritual. Each mismatch produces apparent acknowledgment without actual drift processing.

## Default conditions

Two structural facts obtain in most actual cases rather than being features systems have or don't have. These are not features; they are default analytical situations the framework must handle.

**Nesting is default.** Almost every commitment system is nested in larger commitment systems and contains smaller commitment systems. The Catholic Church is nested in religious tradition is nested in cultural context is nested in geopolitical context. A marriage is nested in family is nested in social network is nested in legal-political context. An identity is nested in relationships is nested in community is nested in society. An AI safety framework is nested in lab governance is nested in industry self-regulation is nested in national regulatory context is nested in international coordination context. The single-level system is the limit case, not the typical case.

This has formal consequences. The framework's analytical machinery must handle multi-level systems where different levels operate with different configurations, different lifecycle phases, different features. The relationships between levels can be sheaves (where levels agree and glue into a globally consistent picture) or presheaves (where levels disagree in structurally generated ways). The presheaf paper's empirical finding that power dominates the sheaf/presheaf boundary while exit and scope cross thresholds within power-level blocks for 89% of manifest presheaves applies directly here: commitment systems with significant power asymmetries between levels are almost always presheaves rather than sheaves. The disagreement between levels is structural rather than residual.

The presheaf framing connects this framework directly to v6.11's apparatus. Observer positions form a category whose morphisms encode structural transitions; classification data forms a presheaf on this category; the sheaf gluing axiom is the formal criterion separating clean splits (where levels agree) from structured splits (where they disagree). The framework's claim that levels often disagree is the claim that commitment systems typically form presheaves rather than sheaves. The cover story analysis, the analyst-position privilege, the distributed-asymmetric acknowledgment feature, the lifecycle-from-longer-time-horizons observation — all of these are positional phenomena that the presheaf machinery formalizes.

**Coupling to other commitment systems is default.** Commitment systems are not isolated. Most systems are coupled to other systems whose dynamics affect their own. Government regulation lags innovation because regulatory commitment systems update slowly while innovation systems update fast. Financial systems precede regulatory systems because financial innovation outpaces regulatory acknowledgment. These are not contingent facts about particular systems; they are typical patterns produced by velocity mismatches between coupled commitment systems with different update rates. The framework's environmental coupling is partly coupling to physical environment but mostly coupling to other commitment systems. The framework's analytical machinery must handle these couplings as the typical case rather than as exceptions.

These two defaults change how the diagnostic order operates. The first question is no longer just "what kernel and authority structure?" but "at what level, given this is a nested system?" and "which other commitment systems is this one coupled to?" The diagnostic must specify the level of analysis explicitly because the same system has different answers at different levels.

## Parameters

The framework's positional analysis is parameterized through four variables that come from v6.11's observer position structure.

*Power* determines what consequences a position can produce. High power positions can act on the commitment system directly; low power positions can only experience its effects. Power is the dominant axis in v6.11's empirical work — it determines whether a commitment system is a presheaf at all.

*Exit options* determine what costs are involved in leaving the commitment system. Positions with high exit options (mobile, arbitrage) have fundamentally different relationships to drift than positions with low exit options (trapped, identity-locked). Exit is one of two axes that cross classification thresholds within power-level blocks for 89% of manifest presheaves.

*Time horizons* determine what temporal patterns a position can observe. Short-horizon positions (immediate, biographical) see different dynamics than long-horizon positions (generational, civilizational). The lifecycle dimension becomes visible from long-horizon positions and is largely invisible from short-horizon positions. This is the formal basis for the analyst-position privilege: analysts can take longer time horizons than participants, and the longer horizon reveals patterns participants cannot access from their positions.

*Spatial-temporal scope* determines what extent of the system a position sees. Local-scope positions see specific instances; global-scope positions see patterns across instances. The product of scope levels and time horizons produces the analytical reach available from any particular position.

These four parameters together specify what any observer position can see and do. The framework's positional analysis is parameterized through them; the cover story analysis predicts what each position's specific parameter values will make invisible.

## The diagnostic order

When examining an actual commitment system, the analysis proceeds in five steps. The order matters because earlier steps condition what later steps can resolve.

**Step 1: Specify the level of analysis and the level's kernel, authority structure, and coupling to other systems.** Because nesting is default, the analysis must specify which level it operates on. The same nested system has different answers at different levels — a marriage at the level of explicit terms has a different kernel from the marriage at the level of operational practice. Once the level is specified, the kernel and authority structure at that level become tractable. The coupling to other commitment systems at adjacent levels and at parallel systems must also be specified because failure modes often appear at the seams between coupled systems.

**Step 2: Identify which drift types are operative and whether each is acknowledged.** Different drift types require different responses. Environmental drift requires bandwidth that tracks environmental rate. Authority drift requires recognition that the interpretive structure itself is changing. Endogenous drift requires cover story analysis to see what the authority structure has an interest in not seeing. Recognition drift requires checking whether participants can see the drift that has occurred. Sabotage drift requires identifying actors who are exploiting the gap deliberately. The acknowledgment pattern across these drift types is diagnostic of the configuration and the authority structure's incentives.

**Step 3: Locate the configuration with relevant features and lifecycle phase.** The five base configurations and five features specify what kind of system this is. The lifecycle phase specifies where in its temporal trajectory it is, with appropriate analytical humility — phase identification is probabilistic and depends on conditions the framework can specify but not determine. Most actual systems will have one base configuration with several features and a tentative phase identification with explicit uncertainty.

**Step 4: Assess functionality given environmental rate and authority structure incentives.** Some configurations are inherently fragile in certain environments. Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer produces accumulating gap under significant environmental change. Marked revision is stable when bandwidth tracks environmental rate but fragile when environment accelerates beyond acknowledgment capacity. Interpretive accretion is stable in slow environments but produces fragility when environmental change exceeds the interpretive layer's absorption capacity. The functionality assessment is conditional on environment and incentive structure; it produces probability distributions over trajectories, not deterministic predictions.

**Step 5: Map standing and predict positional blindness.** Who has standing to acknowledge what drift, given the system's distribution of acknowledgment authority and the authority structure's incentives? Where the analysis predicts specific positions will be unable to see specific drift, the cover story analysis specifies what narrative will replace acknowledgment and what observation would break the cover story. The framework's positional predictions are most testable when they specify in advance what blindness should obtain and what evidence would disconfirm the prediction.

## Trajectory and cover story

Two analytical extensions handle dynamics over time and observer-position variation.

Trajectory analysis starts from a specified configuration at time T1 and describes what configurations are reachable at time T+n. Some transitions are likely; some are blocked. Anchored fixity without interpretive-accretion layer cannot transition smoothly to marked revision without first dismantling the authority structure that requires fixity, which means the transition usually requires authority-structure breakdown rather than authority-structure revision. Identity systems organized around anchored fixity often transition to identity crisis rather than to marked revision precisely because the authority structure cannot accommodate revision without dissolving itself.

Four resolution channels appear in the empirical work. *Absorption* is the standard channel: inner-system drift is accepted by outer-system authority through whatever mechanism the configuration provides. *Rejection* is the standard alternative: outer-system authority refuses inner-system changes, with inner systems either submitting or fragmenting. *Operational separation* is the channel where inner systems continue without outer-system participation, producing parallel commitment systems with overlapping membership. *Dormant-container activation* is the fourth channel, where a system that has previously been an outer container but has been operationally suspended — its kernel and authority structure preserved but not currently operating as the system's effective authority — can be reactivated as alternative legitimacy when the active outer container loses standing. France's Estates-General reactivation in 1789, the Meiji Restoration's reactivation of the Imperial Court, identity deconstruction patterns where pre-conversion identity reactivates, scientific paradigm shifts where pre-paradigm intuitions reactivate — all are dormant-container activation in different domains. The pattern is structurally identical: preserved-but-suspended outer kernel plus inner systems with sufficient independent operational capacity equals possible activation under stress.

Cover story analysis handles observer-position variation and the political dimension of authority structure. The framework's prediction is that positions are systematically unable to see drift they participate in producing when the seeing would threaten the authority structure that grants them their position. This is not a moral claim about institutional actors; it is a structural claim about how authority structures with stake in kernel preservation generate predictable blindness. The blindness is testable in specific cases: for a given configuration and position, the framework can predict what drift the position should be unable to see and what cover story will replace it.

The analyst-position privilege requires explicit acknowledgment. The framework's analysis is performed from positions with longer time horizons, broader scope, and different exit relationships than participant positions. This is constitutive of the analytical move rather than incidental to it. The presheaf framing makes this precise: the analyst occupies a position in the observer category whose morphisms to participant positions include the lifecycle and structural features participants cannot access. The framework does not claim the analyst sees truth while participants see illusion. It claims the analyst's position permits structural description that participant positions cannot produce, and that this is the same kind of positional difference the framework analyzes everywhere else. The framework is subject to its own positional analysis — including the analytical position from which the framework is produced.

## The framework as commitment system

The framework analyzes commitment systems. It is itself a commitment system, and treating it as one is part of the discipline the framework requires.

Its kernel is the structural claims developed in this and successor documents. Its authority structure consists of whoever adopts the framework and applies it — researchers, analysts, practitioners across the domains the framework engages. Its drift is real: the framework's own commitments change as it gets applied, criticized, and revised. Its acknowledgment capacity is whatever revision discipline its practitioners maintain.

The framework's own preference for marked revision over interpretive accretion is not a bias to be apologized for. The framework's purpose is making drift visible. Marked revision is what drift becoming visible consists of. The 50-pound weight gain accumulates through increments below the threshold of recognition; the analytical move that makes the gain visible is necessarily a move that prefers marked recognition over absorbed change. The framework that did not prefer marked over absorbed would be a framework that could not make the distinction it exists to make.

Critics may object that this smuggles in Enlightenment values. The framework's response is that valuing marked acknowledgment is what analyzing commitment systems requires. The framework is not neutral about visibility — it explicitly prefers acknowledged drift over absorbed drift because making drift visible is its purpose. The framework is neutral about which configurations are viable — the empirical investigation showed that interpretive accretion is adaptive across millennia, that implicit practice is stable under appropriate conditions, that anchored fixity with interpretive accretion can produce extreme longevity. The framework's analytical position is committed; the framework's configuration assessments are not.

What would falsify the framework. The framework rests on load-bearing claims that the kernel/authority/drift structure obtains across the domains specified, that the principles hold across all commitment systems, that the configurations describe real attractors rather than analytical artifacts, that the lifecycle phases identify real temporal patterns, that the features modify configurations as claimed, that the failure mode predictions transfer across domains because the structural mechanisms are the same, that the presheaf framing is the right formal machinery for positional analysis, and that the recursive self-application produces real constraint on the framework's development. If any of these load-bearing claims fail, the framework fails. The framework is bold rather than hedged because boldness is what makes these failure modes detectable. A framework that included sufficient qualifications to be immune to falsification would be immune to predictive bite.

## Three demonstrations

The framework's value test is whether it yields analytical moves that domain-specific framings don't naturally produce. Three demonstrations across different domains support the cross-domain generalization claim.

**Mathematics under proof-assistant pressure.** Mathematics is conventionally treated as the paragon of stable knowledge — the canonical example of a tradition that has functioned for thousands of years without crisis. The framework's prediction about marked-revision configurations supports this characterization. But the framework adds a calibration condition: stability is environment-relative. Mathematics' acknowledgment machinery is calibrated for a specific environment: proofs that can be checked by human readers within reasonable time, building on a literature small enough that experts can maintain working knowledge of their subfields. This environment has been changing. Computer-assisted proofs introduce results that no human can verify directly. The 1976 four-color theorem began the controversy; Hales' 1998 proof of the Kepler conjecture made it acute when journal referees spent four years unable to verify the computer code, and the Annals of Mathematics ultimately published an abridged version while explicitly refusing to vouch for the computational part. The Coq formalization in 2005 settled the case but established that human verification was no longer sufficient for an emerging class of results. Subfields proliferate faster than experts can track them; Kevin Buzzard has argued in public talks that no single person understands the complete proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, and that the mathematical literature may contain accepted results that are in fact incorrect — a claim he raises not as scandal but as motivation for formalization. Lean and other proof assistants are now being adopted not as supplements to human verification but as the only feasible way to verify some contemporary work; Peter Scholze's perfectoid spaces work was verified through the Liquid Tensor Experiment. The framework's diagnosis: mathematics' acknowledgment machinery is being stressed by an environment that now produces drift faster than human-paced verification can track. The proof assistant movement is bandwidth extension in response to environmental acceleration. The conventional framing — mathematics as paragon of stability — obscures what they are doing. The framework names it precisely: environmental drift exceeded human-bandwidth acknowledgment capacity, the system is in late operation phase moving toward either renewed stabilization with extended bandwidth or atrophy through unprocessed drift, and the proof assistant work is the renewal mechanism.

**AI development under inverted velocity mismatch.** Contemporary AI development operates in an unusual configuration where the framework's standard failure mode analyses need adjustment. The kernel includes training objectives, evaluation criteria, and safety specifications. The authority structure includes labs, deployment institutions, regulators, and the artifacts themselves. Drift includes distribution shift, reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, and emergent behaviors. The standard configuration has practice drift outpacing authority drift — capability outruns rules. AI development shows both directions simultaneously: capabilities outrun governance (standard velocity mismatch) while safety frameworks and evaluation criteria update faster than the systems they govern (inverted velocity mismatch). The dual-direction velocity mismatch produces specific failure modes the framework predicts. *Meta-misalignment between successive authority states*: a system optimized against safety framework version N may be deployed under safety framework version N+1 with different commitments, producing systems that satisfy neither framework. *Moving-goalposts optimization*: capability development optimizes against evaluation criteria that update during optimization, producing systems whose alignment status is undefined because the targets shifted mid-process. *Diffuse reconstruction of "alignment"*: different labs, regulators, and academic communities operate with incompatible operational meanings of alignment, with each claiming kernel fidelity while producing systems that satisfy only their own interpretation. The framework's prediction: AI development is in late stabilization or early operation phase with both velocity mismatches active, and the failure modes will appear most prominently at the seams between coupled commitment systems (lab safety frameworks and regulatory bodies, training-time alignment and deployment-time evaluation, technical safety and policy governance). The prediction is risky and testable. If AI development proceeds without meta-misalignment failures, without moving-goalposts pathologies, without diffuse reconstruction problems, the framework's analysis of this case is wrong.

**Ritualized renewal across substrates.** Ritualized renewal is a structural feature that operates across multiple base configurations and substrate types. The framework's contribution is identifying that systems with this feature handle drift differently from systems without it, with predictable failure modes when the renewal mechanism becomes performative rather than exercised. Three cases at different substrates demonstrate the feature's reach. *Quaker Faith and Practice* operates documentary renewal: every generation the book is rewritten by the yearly meeting, with the new document treated as continuous with the old while incorporating contemporary understanding. The configuration is interpretive accretion with documentary renewal feature; the renewal mechanism turns interpretive accretion into acknowledgment-through-different-mode rather than failure-to-acknowledge. The Quaker tradition's stability across centuries depends on the renewal mechanism continuing to be exercised substantively. *Ise Grand Shrine* operates physical renewal: every twenty years the structure is rebuilt according to traditional methods, with the new building held identical to the old through ritual identity. The configuration is implicit practice with physical renewal feature; the renewal mechanism preserves form against material decay while requiring active transmission of crafting knowledge across generations. The tradition's continuity has been threatened multiple times when the rebuilding tradition itself faced disruption. *Parliamentary time limits* operate electoral renewal: fixed-term elections or term limits replace interpreters at scheduled intervals, with the constitutional kernel staying formally intact while operational meaning shifts because interpretation has changed hands. The configuration varies (interpretive accretion in common-law systems, marked revision in some others) but the renewal feature is the same. The failure mode is performative renewal: elections become procedural rather than substantive when uncontested or captured, term limits become rotations within elite networks rather than genuine interpretive change, the renewal mechanism continues operating on schedule while ceasing to process drift. The framework predicts that systems with ritualized renewal mechanisms can fail in two ways — either the mechanism stops operating or it continues operating performatively — and that the second failure mode is more dangerous because it produces apparent acknowledgment without actual drift processing. The Le Burnett "When to take my name off the door" speech is a related move at organizational scale: the kernel includes explicit conditions under which the founder's name should be removed, with the renewal mechanism being the conditional removal rather than scheduled renewal. The configuration design carries its own dissolution conditions; the failure mode is when the conditions are met but the removal does not occur because authority structures have developed incentives in retaining the name regardless.

Each demonstration shows the framework producing analytical moves that domain-specific framings don't naturally produce. The mathematics case shows environment-conditional stability of a marked-revision configuration under environmental acceleration. The AI case shows specific failure modes from dual-direction velocity mismatch in an inverted-and-standard configuration. The ritualized renewal case shows the framework identifying a structural feature with multiple substrate-specific subtypes and predicting characteristic failure modes for each. Together they support the cross-domain generalization claim.

## Why this matters

The standard debates about cultural change collapse into moralized disputes — tradition versus progress, fidelity versus innovation, original meaning versus living interpretation. These debates persist because they cannot be settled in their own terms. Whichever side wins one round, the other side returns with the same arguments restated.

The commitment systems framework reframes these debates as questions about architecture rather than values. The question is not whether drift is good or bad but whether the system's authority structure can acknowledge the drift its domain actually produces, given the rate at which that domain changes, the incentives the authority structure has, the configuration the system operates in, the features modifying that configuration, the lifecycle phase the system is in, and the parameters of the positions doing the analysis. Systems whose acknowledgment capacity matches their environment's drift rate adapt without crisis. Systems whose acknowledgment capacity falls behind accumulate gap until something breaks. The framework's contribution is making this match-or-mismatch structural rather than political.

This reframe has practical consequences across domains. It identifies which commitment systems are stable and which are unstable as structural rather than political questions. It distinguishes legitimate reform from rationalized continuity by asking whether reform changes the authority structure's incentives or just relabels the kernel. It locates the source of recurring crises in architecture rather than personalities. And it provides a vocabulary for analyzing systems across domains that previously had no shared analytical framework.

The framework does not resolve the substantive questions any particular commitment system faces. The US Constitution's adaptation pathway is a political problem requiring political work. The Catholic Church's relationship to doctrinal change is a theological problem requiring theological work. A specific marriage's relationship to drift is a relational problem requiring relational work. The framework provides scaffolding for thinking about these problems clearly. The work of doing something about it remains where it always was.

## What this enables

This sketch establishes the theoretical foundation for a research program. The framework's claims are bold and load-bearing; their value is in driving subsequent operational work rather than in being finished theory.

The next phase of work is operationalization. Acknowledgment capacity needs formal measurement primitives — candidate indicators include bandwidth (acknowledgment events per unit time), latency (delay between drift and acknowledgment), resolution (granularity of distinctions the structure can register), cost (social/cognitive/institutional cost of acknowledgment), and authority coupling (whether acknowledgment requires consensus, elite approval, or individual action). Each of these is a separate engine extension. Drift decomposition needs corpus testing in domains beyond organizational containers. The five configurations need empirical verification in identity, relational, scientific, and AI domains. The cover story analysis needs application to specific cases with predefined positional blindness predictions. The lifecycle dimension needs case-study work distinguishing trajectories within actual systems and identifying the probability-shaping conditions the framework predicts. The features need operational distinguishing criteria across substrates. The presheaf framing needs concrete application to multi-level systems showing how the formal machinery from v6.11 extends to commitment systems beyond the original constraint corpus.

The framework's contribution is to specify what this operational work should target. The kernel/authority/drift structure tells the engine what objects to represent. The five configurations and five features tell it what attractors and modifying dimensions to look for. The lifecycle phases tell it what temporal dynamics to track. The diagnostic order tells it what queries to support. The trajectory, cover story, and positional analyses tell it what dynamic and observational patterns to track. The presheaf framing tells it how to handle multi-level systems. The framework-as-commitment-system recursion tells it that the engine's outputs are themselves subject to drift and require their own acknowledgment discipline.

What the framework predicts, taken together, is that commitment systems are a distinct analytical object that has been hiding in plain sight across multiple disciplines, that the same machinery analyzes them across domains, that the failure modes are predictable from structural configuration plus environmental rate plus authority structure incentives plus features plus lifecycle phase plus positional parameters, and that the operational work of making this analytically tractable is the next several years of the research program. The sketch is bold rather than hedged because boldness is what makes the predictions worth testing. The framework either does this work or it doesn't; the next phase finds out.
