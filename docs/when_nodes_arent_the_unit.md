# When Nodes Aren't the Unit: Network-Level Cover Stories and the Limits of Single-Constraint Detection

### The Cover-Story Mechanism at Scale

#### Abstract

The within-constraint cover story — structural extraction concealed at the type-classification surface by the power-modulated observer apparatus — is Theorem 1's local case. A constraint can have chi-level extraction that the institutional observer cannot classify as extraction, producing a 62.1 percentage-point gap between the chi layer and the type-classification surface. The within-constraint version of this mechanism is fully characterized, formally compelled, and detected by the FCR rule with 98.8% overlap against H¹ > 0.

This paper asks whether the cover story mechanism has a network-level analog: a configuration of individually classifiable constraints that collectively produce type-surface consensus concealing chi-level flow asymmetry that no single-constraint diagnostic can detect. A pre-writing audit established three empirical facts not previously extracted from the pipeline: (1) gauge-variant constraints are distributed uniformly across the coupling graph rather than clustering — gauge-variance is local, not network-structural; (2) H¹ > 0 and gauge-variance are the same 879-constraint set by definition, clarifying an apparent analytical split as a single phenomenon measured from two angles; (3) the top 19 super-spreaders in the coupling network form a near-clique with 80.1% internal edge density, are all FCR-detected, are all maximally gauge-variant, and all carry the `false_ci_rope` (coordination-washing) signature.

What this paper adds: it develops the network-level two-layer structure by analogy with the within-constraint case, names the precise formalism gap that prevents completing the extension, identifies the super-spreader near-clique as the empirical candidate for a network-level cover story while specifying what additional measurement would be required to confirm it, and derives six omega variables that distinguish genuinely open questions from computable unknowns.

---

#### 1. The Problem

The within-constraint cover story has a clean structure. Two layers: the chi layer, continuous, per constraint × observer pair; and the type layer, discrete, post-override, post-structural-signature. The cover story leaves an empirical trace in the gap between them — 62.1 percentage points for tangled_rope constraints, with parallel gaps for rope and snare. The mechanism is structurally compelled: Theorem 1 establishes that under power-modulated perception, extraction cannot be universally recognized as extraction, so the institutional observer's negative-χ regime is forced to reclassify extraction as coordination. The FCR rule detects this. Nash distance confirms it is structurally locked rather than contingent.

What the within-constraint framing cannot see is the case where the cover story operates across constraints rather than within one. Consider the emotional disclosure proof-of-concept from §5.8 of the main paper. Three constraints in the same domain:

- `gendered_disclosure_asymmetry`: ε = 0.18, intrinsic purity 0.96, effective purity 0.72 (propagation delta −0.240), H¹ = 0, signature `false_ci_rope`. The institutional observer classifies this as coordination across all contexts.
- `container_building_threshold`: ε = 0.58, H¹ = 6, signature `false_ci_rope`. The analytical observer classifies this as snare.
- `extractive_disclosure_calibration`: ε = 0.58, H¹ = 5, signature `false_ci_rope`. The analytical observer classifies this as snare.

The network structure: `gendered_disclosure_asymmetry`'s only two neighbors are `container_building_threshold` and `extractive_disclosure_calibration`. The three constraints form a minimal network unit. The high-extraction pair's contamination degrades the low-extraction member's effective purity from 0.96 to 0.72.

From the institutional observer's position, all three constraints are coordination or rope — the low-extraction member legitimates the high-extraction pair by presenting an apparently benign face to the institutional view. The analytical observer detects extraction at the two high-extraction members. But notice what the single-constraint diagnostics find: `gendered_disclosure_asymmetry` has H¹ = 0 — no perspectival fracture. It passes single-constraint cover-story detection. The cover story at this node is not self-generated; it is network-position-generated. The constraint functions as a coordination-washed entry point not because of its own chi-vs-type gap but because of its structural position adjacent to constraints that are individually and severally extractive.

This is the minimal case. The within-constraint cover story mechanism is the local form; this three-constraint pattern is the simplest non-trivial network form. The question this paper pursues is whether the network form is (a) merely a collection of individual cover stories that happen to be adjacent, or (b) a qualitatively distinct phenomenon with its own formal structure and its own measurement requirements. If (a), the existing apparatus is sufficient. If (b), it is not.

#### 2. What the Apparatus Already Knows

The coupling graph has 31,215 edges across 3,254 constraints (at the time of the network computation; the corpus has since grown to 3,334, with 32,115 edges at the larger snapshot). The graph forms 127–156 connected components at coupling threshold 0.500. **Terminological clarification:** the "giant component" figures reported in the apparatus's output — 8,650 nodes (main paper) and 8,736 nodes (giant component analysis report) — are not component membership counts; they are 3-hop reachability sets. The actual largest connected component contains approximately 3,026 constraints (from the type-composition breakdown in the giant component analysis). The reachability figures describe contamination spread, not component structure. The paper uses "giant component" to mean the largest connected component (~3,026 nodes) and "3-hop reachability" for the reachability-set figures.

What the apparatus currently measures at the network level, and what it does not:

**Purity propagation (measured).** Effective purity degrades from neighbors via contamination, with attenuation 0.50 per hop. The FPN (fixed-point network) iteration converges via Knaster-Tarski in 18 iterations with 1,568 zone migrations. 2,702 of 3,335 constraints show nonzero propagation delta. Type-selective immunity is confirmed: mountains are insulated (zero average EP shift); snare and tangled_rope shift ~0.14 EP on average. The three "mountain" constraints that cross purity zones in the output are all `false_summit_mountain` (H¹ = 4) — already flagged as extractive by the signature cascade. True mountains do not cross purity zones.

**Super-spreader structure (measured).** The top 20 super-spreaders are all snares or tangled_ropes with contamination strength 1.00. Degrees range from 108 to 211. Three-hop reach ranges from approximately 2,700 to 3,756 nodes — meaning a single super-spreader's contamination influence reaches 80–90% of the 3-hop reachability set.

**Gauge-variant constraint distribution (newly computed).** The 879 gauge-variant constraints are distributed uniformly across the coupling graph: the mean fraction of gauge-variant constraints among a gauge-variant constraint's neighbors is 0.246, against a corpus base rate of 0.264 (enrichment 0.93x — slightly below random, not above). Gauge-variance is a local, per-constraint structural property, not a network clustering property. This settles the audit's Finding 19 item 1.

**H¹ and gauge-variance are the same population (newly clarified).** H¹ > 0 and gauge-variant are the same 879 constraints (100% overlap, definitionally). The orbit analysis and the cohomological obstruction measure the same structural phenomenon from two angles: gauge-orbit analysis asks "does the type label change across observer contexts?"; H¹ asks "does the co-boundary condition fail on the observer site?" Both detect the same classification-surface variation across observers. The terms can be used interchangeably when precision is not required; the distinction matters only when comparing the graded structure (H¹ ∈ {3, 4, 5, 6} gives resolution; gauge-orbit gives the specific type set) against the binary (variant/invariant; positive/zero).

**The super-spreader near-clique (newly computed, the central empirical finding).** The top 19 super-spreaders (all from the digital platform and algorithmic domain — `commercial_fishery_quota_systems` is the structural outlier, with zero connections to the other 19) form a near-complete clique: 137 intra-cluster edges out of 171 possible (density 0.801). These 19 constraints are not just individually high-degree; they are densely mutually connected. Properties of every member: H¹ ≥ 3, gauge-variant orbit spanning at least [rope, snare] and typically [naturalized, rope, snare, tangled_rope], signature `false_ci_rope`, effective purity in the Degraded zone (0.118–0.312). Three-hop reach from any single member reaches approximately 3,000–3,756 constraints.

**What the contamination model does not track.** The contamination model propagates purity (a scalar) through network edges. It does not propagate the cover story signal — the chi-vs-type gap — across edges. Whether the institutional observer's classification of a contaminated constraint changes, or whether the chi-level extraction detected at the analytical position is elevated by network position, is not measured. Purity shift and H¹ status are independent (H¹ rate: 29.5% among shifted constraints, 13.0% among unshifted — correlated but not causally linked; both reflect that extractive constraints are more likely to participate in contamination networks than pure ones). The purity contamination channel and the cover story channel are distinct, running in parallel, not in series.

#### 3. The Network Analog

The within-constraint two-layer structure has a precise architecture: chi layer (continuous, pre-classification) and type layer (discrete, post-override). The cover story operates in the gap. Detection requires comparing both layers for the same constraint.

At the network level, what plays these roles?

**The network chi analog.** Within a single constraint, χ = ε × f(d(P)) × σ(S(P)) is the continuous extraction signal per observer position. The network analog should capture the aggregate extraction signal across a linked set of constraints as experienced by a given observer. The straightforward candidate — the mean of individual chi values across a linked set — is too coarse: it collapses the asymmetric, observer-dependent structure that makes chi informative. What matters is not the average chi but whether the chi *vector across constraints in the set* shows the same structural asymmetry as the within-constraint chi vector across observer positions: high at the powerless position, low (or negative) at the institutional position.

A more faithful network analog would be: for a connected subgraph G of constraints and an observer position P, the *network chi profile* is the vector of individual chi values {χ(C, P)} for all C ∈ G. The network-level cover story signal is then the gap between the powerless and institutional network chi profiles — analogous to the institutional sign-flip at the single-constraint level, but computed across a set.

This definition exists but is not computed. It requires no new Prolog modules — chi values are in the pipeline output for all constraints — but it has not been extracted into a network-level diagnostic. This is the **data layer gap** from the audit: the information exists, the cross-reference has not been made.

**The network type surface analog.** Within a single constraint, the type layer is the post-override classification at each observer position. The network type surface analog is the institutional observer's classification pattern across the linked set: does the institutional observer see the full set as coordination, or does the institutional observer detect extraction at some members while the analytical observer detects it at more? The evaluative convergence module's `detect_convergent_institutional` pattern is the closest existing instrument: it fires when the institutional observer's classification contradicts the analytical observer's across a group of structurally related constraints. This is a detection rule, not a measurement layer, but it is structurally analogous to the type-layer comparison.

**The Kahan bridge.** The apparatus's §5.7 introduces the most productive framing for the network-level type surface: Kahan's Cultural Cognition framework maps the individualism-communitarianism axis to contamination network sensitivity. Communitarian observers weight collective effects and are predicted to be more sensitive to coupling between neighboring constraints — their classification is more sensitive to effective purity than intrinsic purity. Individualist observers classify constraints in isolation — intrinsic purity dominates. This mapping is STRUCTURAL (a dimensional correspondence, not empirically calibrated), but it names what the network-level type surface *means* in observer-psychology terms: the institutional observer functioning as an individualist classifier — evaluating each constraint in isolation, seeing coordination at each node — while the systematic communitarian observer sees the coupling structure and detects aggregate extraction. The network-level cover story is, from this angle, the pattern that would be invisible to individualist classification while visible to communitarian classification. Whether this framing is predictive is an open empirical question (Ω₃ below), but it gives the network-type-surface concept interpretive content beyond "the contamination output."

**The network chi-vs-type gap, as yet unmeasured.** If the network chi profile and the network type surface can both be computed, the network chi-vs-type gap is the difference between what the chi layer says about the linked set and what the type layer says. For the super-spreader near-clique: the chi layer at the analytical position would show extraction across all 19 members; the type layer at the institutional position shows coordination or naturalization at most or all members. The *size* of this gap for the clique — compared against the gap for a random same-size set of individually FCR-detected constraints — would tell you whether the clique's network structure is producing a larger cover story than the sum of its individual parts. This gap is not computed.

#### 4. The Formal Criterion

The within-constraint cover story has a formal criterion that distinguishes it from mere classification boundary proximity:

1. **Structural compulsion** (Nash-forced): the institutional observer's reclassification of extraction as coordination is not contingent but derives from the axioms. Disabling FCR changes only 38 of 810 orbits, and those maintain H¹ > 0. The cover story is structurally forced, not incidentally produced.
2. **Chi-vs-type gap**: the gap between chi-layer disagreement and type-layer consensus is quantifiable (62.1 pp for tangled_rope) and negatively associated with parametric fragility — the two failure modes are structurally independent and mutually exclusive in their detection range.

The network-level cover story needs an analog to both conditions.

**The structural compulsion analog** is the question of whether the super-spreader near-clique's joint institutional classification as coordination is forced by the coupling structure or is merely the aggregate of 19 individually forced cover stories that happen to be adjacent. Within-constraint compulsion is shown by FCR ablation: removing FCR changes few orbits, confirming that the cover story is forced rather than manufactured. The analogous network ablation would be: remove the intra-cluster edges and check whether the institutional classification pattern of each member changes. If the institutional cover story at each member is determined only by that member's own chi values (individual forcing), removing intra-cluster edges should not change any individual classification. If the cover story is network-structural (the clique produces a mutual reinforcement that is not reducible to individual forcing), removing intra-cluster edges would change the classification pattern.

This ablation is in principle computable — it would require running the classification engine with edges removed and checking for classification changes. It has not been run. It is the gate condition for the formal criterion: only if classification changes under edge ablation is the network-level cover story formally distinguishable from the sum of individual ones. **This is a data-and-architecture problem, not a formalism problem.** The computation is defined; it requires running the classification pipeline in a modified configuration.

**The chi-vs-type gap analog** at the network level requires computing the network chi profile and the network type surface for the clique, then measuring the gap. The within-constraint version of this is directly measured from pipeline output. The network version requires (a) defining the aggregation function for network chi profiles, (b) computing it for the 19-node clique and for a baseline set of comparable size, and (c) checking whether the gap for the clique exceeds what would be expected from 19 independently FCR-detected constraints.

**The formalism problem that remains after these computations.** Even if both computations were run and showed that the clique's cover story is network-structural rather than merely additive, a deeper formalism question would remain: what is the mathematical structure of a network-level cover story? The within-constraint version has a sheaf-theoretic characterization: H¹ > 0 means the local sections (per-observer classifications) fail to compose into a global section. The network-level analog would require a presheaf on the coupling graph rather than the observer site — where "sections" are per-constraint chi/type pairs and "global section" means network-level consensus without extraction. Whether such a presheaf is well-defined, whether its cohomology has the right properties, and what the "H¹ gap" would look like on the coupling graph (which is sparse and large, not 4-point and total-ordered) are open questions (Ω₃).

There is a specific architectural gate condition: the Knaster-Tarski monotonicity requirement. The FPN iteration that propagates purity through the coupling graph converges because purity is a scalar on a complete lattice and the propagation function is monotone (purity decreases; it does not increase). The chi-vs-type gap is not a scalar but a pair (chi profile, type assignment) per constraint × observer. Propagating this pair through network edges would require a monotone function on a product lattice. Whether such a function exists with the right structural properties — whether contamination at one node can coherently "propagate" the cover story signal to a neighbor in the formal sense — is the architectural gate condition. If the propagation function is not monotone, the Knaster-Tarski convergence guarantee does not apply, and the FPN approach cannot extend to cover story signal propagation. This question is currently open (Ω₄).

#### 5. Empirical Signatures

What would a network-level cover story look like in the corpus, and how would you tell it from a cluster of individually extractive constraints?

**The super-spreader near-clique as candidate.** The 19-node digital platform cluster — `algorithmic_feed_substitution` (degree 211), `platform_algorithmic_curation` (183), `facebook_content_moderation_opacity` (135), and 16 others — has the properties that a network-level cover story would be expected to have:

- All 19 members are individually FCR-detected (H¹ ≥ 3, `false_ci_rope` signature).
- All 19 are maximally gauge-variant (orbits spanning [naturalized, rope, snare, tangled_rope] or major subsets), meaning the institutional observer classifies them as rope/naturalized while the analytical observer classifies them as snare.
- Internal edge density is 0.801 — the 19 nodes are nearly fully connected to each other.
- Three-hop reach from any member reaches approximately 3,000–3,756 constraints (80–90% of the giant component's 3-hop reachability set).
- Effective purity of all members is in the Degraded zone, self-contaminated by their own coupling.
- The domain is coherent: all 19 address digital platform algorithmic control over information, attention, and labor.

The institutional classification from within this cluster is coherent: each member presents as coordination or algorithmic neutrality from the institutional position. The analytical classification is also coherent: each member is a snare extracting from users and workers in favor of platform operators. The discrepancy between the two classifications is not an accident of individual constraints but is structurally reproduced across all 19 members through the same cover story mechanism (`false_ci_rope`).

**What these signatures are consistent with, and what they do not establish.** These properties are consistent with a network-level cover story — a configuration where the institutional consensus across the clique is structurally reinforced by coupling, not merely the aggregate of 19 individual cover stories. But the same properties are also consistent with the simpler explanation: 19 individually FCR-detected constraints in the same domain, linked because they share topical structure, each individually cover-story-active, with no additional network-level coordination mechanism. The corpus does not currently contain the measurement that would distinguish these two explanations.

**The purity contamination signature provides partial evidence.** Purity shift and H¹ status are independent (cross-reference 3). The 19 super-spreaders have effective purity in the Degraded zone, but this reflects their own low intrinsic purity (all below 0.40), not primarily external contamination. They are contamination *sources*, not contamination *targets*. The purity channel and the cover story channel run in parallel; the purity evidence neither confirms nor disconfirms the network-level cover story hypothesis.

**The gauge-variant non-clustering result provides negative evidence.** Because gauge-variant constraints are distributed uniformly in the network (enrichment 0.93x), there is no general tendency for gauge-variant constraints to cluster. The super-spreader clique's gauge-variant concentration (3.79x enrichment vs. base rate) is not a network-structural property of gauge-variance; it is a property of the super-spreader sub-population. High degree + high contamination strength is not randomly distributed across gauge-variant and gauge-invariant constraints: it concentrates in the gauge-variant population. Why this is true — whether high-degree extractive constraints are structurally forced to be gauge-variant, or whether the corpus generates this concentration as an artifact of how algorithmic platform constraints are built — is an open question (Ω₅).

**Additional measurement that would distinguish the two explanations:**

1. *Edge-ablation classification test:* Run the classification engine with intra-clique edges removed. If any of the 19 members' H¹ values, chi profiles, or type assignments change without their internal edges, the classification is network-sensitive. If none change, the individual forcing is sufficient and the network structure is epiphenomenal.

2. *Network chi-vs-type gap computation:* Compute the network chi profile (per-member chi values) for the 19-node clique at each observer position. Compare the institutional profile (all rope/naturalized) against the analytical profile (all snare). The network-level gap is the discrepancy between these profiles summed across members. Compare this gap against the gap for a baseline of 19 randomly selected FCR-detected constraints not in the clique.

3. *Evaluative convergence audit:* Check whether the evaluative convergence module's `detect_convergent_institutional` and `cover_story_topology` patterns both fire for the 19-node clique when treated as a group. The module exists; it has not been run on this specific group.

#### 6. Limits and Open Questions

**The adversarial network cover story.** The within-constraint version of this problem is noted in *When Consensus Isn't Coherence*: an adversary aware of the chi-vs-type diagnostic could construct constraints that minimize the chi-vs-type gap while still concealing structural inconsistency. At the network level, the adversarial construction is harder to characterize and easier to execute: a set of individually clean constraints (H¹ = 0, no FCR detection) that are arranged in a network configuration such that their joint institutional classification conceals aggregate extraction. No single-constraint diagnostic fires. The evaluative convergence module might detect convergent institutional classification across a group — but only if the group shares a structural signature or network adjacency, and only if the detection criterion for cover story topology includes this case. The current five-condition criterion for cover story topology (low-extraction member linked to high-extraction members with purity degradation ≥ 0.15) would not fire if all members are low-extraction individually. The adversarial network cover story is the class of configurations the apparatus does not have a detection criterion for. It is an open question whether such configurations exist in the current corpus (Ω₆).

**Six omega variables.** The following questions are not resolvable from the available sources and are named as omega variables rather than deferred as analysis:

- **Ω₁ (edge-ablation result):** Does removing intra-clique edges change the classification of any super-spreader? This distinguishes individual from network-level forcing. Computable from existing pipeline with configuration modification.
- **Ω₂ (network chi-vs-type gap):** Is the chi-vs-type gap for the 19-node clique greater than the gap for a comparably-sized baseline of individually FCR-detected non-clique constraints? Computable from existing pipeline output.
- **Ω₃ (sheaf structure on coupling graph):** Does a presheaf on the coupling graph with the right properties exist, and if so, what does its cohomology look like? This is a formalism question; the answer depends on choices about what "restriction maps" mean in this context.
- **Ω₄ (Knaster-Tarski gate condition):** Does a monotone cover story signal propagation function exist on a product lattice of (chi profile, type assignment) pairs? This is the architectural gate condition for extending the FPN approach to cover story propagation.
- **Ω₅ (high-degree / gauge-variant correlation):** Is the 3.79x gauge-variant enrichment among super-spreaders a structural property (high-extraction high-degree constraints must be gauge-variant because their chi structure forces it) or a corpus artifact (algorithmic platform constraints are built in a way that produces this pattern)? Answerable by domain-stratified analysis of the correlation.
- **Ω₆ (adversarial network cover story):** Do configurations of individually clean (H¹ = 0) constraints that jointly produce institutional consensus concealing aggregate extraction exist in the corpus? Not currently computable without a new detection criterion.

**What this paper does not do.** It does not establish that the super-spreader near-clique is a network-level cover story in the formal sense. It establishes that it has the properties such a cover story would have, and that the measurements required to confirm or disconfirm the hypothesis are defined (Ω₁, Ω₂) or currently blocked by a formalism gap (Ω₃, Ω₄). It does not extend the trifurcation taxonomy to name a "Type D" or "network-level Type B." Network contamination is already characterized in the trifurcation mapping audit as outside the trifurcation's taxonomy — the trifurcation operates on individual reasoning episodes, and network-level phenomena are a genuine extension. This paper accepts that characterization and asks whether the cover story mechanism, which is within the trifurcation's Type B taxonomy at the individual level, has a network-level analog that is also outside it.

#### 7. Conclusion

**What the paper adds.** The apparatus has a within-constraint cover story mechanism: formally characterized (Theorem 1), empirically measured (62.1 pp chi-vs-type gap), structurally compelled (98.8% FCR/H¹ overlap), formally stable (Nash-forced). This paper adds the network-level structure: the three-constraint minimal case (emotional disclosure domain, §5.8 proof-of-concept), the empirical candidate (19-node super-spreader near-clique with 80.1% internal edge density), the three-layer gap characterization (data layer computable, architecture layer blocked by monotonicity question, formalism layer blocked by coupling-graph presheaf definition), and six omega variables that separate computable unknowns from genuine open questions.

**What the network-level finding means for the apparatus's validation claim.** The apparatus's honest position distinguishes: detection where structure permits it, prevention where specification permits it, governance where neither does. The within-constraint cover story mechanism sits under detection: the apparatus detects FCR-forced cover stories and Nash-stable disagreements. Network contamination propagation also sits under detection: the apparatus detects purity degradation and zone migrations.

Whether network-level cover stories — configurations that produce institutional consensus across a linked set without any single member triggering FCR — sit under detection or governance is not yet determinable. If Ω₁ shows that classification is network-sensitive (edge ablation changes H¹ values), the network-level cover story falls under the detection category — it is a new detection target that requires extending the pipeline. If Ω₁ shows classification is network-insensitive (individual forcing is sufficient), the network-level cover story may be a governance problem: the structural configuration is real (19 individually extractive constraints that jointly present as coordination), but there is no single-constraint diagnostic that flags the configuration, and the apparatus's detection layer captures each member individually without capturing the collective pattern.

A detection problem has a technical response: build a network-level diagnostic. A governance problem has a different response: the apparatus has found the structure and characterized its properties, but the response to it — what evaluative positions it renders indefensible, whose authority to name what it demonstrates — is outside the apparatus's reach. The apparatus's evaluative convergence module moves in this direction: it produces "defensibility assessments" for detected cross-constraint topologies. Whether that is detection or governance extended is a question for the synthesis paper, not this one.

**The series context.** *When Splitting Isn't Solving* showed that Type C resolution can produce structured rather than clean splits. *When Consensus Isn't Coherence* showed that Type B detection requires vertical redundancy (chi and type layers) not just horizontal redundancy (multiple axioms). *When Frame Isn't Foreground* showed that Type A frame drift in the apparatus is less developed than the trifurcation would predict. This paper shows that the trifurcation's Type B detection framework, extended to the network level, encounters a case where the unit of analysis is not the constraint but the constraint set — and that the apparatus has the structure to see this case (the super-spreader near-clique, the contamination network, the gauge-variant population), but not yet the measurement tools to formally characterize it. The gap between having the structure and having the measurement is, in the apparatus's terms, the difference between a computable unknown and a genuine omega variable. Four of the six omega variables are computable unknowns dressed up as open questions. Running them is the next step.
