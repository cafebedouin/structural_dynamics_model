# The Mountain and the Frame

**All discrimination is seated; what survives seat-variation is not chosen. Physics is the hardest case — and the demonstration, not the exception.**

**Version: v1.0**

---

**The declared seat.** This synthesis is issued from the position that the *realist half* of the framework is load-bearing and must be protected from the framework's own solvent: that "every contentful verdict is seated" is true and must not be allowed to slide into "every structure is chosen." That protection is itself a seat. It cannot compel a radical conventionalist who holds that there are no frame-stable structures at all — that enough perturbation of the framing dissolves even the mountains. Against that position this document does not prove its conclusion; it stakes a falsification condition (below) and shows the condition has been met in the one domain where the conventionalist's case is strongest. The seal is real but local: it binds anyone who grants that the world can decline a demand we make of it.

---

## The frame

In 1964 Cronin and Fitch watched a long-lived neutral kaon decay into two pions — a decay that exact CP symmetry forbids. Physicists had every reason to want CP exact: it was elegant, it was simple, it was the kind of symmetry a beautiful theory keeps. The world declined. That single experiment is the whole of this document compressed into an event, and the rest is an unpacking of why it matters.

Here is the situation it sits inside. Physics looks like the closest thing we have to reasoning from nowhere: choose a coordinate frame, choose an apparatus, choose a formalism, and everyone still agrees on the predictions. A careful friend, reconciling this with the claim that all verdicts are seated, draws what seems the natural conclusion — that physics is simply a *seat so well-chosen it became invisible*, that even its invariances (Lorentz, gauge, diffeomorphism) are methodological virtues we impose for elegance and tractability rather than features we find. The conclusion flatters the seat framework by handing it physics as a trophy. **But it buys the trophy by dissolving the mountain, and the mountain is the thing the framework is realist about.** The framework is realist about structure and deferential about classification; a reading that makes it anti-realist about the speed of light has mistaken its own reach.

So the question is not "is physics neutral" — it plainly is not — but the sharper one the trophy reading skips: *which part of physics is seated, and which part is not, and how would you tell them apart?* The answer is that there are two floors, the confusion lives in running them together, and the world's 1964 veto is exactly the instrument that separates them.

## Discrimination begins at the seat; the seatless point is silent

Start where the conversation has already settled. The point that occupies every position at once discriminates none of them. To hold all seats is to make no cut, and to make no cut is to say nothing — the view from everywhere and the view that says nothing are the same point. Whatever people reach for when they say *the Truth*, *the ideal*, *the view from nowhere*, the seatless thing does not adjudicate, because adjudication is discrimination and discrimination is a cut. Neutrality and silence are one region. This is not a limitation to be overcome by a cleverer vantage; it is what "seatless" means once you take it seriously.

There is a second thing the word *bedrock* can point at, and it is not that silent point. It is the field prior to any framing — everything that could bear on a verdict, before anything has been marked as situation or as parameter. That field is genuinely prior to every description and genuinely unimaginable, not because it is empty but because every act of imagining it already cuts it into a frame. It is bedrock you can stand *relative to* and never stand *on*. The honest use declares it as the limit your looking cannot get behind; the dishonest use lets it *speak* — issues a verdict "from the widest seat" and pretends the field authored it. The first is rigor. The second is the one inconsistency the whole framework forbids: content asserted while the standpoint is denied.

## The two floors

Everything turns on keeping two perturbations distinct, because "invariance" names a different thing on each.

**Vary the seat, hold the framing.** Fix the formalism — fields on a manifold, a metric, the Lorentz group — and now move the observer: boost to another frame, rotate, translate. What does not move under that variation is a *mountain*: defer to it as real, because no seat reclassifies it. The speed of light is the cleanest mountain humans have found. Across every Lorentz boost it does not budge, to fourteen significant figures, and that invariance is witnessed under perturbation, not decreed. This is the realist half doing its work: a property that survives variation of the seat is situation-fixed *over that axis*.

**Vary the framing itself.** Now perturb the other floor — change what counts as an object, a degree of freedom, a symmetry, a law. Choose a Galilean ontology, or a theory with no manifold, or operators on a Hilbert space instead of trajectories. *This* choice is a seat, fully and unescapably. And here is the move the trophy reading misses: a mountain is **silent** on this axis, not invariant. In a Galilean ontology, the invariance of the speed of light is not false — it is *not yet sayable*; there is no boost of the right kind for it to be invariant under. The mountain is maximally solid on the first axis and has no value at all on the second. The two axes are orthogonal in the strong sense: a structure can be rock under one and unrepresentable under the other.

That orthogonality is the resolution. **Frame-invariance is not frame-neutrality.** Invariance says: choose different seats and we still agree on outcomes. Neutrality would say: there is no seat. Physics achieves the first and not the second, and the trophy reading collapses the distinction by treating the framing-choice (a seat) and the within-framing invariance (a mountain) as one thing called "invariance." They are two things. The frame is chosen. What the frame then *cannot make move* is not.

## Why physics is the demonstration

The trophy reading's load-bearing claim is that the invariances themselves are imposed — methodological virtues, not features the world supplies. State the claim's falsification condition plainly, the way the claim's author would if pressed: *if invariances were demanded for their elegance, then no demanded symmetry would ever be overridden by experiment.* We would keep the beautiful ones because keeping them is the whole game.

The condition fails, and it fails in the open. CP symmetry is exactly the elegant, tractable, hypothesis-shrinking invariance the trophy reading says we impose — and in 1964 the world refused it. Galilean invariance tells the same story one era earlier: chosen, relied upon, then declined at high speed and replaced under duress. The symmetries that hold are not the ones we found beautiful; they are the ones the world did not knock down when we leaned on them. *That* is the demonstration. Physics is not the exception to the seat framework and it is not a trophy for it. It is the perturbation move run at the highest resolution human inquiry has ever reached: vary the frame, and the mountain/seat split comes out cleaner than anywhere else, because the held dimension is held to fourteen decimal places and the world's vetoes are recorded with dates.

The friend's reading deserves its dignity here, because it is half-right and right about the harder half. Gauge invariance really is largely a redundancy of description — unphysical degrees of freedom that gauge-fixing removes — so for gauge symmetry "we imposed it for tractability" is close to correct, and a physicist who has spent a career watching coordinates and gauges turn out to be conventions will generalize that lesson honestly. The error is only the over-reach: extending the lesson from the genuine seat-cases (coordinates, gauges) to the mountains (the Lorentz invariance of *c*, the Noether conservation laws that ride on symmetries the world enforces). The discipline the trophy reading skipped is the one discrimination this document insists on: *which* invariances are descriptive redundancy and which are the world refusing to move. Gauge is a seat. The constancy of light is a mountain.

So the rescued statement, the portable one: **choosing to demand a symmetry is a seat; the symmetry holding is a mountain.** You can choose to organize a theory around an invariance, to care about it, to find it beautiful — that is framing, fully seated. You cannot choose whether the world honors the demand. The honoring is witnessed under perturbation and can come back *no*.

## The counterargument that has to land

The strongest opposition is not "physics is neutral" — no one careful holds that — but a *reclassification*: that the two-floor distinction is a dodge. *A mountain is only a mountain relative to a framing*, the objection runs; *you concede the framing is a seat, so the mountain inherits the seatedness of the floor it stands on. It is seats all the way down, and your second floor is realism smuggled back in under a new label.* This objection is correct about everything except the one thing that matters, and it should be felt as correct before the reply arrives, because it is the position of every conventionalist who has ever watched a "law" turn out to be a convention.

The reply is not an argument; it is a control. If the within-framing axis were just another seat — if "the speed of light is invariant under boosts" were a choice we make and could unmake — then the world could never override a demanded symmetry, because overriding requires something on the far side of the choice to do the overriding. But the world *does* override: CP, Galilean invariance, the long list of beautiful symmetries experiment has retired. That is the probe firing on a positive control. A clean invariance is worth nothing until you have shown the same kind of probe can come back *variant* on a case you know is variant — and physics is the discipline that has shown this most often and most precisely. The mountain is not realism smuggled in; it is the residue that survived a perturbation the seats-all-the-way-down reading predicts should not have a far side. The realism is *earned by the veto.*

What the reply cannot do is compel the radical conventionalist who denies there is any frame-stable structure whatsoever — who holds that the appearance of a veto is itself an artifact of a framing we have not yet learned to vary. Against that position the document declines to prove its conclusion and instead names the place it ends: this binds anyone who grants that experiment can decline a demand, and only them. That is the positional limit, stated rather than hidden.

## How far is far enough

The seat is the most useful concept in this whole apparatus and the easiest to ruin by overuse, and the failure is a halting problem in disguise. You can always descend one more level — perturb the framing of the framing, ask for the seat beneath the seat — and the regress never terminates in bedrock, because each ascent to certify a frame as neutral produces a richer system with its own uncertifiable frame. *Too far* is real; the trouble is that you cannot compute it from inside. The drive to keep descending is precisely the seat worst-placed to judge whether the next descent is rigor or evasion, because to the descender the next cut always looks like rigor.

The escape is the only kind the framework allows anywhere else: not an inside decision procedure, but a control on the next move. A descent earns its keep when it *surfaces a previously-concealed seat* — when the deeper perturbation moves a verdict that looked fixed, the way the framing floor moved the apparent neutrality of physics. You stop on a **controlled null**: when you have shown the next perturbation *could* move a verdict and it does not. You are in the trap on an **uncontrolled null** — stopping because the deeper level returned nothing on a probe you never checked could find anything, which is byte-identical to never having looked. "Useful as a tool for seeing the seat" is the operator's own criterion, and this is its operational form: the tool is working while each descent exposes a seat that was hidden; it has stopped working when the descent surfaces nothing *and you have confirmed it would have if there were something there.* That confirmation is where you halt.

The prescription is indexed, because it serves unevenly. It is built for the resourced over-descender — the analyst whose failure mode is one turn too many — and it gives that person a place to stop that is not mere fatigue. It does little for the under-descender, who stops too early and needs the opposite push; a stopping rule is not their medicine. And it presumes the slack to run the control on the next level. Someone without that slack does not get the controlled null; they get the honest admission that their stop is uncontrolled — which is worth more than a stop dressed as completion, but is not the same gift.

## The instrument's own seat

A note held lightly, at lower confidence than the rest. Any engine built to run this move lives at the perturbation floor: it can only ever vary *named, held* axes, because perturbation *is* a cut — "hold the rest fixed" is already a framing. The one thing such an engine cannot perturb is the perturbing. It can vary its own constants, its own thresholds, its own calibrations; it cannot get behind the fact that it varies. The perturbation operation is the engine's stream — the cut its looking cannot look past — and a system rich enough to pose the question of its own seat cannot answer it from inside. It can declare the seat and stake it; it cannot certify it. This is not a defect to be engineered away. It is the same limit, one floor down, that separates a method from the character of the one running it: the discipline can be built, the honoring of it cannot.

## Open questions

**Ω_C — Where exactly does the redundancy/mountain line fall across the symmetries?** Conceptually underspecified, not unanswerable. Gauge invariance reads as descriptive redundancy; Lorentz invariance and the Noether conservation laws read as mountains. The intermediate cases (diffeomorphism invariance, CPT, the discrete symmetries) want a per-symmetry adjudication on the same criterion — *does experiment have a far side that can veto it* — rather than a blanket verdict. The index that dissolves the question is "which perturbation, run on which symmetry."

**Ω_E — Is the cross-floor independence as clean as claimed?** Empirically shaped. The claim is that within-framing invariance (mountain) and choice-of-framing (seat) are orthogonal — a structure can be rock on one axis and silent on the other. The death of a framing that nonetheless preserved a mountain (relativity retaining the constancy of *c* while discarding Galilean invariance) is one confirming case; a framing change that *moved a mountain* would falsify the independence. Worth a deliberate search of the history of physics for a counterexample before the independence is treated as settled.

**Ω_P — Type C or Type B?** Preference-dependent and the live fork. This document treats the physicist-versus-realist dispute as Type C: not a contradiction but two answers at two indices, dissolved by naming the floor. A reader could instead hold it Type B — that physics-as-convention and structural realism are genuinely incompatible foundations and the two-floor move is a refusal to choose. The decision authority is the operator's, and it is a real choice, not a confusion: the document is staked on the Type-C reading and names the Type-B reading as the position it declines, not the position it has refuted.

---

The trophy reading had it backwards. The precision of physics is not the measure of how well we chose our seat. It is the measure of how hard the world pushed back on the seats we tried — and the symmetries that survived are not our most beautiful guesses but the demands the world refused to break. We did not find the view from nowhere. We found the places where something that is not us said no.
