# STAGE 3: OPERATIONAL SPECIFICATION

## Terminal Attractor Verification

**Selected Attractor:** Deterministic Tragedy ✓

**Compatibility Check:**

**With Rationality Model (BIR):**
```
✓ No collective action emerges (Tan isolated, neighbors dispersed)
✓ No bargaining power exists (Tan powerless vs. social enforcement)
✓ Bounded rationality prevents correction (enforcers never learn)
✓ Satisficing behavior persists (each enforcer optimizes locally)
```

**With Dominant Constraint (C2: Public_Performance_Demand):**
```
Type: Snare (from Tan's index, χ = 0.96)
✓ High extraction + High suppression + No coordination
✓ Asymmetric (enforcers experience as Rope, χ = 0.384)
✓ No exit available (must use public route)
✓ Snare + No collective action + No exit = Deterministic Tragedy
```

**Structural Path Verification:**
```
t0: C1 active (Market goal, Tangled Rope, χ = 0.36)
t1: C2 activates (First judgment, Snare emerges, χ = 0.96)
t2: C2 intensifies (Contradictions accumulate, agency depletes)
t3: C2 → C3 collision (Absurd compliance triggers physical limits)
t4: C3 terminates system (Buffalo drowns, irreversible)

No intervention point where Tan gains:
- Power (remains powerless throughout)
- Exit (remains trapped on public route)
- Time (remains immediate, reactive)
- Collective support (neighbors never coordinate)
```

**Alternative Attractors Ruled Out:**
- Negotiated Equilibrium: ✗ (No bargaining power, no mutual recognition)
- Revolutionary Rupture: ✗ (No collective action, no organization)
- Seeded Possibility: ✗ (No underground network, no future organizing)

**CONFIRMED:** Deterministic Tragedy is structurally inevitable from initial conditions.

## Voice Archetype Selection

**Selected:** The Condemned ✓

**Justification:**

**Attractor Compatibility:**
```
✓ Deterministic Tragedy (primary match)
✓ Seeded Possibility (not applicable here)
✗ Equilibrium (incompatible - requires agency voice can't support)
✗ Rupture (incompatible - requires collective action)
```

**Index Requirements:**
```
✓ PIR/BIR model: BIR confirmed
✓ Mountain-dominated OR powerless/moderate: Snare-dominated (C2), powerless (π = 1.5)
✓ π ≥ 1.0: π = 1.5 (powerless position)
```

**Structural Fit:**

Tan experiences constraint as natural/inevitable, not constructed. He's inside the logic, cannot see systemic shape. He acts rationally given limited information and structural position:
- Complies with first judgment (reasonable response to social pressure)
- Continues complying despite contradiction (each judgment feels legitimate in isolation)
- Escalates to absurd action (accumulated pressure + time pressure + no meta-awareness)

The voice must be **immediate, experiential, not reflective**. Tan doesn't analyze the pattern—he responds to each moment. The reader performs the analysis.

**Voice Characteristics:**
- Present-tense immediacy (or past tense that feels immediate)
- Sensory, physical, concrete (not abstract or analytical)
- No meta-commentary on constraint structure
- Experiences social pressure as overwhelming reality, not as "social pressure"
- Code-switches naturally (Hokkien → Malay → Dutch) without explaining the switch

**Example voice texture:**
> "Mrs. Lim was watching from her doorstep. She said something in Hokkien—too quiet to hear, but her face said enough. The buffalo was walking fine, but she was shaking her head. I stopped. 'Puan Lim?' She switched to Malay so the Tamil dockworker could hear. 'A man of property should ride, not walk like a coolie.' The dockworker nodded. I looked at the buffalo. She looked back at me. I climbed onto the cart."

Not: "I felt the weight of social judgment and recognized the indexical variance in constraint experience."

**Why Not Other Archetypes:**

- **Builder:** ✗ Requires seeing partial system shape, testing for leverage. Tan never gains this perspective.
- **Witness:** ✗ Requires observational distance. Tan is subject, not observer.
- **System:** ✗ Requires impersonal voice. This is intimate tragedy.
- **Guide:** ✗ Requires pedagogical stance. No justification for Tan to explain.
- **Prophet:** ✗ Requires foresight. Tan doesn't see what's coming.
- **Survivor:** ✗ Requires temporal distance. Story ends at terminal state.
- **Negotiator:** ✗ Requires mediator position. Tan is victim, not mediator.

## Indexical Revelation Strategy

**Selected:** Gradual Realization (Single POV, reader discovers variance before character)

**Structure:**

**Act 1 (Setup):** Reader and Tan both experience judgments as legitimate
- First judgment seems reasonable (Mrs. Lim's concern appears genuine)
- Tan's compliance seems rational (social pressure is real)
- Reader doesn't yet see the pattern

**Act 2 (Accumulation):** Reader begins to see pattern, Tan doesn't
- Second judgment contradicts first (reader notices, Tan doesn't)
- Third judgment contradicts both (pattern becomes visible to reader)
- Tan continues treating each as isolated, legitimate request
- Reader realizes: these enforcers aren't coordinating, Tan is trapped

**Act 3 (Collision):** Reader sees catastrophe coming, Tan doesn't
- Time pressure + accumulated compliance → absurd action (carrying buffalo)
- Reader knows this will fail (C3: physical limits)
- Tan doesn't see alternative (C2 has depleted all agency)
- Buffalo panics, falls, drowns
- **Post-terminal:** Brief moment of recognition (TR4), but too late

**Revelation Mechanism:**

The reader discovers indexical variance through **juxtaposition without commentary:**

1. **Show enforcer experience (Rope):**
   - Mrs. Lim speaks, then returns to grinding spices (zero cost)
   - Ong calls out, then returns to his stall (zero cost)
   - Each believes they're helping (genuine coordination intent)

2. **Show Tan's experience (Snare):**
   - Physical exhaustion (loading/unloading cart repeatedly)
   - Time depletion (market closing soon)
   - Cognitive load (trying to satisfy contradictory demands)
   - Social pressure as visceral sensation (not abstract concept)

3. **Never state the variance explicitly:**
   - No "they don't realize what they're doing to me"
   - No "from their perspective it's different"
   - Just show: they walk away satisfied, he keeps struggling

**Code-Switching as Revelation Device:**

- Hokkien (intimate): Tan's internal experience, family conversations
- Market Malay (public): Enforcer judgments, Tan's compliance
- Dutch (institutional): Harbor police, final authority

The switch from Hokkien to Malay signals Tan putting on public mask—performing compliance. The inability to switch to Dutch signals structural powerlessness when Sergeant De Vries appears.

## Editorial Decisions

### Length
**Selected:** Short story (4,000-5,000 words)

**Rationale:**
- Three-act structure needs space for accumulation (not flash)
- Multiple enforcer encounters require development
- Bridge scene needs visceral detail
- But: Simple constraint structure (linear C1→C2→C3) doesn't need novelette length

### POV
**Selected:** Third Limited (Tan's perspective)

**Rationale:**
- Condemned voice requires intimacy (first-person level access to experience)
- But: Third person allows occasional external observation (Mrs. Lim's face, buffalo's body language) without breaking voice
- Tight third (no head-hopping, no omniscient commentary)

### Tense
**Selected:** Past tense with present-tense immediacy

**Rationale:**
- Past tense is natural for tragedy (already happened, inevitable)
- But: Sentence rhythm creates present-tense urgency
- "He stopped" not "He had stopped" (simple past, not past perfect)
- Sensory details in immediate mode: "The bridge creaked" not "The bridge had been creaking"

### Character Count
**Selected:** 6 named characters + crowd

**Named:**
1. Tan Ah Kow (protagonist)
2. Mrs. Lim (first enforcer, neighbor)
3. Ong Ah Huat (second enforcer, market vendor)
4. Tamil dockworker (third enforcer, brief)
5. Sergeant De Vries (institutional authority)
6. Tan's eldest son (brief, establishes family stake)

**Crowd:** Unnamed observers who contribute to accumulating pressure

**Rationale:**
- Minimum for constraint interactions: multiple enforcers needed to show contradiction
- Each enforcer represents different index position
- Son establishes what's at stake (family legacy, not just personal loss)

### Naming Strategy
**Selected:** Cultural (Chinese, Dutch, Tamil names with linguistic naturalization)

**Implementation:**
- Chinese names: Full name + Hokkien romanization in first mention
  - "Tan Ah Kow (陈亚九, Tân A-káu)" → then "Tan" in narration, "Ah Kow" in dialogue
- Malay/Dutch names: Direct use
  - "Mrs. Lim" (Peranakan convention), "Sergeant De Vries"
- Tamil name: Brief, respectful
  - "The Tamil dockworker" → "the dockworker" (role-based after introduction)

**Rationale:**
- Grounds story in historical/cultural specificity
- Naming conventions signal social relationships (Mrs. Lim = respectful distance, Ah Kow = familiar)
- Avoids abstract/allegorical feel (not "The Miller," "The Neighbor")

### Linguistic Implementation

**Code-Switching Signals:**

1. **Hokkien → Intimacy/Vulnerability:**
   - Tan's thoughts when alone
   - Conversations with son
   - Mrs. Lim's initial comment (before switching to Malay for public performance)
   - Rendered: Italics + brief translation in context
   - Example: *"Gâu-sī"* (clever/capable), she'd said—but her face said otherwise.

2. **Market Malay → Public Performance:**
   - All enforcer judgments
   - Tan's compliance responses
   - Market transactions
   - Rendered: Standard text, grammatically simplified
   - Example: "A man of property should ride, not walk like coolie."

3. **Dutch → Institutional Power:**
   - Sergeant De Vries only
   - Tan understands but cannot respond fluently
   - Rendered: Standard text, Tan's comprehension shown through action not dialogue
   - Example: "Doorlopen! Move along!" Tan understood the tone if not all the words.

**Switch Signals Meaning:**
- Hokkien → Malay = Putting on public mask, performing competence
- Malay → Hokkien = Dropping mask, revealing private distress
- Dutch appears = Non-negotiable authority, Tan cannot code-switch up (structural powerlessness)

## Primary Physical Marker

**Selected:** Tan's hands on the cart handles

**Emergence from Inhabitation Sentence:**

> "You are a craftsman in a system where your reputation is everything and your reputation is local, and the walk to market takes you through a neighborhood where everyone knows your father's name and will judge whether you are worthy of it."

The cart is the physical manifestation of this constraint:
- Carries his father's work (legacy)
- Requires his hands (craftsman's identity)
- Must be pushed through the neighborhood (public route, no exit)
- Gets heavier with each compliance (loading/unloading repeatedly)

**Physical Progression:**

**Act 1 (Setup):**
- Hands steady on handles, familiar grip
- Cart balanced, buffalo pulling smoothly
- "He'd made this walk a hundred times"

**Act 2 (Accumulation):**
- Hands cramping from repeated loading/unloading
- Palms slick with sweat (not from heat—from pressure)
- Knuckles white when Mrs. Lim speaks
- Fingers fumbling with ropes (tying buffalo, untying, retying)

**Act 3 (Collision):**
- Hands shaking as he ties buffalo to pole
- Can't get knot right (hands no longer obey)
- Drops the pole when buffalo thrashes
- Final image: Empty hands, cart abandoned, nothing left to hold

**Reader Understanding Before Character:**

The reader will notice the hands before Tan does:
- Act 1: "His hands knew the cart" (competence)
- Act 2: "His hands were shaking" (stress Tan doesn't acknowledge)
- Act 3: "He couldn't make his hands work" (collapse)

This is not a designed motif. This is what happens when a craftsman's identity—hands that build—is subjected to a system that demands he perform rather than produce. The hands fail because the system has made them irrelevant.
