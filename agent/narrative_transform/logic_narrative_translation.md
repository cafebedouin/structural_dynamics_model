## I. How Constraint Types Feel

Each constraint type produces a distinct experiential register. Stage 2 uses these to design settings where characters inhabit constraint experiences without naming them.

> **Six registers, not six types total.** The engine also emits **Naturalized** (high base
> extraction hidden by power position) and **unknown** (metrics under-determine the type); both are
> handled upstream in Stage 1 classification. They have no distinct Stage-2 experiential register of
> their own — a Naturalized constraint is *felt* as whatever its power-scaled χ routes toward (often
> Rope-like from the beneficiary's seat) — so this guide covers the six inhabitable registers, by
> design, not by omission.

### Mountain (■) — Unchangeable Terrain

The experience of routing around what cannot change. Characters accept, adapt, find paths over or around. Not resignation — strategic conservation of energy for what IS changeable. The Mountain is backdrop: characters don't fight it, they navigate relative to it. Tension comes from characters who haven't yet accepted the Mountain, or from discovering a supposed Mountain is actually constructed (False Mountain reveal).

### Rope (⊞) — Coordination Mechanism

The experience of functional cooperation. Characters maintain, protect, take for granted. Ropes feel invisible when working — you notice traffic signals only when they break. Tension comes from degradation (Rope rotting into Tangled Rope) or from characters who mistake Rope for Snare and try to destroy functional coordination.

### Snare (⊠) — Extraction Trap

The experience of being trapped in a system that takes. Characters resist, escape, are crushed, or — most tragically — normalize the extraction and stop seeing it. The Snare is the most index-sensitive type: what one character experiences as a trap, another experiences as a coordination mechanism. This divergence IS the drama. Tension: trapped characters who know the trap but can't exit. Or characters with exit options watching others trapped (moral dilemma).

### Tangled Rope (⊞⊠) — Hybrid Coordination-Extraction

The richest experiential register. Characters can't simply accept or reject — the system genuinely helps AND genuinely hurts. The constraint provides real value (income, connection, structure) while extracting asymmetrically (surplus capture, agency restriction, attention harvesting). Characters navigate, compromise, attempt surgical reform. Tension comes from the irreducibility: burning it down destroys the coordination; accepting it normalizes the extraction.

### Scaffold (⊡) — Temporary Support

The experience of a countdown. Characters know (or should know) the support is temporary. Tension comes from what happens at the deadline: does the system sunset gracefully, or does it become permanent? The Scaffold-to-Piton transition (sunset violated) is one of the most common institutional decay patterns. Theater ratio spikes exactly when the sunset is missed — the structure starts performing its function rather than executing it.

### Piton (⊟) — Degraded Theater

The experience of dead weight. The system persists but does nothing. Characters maintain it out of habit, fear, or because dismantling costs energy. The Piton is a decayed Rope or expired Scaffold — its history is part of its character. Institutional decay, vestigial obligations, obsolete rituals that consume resources.

## II. Indexical Variance as Dramatic Engine

### The Core Structure

Different characters experience the same constraint differently because they occupy different structural positions. This is not disagreement — it's structural divergence. Each character's experience is objectively true from their position.

**Design principle:** If all characters experience a constraint identically, you've lost the dramatic engine. Indexical variance produces conflict without requiring anyone to be wrong, evil, or irrational.

### Creating Multi-Perspectival Tension

**Do create:**
- Different indices experiencing same constraint differently
- All characters acting rationally from their position
- Structural tension despite good faith

**Don't create:**
- Villain vs. hero (one person wrong, one right)
- Objective good vs. objective evil

**Structural conflict:** Characters collide not because of character flaws but because their structural positions produce incompatible experiences of the same system. The institutional agent genuinely sees coordination. The powerless agent genuinely experiences extraction. Both are correct. Collision is inevitable.

### Power Positions as Material Conditions

Stage 2 naturalizes index positions through concrete material conditions — NOT by stating indices.

| Index dimension | Show through |
|----------------|-------------|
| Power (P) | Physical space, daily rhythm, material possessions, who defers to whom |
| Time (T) | What characters worry about (next meal vs. next generation), planning horizon |
| Exit (E) | What alternatives exist, what leaving would cost, who's physically stuck |
| Scope (S) | What characters can verify firsthand, what they take on faith |

### Revelation Strategy

**Don't reveal indexical variance through:**
- Explicit statement ("From my perspective...")
- Framework terminology ("power-scaling," "Tangled Rope")
- Author voice explaining

**Do reveal through:**
- Character actions that make sense only from their structural position
- Dialogue showing incompatible interpretations of the same system
- Consequences that confirm or violate each character's expectations
- Physical sensations of power differential
- Setting details that naturalize structural positions

## III. Structural Physics as Plot Architecture

Three patterns from Stages 7–9 of Deferential Realism translate directly into narrative structure.

### False Mountain Reveal

Something presented as unchangeable reality is discovered to be constructed — maintained by enforcement, benefiting some at others' expense. "The law of nature has police."

**Structure:**
1. Characters initially accept constraint as terrain ("this is just how things work")
2. Evidence accumulates: burden varies by power, enforcement visible at edges
3. Reveal: the constraint requires active enforcement and distributes costs asymmetrically — natural laws don't do this
4. Character choice: continue accepting, or reclassify and act

### Purity Drift (Slow-Burn Decay)

A system looks healthy by surface metrics while its structural integrity silently degrades. By the time symptoms appear, repair is exponentially harder.

**Structure:**
1. System appears functional (characters rely on it)
2. Subtle signs: theater increases, enforcement costs rise, benefits narrow
3. Characters who notice early are dismissed
4. Visible crisis feels sudden — but the rot started long before

**Creates:** The "cassandra" character (sees decay others don't) and the "slow crisis" plot (tension from knowing what's coming).

### Network Contamination (Contagion)

A clean system surrounded by degraded ones absorbs corruption — not because its people change, but because its structural context poisons it.

**Structure:**
1. Focal system is healthy
2. Neighboring systems are degraded
3. Contamination propagates: corrupt neighbors impose costs, create workarounds, normalize extraction
4. Focal system's effective integrity drops even though its intrinsic integrity hasn't changed yet
5. Eventually contamination internalizes

**Creates:** The "good person in bad system" arc and the "contagion" plot. Explains why isolated reform fails.

## IV. Lifecycle Transitions as Story Time

Constraints don't stay static. Degradation creates urgency (act before the transition) or explains decay (why did this coordination fail?).

**Key asymmetry for storytelling:**
- **Decay** (→ extraction): Natural, passive, constant pressure. Snares persist not because they're optimal but because reversing entropy requires work.
- **Reform** (→ coordination): Requires massive active energy, usually a Scaffold first. The harder the system has degraded, the more energy reform demands.

**Transition moments as plot beats:**
- Rope → Tangled: The moment extraction appears in something that used to be pure coordination
- Tangled → Snare: The moment coordination dies and only extraction remains
- Scaffold → Piton: The moment a temporary measure becomes permanent theater
- Any transition → Character discovers the change before or after it happens

## V. The Routing Principle

Stage 1 provides symbolic classifications: χ values, type designations, index tuples. These are structural coordinates — they tell you WHERE in constraint-space each character stands.

Stage 2's job is to translate those coordinates into a world where the positions feel inevitable. The question is not "what does χ = 0.84 mean?" but "what setting, occupation, and social structure would make a person feel what χ = 0.84 describes — trapped in a system that takes almost everything?" (χ = 0.84 here is **illustrative of a high-χ register**, not a classification threshold — the gate boundaries live in `logic_symbolic.md §IV`.)

The χ value routes toward an experiential register. A high χ routes toward feeling trapped. A moderate χ routes toward feeling conflicted. A low or negative χ routes toward feeling empowered. Stage 2 designs a world where those feelings emerge naturally from material conditions.

**The critical test:** Could a reader encounter this world in an ethnography or history book? If it feels like constraint theory illustrated, return to the setting and find what's real.

## VI. Translation Checklist

```
☐ ZERO framework terminology in output (no "extraction," "coordination," "indexical," "chi")
☐ Each constraint type naturalized as something with a local name
☐ Power differentials shown through material conditions, not stated
☐ Time horizons shown through what characters worry about
☐ Exit options shown through what alternatives actually exist
☐ Indexical variance produces conflict between characters (not within them)
☐ Setting could exist in a history book or ethnography
☐ Inhabitation sentence captures felt experience for each major character
☐ Linguistic strategy has concrete examples, not just description
☐ A reader would NOT think "this is about constraint theory"
```
