# The Grain
## A World Bible in Eleven Modules

*Working title. A near-future hard-SF setting for a short story collection.*

---

## What this is

A shared world, not a plot. Eleven modules, each deriving from the ones before it, ending in a kit for making individual stories. Nothing here is a premise on its own; everything here is a condition stories happen inside.

The setting is the solar system, after reusable lift and orbital refuelling made offworld industry possible. **It has no dates and refuses to acquire any** — progression is tracked by which thresholds a place has crossed, not by a year, and Module 10 explains how to site a story without a chronology. Travel is slow, power is scarce, mass is precious, conversation at range becomes correspondence, and bodies do not last. None of those constraints lift.

## Someone in it

A suit fitter, three terms into a works, wants her daughter to be able to leave.

The leaving is the cheap part — down the well costs almost nothing, and that is the trap, because the girl grew here and the well will take her apart. So the fitter is saving toward a berth in a spun habitat instead, which is expensive and will not be ready in time, and in the meantime she is teaching her daughter to come off a ladder in a way that will read as certifiable to a board that has never been here.

She may be wrong about the ladder. There is no way to find out that does not involve the girl's whole life.

That is the world. Everything below is why.

## Before any of it

Everything in these modules is a check for **legality** — is this permitted, does it violate a rule. Not one of them asks whether anything hurts. A story can satisfy all eleven and be inert, and the characteristic failure of a world this carefully specified is fiction that exists to demonstrate the world's coherence.

So three questions outrank the entire document:

1. **Who wants something, what do they do about it, and who pays?**
2. **Does the reader know something the character doesn't — and is that gap where the feeling lives?**
3. **Does anything in this cost anyone anything?**

A story that answers those and breaks a rule is a story worth arguing about. A story that keeps every rule and answers none of them is scaffolding.

The modules are here to stop you writing a bad version of this world. They cannot make you write a good one, and if the specification ever gets more careful attention than the prose, the specification has become the project.

**And read Appendix B before the modules.** Nearly every prohibition here is a generator written backwards — it closes a cheap route in order to make something else reachable. Appendix B states the twelve of them as what they open, and sorts out the few rules that open nothing. Working from the prohibitions produces a story that avoids errors. Working from the generators produces a story.

## The build rule

This document grew by collision. Nearly everything worth having in it arrived when a new demand met a rule written elsewhere for other reasons and the two could not both stand — comedy against monotonic instruments, a beneficiary's gratitude against the acknowledgment terminal, a hold's provenance against the referent it was said to lack.

**So the test for any future change: does it conflict with something already here, or does it merely clarify?**

A conflict is a finding. Something has to give, and what gives teaches you what the world actually is. A clarification is accretion — the document gets longer, more precise, and no better, and it will read as diligence.

**Additions are not conflicts, and the difference is where this rule fails in practice.** Any source presents some mechanism this document does not contain, because a bible is finite and fiction is not. *The bible lacks this* is therefore always true and is not a finding. **A conflict requires the new thing to be incompatible with something already stated** — it forces a qualification, a scope limit, an exception, or a retraction. If nothing already here has to give, it is an addition, and additions are how a document gets thicker without getting better.

**The null rate is the number to watch, and it is the load-bearing result.** A probe series that finds a conflict in every input has stopped measuring: an instrument returning the same value on every reading is not an instrument. *Measured across this document's first twenty probes: roughly four genuine conflicts, two nulls, and fourteen additions counted as conflicts at the time.* That ratio is the failure, and it is recorded here rather than corrected away, because the correction is to expect nulls and to state them.

**If several probes in a row return conflicts, suspect the reader rather than the sources.**

**When a change only clarifies, don't make it.** And when a whole new probe — a register, a source, a story — returns nothing but clarifications, that is the signal that this instrument has stopped measuring. Not a rule change per probe; a *conflict* per probe. The day a probe finds nothing to break, the document is finished, and continuing to work on it is the catalog compounding (Module 5 §6): thicker, more careful, and no longer doing anything.

## The spine

**Every standard in this world is scored against a body that formed on one planet, under one star — a baseline nobody chose and no one can defend.** Fitness, insurance, employment, migration, and the word *normal* all resolve against it. That is the setting's grievance, and it is a real one: people want things and are refused by an accident.

**And it cannot be fixed, which is the lock.** Adopting a better reference would require knowing which reference is right, and that cannot be known:

> To learn whether a way of living is required by the world or merely chosen and then kept, a population must commit to it for a generation. A population that has committed is no longer able to stand outside it and compare — the alternative was never raised, so there is nothing to compare against. **The measurement and the commitment are the same act.**

The mechanism is a confound, not a mystery, and it is stated plainly here because a hard-SF reader will attack it first: the adapted population is the only sample, and adapting is what changed it. No better instrument resolves this, because the instrument would have to be a second generation raised otherwise, and raising them is the commitment.

**Falsifier:** if a story treats a disputed way of life as finally decidable from outside it — by a better instrument, a longer study, a recovered archive, a simulation, a fairer authority — the invariant was lost.

**What the falsifier does not forbid.** Local truths are recoverable and stories should recover them. A dose *was* miscalculated. A board *did* falsify a reading. This particular rotation regime *was* unnecessary, and someone can prove it. Stories must be able to close. What stays unreadable is the generational question underneath — whether the way of living was required — and a story that answers *that* has broken the world.

Likewise, a character may be certain, may act on the certainty, may die for it. The falsifier fires only if the text validates them.

## The title

You can only read the grain of a piece by cutting it, and then you no longer have the piece. The word is in the world's mouth for what a body became — how it grew, workable against but not resettable, unfinished until the life is over. It is also the whole problem, in one syllable.

## The grammar

The rule that generates the setting's institutions, customs, and conflicts:

> **Where truth is unavailable and a decision cannot wait, this culture manufactures a vocabulary of provisional status — and the fight is always over who assigns it.**

*Grain* is what a body became, uncertifiable. *Fitness* is a determination against a reference nobody chose. *Closure* is sovereignty as an unverifiable claim. *Out, carried, held, unwritten, returned* are the stages of absence. Any new institution invented for a story is built on this template: **a pressure that cannot wait, a name assigned under uncertainty, and a consequence that follows from the name.**

## How to read it

**Straight through, once.** Then work from Module 11, which is the only module that tells you what to do.

Two axes are independent and easy to confuse. **Module 4 gives kinds of place** — ports, works, spun habitats, stations. **Module 5 gives kinds of polity** — charters, compacts, holds, ports, programmes. A spun habitat is usually governed as a compact but need not be; a works may be run as a charter or, once its rotation regime lapses, as a hold. Do not infer the polity from the place.

*The well* always means Earth.

## What a story owes

Nothing except the canon guard in Module 11 §9. Proper nouns, ships, families, boards, the specific ice — invented per story, binding nobody.

The test: **a reader should finish two stories certain they happen in one place, without either having named anything the other did.**

# Module 1 — Divergence Ledger and Tech Ceiling

*First of eleven. The commitments everything else is built on. The full research baseline behind this module is a separate document; what follows is the ledger.*

---

## 1. Discipline

**Hard by default, speculative only where speculation buys story and costs little to explain.** No physics is violated. Where the setting extends beyond present engineering, the extension is an engineering extrapolation with no new physics in it, and it is stated here rather than smuggled in later.

**Straight extrapolation, no alternate past.** Nothing diverges from the actual record. The setting is the present continued under its own constraints — which is a stronger claim than an alternate history, because it can be wrong.

**No dates.** Progression is keyed to thresholds (Module 10), not to years. A story says which crossings have happened where it is set.

## 2. The baseline

The state of things at the setting's zero point, stated as commitments:

- **Reusable heavy lift works** and has driven cost per kilogram down by roughly an order of magnitude, not three. Launch is no longer the binding constraint. It is also not cheap.
- **Orbital propellant transfer is routine.** This is the crossing that made everything else possible and it is the least dramatic thing in the setting.
- **Electric propulsion is the workhorse.** High exhaust velocity, low thrust, long transits. Chemical for landings and departures, electric for everything between. Cargo is slow. People are slightly less slow and it costs a great deal more.
- **Power is the pacing item.** Fission has climbed from kilowatts to megawatts over decades of unglamorous work. Solar remains adequate inward of the belt and useless beyond it.
- **ISRU is real and unromantic.** Water is split for propellant and air; regolith is sintered, packed, and used as bulk; metals are worked locally into coarse goods. Fine fabrication is not done offworld.
- **Life support is closed on water, mostly closed on air, and imperfectly closed on food.** Food closure is the frontier and the boundary of sovereignty.
- **Nothing meaningful comes down the gravity well.** Extracted mass is consumed where it is extracted.

## 3. Permitted speculations

Four, all stated, none exceeding engineering extrapolation. Anything not on this list is not permitted.

1. **The partial-gravity question is unresolved and stays unresolved.** This is not an invention — it is a genuine present-day void — and the setting's discipline is to fill it with nothing (Module 3).
2. **Bioregenerative life support matures** to high but not total closure. Demonstrated in principle already; needs only engineering.
3. **Megawatt-class fission with manageable radiators.** No new physics; a long, expensive ramp.
4. **High-power electric propulsion becomes practical because the power exists.** Lab-proven concepts unlocked by item 3, not by a new principle.

## 4. Hard nos

Refused regardless of convenience:

- Reactionless drives, inertia-less propulsion, anything violating momentum conservation
- Fusion torch drives, or any net-positive fusion propulsion
- Faster-than-light travel, communication, or sensing
- Artificial gravity without rotation or acceleration — no grav plating
- Cheap bulk return of mass down Earth's gravity well as an economic engine
- Stealth as invisibility; a powered, warm, thrusting vessel cannot hide against a cold sky
- Unobtainium — no structural materials that dissolve the rocket equation
- Instant or magical ISRU; every tonne is hard-won
- Terraforming on any story-relevant timescale

## 4b. Soft nos

Technically possible, story-poisonous. Not physics violations — narrative ones.

- **The naive outsider who sees clearly.** A newcomer who arrives and correctly diagnoses the arrangement collapses the world into direct realism in one scene. Outsiders may notice things. They may not be right about what the things mean.
- **The competent authority that fixes it.** Any body with enough reach, legitimacy, and information to settle a dispute everywhere.
- **The recovered archive** that resolves what a founding generation actually knew.
- **The simulation** accurate enough to substitute for a life.
- **The reformer who reopens the reference body.** There is nothing to reopen it against; a story that stages the reopening has invented a standpoint the world does not contain.

## 5. What the ceiling produces

Stated once, because every later module inherits it: **this technology base makes travel slow, power scarce, mass precious, communication delayed, and bodies fragile — and it makes none of these solvable.** Every institution, custom, and conflict in the setting is a way of living with a constraint that will not lift.

The setting is not about technology. It is about what people become when the constraints hold.

## 6. Checks

- [x] Discipline stated: hard by default, four named speculations, no alternate past
- [x] Baseline stated as commitments rather than as a survey
- [x] Permitted speculations enumerated and closed
- [x] Hard nos enumerated
- [ ] **Reader test:** a reader should never encounter a technology that solves a problem the setting depends on.

# Module 2 — Terrain

*Second of eleven. Depends on Module 1. Supplies the substrate Module 3 requires.*

Module 1 established what is technically possible and when. This module converts that into **what a person cannot argue with**, sorts it from what only looks that way, and marks the band where the difference is undecidable from inside a life. Everything here is world-facing: it is written to be felt, not calculated.

The sorting rule is a single test, applied to every constraint in the setting:

> **Does the burden vary by where you stand? Does anyone benefit? Does it need enforcing?**
>
> No to all three → **hard terrain.** Yes to any → **constructed**, however natural it looks.
> Can't be determined from inside a single lifetime → **the band.** That is where stories go.

---

## 1. Hard terrain

Genuinely unbudgeable. No beneficiary, no enforcement, no exceptions purchasable at any price. State these as conditions of life, never as figures.

**Everything costs everything.** Moving mass costs propellant, and carrying propellant costs propellant. The penalty compounds against itself, so nothing is ever simply added — every kilogram aboard is a kilogram of something else not aboard. This is the first thing anyone learns and the last thing anyone stops resenting. It cannot be negotiated, subsidised, or waived, and the wealthiest polity in the system pays the same exchange rate as a scavenger.

**The sun thins.** Light falls off with distance, fast. There is a remove past which sunlight is decorative and every watt must be made rather than caught. This draws a line across the solar system that nobody set and nobody can move, and it divides the places where power is a bill from the places where power is a reactor and a priesthood of people who tend it.

**Heat only leaves one way.** There is nothing to conduct into, so warmth must be radiated, slowly, through surfaces that are large, fragile, and impossible to armour. Every inhabited thing runs hot and shows it. You cannot hide a working machine, a crowded room, or a body.

**Talk becomes mail.** Past a certain distance, a reply takes longer than a thought. Conversation stops being possible and correspondence begins. People far out do not argue; they exchange positions and wait, and the waiting shapes how they say things — you write to a person who will read it as a stranger, because by then they will be.

**Where things will be is already known.** Orbits are predictable weeks ahead. You cannot approach unobserved; you can only be lost among clutter, mistaken for something else, or dropped from someone's attention. Concealment is never invisibility — it is being uninteresting.

**The sky is always on.** A continuous sift of high-energy particles that thin shielding makes *worse*, because it shatters into secondaries. The only answer is bulk — water, waste, regolith, rock. So habitation is burial: people live inside mass, and the surface is a place you visit and leave.

**The boundary is absolute.** Inside is life, outside is not, and the transition is quick. There is no partial pressure of survivable. Everyone knows exactly how long they have, and nobody says the number aloud.

**The system keeps a calendar.** Departure windows open and close on their own schedule. Miss one and you wait, and the waiting is not a punishment anyone chose to give you.

## 2. False terrain

Constructed arrangements presented as conditions of the world. Each is a live False Mountain reveal — the reveal is not that someone is lying, but that a burden which varies by position was being described as one that doesn't.

| Presented as | Actually | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Rotation limits** — "bodies can't stay past a term" | Liability and labour discipline. Terms track insurance exposure and contract cycles, not physiology | Terms differ by employer for identical work; senior staff extend |
| **Dose ceilings** — "the safe limit" | A number set by underwriters and regulators, defensible but chosen, and different in different jurisdictions | Two workers on the same shift under different ceilings |
| **Allotments** — "what the loop can carry" | Real arithmetic wrapped around a prior decision about who counts as aboard | Guests, inspectors, and executives never seem to strain the loop |
| **Closed corridors** — "the trajectory isn't approved" | Traffic control. Physics constrains the set of possible paths; a body decides which are permitted | Approval times vary by flag and by relationship |
| **Certification** — "they can't work at that gravity" | Grain prejudice (Module 3) with a medical form stapled to it | The exclusions track origin more tightly than they track capability |
| **The reference body** — "normal range" | One planet's accident, installed as the standard everything is scored against | It has never been reopened, because there is nothing to reopen it against |

The last row is the floor invariant from Module 3 and is the one that never gets revealed, because there is no vantage from which to reveal it.

### How false terrain gets made

Nobody builds it. **Somebody recalibrates.**

The sequence is always the same and it is always performed correctly. A reading goes out of range. The person responsible models it — thermal, equipment drift, load, geology — and the models fail. They replace the instrument; the reading persists. They build a fourth model and find nothing. What they now have is a number their instrument reports accurately and their understanding cannot source, running in the background of every subsequent measurement, a question they will check every cycle and never close.

So they recalculate the baseline to incorporate recent data.

**This is correct procedure.** A baseline that does not incorporate recent data measures the past, not the present. Environments are not static; reference standards are starting points, not permanent fixtures; recalibration is maintenance. Against the new baseline the anomaly falls within acceptable variance, and it is *true* that it does.

It is also the anomaly being absorbed into the category of normal, and the person doing it knows this, and sits with both things being true, and has no third option. The alternative was a permanent flagged reading they could not explain and would have to answer for.

Every false Mountain in the setting was made this way, by someone competent, in one afternoon, for good reasons. **There is no scene in which anyone decides to hide anything.** Write the recalibration as what it is: a careful person doing maintenance, with steady hands, who understands exactly what they are doing and does it anyway because the other choice is worse for them and no better for anyone.

**Discipline for the whole register: the tell is structural, not moral.** Whoever is enforcing a false Mountain is usually sincere. They inherited it, it works, and from where they stand it costs nothing and prevents disasters. A character who benefits from an arrangement and cannot see it is not lying — the position supplies the blindness.

## 3. The band

Constraints that cannot be sorted from inside a lifetime. These are the setting's story engine, and each is a candidate seat.

- Whether a gravity protocol is required or merely kept
- Whether a closure rate is a ceiling or just the best anyone has bothered to reach
- Whether a reserve estimate is conservative science or a claim staked
- Whether a corridor closure is prudence or protectionism
- Whether a settlement's founding austerity was necessity or preference, now inherited
- Whether a body's failure was its grain, its dose, its work, or its luck
- **Whether being legible protects you.** The doctrine on one side: people who are recorded can be found, counted, claimed, and owed things; the unrecorded die unnoticed and there are reports of exactly that. On the other: concealment is cheaper for whoever is paying, so a man nobody can see is a man nobody has budgeted against, and he eats. Both hold real cases. To settle it you would have to make a population legible for a generation and compare — and making them legible *is* the commitment, so the unrecorded counterfactual was never raised.

  **This one has a property the others lack.** Because concealment is cheaper for whoever pays, the merits and the institutional incentive point the same way, which makes the honest position and the self-serving one indistinguishable — *including to the person holding it.* Someone opposing a register may be protecting people or protecting a budget and cannot tell which. Every other question here leaves the arguer's motive available to them; this one puts the undecidability inside one head.

  It also splits factions internally rather than between them (Module 9 §8): a keeper who wants a register to protect his people and one who refuses are both keepers, both holds, both right. The refusal to write things down (Module 5 §3) and whether any polity keeps a written emplacement register (Module 8, Ω_E06) are local faces of this same question, not separate items.

- **Whether a practice does anything.** People develop customs that seem to work — a way of sitting, a thing done before a shift, a place where hands go. Something is transmitted; people are steadier afterward. Whether the transmission is between the people or between them and something else is not answerable, because the only test would be to stop, and stopping is the thing nobody will do. **A story may render the experience at full strength and must not confirm or debunk it.** The practice is real. What it touches is a band question, and each position reads it differently: the elder knows, the analyst has an explanation, the young one has never asked.

Every one is a genuine question with an answer that exists and cannot be obtained in time. The people arguing are not being stupid or venal. They are being asked to decide before the evidence can arrive, and their positions predict their readings — which is exactly what makes the readings untrustworthy and exactly what makes them unavoidable.

### Two kinds of unsettled, and they look identical from inside

Everything above treats the band as questions whose answers exist and cannot be reached: the fact is there, the comparison that would settle it was destroyed by the commitment (Module 3 §1a). Grief-shaped. Something is true and nobody can have it.

**Some of these questions are not like that. Some have no answer to be had.** The physics is complete, consistent, and load-bearing, and it simply does not determine the matter. Two settlements adopt incompatible protocols; both are consistent with everything the world requires; neither breaks anything; and there is no fact behind the wall that one of them is failing to find. Not a fact withheld. **No fact.**

**Nobody inside can tell which kind a given question is**, and the two have opposite shapes. The first is a loss. The second is not — it is room.

**What follows from the second, and it is the setting's one non-tragic consequence.** Not knowing does not prevent people building. It lets them build *more*: two compacts with contradictory founding provisions, both correct; two ways of raising a child, both defensible; a dozen protocols that cannot all be right and are not competing to be. **The variety in this world is not failure to converge. It is what the underdetermination permits**, and the arrangements built on either answer are legitimate in a way that has nothing to do with anyone being persuaded.

**And it re-reads the schism.** Two compacts diverged (Module 5 §10) and neither can establish which was right — not because the evidence is unavailable, but because on this reading the question has no content. They were not disagreeing about a fact. They were choosing which world to inhabit, and both got one. This is why the original dispute is now incomprehensible from outside: **there was never anything to be right about**, and everyone involved argued as though there were.

*Design note:* never adjudicate this. A story may be built on a character who is certain the question has an answer, and on another who is certain it does not, and both may be sympathetic and neither may be confirmed. That contest is available where the underlying one is not.

**Load-bearing for Module 3:** the band exists because hard terrain and false terrain are *indistinguishable at a single moment*. Both are stable, both are universally observed, both have people who will tell you it has always been this way. Only a trace longer than the practice separates them, and nobody has one.

## 4. Numeric register

Numbers exist in this world as **objects in specific hands**, never as narration.

The instruments are: the dosimeter, the manifest, the survey, the certificate, the loop readout, the approval. Each belongs to a position. A supervisor reads a dosimeter; a worker feels a headache and a sour taste and a bad week. A depot master reads a manifest; a crewman notices the pallets are stacked differently than last time.

**Rule:** a number reaches the page only when a character who owns that instrument *acts on it in a scene* — reads it aloud, disputes it, falsifies it, refuses it. Never as texture, countdown, tally, or emotional beat. Narration is never denominated in the system's numbers. What the powerless position has is consequence and sensation, and sensation is more reliable than the gauge in every way except the one that counts, which is that nobody has to believe it.

## 5. Modes of attention by environment

Registers, not devices. Stage 4 discovers the specific details.

**Surface.** Attention goes to wear and to seals. Dust is abrasive, electrostatic, and gets into everything that moves; competence is legible in how a person handles a cuff or a hinge. The suit's load rides somewhere specific on the body and the body organises itself around that. People read each other's experience from posture before anyone speaks.

**Spun.** Dropped things curve. You pour off-axis without thinking, and outsiders spill. Climbing in one direction is harder than the other and everyone knows which. The floor rises away in both directions and stops being strange in about a week. People grown in spin have a gait, and it is one of the ways grain shows.

**Freefall.** There is no down, so attention goes to handholds, edges, and airflow. Still air is dangerous — the bad air pools where nothing moves — so people are attuned to draughts the way others are attuned to weather, and a stopped fan is noticed by the body before the alarm.

**Transit.** The register is correspondence. You compose rather than speak, and you learn to write to a person who will have moved on before they read you. Long-haul crews develop a formality with the outside world and an unbearable intimacy inside.

## 6. Omega log

- **Ω_C02** — Whether *grain* is used for surface-grown vs. spin-grown as a hard binary or a spectrum. Recommend spectrum with local binaries: each polity draws its own line and treats it as obvious.
- **Ω_P02** — How visible thermal signature should be socially. Recommend it be common knowledge and mostly unremarked, surfacing only when someone tries to hide occupancy.
- **Ω_E02** — Whether any polity publishes its dose ceiling's derivation. Recommend: one does, and it damaged them, and the others noted this. Awaiting Module 5.

# Module 3 — The Invariant

*Third of eleven. Depends on Modules 1–2. Everything from Module 4 onward is built to protect what is stated here.*

This module is written to be carried **verbatim** into Stage 2 Section 0 of any story instantiation, and from there into Stages 3 and 4. It is not flavor. It is the load the rest of the world is built to hold.

---

## 1. The invariant, as commitment and falsifier

> **The world contains a real — whether a given way of living is required by the world or merely chosen and then kept — that no instrument can read, because reading it requires living it. To learn whether a protocol is necessary, a population must commit to it for a generation; and a population that has committed can no longer stand outside it to compare. The measurement and the commitment are the same act.**
>
> **Falsifier:** if the finished story contains a recoverable true value that a better instrument, a longer study, a released dataset, or a more honest authority could have read correctly, the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

Two properties make this hold rather than merely sound good.

**It has no synchronic signature.** At any single moment, a genuine natural limit and a hollowed convention that has drifted into looking like one are indistinguishable — not hard to tell apart, *identical* at the level anyone can measure. Perfect agreement across every position in a settlement is compatible with the underlying practice being physics, or dead habit, or a foreclosed premise nobody has tested. The difference is temporal, and only a trace longer than the practice will show it.

**And there is a second mechanism, sharper than the first: the real is destroyed by being used, not by being measured.** A thing can be read truly. What cannot survive is reading it *for* someone — for a buyer, for a claimant, for oneself. The reading holds from everywhere at once until a hand goes on the pan, and then it becomes the weight it has for that hand, which is a price rather than a fact.

This is why the invariant has no administrator, and the reason is structural rather than a rule anyone imposed: **an administrator is a hand on the pan by definition.** A body charged with reading grain would be reading it for someone — for the boards, for the insurer, for the polity — and the reading would tilt in the act of being made. Not corruptibly. Necessarily.

It also explains the substrate's name properly. A true grain holds from every position and stops holding the moment it is weighed for one. That is what the word means in the world's mouth, and it is why a clinic can produce a complete workup and still not produce a grain: the workup is for someone.

**The trace never closes for the person who must act.** By the time a cohort's lives are legible, their children are already committed, and the population that would have to be compared against no longer exists — it was never raised. The counterfactual is erased by the process that makes the question urgent. Not withheld. Erased.

## 1a. Why the method is right and the world is wrong for it

The invariant is often heard as a claim that people here cannot measure well, or that their instruments are poor, or that they have failed to be rigorous. It is none of those. **Their method is correct. Their world does not satisfy its precondition.**

The ordinary way anyone learns a place is to move through it and keep a running account, trusting that a long enough sample converges on what is actually there. This works, and it is not naive — it is how a craft is learned, how a body of experience becomes judgment, how a person comes to know a reef, a machine, a trade. It requires two things, both usually true and neither stated:

**The thing does not change while you sample it.** What is dense stays dense; what is common stays common. Nothing you learn about it today is falsified by the act of having learned it.

**Sampling does not consume.** Walking through does not use up. The territory is unchanged by having been crossed, and is still there behind you, exactly as it was.

**Neither holds here.** A protocol is learned by being lived, and living it is what makes the alternative unavailable. A body is read by growing, and growing is what fixes the grain. A population is measured by a generation, and the generation is the commitment. **The sample alters what it samples**, so the running average stops describing the place and starts describing only the path taken through it — and there is no second path to compare it against, because taking one is what closed the others.

So a lifetime of honest, competent observation produces a true account of one trajectory and no account at all of the territory. This is not a failure of care and cannot be repaired by more care. **It is the reason the word is *grain*:** a true grain holds from every position and stops holding the moment it is read for one — and reading it is what makes it that grain rather than another.

**And there is a third condition, which the other two conceal.** A settled account is normally checkable *sideways*: two people using unrelated methods land on the same value, and the agreement is what tells them they are describing one object. Count the parts or count the holes; take the pulse or read the gait; measure it now or measure it after it has been stretched. Where a thing has a fixed character, the methods converge, and that convergence is not a bonus — **it is the permission to describe freely at all.** You may look however you like precisely because something confirms you are still looking at the same thing.

**Here nothing plays that role**, and the consequence is worse than disagreement. Two people who read an arrangement differently (Module 9 §4) may be disagreeing about one object, or may be describing two — a practice as it stands to one position and as it stands to another, which are not the same practice and never were. **There is no cross-check that separates those cases.** So an argument in this world cannot establish whether it is an argument.

The permission remains, and it is unlimited, and it is worth nothing. Anyone may give any account of an arrangement, and no one can confirm they are still looking at it. This is why the people here are so careful about position, and why *from where I stand* does so much work in their speech: it is not modesty and not relativism. **It is the only thing available in place of a check.**

**And the diagnostic that follows is the setting's cruellest, because it is the same observation with opposite meanings.** Where the ordinary conditions hold, a settling account means the sampling is working — the swings narrow because more of the place has been seen. Where they do not, a settling account means there is less left to disagree with it (Module 5 §4b). **Convergence is evidence of thoroughness and evidence of depletion, and nothing inside the practice distinguishes them.** The people whose accounts have come to true fastest are either the most experienced or the ones with the least remaining, and they cannot tell which, and neither can anyone watching.

## 1b. What the invariant does not forbid

Read strictly, the falsifier would forbid a story from resolving anything, and eleven stories of that shape is airless. The scope is narrower than it looks.

**Local truths are recoverable, and stories should recover them.** A dose was miscalculated and the ledger proves it. A board falsified a reading and someone finds the original. This rotation regime was unnecessary, and a competent person demonstrates it in one scene. Stories must be able to close, and closing on a local truth is how they do it.

**What stays unreadable is the question underneath** — whether the way of living was required, or chosen and kept. A story may establish that *this* protocol was excessive without establishing what the right protocol is, and the difference between those two is the whole discipline.

**Characters may be certain.** Someone may believe absolutely that they know where the line falls, act on it, teach it to their children, and die for it. The invariant is violated only if the *text* confirms them — if some authority or instrument inside the world rules that they were right. Let the world stay silent and the belief can be as passionate as you like.

## 1c. The local ruling — required, not permitted

§1b licenses local truth and nothing downstream consumes it. No plotting rule asks for a ruling; the ending generator has no column for one; the ledger tracks what a story deliberately did not decide and has no companion field for what it established. **The only instrument that touches this counts absences**, so a writer working from the document is never prompted to close anything — and a story that establishes nothing still has to end, on whatever the world hands out for free.

> **Every story establishes something. The invariant governs what cannot be established, not whether anything can be. A story that decides nothing has not honoured the invariant — it has avoided the position of having to act under it.**
>
> **Falsifier:** if a story reaches its last page without one thing having been made true by somebody's act, it has substituted the world's difficulty for its own, and that difficulty was never the writer's to inherit.

**The ruler need not be the POV.** A ruling made by someone the story does not follow is legitimate and often stronger — but then it must be *felt as a decision* while the POV reads it as manner (Module 11 §4). A private, unwitnessed ruling that the reader only infers afterward has not been made on the page.

**The diagnostic.** Find the *there is no field for it* sentence and check where it sits. Mid-story, after something has been established, it is the engine: the finding is correct and complete, the record routes, the record holds it, and the story is about living in the room afterward. Terminally, with nothing established, it is what floods in to fill a story that has nothing to close on — a gesture where a decision should be. Same sentence, opposite functions.

**What counts.** A ruling is an act with a name on it that makes one thing true and is answerable afterward. None of these touch the question underneath:

- **A miscalculation demonstrated.** Someone shows the working on an allowance, an arithmetic, a margin.
- **A falsification found.** A reading was altered and the original exists.
- **A protocol shown excessive in this instance** — never what the right protocol is.
- **A cause excluded.** One candidate eliminated, the rest left standing. The most underused and the most native, because it closes something while leaving attribution entirely intact.
- **A status corrected.** Someone held is established as out. Someone unwritten answers. A returnee arrives.
- **A provenance settled.** Whose body, whose gear, whose record, whose section of wall.
- **An origin recovered.** This practice began for this reason, and the person who began it is alive to say so. **Boundary: this establishes why it began, never whether it was necessary.** Those are the two halves of the same question and only the first is ever recoverable — a story that slides from *she can tell you why she started it* to *and therefore it was justified* has lost the invariant by the back door. The inverse of the crux on five, and it should exist so that crux reads as a loss rather than a rule.
- **A self-indictment.** The person who made the call establishes that their own call was wrong. The strongest, because it cannot be received as an accusation and cannot be refused on grounds of interest.

**The hard case: a story may be about the ruling being refused.** Someone holds the authority to settle a thing — an entry, a finding, a word in the right ear — and does not use it, and the refusal is the story's event. This satisfies the requirement only under two conditions. **The reader must see clearly what was on the table**: the specific entry, in that person's hands, one action away. And **the refusal must cost**, and go on costing, in a direction the person did not anticipate.

The commonest reason to refuse is not fear. It is that a settled account cannot be moved, and an unsettled one can still be given to somebody for an hour (Module 6 §4). Write it as that, never as cowardice.

**A ruling may be made for nobody, and this is its purest form.** Someone keeps doing the work — stamps the measure, files the report, signs the certificate — and writes the true thing in the margin where nobody reads. It frees no one. It changes nothing anyone can weigh. It is found occasionally, years later, by someone who thinks they have caught the institution in something and has not: they have found the one part of the measure that was ever true, which is the part that admits whose hand set the pan.

This satisfies every constraint above — instance, cost, unnoticed by the arrangement — and adds the thing the others lack: **the ruling does not require an audience to have been made.** It is the only available form for someone whose position gives them no forum at all, which is most positions in this world.

**A ruling is not necessarily a finding.** If every story establishes something by investigating, the collection formularises into eleven small mysteries under Appendix A §3's gates. Status corrected, provenance settled, and self-indictment are not investigations and should carry most of the load for exactly that reason.

**Three constraints.**

*It is made, not discovered.* Discovery is investigation and investigation is the genre the world forbids. Somebody decides to establish a thing, in front of people, and is thereafter the person who did that.

*It costs the person who makes it.* A costless ruling is the competent authority that fixes it (Module 1 §4b) in different clothes. The cost is what separates establishing a truth from being right.

*It lands on an instance, never an arrangement.* **This** call was wrong, not *calls like this are wrong.* And the arrangement does not notice — the finding is entered, correctly, and the queue absorbs it. That is what stops a ruling reading as reform.

**Reading the terminal.** A ruling is a species of acknowledgment, so Module 11 §4's live variable applies directly. From inside the practice, at substantial magnitude → stable. From a position of standing, about a practice that survived by having no category → appropriation, and extinction, and nobody in the story can say so. Against oneself, at cost, and kept → chosen and kept, which is the most reliable route to the two the collection needs.

## 2. The affordance test

> *In this world, could a better instrument measure the invariant's true value correctly?*

**No — and for a structural reason, not a technological one.** The instrument would have to be a generation of people, and running it consumes the alternative it was meant to compare against. There is no vantage outside. Every reading is taken from inside the thing being read, by someone the reading has already changed.

This is what distinguishes the invariant from a threshold. *"The minimum viable gravity for gestation is 0.38g"* is a number, and a number is in principle readable — that formulation fails the affordance test and collapses the world into direct realism, where the injustice is a value the system got wrong and a better authority could correct. **The setting must never contain that number.** What it contains is people who had to decide before it could exist, and who cannot now be shown what they gave up.

## 3. The substrate

The invariant lives in **bodies that grew somewhere** — the second generation and everyone after. They are the only place the answer is written, it cannot be read out of them without waiting the length of their lives, and by then it is an answer about a world that has moved.

**The name in the world's mouth: *grain*.** How a body grew, in the sense wood has grain — a direction set during forming that can be worked with or against but not reset. Usage is relational and felt, never measured:

- *grown with the grain* / *against it*
- *she has no grain yet* — a child too young for how she turned out to have shown
- *reading someone's grain* — the intimate, unreliable, deeply rude act of guessing where a person was raised from how they move
- *cross-grained* — a body carrying two protocols, usually because a family moved

Grain is not a score, has no units, and cannot be certified. A clinician can say a great deal about a body and still not say this.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence** (the seed Stage 4 grows from):

> *You put your hand on your daughter's back while she sleeps and you cannot tell — you will not be able to tell for forty years, and by then she will have made the same decision about her own — whether the way you raised her was the way it had to be done or only the way your station happened to do it.*

## 4. The missing floor

*Detector B fires. Name it even though nothing in the physics writes it down.*

> **Every standard in this world is measured against a body that formed on one planet, at one mass, under one star — a baseline nobody chose and no one can defend, which is simply where the species happened to occur. There is no neutral floor beneath the comparison. All divergence is scored as deviation from an accident.**
>
> **Somebody decided what nothing weighs.** Somebody set the level that is read before anything is put on the scale, and every measure in this world is read up from that setting, and there is no setting beneath the setting. It is not a lie. It is a choice with the hand hidden — and the whole art of the institutions built on it is stamping the seal over the hand, so that nobody can see whose it was.
>
> **Falsifier:** if the story frames the injustice as a local error a better authority could correct — a standard set wrong that could be reset fairly, a bias in the reference range — the floor invariant was lost.

The floor is what makes the medicine, the liability law, the insurance, the migration rules, and the word *normal* all point the same direction without anyone having aimed them.

**Floor inhabitation sentence:**

> *You are reading a chart against a range, and the range describes a place your patient has never been and you left twenty years ago, and there is no other range, and you write the word down.*

## 5. Naturalized as physics: what this forbids

The invariant is the ground the collisions happen on, not a rule anyone obeys. **If it acquires an administrator, it has become a Snare and the module has failed.** Specifically forbidden across the whole setting:

- **No central program.** No Gravity Trials, no Bureau, no single named institution running the question. The moment one body owns it, the story becomes about that body's corruption — a legible injustice with a villain and a fix.
- **No allocation, licence, queue, quota, or entitlement as the world's spine.** An administered reproductive permission is a *number a system got wrong*; it re-seeds counting and pulls everything toward correctable bias. It may exist as **one polity's local answer**, where it functions as a foil and can be seen from outside — never as the setting's central mechanism.
- **No decisive paper, no reveal, no verdict.** The question does not get answered on stage. Fragments arrive, contradict, get used.
- **No character who is simply right.** Positions differ because they stand differently, and each reading is true from where it is taken.

## 6. What the invariant generalizes to

Gravity is the invariant's most intimate instance — the one that reaches a body — but it is not the only one, and a collection that uses only gravity will repeat itself. The same structure holds wherever commitment outruns evidence:

ice reserve estimates · life-support closure claims · cumulative dose limits · debris and collision risk · the composition of a body no one has sampled · which depot will still be operating in thirty years · whether a settlement's founding compact was necessity or preference

Each is a candidate **seat** for a story: one reference reality, audited against, with the cast arranged around it at different gauges. The world stays constant; the seat changes; nothing repeats.

# Module 4 — Economic Geography

*Fourth of eleven. Depends on Modules 1–3. Produces the position map that Modules 5, 7 and 9 build on.*

---

## 1. What actually settles

Currency exists. It is thin, and everyone treats it as a convenience rather than a fact — a way of keeping score between settlements that clears against something real. What clears is **irreversibilities**: things that, once spent, are gone in a way no payment reverses.

- **Mass moved** — propellant burned to put a thing where it now is. It does not come back.
- **Dose taken** — a body's accumulated exposure. It has no ledger the body can read and no way to spend downward.
- **Years grown** — the time a person spent forming somewhere. Unrepeatable, and it determines where they can go afterward.
- **Loop margin consumed** — the reserve a habitat had before you arrived and doesn't after.

**And the instruments that track them are monotonic.** A dose ledger, a term record, a cohort entry, a standing — each accumulates and none has an operation for removal. There is no field for subtraction, no null, and no way to decline. A person can refuse an assignment and the refusal is recorded; a person can give a credit away and the giving is recorded. **The only zero available is the one you start with, and it is spent by existing.**

**And its response to any human-scale event is negligible.** Someone is grounded, repatriated, or dies, and the account adjusts — favourably, by an amount that does not change anything and would round away in a summary. Not enough to matter. Barely enough to notice.

This is the ledger stating, in its own units and without malice, what a person is worth against the thing they were being measured by, and it is why no individual act registers in it: **the instrument's resolution is coarser than a life.** A character who checks after a loss, expecting the number to have moved, finds that it did, and that the movement is the cruellest fact available to them.

**And the ledger grows fastest when a person can least pay, because survival is billed to the survivors.** After an emergency, the costs of surviving it are real and someone must carry them: the temporary berths, the extra consumption, the loop run past its margin, the shifts covered. They are itemized and apportioned across the people who were sheltered, divided evenly, and added to each account. **Nobody decided this. It is an apportionment, correctly performed**, by someone whose job is to apportion things.

So the emergency that threatened you also bills you for having been threatened, and the bill lands in an instrument with no operation for subtraction. This is the cruelest arithmetic in the setting and it requires no villain at any point in the chain.

*It also reaches the rescue norm* (Module 9 §11). An itemized rescue is a rescue with an invoice. Once the ledger exists, being rescued becomes a debt — which makes declining rescue rational, and makes the person who calls for help the person who has cost everyone something. The ledger does not only turn hesitation into policy on the responder's side. It gives the drowning a reason not to signal.

This is why a character who wants to divest cannot. Asked to be set to nothing, an administrator will turn the tablet around and show them a field with a value in it and no way to empty it, and will not be being obstructive — there is genuinely nowhere to put what is being asked for. And it is why the most meaningful thing a person can do is often work that registers nowhere at all, which the reader can see accumulates nothing and the character cannot.

This is why contracts read strangely to newcomers. A settlement does not owe another settlement money; it owes it *lift*, or *reserve*, or *a term*. Money is what you use when nobody trusts the relationship enough to denominate in the real thing.

The connection to Module 3 is not decorative: **the economy runs on exactly the class of expenditure that forecloses its own comparison.** You cannot spend a term and also see what the term would have cost you elsewhere.

## 2. The four flows

**Power is the master commodity, and everything else is downstream of it.** Electricity converts into thrust, into water split for propellant and air, into heat, into refined metal. A settlement's real wealth is its generating capacity and the people who can keep it running. This is why the sun's thinning (Module 2) is the system's deepest economic line: inside it, power is a bill; outside it, power is a reactor, and reactors are rare, expensive, politically fraught, and dependent on fuel and expertise that mostly still comes up the well.

**Volatiles are the medium of exchange.** Water is propellant, air, drinking, cooling, and shielding — the only commodity that is simultaneously fuel, life, and armour. Ice deposits are therefore not resources in the ordinary sense; they are *ports*. Whoever holds one holds a place where journeys can continue.

**Mass is structure and shielding, and it is cheap where it is and priceless where it isn't.** Regolith, slag, spent hardware, and waste all serve as radiation bulk. A settlement's rubbish is its armour. Nothing is discarded; things are *emplaced*.

**Closure is the slow one.** Water recycles well, air adequately, food barely. Every settlement is somewhere on a curve from fully-fed-from-outside to nearly-independent, and where it sits on that curve is its actual sovereignty, whatever its charter says. A polity that imports its calories is a polity with a leash, and everyone in the system understands this arithmetic without needing it explained.

## 3. The asymmetry of the well

Down is cheap. Up is not. This single fact organizes the whole economy.

Getting to Earth requires almost no energy — you already have it, and the atmosphere takes it away for free. Getting off Earth costs everything. So the flow that exists is: **capital, expertise, fabricated goods, and certification travel up; almost nothing material travels down.** Extracted mass stays where it was extracted, because moving it down destroys its value and moving it up was never the point.

**And it reaches past the person into whatever they are attached to.** An obligation does not stop at the body that incurred it. It reaches through — to a family's holding, a hold's berth, a section's allocation, a partner's standing — and takes whatever is nearest and seizable. **The people who lose the thing usually had no relationship with the institution at all.** They did not sign, were not employed, and in many cases never understood what the person was doing while they were away.

The collection completes correctly. The asset is taken, valued, sold at something under what was owed, and the remainder is written off as not worth pursuing. **The file closes. The arithmetic is satisfied. Nobody was cheated**, and the family is now tenant where it was holder.

**The domestic consequence is the part that matters, and the bible has it nowhere else.** The person comes back with a ruined body and an obligation that has just cost their family everything, and cannot explain. The quotas, the compounding, the way the interval kept closing — none of it is sayable to someone who was not there, and every attempt sounds like an excuse. **They were trying, every day, and it was not enough, and that sentence is unusable.** So what they say is *I'm sorry*, repeatedly, until someone tells them to stop.

And the family is not wrong and not cruel. From where they stand: money was supposed to come home, none did, more was borrowed, the land is gone. Every element true. The attribution problem (Module 6 §4) arriving at the one place where being understood was the only thing left.

**Exit is available in the other sense, and the obligation travels.** A person can simply go — walk off a shift, take a berth, stop showing up. What they owe goes with them, and there is nowhere the record does not reach: the entry, the standing, the flag, the terms. They are not pursued. They are simply *findable*, permanently, by anyone who checks, and everyone checks.

Which makes **returning** the worse condition (Module 8 §3). A person who left and comes back cannot be readmitted, because they are no longer of the place and their obligation is no longer held in the only shape that made it survivable — inside an arrangement that was slowly discharging it. They arrive at the gate as somebody with a claim against them and no position to work it off from, and the people turning them away are not being cruel. There is genuinely no procedure for it.

And then the trap, which is the setting's cruellest structure: **the cheap direction is the one many bodies cannot survive arriving in.** A person grown or long-resident in low gravity can be delivered to Earth for almost nothing and will find that the journey they could always afford is the one that breaks them. Repatriation is a threat, a mercy, and a sentence depending on who is saying it. The door is open, it costs nothing to walk through, and it only opens one way.

## 4. Chokepoints

| Chokepoint | Held by | What it actually controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Advanced fabrication** — fine-process and radiation-hardened electronics | A handful of terrestrial regions | Every control system, every sensor, every reactor's instrumentation. The deepest dependency and the least visible |
| **Fissile fuel and reactor competence** | A very few states, jealously | Whether a settlement can exist past the sun's thinning at all |
| **Biological stock** — seed lines, cultures, pharmaceutical precursors | Terrestrial institutions and archives | Whether a closed loop can be repaired when it fails, which it does |
| **The catalog** — trajectory approval, conjunction custody, the record of what is where | Whoever runs the sensor networks | Movement itself, exercised without force, deniable as bookkeeping |
| **Ports** — accessible ice and the depots on it | Local holders, contested | Whether a journey continues or ends |
| **Certification of bodies** — who is fit for what work, where | Terrestrial-referenced medical boards | Labour mobility, and therefore everything |

The last two rows are the ones that generate stories, and the last row is the floor invariant from Module 3 operating as economics: **Earth does not need to own anything offworld, because it defines what a healthy human is, and every employment, insurance, and migration decision in the system resolves against that definition.**

The catalog deserves its own note. It is the only chokepoint that is purely informational, which makes it the only one that can be closed without anyone visibly doing anything. An approval that takes eleven weeks instead of three is not a refusal. There is no act to point at. This is the setting's characteristic form of coercion and it should almost never be dramatised as a decision — it is a queue, a workload, a staffing issue, a policy under review.

## 5. Node types

**These are kinds of place, not kinds of polity.** Module 5 supplies the political forms, and the two axes are independent: a spun habitat (place) is usually but not always governed as a compact (form); a works may be run as a charter or, after a rotation regime lapses, as a hold. When a story needs both, name the place first and let the form be inferred from how people behave.

- **Ports.** Sited on ice, not on value. Boom-and-bust, transient population, the worst labour conditions and the highest wages in the system.
- **Works.** Deep-buried industrial settlements: power, refining, fabrication of coarse goods. Stable, hierarchical, and where terms are served.
- **Spun habitats.** Rotating structures. Expensive, self-consciously permanent, the only places that plan in generations, and the ones most invested in believing they were right to.
- **Stations.** Small crews at nodes and junctions. Isolated, formal with outsiders, unbearably intimate inside.
- **The well.** Earth, which does not think of itself as a node and is not organised around any of this.

## 6. The rotation economy

Most work offworld is done on **terms** — a fixed period after which a body is returned, reassigned, or grounded. The term exists because employers manage exposure, liability, and the medical consequences of long residence, and it is defended in the language of care.

It also does something else, which nobody designed and everybody relies on: it prevents populations from forming. A workforce on terms does not have children, does not accumulate local claims, does not develop a grain, and does not become a people with standing. **Where terms are enforced, settlement does not happen. Where they lapse, it does.** Every genuinely inhabited place in the system is somewhere a rotation regime failed, was evaded, or was never affordable to enforce.

The extraction here is not a queue or an allocation, and must never be written as one. It is a term served in a body, felt as fatigue, a taste, a slowness on the stairs, a doctor's careful phrasing — and settled afterward, in a jurisdiction that references a body formed on another world.

*Guardrail: rotation is a labour and health institution. It is not a reproductive licensing system, and no polity's rotation regime may be written as one. Where reproduction is administered, it is one polity's local arrangement (Module 5), seen from outside, and never the setting's spine.*

## 7. Where the band questions land

The payoff. Each of Module 3's undecidable questions resolves differently by position — not because anyone is lying, but because position supplies the reading.

| The question | Port | Well | Spun hold | Earth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is the protocol required? | Nobody asks; nobody stays long enough | It's what the company certifies | It is the reason we exist, and we paid for it | A research question with funding attached |
| Is the reserve estimate honest? | Our survival; we take it on faith and resent it | An input to a production plan | Someone else's problem | A market signal |
| Is the corridor closure prudent? | It's the difference between a season and no season | An operational annoyance | Correctly cautious | Sound governance |
| Was the founding austerity necessary? | No founding to remember | It's called the standard | It's called the compact, and it is sacred | It's called a historical decision |

Read down a column and you have a worldview. Read across a row and you have a scene in which nobody is wrong.

- **Ω_E03** — Whether any settlement has achieved genuine food closure. Recommend: one claims it, the claim is contested, and the contest cannot be settled from outside — a band question in its own right.
- **Ω_P03** — How present Earth should be. Recommend: rarely on stage, constantly determinative. Earth is a weather system, not a character.

# Module 5 — Political Ontology

*Fifth of eleven. Depends on Modules 2–4. Supplies the institutional field for Modules 7 and 9. Revised: institutional forms retyped on the commitment-systems taxonomy.*

---

## 1. Sovereignty is closure

Nobody offworld holds territory in a way that would satisfy a terrestrial lawyer, and the argument about whether they could has run, unresolved, for as long as anyone has been out there. What settles in practice is never written down: **a polity is as sovereign as its loop is closed.**

A settlement that grows most of its food and makes most of its air can refuse things. One that imports calories cannot, whatever its charter says, and both parties know it without discussing it. The political map is not a map of claims. It is a map of dependency, redrawn quietly every time a harvest fails or a reactor comes online.

**Closure figures are therefore the most guarded and least verifiable information in the system.** Everyone overstates. Everyone knows everyone overstates. Nobody can check, because checking would mean living inside the loop for a year, and by then you are part of it. Sovereignty here is a provisional status assigned under unavailable evidence, like everything else that matters (Module 11 §7).

## 2. Charter forms

| Form | Origin | Closure | What it cannot afford to believe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Charters** | Corporate or consortium industrial settlements, staffed on terms | Low, deliberately | That the people serving terms are a population |
| **Compacts** | Spun habitats founded on a written agreement, usually by people who left somewhere | High, and central to identity | That the founding austerity was a preference |
| **Ports** | Whoever reached the ice and stayed | Very low, transient | That the deposit is finite on any timescale that matters |
| **Holds** | Crews and families who inherited a station and never left | Middling, improvised, robust | That their claim rests on nothing but continuous occupation |
| **Programmes** | National operations under terrestrial law | Low, subsidised | That legal strength is inversely proportional to practical relevance |
| **The well** | Earth | Total, unremarked | That anything offworld is a polity rather than an industry with staffing problems |

A polity is defined by the belief it cannot afford to abandon, because abandoning it converts a spent irreversibility into a mistake. This is why they behave irrationally in ways entirely rational from inside.

**Holds matter most and are least legible.** Module 4 established that inhabited places are where rotation lapsed. Holds are what that looks like after two generations: no charter, no founding document, no claim anyone recognises, and an unbroken record of having been there — which turns out to be the only claim the system's practice honours.

## 3. How each form holds its commitments

Forms differ less in what they believe than in **how the belief is held**, and the how determines the failure. Five architectures, and the setting is neutral about which is better — each is adaptive in the right environment.

**Charters run on marked revision.** They have procedures. Policies are proposed, checked, adopted, superseded, and the revisions are competent and continuous. What kills a charter is never in the procedure's scope: the drift that matters is a workforce becoming a population, which is not a policy question and cannot be tabled. They revise excellently, on everything except the thing.

**Compacts run on anchored fixity**, and this is the module's most important split, because it gives one form two fates.

- *With an interpretive layer beneath the document*, the compact persists indefinitely. The founding text stays unrevisable and untouchable, and a substructure of readings, precedents, and customary exceptions quietly absorbs everything the environment changes. Nobody ever amends the compact. Everybody knows what it now means.
- *Without one*, the compact is brittle. The document is supposed to govern practice directly, so gap accumulates invisibly and the failure, when it comes, is not gradual — the environment presents something the kernel cannot govern, and the whole arrangement fails at once, at full strength, in a single season.

**Both look identical from outside**, and mostly from inside, right up until the second kind breaks.

**Holds run on implicit practice.** There is no codified kernel; the kernel is whatever they do; authority is grounded in the practice itself. Drift is not a failure mode here — it is the mechanism, and a hold changing is a hold working. The failure is specific and severe: **when practice loses coherence there is no fixed referent to reconstruct from.**

**And the referent is not merely absent — it is a sediment of private improvisations with their reasons destroyed.** Every part of what a hold knows arrived the same way: somebody, alone, at some hour, did the only thing available to them, and it held, and it was passed on, and then they died and the reason went with them. What a keeper inherits is not knowledge. It is other people's decisions, received faithfully, each of which was contingent and none of which can now be checked.

**Fidelity therefore requires not checking.** To audit the practice would be to stop being someone who carries it — you cannot verify a designation without treating it as a claim, and treating it as a claim is already outside the relation that makes it binding. This is the invariant arriving in transmission rather than in measurement, and it is why a hold's refusal to write things down is not stubbornness. Writing it down would convert improvisation into record, record invites audit, and audit is the end of the thing. A hold that loses its people does not lose its government. It loses its constitution. This is why the unwritten emplacement map (Module 8) is load-bearing rather than sentimental, and why holds refuse to write it down: writing it converts them into something else.

**Ports run on diffuse reconstruction.** The kernel is under-specified, no authority adjudicates, and many parties produce incompatible readings claiming the same source. The pattern persists indefinitely without ever achieving operational coherence, and it survives because the ambiguity serves everyone who benefits from it.

**The catalog and the certification boards run on principle-anchored accretion.** Formal apparatus — tests, standards, holdings — with no canonical text, developed through a precedential lineage. This is why nobody can locate the decision that harmed them: there isn't one. There is a lineage, and each link was reasonable.

## 4. The law of acknowledgment

The general law beneath every institution in the setting:

> **The people with standing to acknowledge drift are usually the people whose standing depends on there being none.**

A board that admits its standard is provincial undermines its claim to be measuring anything. A compact that admits its austerity was a preference undermines the sacrifice. A charter that admits its workforce is a population acquires a population. So institutions develop elaborate ways of not admitting drift — reinterpreting old commitments to mean new things, or doing something different while insisting nothing has changed.

Whether a system survives depends on whether its **acknowledgment capacity matches the rate at which its environment produces drift**.

**Here, it never does, and this is derivable rather than asserted.** The drift rate is set by physics — margin calls, ratchet crossings, cohorts aging out, deposits declining — and is not negotiable. Acknowledgment capacity requires the people who would have to name a thing to be in a room together, and distance dissolves that continuously (Module 9 §5). Every institution in the system is structurally under-capacity, permanently.

**That is why the husk dominates.** Not authorial preference. Consequence.

## 4b. Agreement arrives faster as things thin

A consequence of §4 that nobody in the setting has articulated and several people are close to.

A rich arrangement is slow to agree with itself, because there is a great deal in it to argue about. As it hollows, the arguing stops — not because anyone was silenced, but because the things that used to generate disagreement are no longer there. **A depleted institution reaches consensus faster than a healthy one, and the consensus is real.**

So the smoothest polity in the system is the emptiest, and its people are not deluded: they agree because there is nothing left to disagree about. Harmony is a symptom, and it is indistinguishable from health at any single moment (Module 3) — the only available diagnostic is one nobody thinks to run.

*When did we last argue about this?* A character who asks that question out loud has done something dangerous, and will mostly be told that things have simply settled down.

## 5. Blocs

Blocs form by **complementarity, not affinity**. The four things a functioning ecosystem needs — generating capacity, volatiles, fabrication, and people who can run the first three — are never held by one party.

- **Membership is unsentimental.** A partner who loses their reactor loses their seat, and nobody pretends otherwise. Bloc rhetoric is thin and nobody believes it, including its producers.
- **Alignment is not exclusive.** A hold may sit in one bloc for propellant and another for fabrication and treat the contradiction as ordinary.
- **The instability is structural.** Each bloc works to internalise the inputs it depends on, which if successful dissolves the reason it existed. Every bloc is working toward its own obsolescence and cannot stop.
- **Two treaty frameworks exist, neither universal.** Most working arrangements are bilateral and ignore both.
- **Terrestrial politics do not survive the vacuum** (Module 9). Flags are paperwork. Two workers from rival programmes at the same port share an exposure and a loop, which beats a sponsor.

## 6. The catalog regime

Someone must track what is where, since orbits are predictable and collisions are not survivable. The function is real, universally relied on, genuinely coordinating. It is also held by whoever operates the sensor networks, which means the body that prevents collisions also approves trajectories.

**Its power is not the power to decide. It is the power to determine what counts** — a comparable urgency, a valid claim, a person with standing to make one. Distance prevents direct aggregation, so authority passes to whoever renders dispersed people legible, and the form produces a decision-shaped world before anyone enters the room.

From inside it is a safety service run by tired people with a backlog. To a small hold waiting eleven weeks for routine clearance it is the wall. Both are true.

Write it as bureaucratic weather. There is no scene in which someone decides to obstruct you — there is a queue, a staffing shortfall, a policy under review, a form returned for correction. **The catalog never refuses.**

**And it has an organ for what it cannot categorise.** Reports that fit no existing domain are not rejected — they are received, and marked pending, and held. There is no assigned reader. There is no timestamp on the pending status. The queue for unassignable items and the queue that would assign them are, on inspection, the same queue.

This is not neglect and not corruption. **It is how an institution maintains itself against phenomena it has no instruments for**, and it works: the report exists, the filer has discharged their duty, the record is complete, and nothing happens. A character who submits a correct, precise, well-evidenced report and receives *pending response* has not been ignored. They have been processed.

Cross-domain is where this bites hardest. Each domain measures accurately and reports truthfully; the thing itself is visible only in the gap between two instruments; and every domain's protocol says that if a cross-domain pattern requires action, another domain will initiate it. Everyone is correct. Nobody is responsible. **A story can be built entirely out of two competent people discovering they cannot jointly see a thing both of them are looking at.**

And note its architecture: accretion is adaptive. The catalog is not dying. **It is compounding**, which is worse.

## 8. What is genuinely unsettled

Unsettled because settling it would cost more than living with it.

- Whether a person born somewhere is of that place in any sense with force

Each can start a story as paperwork and end it somewhere unrecoverable.

## 9. The foil

Its function is to be **seen from outside by someone who cannot unsee it**, and to fail, when tested, in a way that has nothing to do with corruption. The horror is that it works as designed.

*Guardrail: one polity, a minority of stories, never the spine. If the collection starts reading as being about reproductive licensing, the foil has become the frame.*

# Module 6 — Bodies

*Sixth of eleven. Depends on Modules 2–5. Where* grain *stops being a word and becomes physiology.*

---

## 1. What a body knows

The body is the setting's most reliable instrument and the one with no standing. It registers everything and can certify nothing.

People know their own condition the way anyone knows their own condition — by what has become harder. Stairs, first. Then the way a cut takes longer to close than it used to, and the cold sores that come back when the shift pattern turns bad, and the sinuses that never quite drain, and the way food stops tasting like much so you salt it until someone comments. Vision changes and people mostly don't mention it, because mentioning it starts a process. The bones are the quiet one: nothing is felt until something breaks that shouldn't have, and then the whole history is suddenly legible in a single radiograph belonging to someone else.

None of this is narrated as a measurement. A person does not know their density or their dose. They know they have started taking the ladder one rung at a time and hoping nobody notices.

## 2. Grain

*Grain* (Module 3) is how a body grew: a direction set during forming, workable against but not resettable.

**Physiologically** it is not one thing. A body grown in spin, a body grown on a surface, and a body grown in freefall are three different outcomes, and they differ in ways that don't line up neatly — bone and posture and vestibular sense and cardiovascular tone all track differently, so a person can be robust in one respect and fragile in another. The folk taxonomies are all binaries and all of them are wrong in a locally convenient direction.

**Socially** it is read, constantly, and reading it is rude. Grain shows in gait, in how someone catches a dropped thing that curved, in whether they reach for a rail before they need it, in breath at rest. Spun-grown people have a way of turning corners. Surface-grown people plant. Everyone can do this and nobody admits to it, in the way that everyone places an accent.

**A clinic can produce a complete workup and still not tell you a person's grain**, because grain is not a finding — it is the whole shape of how a life turned out in a place, and it isn't finished until the life is. What a clinic produces is a set of values within or outside a range, which is a different thing, and the difference is the module.

## 2b. The hand

A second kind of unreadability, and it works in the opposite direction from grain.

Grain cannot be read by anyone. **This can be read, by some people, reliably** — and by others not at all, no matter how long they attend. Some workers hear a bearing going a week before the readout moves. Some clinicians feel a thing in a pulse that has no name and is there. Some fitters know a suit is wrong from the way it hangs. The people who have it agree with each other. They are right at a rate that cannot be luck.

**And it cannot be taught, though where to attend can be.** A good teacher will show a student exactly what to reach for, name every distinction, and mark precisely which one they missed — and the student will be able to say afterward what they should have felt and will not have felt it. **Teaching widens the gap rather than closing it**, because the student learns in detail the dimensions of what they lack. Three years of honest instruction produces a person who can see the boundary of their own perception at high resolution and cannot cross it.

**The cruelty is that the student is not failing.** Their reading is correct for what they perceived; their work is competent; their outcomes are adequate and genuinely adequate. There is no error to correct, no negligence, no harder trying available. *You were not wrong. You were competent* is the exact sentence, and it closes every door at once.

**And it cannot be certified, which is why it wrecks people quietly.** No board tests for it. It confers no standing (Module 6 §6) and appears in no instrument (Module 4), so a person can spend years and a marriage acquiring the knowledge that they do not have it, and there will be no record anywhere of what they were reaching for or that they failed to reach it. What the record shows is a competent practitioner with an unremarkable career.

**Craft note.** This is not a story about talent and must not be written as one. The register is *good enough* said flatly, over decades, by someone who is right that it is good enough and knows exactly what it is not good enough for. And the person who has the hand should be kind, precise, and unable to help — their honesty is the instrument that does the damage, and they know it, and telling the student less would have been worse.

## 3. Dose as biography

Exposure accumulates and does not reverse. A person's history is written in it: where they worked, how long, how well they were shielded, whether their employer was careful, whether they were ever caught outside during a bad stretch.

The ledger exists. It belongs to the employer and the boards. **The person does not hold their own record**, and this is so normal that it takes an outsider to find it strange. What people have instead is a shared vocabulary of consequence and a great deal of rumour — *he did four terms at a port*, said the way another culture would say *he was in the war*.

## 4. The attribution problem

The centre of the module.

A child is born and something is wrong. Or a worker's bones go before they should. Or a woman miscarries twice and then doesn't. Every one of these outcomes has five available causes — grain, dose, nutrition, the specific reactor she worked beside, plain luck — and **no procedure exists that can separate them**, not because the medicine is bad but because the comparison population was never raised.

So attribution goes where the social structure already points it. In a charter, it points at the worker's history before hire. In a compact, it points at whoever departed from protocol. In a port, it points at nothing, because nobody stays to ask. Everywhere, eventually, it points at the mother.

**And it can collapse inward instead of outward.** The social version distributes blame where the arrangement points. The private version is worse: **a person supplies their own candidate cause and can never eliminate it.**

A wish made over something. A decision not to tell. A shift traded, a word said or withheld at a particular hour, a silence held for three days. The candidate is always small, always theirs, and always survives every attempt to rule it out — because ruling it out would require the comparison that does not exist. The other four causes remain equally live and equally unprovable, so nothing about the fifth can be settled, and its holder is left with a permanent, unfalsifiable self-indictment that no evidence will ever touch.

**They cannot be talked out of it, and the people trying are not wrong either.** *It was the machine. It was the speed. It was this place.* All true, all complete, none of it eliminating the fifth candidate, and the person offering the comfort will often not believe it themselves. This is the attribution problem experienced as guilt rather than as injustice, and it is the more intimate of the two.

**And an account nobody can settle has to be told, which is how it spreads.**

A person carrying an unfalsifiable self-indictment needs to give the account to someone. Not for absolution — they will refuse absolution, correctly, since nobody is positioned to grant it. They tell it because a thing that cannot be adjudicated cannot be set down, and telling is the only motion available to it.

**It does not lift, and it does not transfer. It copies.** The teller is no lighter afterward. The listener now carries a version, and after enough tellings the listener sees the thing themselves — not as sympathy but as the same unresolvable object, with the same candidate causes and the same absence of any procedure that separates them. Two people now hold it. Then three.

**The listener passes it on without deciding to**, and the mechanism is this world's ordinary one: somebody notices a tell — a look, a pause, an odd instruction — and asks *why do you say it like that*, and the account arrives before the teller has decided anything. In a place where everything is read pre-verbally (§2), an unbearable thing moves the way grain does: by being visible in the body before it is available to speech.

**And there is an exit, which is why refusing it is a choice.** A finding, a ruling, an entry in a record would close the account and end the circulation. Whoever holds it usually has access to exactly that and does not use it — because a settled account cannot be moved, and while it can still be told it can still, for an hour at a time, be someone else's. That is not cowardice and must not be written as cowardice. It is a person choosing circulation over closure, knowing the cost lands on whoever is nearest.

Three rules follow, and they govern every medical scene in the collection:

- **No character ever learns why.** Not at the end, not in a document, not from a sympathetic specialist. The cause is not withheld; it is unrecoverable.
- **Doctors are not liars.** A good clinician in this world says *I can't tell you that*, means it, and is disbelieved — because the alternative to a cause is unbearable and a person needs one.
- **The blame is structural.** Whoever gets blamed is determined by the polity's arrangement, not by anyone's malice, and the person doing the blaming is usually kind about it.

## 5. Adaptation and its trap

Somatic and germline modification exists. It is expensive, marginal, and modest in what it achieves — better repair of radiation damage, bone maintenance under low load, some vestibular work for spun environments. Nothing dramatic, nothing that produces a visibly different kind of person, and it is pursued as ordinary adaptation to a place, the way people have always adapted to places.

**Some capabilities are installed rather than acquired, and they do not stay inside their brief.** An institution has a use for a faculty — steadier hands, faster hazard recognition, better tolerance — and arranges for people to have it. The specification is honest, the work is competent, and the result performs exactly as designed.

**And a faculty is not a task.** Whatever lets someone see a hazard forming lets them see anything else that forms the same way: a sequence of individually reasonable decisions arriving at a body, a pattern in who gets flagged, the shape of the thing they are standing inside. **Nobody built that. It is the same capacity, pointed somewhere the specification did not mention**, because the specification named an application and installed a faculty.

**The person who built it is inside what it reveals**, and is usually the first thing seen clearly — they are proximate, documented, and the most recent link in the chain. Their position offers them the ordinary defence, that they built what was contracted and delivered to spec, and it is true and it does not help.

**And this is the one case where clarity is not free** (Module 9 §9b3). Elsewhere the account is produced by the position and changes nothing. Here the seeing was manufactured, on purpose, by someone who then behaved as though seeing clearly were itself sufficient — and the person carrying it is not obliged to agree. *You built the question* is the accusation, and there is no answer to it, because the person who built the question did not think of it as one.

**Some adaptations run on something that runs out, and this is the sharper trap.** Not every adaptation is a one-time change. Some are maintained — a compound, a treatment, a regimen, a supply — and the maintenance works, exactly as promised, for as long as the supply lasts.

Three things make it worse than a straightforward dependency.

**The body changes underneath.** Extended maintenance remodels what it was maintaining, so the person is no longer returning to a baseline when the supply ends. There is no baseline left. What waits at the end is not the old body without the help; it is a configuration that requires the help and can no longer receive it.

**Everyone sees the wrong thing.** Colleagues see the tremor, the weight, the hours, and read overwork — accurately, since it is also overwork. Nobody sees the arithmetic, because the arithmetic is private and the person doing it is competent and has no reason to share it.

**And the person who could stop it is the person the adaptation replaced.** The one who chose it, weighed it, and could reconsider is progressively less present, and what is present has no use for the reconsidering. The old worry surfaces occasionally — *this is not enough time, we should stop* — and is filed as belonging to an earlier and less capable version. This is not weakness of will. **It is the capacity to make the comparison being consumed by the thing the comparison would have been about**, which is the invariant arriving inside one skull.

**And it is self-defeating, because of the floor.**

Fitness is certified against a reference body (Modules 3, 4). A modified body is, by construction, outside that reference — not worse, *outside*. There is no category for it. So the person who modified themselves in order to survive the work becomes, on paper, a person who cannot be certified for the work. The boards are not hostile; they simply have nothing to compare against and no procedure for producing one.

This gives the setting its transhuman layer without any of the usual triumphalism: **adaptation is real, it works, and it costs you your standing.** The people who do it anyway are doing it because they have already lost their standing or never had any — which means modification concentrates exactly where it is least protected, and reads to everyone else as confirmation that those people were always outside the standard.

## 6. Certification

The mechanism by which the floor invariant touches every life.

**And it moves under people.** The reference body is fixed; the rulings against it are not. Standards are clarified rather than amended, and a clarification governs bodies certified long before it. A person serving a long term is being reclassified the entire time they are away — not by anyone's decision, by an accumulation — and will come back to find the category around them narrower than the one they entered, with no hearing to attend, because nothing was decided.

A board issues fitness for a class of work in a class of environment, referenced to the range. The boards are careful and mostly decent and staffed by people who believe, correctly, that the ranges prevent deaths. Nobody in the system has ever proposed a different reference body, because there is nothing to propose one against.

**And it certifies states of mind by the same procedure, which is where it does its worst work.** A person's condition is read against a range: are they settled, are they cooperative, is the agitation resolved. The instrument is decent and the readings are accurate. **What it cannot distinguish is a person who has got better from a person who has stopped registering.**

Compliance reads as improvement because compliance is what improvement looks like from outside. So a body that has quieted because the thing in it went out is indistinguishable, on the record, from one that has quieted because the thing in it is at rest — and the record is the only account that will survive.

**Nobody falsifies anything.** The family who said the person was working too hard were right; they were. The readings are genuinely good. The cooperation is real and freely given. Every position in the chain reads its own instrument correctly, and the readings assemble into a finding of success that no one authored (Module 5 §6).

**And the subject cannot dispute it.** Their silence is entered as acknowledgment — the third form of the acknowledgment exception (Module 11 §4), and the worst, because the other two require someone to say something. Here the person acknowledges nothing; **their not-speaking is read as acknowledgment by whoever holds the instrument**, and to object they would have to contest a finding they have no standing to contest, in a register they are by then no longer fluent in.

This is the only mechanism in the setting that can harm someone without their being able to notice it happening, and the correct register for it is that everyone involved is pleased.

The consequences are ordinary and total: who may take a term, who may travel, who may be insured, who may be hired after a term, who may return, who is grounded. **Grounded** is the word, and it is used gently.

## 7. Bodies that fail

There are three outcomes and everyone knows all three.

**Grounded in place** — kept on, moved to lighter work, gradually to no work, supported by a polity that can afford it. Holds do this. Compacts do this and are proud of it. It is the good outcome and it is also a long slow removal from everything a person was.

**Repatriated** — sent down the well, which costs almost nothing (Module 4). For a body long resident or grown offworld this ranges from difficult to fatal, and which it will be cannot be predicted. It is offered as care and received as a sentence, and the people offering it usually mean it kindly. In the world's own vocabulary such a person is **carried** (Module 8) — the same word as freight on a manifest, and everyone hears it.

**Neither** — ports, mostly. The term ends, the contract ends, and the person is somewhere with no closure, no claim, and no passage.

## 8. Modes of attention

**Grounded people** watch other people's bodies with a specificity that reads as intrusive to newcomers. They notice a hand on a rail. They notice who takes the long way round. This is not cruelty; it is a population that has learned to read the only instrument it owns.

**People near the end of a term** develop a superstitious relationship to their own symptoms — cataloguing and dismissing in the same breath, negotiating with a process that isn't listening. They will tell you they're fine before you ask.

**Parents** watch children for grain, which cannot be seen yet, constantly, and hate themselves for it.

**The modified** carry a doubled awareness: how they actually feel, and how it will read. They become skilled at presenting an unremarkable body — a gait held slightly wrong on purpose, a reach corrected mid-motion, a hesitation suppressed at a cost that lands hours later somewhere else entirely. The skill is real, it works, and the people best at it are the ones you would never identify, which is why nobody knows how many there are, including the boards.

- **Ω_C05** — Whether *grounded* is a formal status or a social one. Recommend: formal in charters and compacts, social everywhere else, and the mismatch is a source of stories.
- **Ω_E05** — Whether modification is illegal anywhere or merely uncertifiable. Recommend: uncertifiable everywhere, illegal nowhere. Prohibition would give it an enemy; absence of category is worse and truer.
- **Ω_P05** — How much clinical vocabulary the prose carries. Recommend: very little. Symptom and consequence, almost never diagnosis.

# Module 7 — Violence

*Seventh of eleven. Depends on Modules 2–6 and 8. Written after the culture rather than before it, and inherits from it.*

---

## 1. There is no war

There is also no peace. There is a condition, and it has intensities, and the word people use for a bad one is something like *a stretch*.

The reason is structural and follows from three things already established. Nothing has a moment (Module 8). Nothing has a cause anyone can establish (Module 6). And the instrument of coercion is a queue that never refuses (Module 5). Put together, these mean that **the characteristic hostile act is one with a mundane explanation available** — and the mundane explanation is usually also true, because the same wear, delay, and error happen constantly without anyone intending them.

So violence in this setting is not an event. It is a rate. A settlement notices that things have been going wrong slightly more often for eight months, and that the pattern would be invisible to anyone not living inside it, and that saying so aloud would be a claim they cannot support.

## 2. Everything is dual-use

Nothing here was built as a weapon and nothing needs to be.

A tug moves mass, which is the only thing violence in space actually consists of. A survey radar illuminates. A repair drone opens things. Propellant is what you have and what everyone needs and what burns. A shielding contract is a chokehold. An inspection is a search. The catalog is a targeting system that also prevents collisions, and it is genuinely both.

Two consequences. **There is no arms race that anyone can point at**, because capability is indistinguishable from industry — a polity that can move ore can move anything. And **there is no disarmament**, because disarming means not having tugs.

Anyone who does build a purpose-made weapon has announced something about themselves that they cannot retract, which is why it is rarely done and enormously significant when it is.

## 3. The ladder

The escalation ladder is a ladder of **deniability**, not of force. Each rung is more damaging and less deniable, and the second property is the one that governs behaviour.

| Rung | The act | Available explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Delay** | Clearances slow. Inspections thorough. Forms returned | Backlog. Staffing. Policy under review |
| **2. Denial** | Sensor interference, comms degradation, a survey that keeps illuminating you | Environment. Solar conditions. Routine operations |
| **3. Interdiction** | Boarding, holding, inspecting, a vessel that is not free to leave | Safety. Compliance. Documentation irregularity |
| **4. Attrition** | Things fail. Seals, thermal loops, a depot's pumps. Slightly more often than they should | Wear. Bad parts. Bad maintenance. Bad luck |
| **5. Loss** | A vessel does not arrive. Nobody sees anything | Anything. Space is enormous and unforgiving |
| **6. Strike** | Open action against infrastructure — a depot, a reactor, a hull | None. This is a declaration and everyone knows it |
| **7. The unthinkable** | Mass on a trajectory | — |

**Rungs 1 through 5 are where the setting lives.** Almost every story that touches violence should sit there, and the ambiguity is not a writerly trick — the characters genuinely do not know, and half the time nothing was done to them at all.

Rung 6 is rare and terminal for whoever does it, not because of retaliation but because of §5.

## 4. Casualties are statuses

An attack does not produce dead people. It produces **held** people (Module 8) — an absence with a plausible cause and no recoverable moment.

This is the single most important consequence of the regime, because it means **the political act is not the attack. The political act is the status assignment.**

A polity that declares its missing crew *out* has said that something happened, and has thereby made a claim it must be prepared to support. A polity that keeps them *held* has said nothing, and everyone understands the silence, including the party responsible. Families are on the wrong side of this constantly: a hold's people know perfectly well their kin are dead, and the hold will not say so, because saying so is an act of foreign policy.

The reverse also happens. A polity that wants to escalate declares people *out* early, and the families are given a certainty they did not ask for and do not believe, and a wall section, and a date.

## 5. The deterrent: nobody comes

Everyone lives inside a pressure vessel. Everyone is fragile beyond the possibility of defence. But everyone is also self-sufficient in air and water and increasingly in food, so nobody can be starved out either. Siege does not work. Annihilation does.

That combination should produce a terrifying world, and it doesn't quite, because of what stands in the middle: **the rescue obligation.** You go. Whoever they are, whatever flag, whatever they did last season — you go, because next time it is you, and because a system in which people do not go is a system in which everyone dies alone eventually.

**The sanction is exclusion from it.** To be flagged as someone nobody comes for is the heaviest thing that can be done to a polity or a person in this setting. It requires no weapons, involves no act, is entirely deniable — the response was slow, the vessel was committed elsewhere, the flag was unrecognised — and it is understood by everyone as what it is.

This is the deterrent, and it has the shape of everything else here: **a sanction made of absence, applied by nobody in particular, which cannot be proved and cannot be appealed.**

It is also the setting's one genuinely healthy institution, and it is eroding by exception (Module 5) — flags excluded, then conditions attached, then a standard of documentation, then a delay that is only a delay. **The real trajectory of this world is not whether a war breaks out. It is whether "you go" survives.** That is the collection's largest arc and it should be visible in the background of stories that are about nothing else.

## 6. Who does it

Not soldiers. There are almost none, and the few that exist are ceremonial or terrestrial.

Violence is done by traffic controllers with a backlog, inspectors with a checklist, insurers with a schedule of exclusions, tug crews taking a contract, survey operators pointing an instrument, procurement officers choosing a supplier. **None of these people think of themselves as combatants and most of them are not wrong.** A controller who deprioritises a flag has done something with a defensible operational rationale, has probably not been instructed, and would be genuinely offended by the suggestion.

This is why it cannot be stopped by anyone deciding to stop. There is no order to rescind. And it is why the perpetrator in any story about it must be sincere, competent, and unaware — which is Module 5's structural blindness arriving as the mechanism of war.

## 7. The unthinkable, and why it is not morality

Anyone with a tug and patience can put mass on a trajectory. The energy is free; you are only redirecting what is already moving. It is the one act in the setting that is unambiguously decisive.

Nobody does it, and the reason is not restraint. **It forecloses the catalog.** Debris and unpredictability propagate, and everyone — including the party that did it, including their descendants — depends on the sky being predictable enough to move through. The taboo is infrastructural rather than moral, and everyone will tell you so, slightly too quickly.

There is one incident. Everyone references it. Nobody agrees on what it was, who did it, whether it was deliberate, or whether it happened at all in the form the story tells. It functions as scripture: cited constantly, examined never, and load-bearing for a prohibition nobody could otherwise justify.

*Design note: do not resolve it. A story may approach it. No story may establish it.*

## 8. Why nothing ends

The exit problem, in full.

Wars end when accumulated pain exceeds expected gain, and the fastest transmission from harm to political decision is bodies. Here there are no bodies — there are statuses, which are deniable, and industrial costs, which are financeable and diffuse. There is no attribution, so there is no casus belli and no war aim. There is no territory, so there is nothing to concede. And no polity has a public that can force a settlement, because most polities have workforces on terms rather than publics.

So conflicts subside and resume. They are described afterward as a bad stretch, or a period when things were difficult, or the year the seals kept going. The people who lived through them do not think of themselves as veterans, and there is no word that would let them.

# Module 8 — Speech, Time, and Parting

*Eighth of eleven. Depends on Modules 2–6. Supplies the shared cultural surface every story draws on. Revised after review.*

---

## 1. The organizing fact, and its fracture

Offworld death does not produce a corpse at a nameable time.

It arrives one of two ways. **Fast and total**: a loss of pressure, a strike, a fire in a place with nowhere to go — nothing recovered, nothing to look at, and a person who was present at breakfast is an absence with paperwork. Or **slow and administrative**: a grounding that took four years, a term that ended and a contract that didn't renew, a body that stopped being certifiable somewhere in the middle of a decade. There is no day on which it happened.

Neither gives what a funeral needs. The culture's whole apparatus of grief is therefore built for a loss whose moment cannot be located — the same shape as Module 6's attribution problem, and the same shape as the invariant. **These people are practised at mourning things they cannot date, and unpractised at closure.**

**And then the fracture.** Insurance, liability, inheritance, and pension all require a moment, so the procedural register supplies one. A determination is issued. It has a timestamp. Everybody involved knows the timestamp was manufactured by a process, and everybody must act on it — benefits commence, a berth is reassigned, a partner is free to marry, a claim expires. So there *is* a date. Nobody believes it. It is binding.

This is the module's live wound and the reason its customs are contested rather than merely various. Every practice below is a way of living beside a date the culture rejects and obeys.

## 2. Three registers, and the refusal

The linguistic strategy is **code-switching**, and the switch signals whether a person is speaking as a person or as a position.

**Procedural.** The language of the catalog, of certification, of manifests and clearances. Terse, standardised, stripped of affect by design, English-based with a sediment of Russian and Mandarin operational vocabulary from the first programmes that nobody thinks of as foreign anymore. It has no first person and no tense but the present. You speak it *to* the system and to anyone acting as the system. Speaking it to a friend is a slap.

> *Confirm on the manifest. Not carried. Not carried, confirm.*

**Working speech.** The creole that formed at ports and depots where crews from everywhere had a season to learn each other. Lexically promiscuous, grammatically flattened, fast. It is the language of labour and of most intimacy in the system, because most intimacy in the system happens between people who met eight weeks ago. Under pressure it breaks off rather than completes — a sentence simply stops where the missing moment would have been named, and the silence is understood and not filled.

> *She's cross-grained, that one — grew half a term up, half down. Reads wrong on the stairs and she knows you're looking.*

**Home speech.** Whatever a hold or compact carried up and kept: a terrestrial language, often generations out of date and preserved in forms the homeland abandoned. It marks lineage, is used for children and for grief, and outsiders are not taught it. It also retains older constructions that *presuppose a moment of death* — verbs that assume chronology. Using one in front of someone who has only ever lived offworld is either deep intimacy or a quiet cruelty, depending on what you are to them.

**The switch is tactical, not only emotional.** A superior dropping into working speech is doing a kindness and is understood to be doing one. A subordinate answering in procedural is refusing, and everyone in the room hears it. Home speech in a mixed group is a weapon.

**The refusal.** Not a fourth register — the exit from all three. Someone who normally switches freely and stops has said something, and what they have said is legible: a hold gone silent in procedural when a charter representative enters is not being deferential, and the representative will report that the mood was cooperative.

## 3. The grammar of absence

Because the date is not believed, people need working terms for what someone *currently is*. These are not legal categories. They are what you say to decide whether a person is still owed things.

| Status | The fact | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Out** | Dead or unrecoverably gone | May be emplaced, named, sworn by |
| **Carried** | Repatriated, transferred, shipped — alive, gone from the local future | The same word as freight on a manifest, and everyone hears it |
| **Held** | Missing, unconfirmed, detained, medically suspended, out of contact | No wake. Gear cannot be passed. A partner cannot openly begin again |
| **Unwritten** | Alive and no longer answering | The most contested state — abandonment, mercy, incapacity, or a death nobody has admitted |
| **Returned** | Someone treated as out, carried, or unwritten who comes back | Forces everyone present to disclose what they had decided |

Procedural recognises only **carried**, **not carried**, and **unresolved**. Working speech invented the rest because the three were unlivable.

**The assignment is the fight.** Calling someone *out* who others hold as *held* is an accusation — that you want their berth, their suit, their partner, their share — and is sometimes true. Whole disputes in this setting are nothing more than two people using different words for the same absence, each sincerely.

*Returned* is the most destabilising thing that happens to anyone. A returnee finds their section reassigned, their relationship ritually stopped, their name already spoken by the wall — and every person who moved on has to stand there while it becomes visible that they did.

## 4. Naming

| Position | Tradition |
|---|---|
| **Charters** | Crew designations, which stick. A worker is known by a section-and-number, used affectionately by people who never learned the other one. Given names survive only in home speech |
| **Compacts** | Children named from the founding document — phrases, clauses, signatories. A compact's population is a concordance of its own charter, and everyone can hear which passage you came from. **The names must be semantically inert with respect to the person.** A real clause-name is arbitrary: *Notwithstanding, Third Watch, Remainder, In Common, Save Where.* A compact does not name ironically and cannot see the future, so a man called Assent who has never refused anything is the author's joke wearing the compact's clothes, and it is the one place a story explains itself |
| **Holds** | Family names that are the station's name, or a founder's trade, or the thing that broke the year someone was born |
| **Ports** | Nicknames only. Nobody is staying. A person who acquires a real name at a port has been there too long and everyone knows it |
| **Earth** | Ordinary terrestrial naming, which reads offworld as strangely elaborate — inherited names for people who never had to prove they'd stayed anywhere |

**The taboo:** you do not name a child for someone *held*. Naming for the emplaced is honour; naming for the unresolved is understood as a bid to close a question that isn't closed, and the family of the missing will treat it as theft.

## 5. Time

There is no daylight and no seasons.

**The shift** is the real day, set by the loop and the work, and it drifts. People are chronically slightly out of phase with each other and treat this as ordinary.

**The window** is the year: departure opportunities opening and closing on the system's own schedule. *Two windows* is a duration people feel in the body. Children's ages are given in windows.

**The term** is the life unit. A person is *four terms in* the way another culture would say middle-aged.

None of these stay put. Shifts drift, windows slip, terms are extended by a clause nobody read. The anxiety this produces is specific and constant: not that time passes, but that it will not hold still long enough to be counted on — which is why a procedural timestamp is both offensive and irresistible.

## 6. Emplacement

You cannot ship a body anywhere. Mass is mass, and the rocket equation is indifferent to whose it was. Mass is also shielding. So the dead are **emplaced** — worked into the bulk that holds the sky off.

Each polity's practice states its politics, and each has a texture a child learns before anyone explains it.

- **Holds** keep it in memory, not in a record — *wall-kin*, the people whose dead lie in the same run of shielding. A hold-child learns the sections by watching where adults don't lean, and by one patch at hand height that is a half-degree warmer than the plate around it because it is touched so often.
- **Compacts** emplace formally, by the founding document's provisions, whole population present. There is a register, and its pages are worn unevenly.
- **Ports** emplace anonymously into the bulk, behind a hatch that is never opened. This is one of the reasons people leave ports.
- **Charters** cannot acknowledge the practice, because acknowledging it would mean the workforce is a population.

Nobody will tell you emplacement is sentimental. Everybody treats it as the most serious thing their polity does, and both of these are held at once without strain.

*By the wall* is the serious oath, sworn on the dead who are physically keeping you alive. It is not used in front of children.

**How the unwritten map moves: by interruption.** A hand on a child's arm at the right moment and nothing said. A body turned slightly so a person takes the other route. A pause that has to be filled in later or not at all. Nobody is ever told which section is whose; they are *stopped*, repeatedly, until the map is in the body, and they generally never learn why that particular plate.

**Which makes the method fragile in a way nobody inside it can see.** When there is no time, or no hand available, someone says a designation aloud instead — once, because they had to — and the method has changed. The person who did it experiences an inconvenience. The person who received it has been handed something in a form nobody before them received it in. **Neither can perceive this as an event**, and there is no record that would show it as one, and it is how practices actually mutate: not by decision, at a moment nobody marks, under pressure, in a form that felt at the time like a shortcut.

**It is not always available.** Remains can be contaminated, inaccessible, held as evidence, claimed by Earth kin, or removed by a charter for compliance — which everyone understands as preventing the dead person becoming wall-kin. Each polity has its scandal here, and each scandal is about who a body belongs to.

## 7. Parting

**The long form.** Ordinary partings are aggressively minimal — a nod, a word, nothing. Elaboration is reserved for a parting someone believes is final, so a full goodbye is a verdict. *He said the long form to me* is a sentence people repeat afterward in a particular flattened voice. Someone who receives one doesn't sleep that night, and the person in the next berth doesn't ask, and both of them know what the silence is.

Every long form contains one invariant: a release from obligation. *You owe no return.* Holds speak it beside a named section of wall; compacts enter it in a register; ports say it while sealing a locker; charters have an authorised corporate equivalent that everyone despises and everyone signs.

The long form never accounts for causes. Asking why someone is giving it to you is worse than receiving it — you have made them explain a verdict already delivered, and the explanation is always crueller. People who ask repeatedly are handled gently and avoided.

Giving the long form to someone who then returns is an injury that takes years to repair.

**And there is no form for a large thing that is not final.** Ordinary speech is minimal; elaboration is reserved for the long form; the long form is a verdict. So the culture has a complete apparatus for saying goodbye and **nothing at all for declaring.** A person who wants to say something enormous and ongoing — that they intend to stay, that they have chosen someone, that this will go on — reaches for the register and finds only the one that means it is over.

What people do instead is improvise, in public, badly. They borrow the long form and have it misheard as an ending. They say something procedurally and are understood to be refusing. They give an object, or take on a task nobody asked them to take, or use home speech in a room where it will be noticed, and hope. **The confession scene is structurally unavailable**, and what replaces it is someone attempting a form that does not exist while everyone present applies the nearest one that does.

The cruelty and the comedy of this are the same mechanism, and which one arrives depends only on whether the misreading can be recovered (Module 11 §6f).

**Stopping.** Correspondence at range arrives late and to a changed person, so every distant relationship has a point where writing ends. Nobody agrees where it is. A port stops within a window or two and calls it honest; to a hold this is erasure. A compact writes for decades and calls stopping a form of murder; to a port this is an ongoing claim on the living, and a refusal to let anyone go. Once two people have crossed that line in opposite directions, goodwill does not repair it. **This is the cleanest interpersonal engine in the setting — use it as one.**

**Late word.** Mail in transit outlives its sender. Letters arrive from the dead for weeks, and they ask questions, and they contain plans, and they arrive in bundles that smell of the depot they passed through. There is no custom about answering. Some people do, at length, for years. Whether someone answers is among the more revealing things about them — and when the sender was declared out on a manufactured date, a letter postmarked after it is either delay, fraud, or evidence.

**Worn-in.** Gear takes the shape of the body that used it: load paths, a grip, an adjustment someone made and never wrote down. This is the setting's real inheritance, in a place engineered to prevent inheritance. A shoulder harness creaks the way its last wearer always complained about. Gear fitting you badly in one specific way is how you know whose it was. **The suits remember when the manifests do not**, which is the quietest evidence that a charter's workforce is a population.

**The sending.** When someone is repatriated, the gathering is indistinguishable from a funeral, and nobody present can say which it is. The person attends. They are toasted. Someone gives the long form and the room hears the release spoken to a living man across a table. There is no form of words for the ambiguity, and the moment it becomes unbearable is always the same: the sending ends, and the person is still there, and has to be talked to.

## 8. Charters

Every practice here is a population's practice, and a charter cannot have a population (Module 5). So charter ritual is clandestine: emplacement unacknowledged, sendings off-shift in a maintenance space, a worn-in suit passed to someone on a different manifest, home speech used quietly and denied. Management knows and does not know — the blindness of a beneficiary position, sincere and structural rather than hypocritical.

**A charter where the ritual has surfaced is a charter that has stopped being one**, and nobody involved will have noticed the moment that happened either.

## 8b. What survives

An austere world that is only austere stops being inhabited and becomes a case study. These people are not enduring their lives; they are living them, and the collection needs the evidence.

**Food is the big one**, because closure is imperfect and anything grown locally is an event. A hold that has managed strawberries will make you eat one in front of them and watch your face. Trade in seed and culture runs on friendship rather than contract, and a good cook is a form of infrastructure.

**Jokes here have no punchlines, and this is structural rather than a failure of wit.** A punchline is a moment, and the culture has no apparatus for moments (§1). So the timed reveal — setup, misdirection, snap — is not a form these people produce; a story that gives them one has imported a shape from a world that can date things.

**The native comic form is the running joke**, and it is the same object as the husk (Module 11 §4). A phrase said every year that lands in the same soft place. A joke whose subject died two generations back and still gets the laugh. Something repeated so long that it now refers to the repeating. From outside, that is a practice with nothing at the far end of it. From inside, it is the funniest thing anyone has, and the warmth is real, and both descriptions are correct.

Which means **the collection's saddest mechanism and its funniest one are identical**, and any scene using either is available for the other at no structural cost.

**Freefall is funny**, and everyone who has spent time in it has a repertoire — the drink that gets away, the newcomer's first attempt at a sneeze, the specific dignity of an older worker executing a turn perfectly while a younger one bounces off a wall. Flirtation in freefall is largely about competence, and being caught by someone who was watching is the whole grammar of it.

**Spun habitats have a horizon that curves up**, and people who grew there find flat ground disturbing rather than beautiful. What they have instead of sunsets is the shift change in the agricultural sections, when the lamps come down slowly and the whole ring smells of wet green at once.

**And the dead are close.** Living inside them is not only grim. A hold-child who is frightened at night is taken to put a hand on the warm patch, and it works.

# Module 9 — Faction and Person

*Ninth of eleven. Depends on Modules 2–8. The roster every story draws its cast from. Revised after review.*

---

## 0. The short version

**Factions are interests grounded in spent irreversibilities. They cut across polities, they cannot combine at range, and nobody in the world calls them by these names.** The roster is §4; the person is §10; the derivation is §§1–3 and can be read later or never.

## 1. Madison's problem, and its inversion

Federalist 10 holds that faction cannot be prevented, because its causes are sown in human nature and its most durable source is the various and unequal distribution of property. You cannot remove the causes without destroying liberty or making everyone identical, so a republic must control *effects*. Madison's mechanism is the **extended sphere**: enlarge the polity until it contains so many interests that no majority can form, and until those who might combine cannot easily discover their common strength or act in unison.

This setting has more extension than Madison could have imagined.

**And the remedy produces the disease.** Majorities cannot form here — but neither can anything else. No aggregate interest is ever assembled, because assembling one requires a conversation, and conversation at range is correspondence between people who have changed by the time they are read (Module 8). So every polity is governed by whichever concentrated minority interest was physically present when a decision had to be made. **Nobody in the room experiences this as illegitimate**, because no visible majority is being overridden. Outside the room it is experienced very clearly indeed, by people who cannot assemble the perception into a force — which is a different thing, and the more useful one.

**Distance does not eliminate aggregation. It creates proxy aggregation.** Since direct majorities cannot form, authority passes to whoever consolidates dispersed signals into something actionable: catalogs, insurers, schedulers, standards boards, dispatch protocols. These are the setting's real rulers, and their power is not the power to decide. It is the power to determine what counts as a comparable urgency, a valid claim, a person with standing to make one. **The dashboard produces a decision-shaped world before anyone enters the room**, and nobody has to give an order.

**All factional action is post-mortem.** News is history. When a port fails, the sensor-holders know first, and by the time outrage reaches anyone dispersed enough to be outraged, the crisis is weeks over. Nobody at range can act in the present. The only people who can are the ones physically there, which is the same handful who were governing anyway.

## 2. The property that grounds faction

Madison locates faction's durable source in unequal property. Here property is thin (Module 4) and what people hold is **irreversibilities**: dose taken, terms served, years grown somewhere, loop margin consumed, a body's grain.

Faction is therefore grounded in *what a person has already spent and cannot recover*. Madison would recognise that interests differ because holdings differ, that they are permanent rather than negotiable, and that each holder reasons from theirs sincerely.

What he would not have anticipated: **these holdings cannot be liquidated, transferred, taxed, or redistributed.** A dose does not change hands. A grain cannot be sold. This removes every ordinary mechanism by which factional interest is softened — no compensation, no buyout, no settlement. An interest here is a body, and the only exit from it is the body's.

People try anyway. Insurers, arbitrators, and certification boards construct rough equivalences — so many additional terms against that much exposure, this concession against that adaptation — and everyone uses them and nobody believes them. The equivalences do not hold, they are recognised as fictions by all parties including their authors, and they are produced under pressure because deadlock is worse. **They are never written as numbers on the page** (Module 2). They appear as an argument someone makes in a room and loses slowly.

## 3. Nobody may be judge in his own cause, and everybody is

Madison takes as axiomatic that no man should judge his own cause, then observes that legislators do it constantly.

Here it goes further. Every consequential question (Module 3) must be decided before evidence can arrive, by whoever is positioned to decide, whose position determines their reading. There is no disinterested party — not because everyone is corrupt but because **the only people who know enough to judge are the people who have already committed.** A clinician with twenty years in a hold knows more about that hold's grain than anyone alive and is also its most invested defender. Same fact.

The characteristic injustice is not that interested parties judge. It is that no other kind of judge exists, everyone knows it, and the decisions must still be made.

## 4. The roster

Factions are **interests, and they cut across the polity forms of Module 5**. A compact contains several; a port hosts three; a person may belong to more than one incompatibly.

Each is defined by what it cannot afford to believe — abandoning it would convert a spent irreversibility into a mistake — and each has a circumstance in which a member must act against it. The betrayal does not purify anyone. It leaves them more compromised, holding a debt, having set a precedent that will hurt someone later.

| Faction | Stake | Cannot afford to believe | Must betray itself when | Drifts toward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Certified** | Standing built on the reference body | That the reference is provincial rather than universal | A certified parent must decide what their cross-grained child is excluded from | Settlement. They call it stability until the level changes |
| **The Outside** | Bodies that adapted and lost standing | That the standard is legitimate | A member needs certification for evacuation or treatment | Dispersion, per §5 |
| **The Catalog** | Neutrality, professionally held | That its service is asymmetric | Neutral queue order will kill someone the controller owes | Coordination-with-a-toll, unacknowledged |
| **Ice** | Deposits and the traffic they command | That the deposit is finite in the only relevant sense | Rationing would strand the workers extraction requires | Boom, then a claim nobody honours |
| **Power** | Irreplaceable competence | That it can be learned, shared, or audited | A failure requires handing authority to an outsider with local knowledge | Stable, and it knows it |
| **The Compact** | Generations spent on full rotation | That the founding austerity was a preference | A dependent needs an exception the document forbids | Husk — recited, unread, obeyed |
| **Surface** | Children already grown in low gravity | That grain diverges badly | A particular birth outcome makes denial personally impossible | Denial hardening into doctrine |
| **Rotation** | A workforce that is not a population | That it has already become one | The workforce must be abandoned and the dependents turn out to be theirs | Discovery, always too late |

**These are the writer's terms, not the world's.** Nobody calls themselves Certified or Ice. They are people who follow safe practice, who keep the queues moving, who know the plant, who have already paid for the place they are defending. The reader should notice only that everyone with a clean record says the same kind thing in slightly different sentences.

**The Certified and The Outside are the same wound from the side that names it and the side that bears the name.** Never make both protagonists in one story — pick a seat.

**The Catalog is the setting's Beneficiary voice**: warm, competent, likeable, genuinely helpful, structurally unable to see the asymmetry it administers. The reader must enjoy being inside that perspective. The asymmetry must stay invisible to the controller for as long as the story stays in their head; the cost appears downstream, or in a later story occupying a position the Catalog has quietly sorted.

**Terrestrial politics do not survive the vacuum.** Earth cares enormously whether a node is American or Chinese or European. Offworld, two workers from rival programmes at the same port share an exposure, a loop, and a season, and will quietly collude against both their sponsors. Flags are paperwork. Blocs are a thing that happens to procurement.

**What The Outside has instead of organisation.** Not a movement — an infrastructure of recognition that fails at collective action and succeeds at everything smaller. Clinicians' informal notes on bodies the standard calls anomalous. Suit fitters who know adjustments that are not supposed to exist. Circulation of worn-in gear along routes nobody wrote down. A repertoire of jokes, stair etiquette, and warnings. Particular repair bays and transit berths where cross-grained people keep being assigned. Parents routing children toward each other because no system will. They can recognise one another, and save one another individually, and cannot assemble into anything that changes a policy. That is worse than being unorganised. It is being organised at exactly the scale that cannot help.

## 5. Why factions cannot combine

**Concentration acts; dispersion doesn't.** Small groups with a large per-head stake organise; large groups with a diffuse stake do not, however much they collectively lose. Every faction holding a chokepoint is small and coordinated. The Outside is the largest *unrepresented* interest in the system and has no organisation, and the reason is arithmetic rather than apathy.

**Distance defeats combination directly.** A hold writes to another hold proposing they act together. The window closes. By the time the reply arrives, the sender has lost someone, or taken a contract, or had a child, and the reply is answering a hold that no longer exists — courteous, warm, and about something else. The letter is kept. It is re-read. It becomes a private grief of its own: the knowledge that solidarity was available for about six weeks and the geometry ate it.

**And a room can be started, not only used.** The other move available at that scale is to build a **second circuit** alongside the first: a small arrangement that does not oppose the existing one, does not compete with it, and was never meant to. Six people in a space too small for six. A standing thing that recurs. Nothing announced.

Four properties make it work, and each is the inverse of the arrangement it sits beside.

**It asks nothing.** No test to pass, no sincerity to demonstrate, nobody rewarded for having said a costly thing. Someone says the difficult sentence, it sits there, the talk moves on. Where the first arrangement requires performance and can detect a genuine thing said fluently as *rehearsed*, the second requires nothing and therefore cannot be failed.

**It recruits by ordinary invitation**, one person at a time, from people already at hand. *Same time next month? — Already told Finn.* Nobody joins anything. There is nothing to join.

**It does not threaten the original, which is why it survives.** The moment it becomes opposition it is legible, and a legible thing can be answered. Unnamed, it is invisible to an arrangement that has no category for it — the same protection the unrecorded practice has (Module 5 §3), and the same fragility: **naming it warmly from a position of standing would end it** (Module 11 §4).

**And it changes nothing.** The first arrangement continues, the exclusions hold, the person who built the second circuit is still outside the first and always will be. What changes is that a jaw unclenches, and the person notices only afterward that something hurts less.

**But solidarity at the scale of a room is always available, and this is not the same thing.** A shift crew, a bunk row, a bay, a watch — these people are permanently co-located, and nothing in the geometry prevents them from acting together. They can match pace instead of racing. They can make an infraction land on everyone or nobody. They can decide, without announcing it, that the quota will be met at the speed the slowest of them can survive.

This is the ordinary and unglamorous form of collective life in the setting, it is available in almost every story, and it does not scale past the room. It changes nothing structural. It changes what the structure costs the people in that room, which is not nothing, and the difference between those two sentences is the setting's entire politics.

**And the room's interest is not unitary, which is the limit on all of this.** Everything above assumes the people in the room want the same thing. Often they do not, and the split is generational and structural rather than a failure of nerve.

**A capability held by the young is a threat to the old.** If a few people can do the work substantially better — fewer hands, less consumption, higher yield — what they have built is the case for needing fewer hands. The people whose contracts, terms, and remaining years depend on the current headcount are not being timid when they oppose it. They are correctly identifying that competence at this scale is indistinguishable from a redundancy proposal, and that the ones made redundant will be whoever is slowest, oldest, and closest to the end of their usefulness.

So the elder's argument is not *don't make trouble*. It is: **better for everyone means fewer of us, and I know which of us.** Both positions are right, neither can be argued out of, and the younger one cannot act without spending someone else's remaining time. This is Founders and Inheritors (§8) with its consequence worked out — the inheritors' *capability* is what the founders cannot afford.

**And the coercion that follows never threatens the person who can act.** An institution facing a capability it did not sanction does not punish its holders; it offers them a choice between giving it up and having their families' obligations renegotiated (Module 4). The offer is genuinely the lenient one, made by someone reasonable, who would prefer not to do the other thing and means it.

**Nobody has to enforce this.** The people whose obligations are at stake will tell them to take the deal, and will be right, and the person who could have acted will hear it from the person they were acting for. That is the setting's most efficient form of coercion and it requires no threat at all — only the arithmetic being visible to everyone in the room.

**And the sharpest suppression comes from people who already escaped.** Those who got out — off the contract, off the register, off the books — survive on the fact that they are no longer connected to anyone inside. Coordination with people still under obligation would make them findable, and being findable is the one thing that would end them.

So they refuse it, and they are right to, and they enforce the refusal on their own: **someone who proposes reaching back is exiled, by the people he escaped with, for endangering all of them.** No institution had to do anything. The boundary is held from the outside by the only population with both the freedom to act and an absolute interest in not being associated with the people who cannot.

**This is more effective than any enforcement the institution runs**, because it comes from the one direction the trapped were still hoping for, and because the reasoning is unanswerable: the escaped have no leverage, only invisibility, and invisibility cannot be spent twice.

**What fails is combination across distance, not solidarity as such.** A story featuring successful large-scale organising has broken the setting unless it pays for it. The only legitimate exception is a temporary collapse of distance — a conjunction, a shared port season, a forced window overlap, a common emergency — and the coordination lasts exactly as long as the bodies remain co-located. Any story letting it survive the separation of its participants has broken the geometry. It is remembered afterward as a season, not a movement.

## 6. Two kinds of evidence

The correction that matters most for plotting.

**Operational evidence** — a failed seal, a dose spike, a valve behaving wrong, a blight — is incorporated fast and proudly, because competence requires it and because the consequence is immediate and observable. These people are excellent technicians and they revise constantly. A story where they don't is a story that has confused this setting with a stupid one.

**Constitutive evidence** — that the reference body is parochial, that a founding sacrifice was unnecessary, that a population was knowingly excluded — is a different object entirely. It is narrowed, contextualised, referred for review, attacked on methodology, and eventually converted into a reason for stricter control. Commitments here are public and irreversible, which is precisely the configuration in which disconfirmation intensifies conviction rather than reducing it.

A compact will replace a flawed pump protocol overnight and resist for four generations the conclusion that its rotation regime was not morally necessary. **Same people. Same week. Both responses are competent.**

So: **evidence is rarely a conversion. It is a test of what a person will protect.** A fragment suggesting a protocol was unnecessary produces a longer, better, more moving defence of it, delivered by the faction's most decent member.

## 7. Grain as durable inequality

Grain behaves like a **status distinction** — an honour ranking carried in the body rather than in income, which is why it survives changes of employer, polity, and fortune, and why insulting it lands harder than insulting someone's work.

Its mechanism is the ordinary one: a categorical pair (spun/surface, grown/served) onto which unequal rewards are mapped, then reproduced without anyone enforcing it — the favoured side quietly reserves the opportunities that maintain it, the other side adapts its expectations downward, and institutions copy the boundary forward because copying is cheaper than designing. Nobody intends the outcome. Every link is reasonable.

**On the page it is almost always pre-verbal.** A hesitation before a ladder. A reach that arcs wrong for the local gravity. A load-path that no longer matches the suit. Speech about grain is rare and usually a mistake, and an insult lands as physical recognition rather than as ideology. If a character is explaining grain, the scene is broken.

## 8. Founders and Inheritors

The fault line that runs inside every faction, and it is biomechanical rather than generational temperament.

A founder spent something. An inheritor **was spent upon** — carries the irreversible choice in the body, having made no choice, owing no allegiance to the reasoning that produced it. The founder defends the commitment with desperate, moving rhetoric because the alternative is that the cost was vanity. The inheritor experiences the same commitment as the shape of the room they were born in.

This is already encoded in the culture: compact children are named from clauses of a document they never signed (Module 8), and can hear which passage they came from every time someone says their name.

Neither side is right. The founder knows what it cost and the inheritor knows what it is, and these are different knowledges about the same object. **Most intra-factional conflict in the collection should be this**, rather than eight bespoke doctrinal splits.

**The one other general fault line is doctrinal rather than generational: whether to be legible.** It runs inside every faction, not between them. A keeper who wants a register so his people can be counted and owed things, and one who refuses because a counted person is a person somebody can budget against — both are keepers, both are right, and neither can establish which of them is protecting anyone (Module 2 §3). Where Founders and Inheritors divide by what a body was spent on, this divides by what a name is for, and the two cut across each other.

## 9. The Margin Call

A person can carry an unresolved bundle for decades in quiet, functional hypocrisy. Something must force it, and the forcing function is physical.

Conflicting loyalties are livable **exactly as long as there is slack**. A margin in the loop, a spare berth, a window with room in it, a reserve nobody is counting. The Margin Call is the moment slack vanishes — a crop fails, a window is missed, a storm arrives, a reactor derates — and the accommodations that let a person be two things at once stop being affordable.

Everything the setting forbids as plot machinery, physics supplies for free. **No antagonist is required to force a decision. The margin does it**, on its own schedule, to everyone at once.

## 9b. The one who declines to measure

A position the roster does not contain and the collection needs.

Someone holds the instrument, knows the protocol, has run it many times, and does not run it here. Not from cowardice and not from doubt — **because measuring generates a record, and a record routes, and routing requires a response, and they have seen what responses look like.** Monitoring and decoding are different actions, and a person can hold the difference precisely for years.

Three properties make this a character rather than an excuse.

**The not-doing accumulates its own record.** They keep a second track beside the official one — unlabelled, because labelling it would be the classification decision they are declining to make — and the count of the deferrals becomes a series as legible as the data.

**They are the only person who could have known**, and everyone downstream is living inside the consequence of a competence that was never exercised. Nobody will ever be able to name them, because there is no record of a thing not done.

**And when they finally run it, nothing changes.** The finding is correct, complete, the best work of their life, and it goes into the queue, and the queue holds it. The deferral was never wrong about what would happen. It was only wrong about what that made it acceptable to do.

**A related note on the analytical position generally.** Anyone who has been observing one thing for twenty years has been in contact with it for twenty years, and contact is not neutral. The auditor is part of what they audit; the record of their auditing is part of the record. There is no vantage outside, which is the invariant arriving at the one position that thought it was exempt.

## 9b2. What complicity is bought with

§9b explains why people decline structurally — acting is illegible, unattributable, and routes to a queue. That is true and it is not what a person feels. **What they feel is a price, and the price is made of ordinary goods.**

Name them concretely, because vagueness here is what turns this into a story about cowardice. The particular table. The nod in a corridor from someone who knows your name. A colleague who sits down without asking. A room with your name on the door. A retirement thing next month that people are already joking about. The route you have walked for twenty years. **Individually trivial, collectively a life**, and none of it appears in any instrument.

And the threatened cost is rarely punishment — it is **reassignment**. A transfer, a review, a position somewhere the same job is done among people who do not know you. Nobody is being cruel. The reassignment is standard, and it is correctly described as an opportunity.

So the person who declines is not protecting a principle or avoiding a penalty. They are protecting the specific, modest, unrecorded set of things that make a place bearable, and **they are not wrong that those things are worth something.** A story that suggests they are wrong has made the choice easy and lost it.

**And then they lose them anyway, and this is the part that must not be omitted.** The colleague transfers out. The nod stops. The door is no longer held. The retirement thing is postponed and then quietly not mentioned again. They take a different route now, to avoid the people who used to expect things of them.

**Nobody punishes them. Nobody says why. There is nothing to appeal.** The goods were never held by an institution that could withhold them — they were held by people, who withdrew them without deciding to, over weeks, in a way no one involved could have named at any point. The person paid for a life with an act of omission and the life left regardless, and there is no ledger anywhere that records either transaction.

## 9b3. Articulacy is not purchase

The single most reliable mistake a reader makes in this world is waiting for a character to understand their situation. **They already do**, usually better than the reader, and it changes nothing.

The pattern recurs at every position and is one mechanism rather than several. Someone recalibrates a baseline to absorb an anomaly and knows precisely that this is what they are doing (Module 2 §2). Someone writes a true and incomplete report and can state exactly which omissions make it incomplete and why (§6 of Module 11). Someone can name the distinction they cannot perceive, in the correct technical vocabulary, having been taught it by a good teacher (Module 6 §2b). Someone declines to measure and keeps an accurate second record of the declining (§9b).

**In each case the account is produced by the position it describes**, and issues from the same place, at the same time, under the same constraints. It is the next thing that position does. There is no interval in which the person steps outside, inspects, and returns — that interval is exactly what the world does not supply (Module 3 §1a), and understanding does not create it.

**And the account is often the most competent thing they produce.** Not a rationalisation and not self-deception: precise, unarguable, better than any outsider could manage, because nobody else knows the constraint as intimately. A person can hand you a complete and correct analysis of why they will do the thing, and then do it, and the analysis was not a warning and was never going to be.

**Craft consequences.**

*Never write recognition as a turn.* The scene where a character finally sees it is unavailable, because they saw it in chapter one and mentioned it in passing.

*Let them be the best analyst in the story.* Give the sharpest account of an arrangement to the person most caught in it. This is more disturbing than blindness (§9c) and more accurate.

*And never let the account cost them anything by itself.* Insight is free here. What costs is acting, and the account is not an act — which is why they can afford to be so exact about it.

## 9c. The one who sees and complies

Blindness is not the only way this world protects an arrangement, and treating it as the only way is a weakness in the roster.

**There is a position that sees completely.** Someone who has watched the procedure many times, understands exactly what it does and to whom, holds no illusion about its necessity, and would tell you plainly if you asked — *for what it's worth, we all think it's horrible* — and then performs it, competently, kindly, on schedule. They are not conflicted in the dramatic sense. They resolved this years ago.

**What makes the position work is that the seeing costs nothing and buys nothing.** There is no forum to take it to, no colleague who does not already agree, no version of refusing that reaches past their own dismissal and someone else doing it less kindly. Their clarity is real, complete, and inert — which is the same structure as a correct finding routed to a pending queue (Module 5 §6), relocated from a record into a person.

**This is the honest answer to the reader who asks why nobody notices.** People do notice. The world does not run on ignorance; it runs on the noticing being worth nothing. Structural blindness (Theorem 1, §4) explains the beneficiary. This explains everyone else, and it is the harder cruelty, because it cannot be relieved by information.

**Craft note.** Give them one line and no more. The moment they explain themselves at length they become a conscience, the scene becomes about their difficulty, and the reader is invited to forgive them. Said once, in passing, while their hands continue, it is unbearable and correct.

## 10. The person

An individual is a bundle of position markers, usually incoherent:

**grain** · **status** (out/carried/held/unwritten/returned) · **term history and dose** · **certification** · **register fluency** · **wall-kin** · **closure dependence**

Each assigns them to a different faction, and the assignments conflict. A certified clinician with a cross-grained child belongs to The Certified and to The Outside and cannot act for one without betraying the other.

**This is the protagonist position**, and it is a predicament, not a diagram. Every decision they make will read to one of their factions as betrayal, they know it, and they still have to decide — in front of people who made them, and who will be present afterward.

**The hard plotting constraint:** every significant decision the protagonist makes must betray at least one of their own markers. There is no clean side. The correct register is that the decision was necessary, costly, and partly wrong, and that the person is smaller afterward in a way insight cannot repair. **If the ending feels like growth, the story has broken the module.**

**Everyone else stands cleanly with respect to the story's central decision** — not cleanly in all of life. The adversary carries one claim cleanly here and another somewhere the story does not follow, which is what keeps a persuasive opponent from becoming a faction monologue.

And the corollary: **everyone standing cleanly is also, from somewhere else, monstrous, and cannot feel it.** The warm competent controller is a functionary of slow violence to the hold his queue is starving, and his position is built to prevent him from perceiving it. The story does not adjudicate. It holds both readings in frame.

## 11. The rescue norm as a commons

*You go* (Module 7) is a common-pool institution: everyone benefits, everyone must contribute, defection is cheap and collectively fatal. Such institutions survive on clear boundaries, graduated sanctions, and a forum for disputes. This one has none, so its erosion is a predictable institutional failure rather than a moral decline.

**The specific reform: the rescue ledger.** Every call logged, scored, verified afterward, with a contribution obligation assigned. Its purpose is fair burden-sharing and fraud prevention, and it was introduced after a genuine and infuriating abuse, by a person of unimpeachable decency, with an excellent argument.

Its effect is that uncertain calls stop being made. The old norm survived because a responder could say *I thought they needed us*. The ledger requires them to demonstrate afterward that they were right. Hesitation becomes policy.

**Nobody is destroying it. It is being improved to death** — and the ledger should recur across the collection, carrying a different moral valence each time.

## 12. Omega log

- **Ω_C09** — Whether The Outside has a name for itself. Recommend: no, and the namelessness is the point. Others have names for it, all slurs or euphemisms.
- **Ω_C10 — [resolved in Module 5 §10]** Factional schism has occurred once, in a compact, a generation back. The dispute was over whether to build an interpretive layer beneath the founding document; it produced two compacts that no longer recognise each other and now sit on different failure curves without being able to tell which is which. The original argument is incomprehensible from outside, which is how such things work.
- **Ω_P08** — How many factions one story should touch. Recommend: two, plus the protagonist's unresolved bundle. Three is a novel.
- **Ω_E08** — Whether cross-polity organising has ever succeeded. Recommend: once, briefly, at a port during an unusual conjunction, remembered as a season.

## 13. Grounding

For the writer, not the page. Madison, *Federalist 10*, on faction as ineradicable and remediable only in its effects, and on the extended sphere here taken past its useful limit. Olson on collective action, for why the largest interest is the least organised. Festinger on disconfirmation strengthening irreversible public commitment — scoped to constitutive evidence only. Weber on status groups as distinct from classes, and Tilly on durable inequality reproduced through categorical pairs, opportunity hoarding, emulation, and adaptation. Bourdieu on bodily hexis, for why grain is read before speech. Hirschman on exit, voice, and loyalty, against Module 4's asymmetric exit. Ostrom on common-pool governance, for how *you go* fails. Scott on legibility — for why holds survive by being unreadable, why certification is a project to make them otherwise, and for proxy aggregation in §1, which is the module's sharpest political claim.

None of this appears in any story.

# Module 10 — The Ratchet and the Calendar

*Tenth of eleven. Depends on Modules 1–9. Lets a writer site a story in time without committing the setting to a history.*

---

## 1. Why there is no chronology

A timeline would betray the invariant. This is a world in which consequential truth arrives after the commitments that depend on it, and nothing about that is confined to biology — it applies equally to the past. Events here have the same structure as deaths (Module 8): the ones that mattered have no moment, and the ones with moments were recorded because an institution needed a date for a form.

There is also no system-wide calendar to hang a history on. Time is shifts, windows, and terms (Module 8), all of which drift, and Earth's annual cycle arrives as procedural weather from a place with different physics.

So this module gives an **ordered ratchet** rather than a chronology: thresholds that, once crossed, do not uncross. A story is sited by saying which have been crossed where it happens — not by a year.

## 2. The ratchet

Each threshold is irreversible in practice. They are ordered but not evenly spaced, and **different places sit at different positions simultaneously** — a port may be three crossings behind the compact buying its propellant.

| | Threshold | Dated? |
|---|---|---|
| **R1** | Propellant transfer becomes routine. Cislunar stops being a destination and becomes a distance | Yes — precisely, by three agencies with slightly different figures |
| **R2** | Power crosses from kilowatts to megawatts. Industry becomes possible rather than demonstrable | Yes — the commissioning is a matter of record |
| **R3** | A deposit becomes a port: the first ice that sells propellant instead of consuming it | Yes, and disputed by two claimants |
| **R4** | A rotation regime lapses somewhere and is not restored. The first population | **No** |
| **R5** | A cohort grown offworld reaches adulthood. Grain becomes a thing that can be read | **No** |
| **R6** | A settlement carries itself through a bad year without resupply | **Contested** |
| **R7** | Trajectory approval consolidates into a single practical dependency | Yes — as an administrative merger nobody attended |
| **R8** | The incident (Module 7) | **Contested to the point of unreality** |
| **R9** | The rescue ledger (Module 9) | Yes. Signed, published, celebrated |

## 3. What can be dated and what cannot

The pattern in that last column is the module's actual content, and it is worth stating flatly.

**The technical thresholds have dates because institutions needed them for forms.** A commissioning, a merger, a first delivery — someone had to file something, so a moment exists.

**The thresholds that made these people a people cannot be dated at all.** Nobody noticed the shift when a workforce became a population; it happened across a decade of individually unremarkable decisions not to enforce something. Nobody can say when grain became readable, because grain is not finished until a life is, and by the time the first cohort's lives were legible their children were already grown. Closure claims are unverifiable from outside by construction (Module 5), so R6 has as many dates as there are interested parties.

**The things that made them useful are recorded. The things that made them themselves are not.** This is the same asymmetry as the manufactured death-date (Module 8) — an institution supplies a moment because a process requires one, and everyone acts on a figure nobody believes.

A writer should feel free to let characters argue about R4 through R6 and never settle it. That argument is the setting talking about itself.

## 4. The pressure cycle

What recurs, and what it does to people.

**The window.** Everything clusters here: departures, arrivals, resupply, contract endings, terms expiring, the mail, the recruiters, the inspectors. A window is simultaneously the only opportunity and the only exposure a place gets, and the weeks before one have a particular quality — decisions deferred all season arriving at once, everyone slightly reckless, love affairs conducted on a clock.

**The between.** Longer, and where the collection mostly lives. A settlement is alone: nothing can be fixed from outside, nobody arrives, no one leaves, and any problem that appears must be absorbed locally. **Margin Calls happen here** (Module 9), because the between is precisely when slack cannot be replenished.

**Term boundaries.** People hired together age out together. A charter runs on cohorts, so a settlement periodically loses a third of its competence and institutional memory at once and replaces it with people who have to be taught which things are not written down.

**Renewal season.** Terrestrial filings, reviews, and certifications land on Earth's schedule, which corresponds to nothing local. A polity's fate is decided in an annual cycle that its inhabitants experience as arbitrary weather — a bad month for reasons occurring on a planet most of them have never seen.

**Conjunction.** Rare alignments that briefly collapse distance: several crews in one port, three holds within days of each other, a shared emergency. This is the only condition under which combination is possible (Module 9), and it lasts exactly as long as the bodies remain co-located. Conjunctions are remembered like weather events, with the same mixture of nostalgia and disbelief.

## 5. What everyone can see coming

Scheduled crises: visible years out, arriving anyway, because the mechanism that would prevent them is the mechanism that cannot assemble (Module 9). Each is a story premise and several are a whole collection's background pressure.

- **A cohort of inheritors reaching adulthood in a place that planned for workers.** They were not budgeted. They cannot be rotated out, because they are not from anywhere else. Nobody wrote a rule because nobody expected them.
- **A deposit's decline becoming undeniable** at a port that cannot afford to have known.
- **A reactor reaching end of design life with no replacement approved**, and the approval process running on a chokepoint held elsewhere (Module 4).
- **The last signatory dying.** When the founding generation is gone, the document stops having a witness and becomes only a text — the moment a compact tips from constitutive into husk, and the moment nobody will be able to identify afterward.
- **The ledger's first correct refusal**: the first time someone does not go, and the record shows they were right to hesitate, and the finding is filed without objection.
- **A grain cohort aging into certification** in numbers the boards have no procedure for — not an exclusion anyone decides, just a rate of failure that becomes a policy by accumulating.

## 6. The contested past

Events every polity references and dates differently. None should ever be settled by a story.

- **The incident.** Cited constantly, examined never, load-bearing for a prohibition nobody could otherwise justify.
- **Every compact's founding**, self-dated with precision, disputed by neighbours who date it to a different act — the signing, the departure, the first winter, the first death.
- **The first grain.** Several polities claim it. The claim is about status, not chronology, and everyone knows this and argues anyway.
- **When a hold became a hold.** There is no date and no event, and holds are proud of this in a way that infuriates everyone with a charter.

## 6b. Local periodisations

Nobody uses the ratchet in-world. Each polity dates from its own founding act, and the datings do not align or convert.

Compacts date from **the Signing**. Holds date from **the first winter**, meaning the first season they got through without help, which is not a date and is remembered as a story. Ports date from **when the ice was good**, and are always describing a period that has just ended. Charters date from **commissioning**, precisely, and put it on the letterhead. Programmes use Earth's calendar and are the only ones who do.

Two characters comparing when something happened is therefore a small act of translation, and usually a small act of hostility.

## 7. Siting a story

Three choices, in order. They take a minute and they determine the story's whole pressure.

**1. Ratchet position, locally.** Which thresholds have been crossed *where this happens*? A port at R3 with no R6 is desperate and rich. A compact past R5 and R6 is stable, closed, and about to discover its inheritors. Places at different positions in the same story is where most of the friction is.

**2. Where in the cycle.** Before a window — compressed, reckless, everything at once. Deep in the between — isolated, and where the Margin Call lands. At a conjunction — the rare story where people can actually combine, and must pay for it afterward.

**3. Which scheduled crisis is near**, and how near. Not necessarily the plot. Usually the weather the plot happens in.

**A note on the collection's shape.** Nothing requires the stories to be told in ratchet order, and they will read better if they are not. What holds them together is the world, not a sequence — a reader should be able to take them in any order and accumulate a sense of the place rather than a plot. The one thing that should progress, quietly and in the background, is the erosion of *you go* (Modules 7, 9). If the collection has an arc, that is it, and no single story should be about it.

## 8. Omega log

- **Ω_E09** — Whether R8 precedes or follows R7. Recommend: nobody knows, and the two are causally entangled in the standard account, which is one reason the standard account is suspect.
- **Ω_C11** — Whether the ratchet is ever discussed in-world as a sequence. Recommend: no. Institutions have their own local periodisations that don't align, and characters use those.
- **Ω_P09** — Whether the collection should ever site a story pre-R4, before there was a population. Recommend: at most one, and it should be unrecognisable as belonging to the same world until the last page.

## 9. Checks

# Module 11 — Instantiation

*Eleventh of eleven. Depends on all prior modules. How a story gets made from the world, and what a story may not undo.*

---

## 1. Inherit and invent

**Inherited, non-negotiable:** the terrain (Module 2), the invariant and the floor (Module 3), the economics of irreversibility (Module 4), the charter forms and the catalog (Module 5), grain and attribution (Module 6), the deniability ladder and the rescue norm (Module 7), the registers, status grammar, and parting customs (Module 8), the faction logic (Module 9), the ratchet (Module 10).

**Invented per story:** every proper noun. Settlements, ships, families, companies, boards, the specific ice, the specific reactor. The world supplies kinds; the story supplies instances, and no instance from one story binds another unless a writer chooses to reuse it.

The test: a reader should finish two stories and be certain they happen in one place, without either story having named anything the other did.

## 2. The seat

**One seat per story.** The seat is the reference reality the story's verdict is audited against — the thing everyone in the story is standing in different relations to. Not a theme, not a question. A specific reality: *this ice reserve*, *this cohort's outcomes*, *this margin*, *this body*.

Characters do not have their own seats. They share the story's one seat and differ in **gauge** — where they stand over it — and in **orientation**: whether they hold their position as declared or as natural fact, and whether they have acknowledged their own drift. That's the anti-villain engine. A character who cannot see the extraction they benefit from is not lying; the position supplies the blindness.

**A story usually has two seats, and the second one is better.** The **declared** seat is the one chosen before drafting, and it tends to come from the bible — which means the scenes that serve it were pre-authored, and a reader who knows the world will feel them arrive already written. The **found** seat is the one the story locates while being written, usually in a scene that was supposed to be minor and turned out to be load-bearing.

When they differ, the move is **not** automatically to rebuild. There are three outcomes, and a diagnostic that only ever returns the first has stopped measuring and is simply rewriting in a circle.

**Displacing.** The strongest scene sits on a reference reality the architecture does not serve. Re-site the story on the found seat; the declared one becomes pressure rather than subject, which is generally where it belonged. **The cost is usually the most quotable passage in the draft**, because inherited material reads well and reads as demonstration, and that is the right trade.

**Nested.** The found thing is where the story already arrives. Do not rebuild — restructuring around it means putting the last paragraph first, and **a story that opens on its own terminal insight has named its theme.** The fix is a swap and it is small: *plant it early, unmarked, and cut the statement of it.* Seed the thing in an ordinary early scene where nobody remarks on it, then delete the closing sentence that says what it was. The reader has been holding it since page one and does not need to be told.

**Null.** What the story found is a correction to the world, not to itself. The revision is a spec change and the story is left alone. This is a real channel: **the stories audit the bible**, and a diagnostic that produces no revision has still produced its result. Do not manufacture a rewrite to justify having run the test.

**The stopping condition.** If successive stories all come back *displacing*, the instrument is finding its own shape everywhere. Nested and null results are what license the hits.

**The collection varies the seat and holds the world constant.** Story one audits against a reserve estimate, story two against a cohort's grain, story three against a rescue call. Same physics, same forms, no repetition. Choosing a new seat is how you avoid writing the same story twice, and it is the only reliable way.

## 3. Cast size is a structural choice

Disagreement across positions is quantized, and cast size determines which disagreement structures are available.

| Positions | Available disagreement |
|---|---|
| **2** | none, or exactly one |
| **3** | none, two, or three |
| **4** | none, or three and up — *never one or two* |

The reason is combinatorial rather than aesthetic: agreement comes in blocks. Four positions make six pairs, and any way of splitting four people into agreeing groups leaves either zero disagreeing pairs or at least three. There is no arrangement of four that produces exactly one or two.

**A four-hander cannot sustain mild disagreement.** Either the ensemble agrees or the fracture is wide and takes in most of the cast. If you write gentle partial dissent among four positions, you have written something the topology does not permit and it will read as false. A two-hander can hold exactly one disagreement and stay stable — which is why most of these stories should be two-handers.

Pick the cast for the fracture you want, not for the scale.

## 4. The ending generator

Institutions here end in patterned ways, and the pattern is mechanical. Choose three things about the story's institution — the **direction** of its gap, the **magnitude**, and whether anyone has **said it out loud** — and the ending follows.

| Direction | Magnitude | Acknowledged | Ends as |
|---|---|---|---|
| *(no drift)* | — | — | **Stable** |
| any | minor | either | **Stable** |
| Authority erosion | substantial | no | **Husk** |
| Authority erosion | substantial | yes | **Stable** |
| Authority erosion | severe | either | **Repudiation** |
| Practice drift | substantial | no | **Husk** |
| Practice drift | substantial | yes | **Stable** |
| Practice drift | severe | no | **Extinction** |
| Practice drift | severe | yes | **Revival** |
| Premise overridden | substantial | no | **Foreclosure** |
| Premise overridden | substantial | yes | **Stable** |
| Premise overridden | severe | either | **Foreclosure** |
| Record destroyed | substantial | either | **Husk** |
| Record destroyed | severe | either | **Extinction** |
| Revival pressure | not minor | either | **Revival** |
| Repudiation pressure | not minor | either | **Repudiation** |

**Reading it.** *Authority erosion* — the body that decides is losing standing to decide. *Practice drift* — what people do has quietly parted from what they say. *Premise overridden* — the thing the arrangement rests on has been contradicted by its own commitments. *Record destroyed* — the medium itself is gone.

Three properties worth internalising:

**Acknowledgment is the whole mechanism at substantial magnitude.** Acknowledge the gap and the thing stabilises; don't and it hollows. This is the setting's only reliable form of rescue and it is available in nearly every story.

**But acknowledgment is rarely spoken, and in this world usually isn't.** In a culture where a full goodbye is a verdict and asking why is worse than receiving one, the naming is done with the hands. Handing someone the better knife. Teaching a grip you were not asked to teach. Taking the other hinge without discussing who takes which. Working beside someone for four hours at a task that has to be done well, and doing it well, together. **That counts, and it counts fully.** A character who says the thing out loud in this setting is usually doing something else — making a claim, or delivering a verdict, or losing an argument.

The corollary matters more than the rule: **the loud reckoning would have closed nothing.** A story that stages the confrontation everyone has been waiting for, and lets it work, has written a different world. Here the confrontation either never comes or comes and changes nothing, and the repair — where there is repair — happened somewhere in the middle of an ordinary task, on no particular evening, and nobody can afterward point to it.

**The exception, and it is seat-indexed like everything else here.** Acknowledgment saves when it comes from *inside* the practice — someone who lives under the arrangement names the gap and the arrangement adjusts. **Acknowledgment from a position of standing does the opposite: it appropriates, and appropriation kills.**

The mechanism is not malice and the words can be identical. A practice that survived because no institution had a category for it — a private observance, an unrecorded kindness, an hour nobody sanctioned — depends on being outside. Someone with standing names it warmly, finds it creditable, takes a kind of ownership of it by admiring it aloud. It is now a thing the institution can hold. And it stops.

**The lethal variant: standing plus debt.** Sharper than standing alone, and worse in three ways.

*The debt disarms the only available defence.* Ordinary admiration can be deflected — the practitioner changes the subject and it passes. Gratitude cannot. Refusing a debtor's thanks means telling him that what he received was not what he thinks it was, which takes back the thing he was given. The one move that would have saved the practice is the one move the debt forecloses.

*It makes the naming obligatory rather than presumptuous.* The force that stops a stranger — *this isn't mine to remark on* — reverses. Silence would be ingratitude. He is not overstepping; he is discharging something, and **his account is accurate.** He really was saved, by that, and there is no error anywhere for anyone to correct.

*And it scales with the size of the debt.* Gratitude wants an audience — a life-sized thanks gets said in front of people, because that is what discharging a debt of that size requires. **The magnitude of the debt sets the publicity of the naming, and the publicity is the conversion.** The more the practice did for him, the louder he must be, and the faster it ends.

**The forced decision, which is the scene:** accept the thanks and lose the thing, or refuse and unmake what she gave him. There is no third option and no way to explain the problem to anyone present, including him. She will usually accept, graciously, and watch it die in her hands, and nobody in the room will see that a decision was made at all.

The person who did it performs the rescue the table promises and produces a kill, never learns this, and may try the same sentence again later and be unable to find where it went wrong, because the words were the same words. **Check the acknowledger's position before reading the terminal off the table.** From inside: stable. From above: extinction, and nobody in the story will be able to say so.

**Severe overrides acknowledgment.** Past a point, naming it changes nothing — with exactly one exception, and it is the setting's most valuable story shape: *practice drift, severe, acknowledged → revival*. Someone says the true thing at the last possible moment and the thing comes back. Use it rarely. It is the only redemption the world permits.

**Record destruction ignores acknowledgment entirely**, because there is nothing left to act on it. A hold that loses its people loses its constitution (Module 5): implicit practice has no fixed referent to reconstruct from.

**A tenth, and the generator cannot reach it: the demand is granted.** People hold something costly for years and **win** — the institution concedes the thing that was asked, in full, in good faith, and the concession is real. Then the arrangement absorbs it: the new pathway is applied fairly, the denials are documented and correct, and the distribution of outcomes is the one that obtained before. **What was prohibited is now merely improbable**, and the improbability is justified case by case, on genuine grounds, by people following the new rule exactly as written.

**The mechanism is not bad faith and must not be written as a trick.** A demand has to be *sayable* to be made, and what is sayable to an institution is procedural — *let us apply on the same terms as anyone else.* The thing actually wanted has no procedural form (Module 8: there is apparatus for refusal and none for declaring). So the winnable demand and the real want come apart at the moment of asking, and the institution grants the first while never having been asked for the second. The person who framed the demand chose the only formulation their position could make legible, and was right to.

**And nobody can say whether it was worth it.** The counterfactual — what they would have had by not holding — was never raised (Module 3 §1a). **A collective action cannot audit itself for the same reason a population cannot.** So the person who carried it will be asked whether the years were worth it, by people who held with them, and will have nothing to offer but thanks, and will know that thanks is not enough and is all there is.

*The scene to write toward:* not the negotiation and not the vote. **The one who could not hold saying so afterward** — that they nearly broke, that holding cost them something nobody counted, that they voted to end it because they were tired rather than because it was won. Everyone in the room recognises themselves. Nobody can tell them the arithmetic came out right.

**An eleventh, and it is not the queue: the finding is correct and below threshold.** Someone assembles a case over weeks. Every element is real — the correlation, the capability match, the motive, the opportunity, the pattern that only they can see. **And it does not meet the standard**, and the standard is not a pretext.

The bar exists because findings below it have destroyed people who did nothing. It is applied here exactly as it protects everyone else, by someone who believes in it and is right to. **Nothing was obstructed. The case was adjudicated, properly**, and the answer is that a correct account of what happened is not the same object as evidence that it happened, and the difference is the whole of what a standard is.

*Distinct from the pending queue and from dispersal.* The queue is indifference — nobody read it. Dispersal is diffusion — it was read and went nowhere. **This is a decision, made competently, in favour of a principle the finder also holds.** They cannot even be angry at it without arguing against the thing that would protect them.

*The register:* the file stays open, marked awaiting further evidence, which means it stays open forever, which means the person who assembled it will check it periodically for the rest of their career and it will always say the same thing.

**A ninth: the finding disperses.** Not held in a queue and not acted on — *cited.* A record nobody would publish becomes a footnote in a paper, and the paper is cited in a committee report, and the report informs a proposal, and the proposal fails. **The reform did not pass. But it existed**, and it contained language — about sight lines, about inspection access, about what must be monitorable — that did not exist before and now does, in circulation, available to someone who has not yet needed it.

Nothing was fixed. Nobody was helped. The thing being documented continued and expanded. And the vocabulary for describing it is now in the record where a later person can find it, which is a real outcome and is not the same as being absorbed.

The register to hold: **the person who did the documenting does not get to know whether this counts.** They add the citation to their own file as another entry and go back to work.

**An eighth ending, and the table cannot produce it either: the decisive act against nothing.** A person identifies what they believe is the mechanism and destroys it — burns the object, resigns the post, ends the practice, tears up the paper, refuses the settlement. The act is real, costly, and irreversible. **And the world is unaffected**, because the thing they destroyed was a candidate cause rather than a cause, and the price goes on coming due at the same rate it always did.

This is not husk, not extinction, and not the cost-changes ending — nothing hollows, nothing dies, nothing gets lighter. It is what the invariant does to a person who tries to act *on* it rather than under it, and the collection should contain one, because it is the shape that most honestly reports what this world does to a decisive person.

The register to avoid is futility. They were not foolish. Destroying it was the only available action and doing nothing would have been worse, and the reader should finish unable to say they were wrong to do it.

**The one ending this world cannot have: the closed loop.** The story returns to its first image, unchanged — the same shuttle at the same hour, the years indistinguishable, nothing established and nothing spent. It is the shape every draft in this setting drifts toward, because the world genuinely does not end and stasis feels like fidelity to that.

**It is the failure Module 3 §1c exists to prevent.** Nothing ends here, which is why *something must be established* — and a story that closes its own loop has reported the world's condition instead of putting a person inside it. If a draft's last page could be its first page, it has no ruling in it.

**The one earned exception: the frame returns and the people are differently placed inside it.** The work continues, the quotas rise, the room is the same room — and one person chose their exit and another was ground out of theirs, and both know which. Nothing improved. What was established is *whose it was*: one of them can say **it was my choice** and mean it, and the other cannot, and that difference is permanent and cost each of them something specific.

The test that separates this from stasis: **at the end, could each person say a different true sentence about how they came to be where they are?** If yes, a ruling was made — on authorship, which is the smallest thing anyone can establish here and sometimes the only one available. If everyone would say the same thing, the loop is closed and the story reported the world instead of putting someone in it.

**A seventh ending the table does not produce, and the most honest one available.** The institution's fate is not the only thing a story can settle. **The structure holds, and the cost changes.** The queue is still the queue and the term is still the term, but the people in one room have arranged themselves so that it takes less out of them — they matched pace, they made the mark land on everyone, they built a second circuit beside the first that does not threaten it and was never meant to.

Nothing is repaired. Nobody is saved. The thing that was crushing them is still there and will outlast them. And it costs less than it did on the first page, and the person it costs less for can feel the difference in their hands.

Use this freely. In a world where nothing ends, it is the ending most stories should have, and it is the only one that leaves a reader something other than grief. **Do not let a character name it as a victory.** They will not experience it as one; they will experience it as Tuesday, slightly better.

**Exit is sometimes available, worse, and taken — and the bible had it only as economically foreclosed.** Module 4 says leaving is cheap in the wrong direction and ruinous to the body. What it did not have: a person who *can* go, goes, and is measurably worse afterward. Their numbers decline. They sleep badly. Their body had adapted to the arrangement and has to relearn what it knew before, badly, without help.

**And they keep it.** Not because it improves — it does not — but because it is theirs. This is the strongest form of the chosen-and-kept ending, and it has one further constraint that makes it land: **they cannot say why.** They signed something, or the saying would cost someone else, or there is no form in the language for a declaration that is not a verdict (Module 8). So when asked, they say the true and useless thing — *the food was excellent, the doctors were attentive* — and both facts are accurate and neither is the reason, and the person asking goes away satisfied.

Nobody will ever know a choice was made. That is the cost, and it is the whole of it.

**And one shape the document's instincts push away from, which is legal and needed.** A protagonist may choose their position knowingly — take the berth, sign the compact, accept certification on the terms offered — and keep it, and be right to, and pay for it.

This is not a happy ending and it does not break the no-growth rule. It betrays a marker like every other decision. It is accretional: they end more exactly themselves and with fewer things available. What distinguishes it is that they *saw what they were choosing*, which almost nobody in this world can, and chose anyway, and did not lose it.

**The collection needs at least one, and probably two.** Without it, every loss elsewhere reads as the setting's mood rather than as something a person could have done differently. With it, the other ten become choices that went another way. The cost is usually borne by someone who did not get to choose, and the story should let the reader see them.

Husk is the most common institutional outcome and should stay so.

**A note on this section's own shape.** The table above generates six terminals. Below and around it sit roughly eleven more that the table cannot produce — the cost-changes ending, the decisive act against nothing, dispersal, the granted demand, the below-threshold finding, chosen-and-kept, the earned closed loop, and the rest. **At that ratio the table is not a generator. It is the first entry in a list**, and continuing to present it as the engine is this document performing a function it stopped serving (Module 5 §6).

Stated rather than repaired, because repairing it would mean either forcing eleven outcomes into a mechanism that does not reach them, or rebuilding the mechanism around outcomes that arrived from twenty different directions and share no axis. Both are worse than an honest list.

**So read §4 as a list with a table at the front, and do not add a twelfth ending without first asking whether it differs from all eleven or is one of them in different clothes.** But this table is here to stop you writing husk every time by reflex.

**How to write a husk from inside, which is the collection's hardest tonal problem.** From outside, a husk is bleak: a dead practice maintained by people who will not say so. From inside it is *warm*, and if you write it as bleak you have written it from a vantage nobody in the story occupies.

The people in a husk are not deluded and not cowardly. The practice works — it does what practices do, which is hold people together and give the day a shape. A phrase gets said that once referred to something and now refers to the saying of it, and it lands anyway, every time, in the same soft place. A joke whose subject is two generations dead still gets the laugh, and the laugh is real, and the warmth is real, and nothing about the emptiness underneath is available to anyone in the room. It is a closed circle warming itself, and it is the truest thing they have.

**The test, and it runs backward.** Take any husk you have written and ask whether it would work as a joke. If the warmth survives the referent's absence — if the thing still lands with nothing at the far end of it — you have a husk. If it needs the referent to work, you have written **decay**, which is a different and much less interesting object: a practice that is failing rather than one that has completed its hollowing and gone on being warm.

The two are easy to confuse in a draft because both look like a dead thing being maintained. The joke test separates them in one pass.

**Write the warmth at full strength and let the reader assemble the rest.** The reader can see there is nothing at the far end of the wire. The characters cannot, because seeing it would require a comparison nobody can make — which is the invariant, arriving as tone.

**And the same applies to loss of standing.** Being stripped of certification, of prospects, of a future anyone expects you to build, takes everything — and it also takes the weight. A person of whom nothing is expected is free of being anyone's disappointment, and some of them know it, and none of them could say it to anyone who still has a life. Module 9's Outside must carry this or it becomes a category of suffering rather than a way of living. Whether a given character's shrug is grief or deliverance should be undecidable, including to them.

## 5. Cruxes

Forced-decision scenes, pre-built. Each names its seat and puts one person with an unresolved bundle in the room. All seven are Margin Calls: the slack that let someone be two things has gone.

**The hearing.** *Seat: whether a body is fit.* A cross-grained worker petitions for recertification after a long surface stay. Admitting them validates the adaptation that undermines the standard; refusing loses a needed skill and tells The Outside the door is decorative. The petitioner is not in the room. The story belongs to the voter who shares their grain.

**The notice.** *Seat: whether this worker goes down the well.* A term expires the same window a board calls someone borderline. Rotation says ship them — liability, precedent. Surface says keep them — they have adapted, they are needed, and the well will kill them. The worker has no vote. The story belongs to the clinician who signs.

**The dispute.** *Seat: whose dead this is.* Two polities claim one body, for shielding mass and for the wall-kin record. A controller adjudicates, and every ruling sets precedent. The story belongs to the controller who realises he has been writing to someone already in a wall.

**The bid.** *Seat: how a margin gets spent.* A port's reserve runs low before conjunction. Ice wants rationing; Power wants to divert output to crack more water and thin the loop. The transient population has no voice and dies first. The story belongs to the depot master whose wall-kin are in that population.

**The call.** *Seat: whether you go.* A distress signal, unclear registry, no confirmed cover. The responder is a compact vessel with a narrow window and children downstream of its margin. The story belongs to the captain deciding whether her founding document's provisions on mutual aid reach a charter crew.

**The clean answer on five.** *Seat: an arrangement whose origin is unrecoverable.* Someone runs the whole discipline on a practice — who benefits and who pays, how it looks from the worst position, coordination against transfer, who is not in the conversation, what would rearrange if it vanished — and gets clean, honest, unarguable answers on all five. Then the sixth: *why was this built, and is the reason still live?* And there is no way to find out, because the people who built it are gone, the record has one field, and the comparison was never raised. Five-sixths of a complete analysis, and the missing sixth is the one that would tell them whether the cost they can now measure exactly is the price of something worth keeping. **They must act on it anyway.** The story belongs to whoever they tell.

**The taught hole.** *Seat: what a place used to be.* Someone who remembers a fuller version of a settlement, a practice, or a body tries to teach a younger person to carry the absence — to keep an empty place on purpose in the middle of an account that is complete without it. The younger one experiences it as a flaw and wants to close it, because their version is true and comes to true faster for being smaller. The story belongs to the older one, who knows the hole is one life deep and teaches it anyway.

**The audit.** *Seat: a record that was never written.* A charter's counsel requests a hold's emplacement record for a grounding settlement. The hold refuses. Transit access is mentioned. The Catalog offers to mediate, which both sides understand means the Catalog will hold the data. The story belongs to the keeper who has never written anything down.

## 5b. Passing

A neglected engine, and one of the setting's richest.

Certification demands proof of things that cannot be proved (Module 6), and people meet that demand by **producing what the assessment is looking for** — a gait, a history, a manner, an account of themselves. This is not fraud in the eyes of the person doing it. It is what you do to keep working.

Three properties make it a story rather than a texture.

**The test often cannot be passed sincerely.** Where an institution wants evidence of something interior — reliability, adjustment, having accepted a protocol — a true answer given plainly reads as rehearsed, and the only thing that reads as genuine is a performance built to read that way. So passing requires becoming someone who performs, and the performance is then received as the moment of real meeting.

**The cost registers somewhere unrelated.** Never where the strain is. The jaw, the sleep, the stomach, a shortening of temper with the person who has done nothing. A character does not know they are paying until something they never connected to it begins to hurt.

**And the tell is defending it.** The moment you know someone is inside is the day they explain to a person still outside that the institution is not unfair, only particular — and hear themselves do it, and cannot take it back.

## 6. Plotting rules

**Open on a Margin Call.** Slack vanishing is the forcing function; no antagonist is needed and physics supplies the schedule.

**Evidence is rarely a conversion.** Operational evidence — a seal, a dose spike, a blight — is incorporated fast and proudly, because competence requires it. Constitutive evidence — the reference is provincial, the sacrifice was unnecessary — is narrowed, referred, and converted into a reason for stricter control. Same people, same week, both competent.

**Every significant protagonist decision betrays one of their own markers.** There is no clean side. The register is: necessary, costly, partly wrong.

**What "smaller afterward" means.** Forbidden is *expansive* growth — the protagonist transcends the constraint, resolves the bundle, sees clearly, earns insight. Permitted, and wanted, is *accretional* growth: they become more exactly themselves, and what they are is now a narrower thing. They do not rise above their position; they sediment into it. A reader should finish knowing this person more precisely and seeing that fewer things are now available to them.

**Everyone else stands cleanly with respect to this decision** — not cleanly in all of life. The adversary carries one claim cleanly here and another somewhere the story does not follow.

**And everyone standing cleanly is monstrous from somewhere and cannot feel it.** Hold both readings; adjudicate neither.

**Beware the undeclared seat.** The characteristic sin here is not lying. It is being asked for one number by people who need three different ones, splitting the difference, and calling the result caution. Everyone does it, it is usually the decent choice available, and it is how the honest and the usable stop being distinguishable. Write it as ordinary.

**Never resolve attribution.** No character learns why. The cause is unrecoverable, not withheld.

**Numbers only in hands.** A figure reaches the page only when a character who owns that instrument acts on it in a scene. Narration is never denominated in the system's numbers.

**And one form has no narration at all: the artifact as the whole text.** A log, a register, a form, a maintenance record, a run of entries — presented raw, with no narrator anywhere. The numeric rule (below) assumes someone is telling the story and governs what they may denominate it in. **When nobody is telling it, the rule does not apply and its purpose is served better than any prose could serve it**, because the reader watches an instrument record everything and understand nothing.

Three things make this work rather than merely look clever.

**The drift carries the content.** A value that moves across entries — an interval lengthening, a reading trending, a field that was populated and is now null — does the work a scene would do, and the reader performs the interpretation the record cannot. Nothing is described. Everything is legible.

**The layers date themselves.** An old annotation sits beside a recent failure: a cheerful note from whoever set the thing up, still in the file, next to the entry that closed it. This is accretion (Module 5 §3) as a readable object — **the earliest layer written by someone who could not imagine the latest**, and neither aware of the other.

**And the counts do not reconcile.** Where a record spans more than one component, the reader can put the columns side by side and find what nobody inside can: that most of what was sent arrived after the recipient had gone, that the interval was already fatal when the last entry was filed, that two accurate accounts describe incompatible worlds. **No position inside the system can perform that subtraction** — each part sees only its own channel, correctly (Module 5 §6). The arithmetic is available only from outside, and the only one outside is the reader.

*Constraint:* the record must be one a real institution would keep, for reasons that have nothing to do with the story. The moment it is shaped to be read, it becomes narration with the serial numbers filed off.

**One form uses both registers at once, and it is the strongest structure available for the numeric rule.** Interleave two accounts of the same period: one kept by a person with the instrument, in its units — the remaining quantity, the projected interval, the margin — and one kept by a person without it, in sensation and consequence: *he is losing weight, the tremor started, his kindness felt refrigerated.*

Neither is wrong. Neither reaches the other. The reader holds both and can do the arithmetic that neither party can, because one of them lacks the numbers and the other lacks the room in which they mean anything.

**This satisfies the numeric rule rather than evading it:** every figure is read by someone with access, acting on it, in their own record. Nothing is narrated in the system's numbers. And it puts the gap the setting runs on (front matter) into the structure of the document rather than into a scene.

**And when a number does appear, the harm is usually in the schema, not the value.** A reading is taken at the moment a protocol requires one, and something else is true a few seconds later, and the record has one field, and the field holds the reading the protocol asked for. Nothing entered is false. Nothing omitted was hidden. There was no field for it.

This is how the setting produces institutional violence without producing a lie, and it is the shape to reach for whenever a story needs a record to do damage — **because it leaves no recoverable true value to expose.** Both readings happened. The form has one line. Anyone auditing it afterward will find it complete and correct, and it will be.

**And there is a second form, dispositional rather than structural, which is worse because nothing forces it.** Sometimes the writer has no schema constraint at all — full access, full clearance, room to say anything, and the whole pattern in front of them. What they produce is true in every sentence and incomplete in the only way that matters.

The omission is not caused by a field. It is caused by **the audience**: a person writing for the body that will receive the document produces the document that body can act on, and what gets left out is whatever would require the reader to believe something they do not. Nobody instructs this. Nobody would need to.

**The mechanism is question-substitution, and the story should show only its symptom.** Somebody stops asking whether an arrangement is just and starts asking whether it is stable. Both are real questions, both have honest answers, and only the second is one an institution can act on — so a competent person migrates toward it the way water finds a channel. This is not moral decay and must never be written as a fall. It is a professional getting better at their job.

**The scene it produces:** the writer finishes, reads it back, and finds they have produced exactly what the person they were auditing would have produced. Not under pressure. By doing it well.

*Two failure modes specific to this shape.* The writer must not narrate their own convergence — a character who can think *I am becoming him* is the analytical narrator (§8) and cancels the effect. And no other character may state it for them; the reader assembles it, or the story has explained itself.

## 6b. The background arc

The one thing that progresses across the collection is the erosion of *you go* (Modules 5, 7, 9). It must never be any single story's subject, and the stories must be readable in any order — so the arc cannot be a sequence.

**Make it a property of curation rather than plotting.** Each story touching rescue sits at a different point on the erosion without announcing which: a responder hesitates and goes anyway; a ledger exists and a call is quietly not made; someone goes despite the ledger and is penalised for it; someone files correctly and is commended and nobody went. A reader encountering these in any order assembles the trajectory themselves out of fragments that never claim to be a sequence.

Two rules. **No story may state the trend.** And **no story may show its stage as a change** — in every one, the current state is simply how things are done now, which is exactly how the erosion is experienced from inside.

## 6c. Generation checks

Not world rules — production rules, for catching the failure modes a generator falls into by default. Each is checkable against a draft in under a minute.

**No counting — with one exception, and the exception is precise.** The default holds: counting is a generator tic that reads as interiority and isn't, substituting a tally for attention. A person under strain notices the stairs have got worse; they do not count the stairs. **Cut every count that is atmosphere.**

**The exception is counting as a self-made instrument.** Where the system owns every gauge and a person owns none (§6 below), someone may build one out of the only thing available to them — breaths, the interval between two sounds, how far into a task the hand gives out — and use it, seriously, over time, to measure something real. This is not a tic. It is the powerless position acquiring a measurement, and the readings are accurate, and nobody will ever receive them.

Three conditions, all required:

**It must drift, and the drift must be the content.** A count that stays the same is decoration. Twelve, then sixteen, then eighteen, then twenty-eight — the number is uninteresting and the movement is the whole story, and it records a degradation the official instruments are also recording and not acting on.

**It must be recorded for nobody.** Notes kept on a tablet no one reads, in a form no one asked for. This is the ruling made for nobody (Module 3 §1c) in its smallest form: the one true measurement of the arrangement, taken by the person it is happening to, and never received.

**And it must break.** At some point the count will not hold — the rhythm goes, the numbers restart, seven, nine, one again. Put that break where it costs: usually the moment the counter uses the instrument to tell someone else the truth. The private measure survives being carried alone and does not survive being shared.

If a draft's counting fails any of the three, it is a tic. Cut it.

**Register must modulate.** If the voice holds the same register for more than about two pages, something is wrong. Pressure changes, and syntax changes with it — shorter under load, longer when there is room. A uniform voice across a whole story means the constraint was described rather than felt.

**The record is a device and needs a budget.** The bible's sharpest mechanism — the form is complete, nothing entered is false, there was no field for it — appears as recalibration (Module 2 §2), the pending queue (Module 5 §6), the declined measurement (Module 9 §9b), the one-field log (§6 above), and gate 4 of the appendix. Five faces of one insight. **At collection scale that is a prop, and a reader will have it by story three.**

Budget: **two stories in which a record does the damage.** Elsewhere the harm must arrive by another route entirely, and the world supplies four:

- **A body.** Grain, dose, attribution — a thing that goes wrong with no institution involved at any point.
- **A person.** The long form given, the stopping, a status assigned by someone who loves you. Intimate harm, no form anywhere near it.
- **The physics.** A window missed, a margin gone, a repatriation that costs nothing to arrange. Nobody did it.
- **A room.** Solidarity that fails at the scale where it was actually available, among people who could have.

**And the ledger is per collection, not per story.** Everything in §6c catches repetition inside one story. The failures that actually accumulate are the ones no single story can see, so keep a running record and check a draft against it before writing:

| Track | Why |
|---|---|
| **Seats used** | Two stories on one reference reality read as one story |
| **POV gauge, and whether they act or decline** | See §6d — the default is decline, and it compounds |
| **Terminals** | Husk dominates by design; make sure it dominates rather than monopolises |
| **Devices** | The record-does-the-damage shape, budgeted at two |
| **Character types** | The sincere administrator who sorts by accretion and cannot locate a decision — Kwan, Recital, the controller. One per collection, then find another kind of institutional presence |
| **Closing gestures** | Hands doing something small and unroutine is a fine ending once. Twice is a rhyme, three times is a signature, four is a tic |
| **Ω logged per story** | What the story deliberately did not decide (§7b). A story with nothing in this column either avoided the invariant or answered it |
| **What the story establishes, and who paid to establish it** | Module 3 §1c. A story with nothing here has avoided the invariant rather than obeyed it. Two consecutive blanks and the next story is a crux chosen for a seat where somebody must rule in public |

**Read the last two rows together.** Ω records what was left open; the row above it records what was closed. A story with entries in both has taken the world's position seriously. A story with only Ω has taken the world's difficulty as a substitute for its own.

Two of anything is a coincidence you should notice. Three is a decision you have made without deciding.

**No device may carry the story.** If one image, gesture, object, or noticing appears more than three times, the story is leaning on a prop instead of living in a world. The inhabitation register (Module 2 §5) supplies a *mode of attention*, which generates variety. A device generates repetition and reads as competence the first time and as tic the fourth.

**The pre-authored check: if a scene could be reconstructed from this document, cut it.** A passage that faithfully renders a module — a character enumerating the candidate causes of an illness, someone walking through why a queue is never a refusal — is the world demonstrating its own coherence, and it will be the most quotable thing in the draft and the least alive. The test is blunt: hand the module to someone who has not read the story and see whether they can produce the scene. If they can, it was written before you got there.

**Loss is loss.** If a character loses something, it lands in the body — the hand that will not close, the word that will not come, the reflex that fires wrong. Do not convert it into meaning, growth, or structural understanding on the page. Meaning arrives later, from a different direction, and usually to the reader rather than the character.

**Ending budget: 350 words after the decisive scene.** Hard. The attractor arrives and the story stops. Everything past that limit is the story explaining itself, and it will read as explanation no matter how well written.

## 6d. The spectator default

The world produces protagonists who watch and decline, and this is a consequence of its own construction rather than a failure of nerve.

Acting here is unattributable, unrewarded, and mostly unavailable: attribution is unrecoverable, the catalog never refuses, nobody is concealing anything, there is no forum, and the largest interest cannot combine. A person who acts changes nothing they can point to. So the generator — human or otherwise — converges on the unasked question: the one who does not tell, the one who does not ask, the one who declines to measure. **It is a good engine and it exhausts itself in about three stories.**

**The fix is not to pick an active protagonist.** Drop an actor into an ordinary seat and the story reverts, because the world will make their action illegible and the interest will migrate back to what they didn't do.

**The fix is seat selection: choose a seat where declining is itself a public, answerable act.** A depot master spending a margin in front of the people it will cost. A captain ruling on whether her compact's provisions reach a crew outside it. A keeper refusing to produce a record while transit access is discussed in the room. In each, there is no available non-decision — silence is a ruling, everyone present sees it, and someone can name them for it afterward. Spectatorship becomes structurally unavailable rather than authorially forbidden.

**One drift, three prescriptions.** The acting protagonist here, the chosen-and-kept ending (§4), and one story in which somebody says the unsayable thing in public and is answered (Appendix A §1) are not three separate requirements. They are three ways out of the same convergence — a collection of people who watch, decline, and cannot speak. Any of them breaks it. Using none of them across eleven stories will not read as discipline; it will read as one story told eleven times.

**Track it.** For each story, record whether the POV acts or declines. If two consecutive stories decline, the next one is a crux from §5, chosen for a seat where the decision is made in public.

## 6e. The second battery

The seat matrix (Appendix A §1) varies seat, gauge, cast, terminal, and the unsayable. **It cannot see anything that is invariant under all five**, and that foreclosure-set is where the real repetitions live: three completed stories varied on every matrix axis and shared a closing gesture, a sensorium, and a stance toward the person who cannot speak.

A battery that returns the same value on every input is not measuring. So run a second one, on the axes the first holds fixed. Answer all three before drafting, and **state the previous three stories' answers as forbidden.**

**1. What is the closing image made of?**
Not what happens — what the last thing on the page is. Hands doing something small on an object is a fine ending once. Alternatives, all unused: speech (someone says a thing and it lands or doesn't), an absence (the thing that isn't where it was), a document (a field, an entry, a form completed correctly), a body that isn't the POV's, a sound, or nothing at all — the scene simply ending mid-motion.

**2. Which sense carries the load?**
Not which senses appear — which one the story's meaning travels through. Hand and temperature are the default and will recur unbidden. Underused: smell, which does background work everywhere and has never been load-bearing; sound, which in a place of fans, hulls, and machinery ought to be the most available sense in the setting; taste, which the loop makes political; and proprioception, which is what grain actually is.

**3. What is the POV's relation to the person who cannot speak?**
Custodial — holding something on their behalf — is the default and produces a specific warmth that will read as one register across stories. Unused: **indebted** to them, **dependent** on them, **being the reason** they cannot speak, being **spoken for** by them, or — the hardest break available — **not knowing they exist**.

**If a story comes back having varied every seat-matrix axis and still ends on a hand, the matrix was not the thing that needed varying.**

**And run the same check on this document.** Both batteries look for repetition in stories. Nothing looks for it in the rules, and the rules converge the same way prose does — arrived at separately, from different sources, on different modules, saying one thing.

**Worked example, left standing as the demonstration:** §6d (the spectator default), §9b3 (articulacy is not purchase), and §9c (the one who sees and complies) were written weeks apart from three unrelated probes, and all three say *understanding is inert here*. Three is a signature by this document's own count. They are not merged, because they operate at different scales — a POV tendency, a general property of accounts, a specific position — but **the convergence is recorded, and a fourth rule saying the same thing should not be written.**

*The check:* before adding a rule, state its claim in one sentence and search the document for that sentence. If two rules already make it, the third is a signature; if three do, the fourth is a tic, and the correct action is to cut rather than to phrase it differently.

## 6e2. The clock does not tick

The most common tension device in fiction is unavailable here, and nothing in the modules said so.

A story generates urgency by putting a competent person against a deadline: can they finish in time. **This world has no failure state for competence.** The finding will be correct, complete, possibly the best work of the person's life, and it will route to a queue and be held (Module 5 §6). Whether they finish on Tuesday or Thursday changes nothing, so a countdown is a promise the world cannot keep, and a reader who has been given one will feel the cheat when nothing detonates.

**Every clock in this setting is real and none of them is about the work.** Windows close, margins go, terms expire, bodies fail — those are physics and they arrive on their own schedule (Module 9 §9). They are pressures, not deadlines. Nobody beats them and nobody is supposed to.

**What replaces the deadline:** the tension is not *can they finish* but **whether a person can go on working correctly with no prospect of the work mattering.** The failure available to them is not lateness or error. It is stopping, or getting sloppy, or beginning to perform the work rather than do it — which is the theater ratio arriving in one person instead of an institution.

**The compiler is not only a voice (§8) but a life, and this is its sharpest form.** Someone accumulates a record over years — entries, timestamps, readings, the same pattern documented from sixteen vantage points — and never once states what it shows. Asked what they are trying to prove: *nothing. I'm documenting.* Asked for whom: *for whoever looks at it later.*

**And the failure available to them is not stopping. It is concluding.** The moment the record states what the pattern means, it becomes an accusation, and an accusation can be answered, denied, and dismissed — where a record of readings cannot, because it asserts nothing.

**But that holds only against an institution, and the exception is the better story.** The rule assumes the record's audience is a body that can refuse it. **When the audience is the people the record is about, concluding is the only thing the record was ever for.** They cannot dismiss it, they were never going to be persuaded by neutrality, and what they need is not the readings but what the readings mean and which of them matters.

So the compiler's real discipline is not silence. It is **choosing the one audience worth spending it on**, and knowing that spending it is irreversible: once they have concluded, out loud, to someone, they are a person who concludes, and the next institution to receive their work will read it as advocacy and be right to. They have exchanged a permanent capacity for a single use of it.

**The scene this produces is the strongest thing available to the position:** a compiler who has carried a record for years, refusing to say what it shows, hands it to the four people in the room it concerns and tells them plainly. The structure does not change (§4). What changes is that those four now know, and the compiler is no longer the person they were.

*Craft note:* the story is usually mis-declared when this happens. The apparent subject is whoever receives the numbers; the actual seat is the compiler spending their discipline (§2, displacing). Check which one the story's best scene sits on. Their discipline is the refusal to draw the conclusion they are certain of, held for years, at the cost of never being believed and never being able to say why they persist.

This is a register in its own right and the collection should contain one: **the story where the work is the point and the outcome is not.** It is the natural home for the keeper, the clinician, the person who declines to measure, and anyone whose competence exceeds what their position can convert into consequence.

## 6f. Comedy

The world runs on positions that are each correct from where they stand and incompatible with each other, and that is not only the engine of its tragedies. **A room where everyone is right is farce.** The mechanism is already built and only one thing needs changing to run it.

**The valve is monotonicity, and this is more precise than "no cost."** Module 4 states that every instrument here accumulates and none has an operation for subtraction — dose, terms, entries, standing on the record. **That is a tragic ledger by construction**, and it, rather than any squeamishness about bodies, is what makes comedy hard to reach in this world.

Farce runs on **reversible expenditure**: dignity, position in a room, the location of a secret, who is standing where when the door opens. Spent freely, recovered by the last page, nothing carried forward. So comic stakes must be denominated in the one currency this world has that is not monotonic — **standing in the moment, as distinct from standing on the record.** Herein's misfire in the queue cost him precisely that and nothing accumulated. That is the seam, and it is the only one.

The practical form: **if the scene's cost would enter a ledger, it is not available for comedy.** Everything at risk must be visible and nothing may be entered. Nothing else in the physics changes — the clause is still read correctly by its holder, the queue still never refuses, position still supplies the blindness.

**The voice is the beneficiary, unchanged.** Warm, competent, likeable, structurally unable to see what the reader sees. Dread and farce are the same gap at different tempos: slow, with a cost, and the blindness is tragic; fast, with no cost, and it is comic. Herein's remark misfiring in the queue is farce played at funeral tempo — speed it up, give it a second misfire, and the engine is running.

**Rules, all of them versions of things already stated.**

*Nobody may know the joke.* A character who signals awareness has become a wit, and the comedy collapses into irony-signalling. Same rule as the winking beneficiary.

*Every position must be defensible in full.* If one person is being stupid, it is a farce about a fool. If all six are reasoning correctly from incompatible premises, it is a farce about the arrangement, and the reader follows the reasoning while laughing at it.

*Nothing may be destroyed.* If someone is grounded, repatriated, held, or loses standing, the valve has closed and the piece has reverted. Comic stakes are inconvenience, embarrassment, and a great deal of work performed for nothing.

*It must be as rigorous as everything else.* Farce is not the licence to loosen the world. It is the world at speed, and the pleasure is watching the machinery run correctly to an absurd place.

**The obvious candidate, and it should be written.** A compact assembly. One seating chart. Forty holders, each reading their own clause correctly, and notes appended to notes appended to notes that everyone has honestly construed. Anchored fixity means the founding text cannot be amended and everybody knows what it now means, so an entire polity performs continuous exegesis on a document it is forbidden to change.

**And there is a second layer available for free.** Module 8: the culture has a complete apparatus for goodbye and none at all for declaring — no form for *this will go on*. So forty people construe a document at length, and **none of them can say the one sentence that would settle it, because the sentence does not exist in any of the three registers.** The exegesis is not avoidance and not mere absurdity. It is the only available form for a thing that must be said and has no words.

That is the same engine as the collection's tragedies, running at comic tempo, and it is what raises the assembly above *look at this machinery*.

**The stakes are the chart itself, not its consequences.** Who ends up where, tonight, in front of everyone. Nothing enters a ledger; everything is visible; it is all recovered or not by the end of the evening. Consequences that outlast the assembly close the valve.

**And the joke is on the healthy compact, not the failing one.** Module 5 §3: a compact *with* an interpretive layer persists indefinitely, and one without it shatters. The layer is the working fate. So this is a functioning institution being ridiculous *because* it functions — the exegesis is doing real work, the accretion is what keeps the polity alive, and it is absurd. **The brittle compact is not available for comedy at all**, because its failure is total and lands on people who cannot recover it.

**Why it should be the first one written.** It puts an argument in the room that no essay can make. Nobody laughs at machinery they believe is stupid — they sneer at it. **The laugh is the reader conceding that the exegesis is legitimate**, which is assent to the political ontology extracted by a route no declared seat can reach.

**What comedy buys structurally.** It is the most reliable route to **revival** — practice drift, severe, acknowledged — which is otherwise the least reachable terminal in the generator. Acknowledged departure plus active rebuilding is a comic shape, which is why marriage plots end in weddings. And it satisfies the second battery (§6e) by construction: comedy lands in speech, cannot end on a hand on an object, and cannot run on a custodial stance.

## 6g. On probing this document

The document has grown by conflict (front matter). The probes that produce conflicts are registers and forms that make a demand the existing set has never made — and there is one systematic error in running them.

**A probe that shares the form of the thing being probed will return a false null.** A specification is a case study: evidence assembled toward a conclusion. So probing this document with the case-study register found nothing, and the nothing was a property of the probe rather than a finding about the world. Sort probes by *what they require of the world*, and discount any whose form resembles the document's own.

**The recipe is the correction, and it conflicts.** A recipe is instruction for reproducing a result: same steps, same outcome. **This world's central content is that following the procedure correctly does not reproduce the outcome**, because the outcome was never determined by the procedure — it was determined by a grain, and a grain is not reproducible. Two people raised identically do not come out the same and nobody can say why (Module 6 §4). Every training, protocol, and standard in the setting is a recipe that does not work, administered by people who are not wrong to administer it.

**The open question about the method itself:** it is not yet established whether *registers* are what find conflicts, or whether any sufficiently different demand does. The recipe is weak evidence for the second — it is not a narrative register at all and it conflicted anyway. A second sweep discriminates. Until then, run anything that makes an unmet demand and do not assume the demand has to come from fiction.

## 7. The general rule the world runs on

Stated once, because it unifies four modules that were only thematically related.

**Where truth is unavailable and a decision cannot wait, this culture manufactures a vocabulary of provisional status — and the fight is always over who assigns it.**

*Grain* is one (Module 3): what a body is, unfinished and uncertifiable. *Fitness* is another (Module 6): a determination against a reference nobody chose. *Closure* is a third (Module 5): sovereignty as an unverifiable claim. *Out, carried, held, unwritten, returned* is the fourth (Module 8), and the sharpest, because assigning it is openly political.

Any new institution, custom, or conflict invented for a story should be built on this template. It is the setting's grammar.

## 7b. What the author is allowed to know

The temptation is to hold the answers privately and write around them. **Don't.** A withheld fact and an unavailable one produce different prose: withholding reads as coyness and invites the reader to reconstruct what is being kept from them, which relocates the view from nowhere into the audience instead of removing it.

**Underdetermine instead.** When a story needs something the invariant covers — what the black soil did, whether the compact's austerity was necessary, why this cohort came out the way it did — the correct move is not to decide privately. It is to **decide that it is not decided**, and log it.

Author the mechanism space, never the fact: which causes are candidates, why no procedure separates them, and what was never raised to compare against. That is a complete specification. It contains no answer because the world contains no answer, and a reader will feel the difference between a world with a locked door and a world with no room behind it.

**Two hard boundaries.**

Underdetermination is licensed **only where the invariant bites.** Delta-v, chokepoints, who holds which ice, what a term costs, which polity feeds itself, where the reactors are — these must be known exactly. Vagueness there is not discipline; it is vagueness, and it will show up as a world that cannot be walked around in.

And it is licensed **only in advance.** Deciding privately and then declining to write it down is the withheld version with better manners. The commitment has to be made before drafting and recorded in the ledger.

**The check, per story:** state what you do not know about your own story. If the answer is nothing, either the story does not touch the invariant, or you have quietly become the authority the setting says does not exist.

**Why the bible can hold this without contradiction.** A contentful verdict is always made from somewhere; there is no view from nowhere, and pretending otherwise is the one incoherent position available. But the coherent alternative to standing nowhere is not concealment — it is declaration. **The bible is the declared seat**: the place where the choices are visible, dated, and contestable, held in a document that is not the fiction. The page stays local because the seat has already been declared elsewhere, in writing, where someone can argue with it.

## 8. Voice

Pick the voice from what the reader must see that the character cannot.

Most of these stories want a narrator inside the constraint who reads it as ordinary — the person who experiences the arrangement as weather. The Catalog controller is the setting's beneficiary voice and must be warm, competent, likeable, and structurally unable to see what he administers; the reader has to enjoy being in there. A compact's most decent member delivering its best defence is the same voice from a different seat.

Avoid the analytical narrator. Anyone who can articulate the system will name the theme, and the theme named is the collection dead.

**One permitted exception: the compiler.** A narrator assembling an account from records that are incomplete by construction — a case file, a register, a set of logs, a memory nobody wrote down — who says so at the outset and whose authority is documentary rather than omniscient. This position gets the full analytical register, and it is the register this world otherwise has no room for.

**The line it may not cross: the compiler analyses the record, never the people.** It may state what can be established, what cannot, what is missing, who kept it, what the keeping was for, and what the form had no field for. It may be scathing about all of that. It may not report what a character felt that the character could not have said, and it may not deliver the verdict the story exists to withhold. Its intelligence is aimed at the limits of the account, not at the meaning of the events, and the world guarantees it can never close — the trace does not close for the person who has to act.

**And a position that is not available**, stated because it is the one most worth reaching for: the narrator who knows what an institution or a person is *actually* doing and declines to say. That narrator holds the view from nowhere and merely withholds it (§7b), and in a world where the invariant holds, no such position exists. It belongs to stories about worlds that have an archive. Not this one.

**The vocabulary problem, and the story that solves it.** Every explanatory device is banned — no explainer, no analytical narrator, no anthropology, no character explaining a ritual — and a reader still has to learn *grain, term, held, carried, unwritten, wall-kin, the well, the catalog, emplacement, the long form.* Through use, with a 350-word ending budget. This is the hardest demand in the document and it should not fall on every story equally.

**The better solution is a child inside the world, and it is not the outsider.** An outsider misreads — they have a prior framework and apply it wrongly. A child has no framework and is simply **corrected**: a hand on the arm at the right moment, a body turned slightly, a pause that goes unfilled, and nobody ever says why (Module 8 §6). That is how this world actually teaches, and a POV inside it carries the vocabulary load better than any newcomer, because the reader learns each term the way the culture transmits it — by being stopped, repeatedly, without explanation — and assembles the meaning from the pattern of corrections rather than from a wrong guess being adjusted.

It also does something the outsider cannot: the child does not know they are being taught, so nothing in the prose can be mistaken for exposition, and the reader is ahead of the POV from the first correction onward, which is where the feeling is kept.

**The outsider remains available, permitted in Module 1 §4b.** Someone newly arrived who notices accurately and misreads what they notice: they encounter each term in use, they mistake its meaning in a way the reader can correct, and nothing is ever explained. One story in that POV carries most of the vocabulary load for the collection, and it works precisely because the outsider is wrong — the reader learns the word from the gap between what it does and what the newcomer thinks it means. Place it early, and do not let them arrive at the right answer.

**A second kind of blindness, and it is not blindness.** The controller below cannot see what he administers. There is a harder and better figure available: someone who *can* see the gap and decides to carry it alone, because naming it would cost the person they are protecting more than the protection is worth. A supervisor who files the softer version. A clinician who does not say the word. A partner who answers the letter as though the question in it still applied. They translate an unlivable situation into a livable one out of love, and in doing so they close the very gap they are carrying, and they never mention its size. This is the setting's most sympathetic character and its most damaging, and the two facts do not come apart.

**A beneficiary's first move is diagnosis, not wonder.** Confronted with something that does not fit — a practice, an absence, a woman standing at a wall — the position does not produce curiosity. It produces a verdict about propriety: this is a failing in her, an impropriety, a misallocation, a thing that should have been seen to. Let them hold that verdict, and hold it too long, and only afterward find a way to make it flattering. Wonder arriving first is how the voice collapses into a growth arc with the serial numbers filed off.

**The beneficiary voice, worked.** This is the hardest thing in the collection, so here is what it sounds like — a controller, warm, competent, entirely sincere:

> Third time this month for the Cassia queue. I've bumped them twice already and I'll bump them again if the window's kind, but there's a compact transit with four hundred aboard and a hard burn date, and Cassia's got — what, nine people and a pump that's been failing since spring. They're good about it. They're always good about it. Marta signs off with her little joke about the weather, every time, like we've got weather. I keep meaning to send something back.

Nothing in that is a lie and nothing in it is cruel. The reader assembles the pump, the nine people, the spring, and the phrase *they're always good about it* — and the controller assembles a nice woman being patient with him. He is not withholding sympathy; there is nothing in his position that would produce it. **The last line is the whole technique:** he is a person with a warm impulse he will not act on, and he does not know that is what he is.

## 9. Canon guard

What no story may repeal, however good the premise:

- The invariant, **scoped as in Module 3 §1b.** Local truths are recoverable and stories should recover them — a dose *was* miscalculated, a board *did* falsify, this regime *was* unnecessary. What no story may establish is an answer to the question underneath: whether the way of living was required. A story may prove a protocol excessive. It may not establish what the right protocol is.
- No threshold figure for gravity, dose, or closure ever appears.
- The invariant has no administrator. No Bureau, no Trials, no central programme.
- Attribution is never resolved.
- The incident is never established.
- No large-scale organising survives the separation of its participants.
- Reproductive licensing remains one polity's local arrangement, seen from outside.
- No protagonist's conflicting markers resolve into coherence as a reward.
- **No charter form is intrinsically emancipatory and no appeal to the baseline is intrinsically oppressive.** Every form of protection creates a population it fails to recognise; every route out requires an authority able to name what counts as coercion. A compact may protect a genuinely necessary adaptation while trapping those who do not fit it. An Earth standard may be arbitrary in origin and still be the only language in which a person's injury becomes sayable.
- No naive outsider diagnoses the arrangement correctly (Module 1 §4b).

A story that needs one of these is a story for a different world. Write it there.

## 10. The Ω ledger

Carried forward unresolved, by design — and classified, so a writer knows which decisions are free.

**Structurally undecidable** — the setting's own logic prevents resolution; never settle these, in any story.
Ω_E03 (whether any settlement has genuine food closure) · Ω_E04 (the rescue norm's founding incident) · Ω_E08 (whether cross-polity organising ever succeeded) · Ω_E09 (ratchet order of R7 and R8).

**Decide per story, may vary across the collection** — local answers, and the variation is the texture.
Ω_C02 (grain binary or spectrum) · Ω_C04 (holds' collective identity) · Ω_C06 (wall-kin extending to the living — the most fertile of these) · Ω_C07 (reversing *unwritten*) · Ω_E06 (written emplacement registers).

**Craft preference — settle once, for yourself, before drafting.**
Ω_P03 (how present Earth is) · Ω_P06 (how much creole reaches the page) · Ω_P09 (whether to attempt a pre-population story).

**Load-bearing — decide before writing anything, because the world's logic bends around it.**
Ω_C09 (whether The Outside has a self-name; the recommendation is no, and changing it changes Module 9 §5) · Ω_C11 (whether in-world periodisation is ever discussed as a sequence; the recommendation is no, and changing it gives the setting a chronology).

**Resolved and closed:** Ω_P01 (the substrate is *grain*, not *weight*) · Ω_C10 (the compact schism was over building an interpretive layer — Module 5 §10).

A story may settle an open one locally. None may settle one globally.

# Appendix A — Sources and the Pipeline

*Companion to Module 11. Two jobs: how an existing story crosses into this world, and what changes when a fixed world replaces Stage 2's invented one.*

---

## 1. The architectural change, stated first

The UKE narrative pipeline gets its displacement guarantee **structurally**: Stage 1 strips the source to a symbolic network, Stage 2 invents a setting from that topology alone, and because the setting was never derived from the source's surface, nothing of the source's surface can survive. Maximum displacement is a property of invention.

**A fixed shared world removes that guarantee.** Two sources placed into The Grain both arrive speaking about grain, terms, wall-kin, the catalog, and the well. The vocabulary is now common, so it cannot do the work of separation.

**Displacement moves to a four-axis check, and seat alone is not enough.** An earlier draft of this appendix claimed seat selection was sufficient, and its own §4 falsified the claim: Hamlet-gated and Oedipus-inverted were worked out on different seats — a record, a fact about a life — and came out as the same story, because both put the inquirer behind the camera, both ran two-handed, and both ended in the same place.

Two stories separate when they diverge on **at least three of five**:

1. **Seat** — the reference reality audited against (Module 11 §2).
2. **Gauge of the POV** — which position holds the camera, and **whether that position acts or declines.** This is the axis most likely to look different and be identical: "the one who lives beside it" and "the beneficiary" are distinct positions and both are spectators, so two stories can diverge nominally here and converge completely. The cut that matters is act-versus-decline (Module 11 §6d). Four of the five obvious positions are non-actors, so the default divergence on this axis is fake.
3. **Cast size** — which fractures are available (Module 11 §3).
4. **Terminal** — where it lands on the ending generator, including the cost-changes ending and the chosen-and-kept.
5. **The unsayable, and what becomes of it** — who in the story holds a thing they cannot say, and whether it stays unsayable, is refused, is said and unheard, or is said and answered.

**The fifth axis was added because the first four missed the real constant.** Four sample stories varied on seat, gauge, cast, and terminal, and read as one story anyway, because in every one of them somebody could not say the thing: a girl who does not tell, a husband who does not ask, a man who begins *I taught you to ride a* and watches the sentence spoil, a man who reaches for a name and kindly decides he did not need it. That relationship — narrator to the person who cannot speak — is a superb engine and it will read as one engine by the fifth story no matter how many seats are rotated through.

**The insurance policy:** one story in which somebody says the thing, in public, correctly, and is answered. It is the same countermeasure as the acting protagonist (Module 11 §6d) and the chosen-and-kept ending (§4) — three prescriptions for one drift.

Record all five per story.

**And declare what this battery cannot see.** Seat, gauge, cast, terminal, and the unsayable are all *structural*. None of them varies texture — how a story closes, which sense carries it, what stance the POV takes toward the person who cannot speak. Those are invariant under the whole matrix, which means the matrix will keep returning *sufficiently divergent* while every story shares them. Run the second battery in Module 11 §6e alongside this one; neither is sufficient alone. Two sources matching on three of them will converge no matter how far apart their origins were, and the convergence will not be visible until both are drafted.

So Stage 2's job changes from **invention** to **placement**, and its checklist changes with it:

| Stage 2 check, invented world | Same check, fixed world |
|---|---|
| Setting could exist in a history book | *Inherited from the bible; not re-derived per story* |
| Zero source-work vocabulary | *Still applies, and is now easier* |
| Displacement structural, not instructional | **Replaced:** displacement comes from seat selection — record the seat and verify no prior story used it |
| Choose a setting whose physics affords the invariant | **Replaced:** verify the bible's invariant can carry this source's Detector A, or route per §3 |

**A worked ruling of no fit.** A story whose mechanism runs past the death of a star cannot be placed here: Module 1 caps the technology and Module 10 refuses chronology, and this world is a solar system across generations, not cosmological time. Rule it out rather than compressing it. But note that its *mechanism* — a practice whose referent is gone, still warm, still working, with nothing left at the far end of it — already exists at this world's scale as the last signatory dying (Module 10 §5), and that version is stronger, because a compact after its document stops having a witness still contains someone alive who can be surprised.

The one thing a fixed world costs you: **the affordance test can now fail.** In an invented world you choose a setting that has a home for the source's invariant. Here the world is already built, and some sources will not fit it. That is a legitimate outcome and should be ruled explicitly rather than forced.

## 1b. A warning about testing this world against your own material

If the bible was built partly out of a body of existing work, then running that work back through it is not a test. A world derived from an author's recurring attractors will receive those attractors and hand them back with new furniture on them, every time, and the exercise will read as confirmation while establishing nothing.

The extractions are still worth having — mechanisms found that way are real. But they are extractions, not validations, and the distinction matters because the failure mode they mask is precisely the one in §1: convergence that only becomes visible after several stories exist.

**A genuine test uses a source with no relation to the author's habitual concerns**, chosen adversarially, and its most useful outcome is a ruling of *no fit*.

## 2. Reading a Stage 0 contract into this world

Stage 0 is the only source-sighted stage, and its `<invariant_contract>` and `<break_contract>` carry everything the crossing needs. Map them:

**`untranslatable_real` (Detector A)** — the real that binds everyone and stops being itself when measured. This world's version is fixed: *whether a way of living is required or merely kept*, held in bodies as **grain**. If the source's Detector A can be carried by grain, the source translates natively. If it names a genuinely different real, either the story is placed on a seat where the substrate is something other than a body — a practice, a record, a name — or the source does not belong here.

**`missing_floor` (Detector B)** — the baseline someone set. This world's version is also fixed: **the reference body**. Sources whose injustice rests on a chosen zero-point translate with almost no work, because the floor is already load-bearing in every module.

**`primary`** — which of the two dominates. This decides seat selection directly. `missing_floor primary="yes"` → the seat is a determination made against the reference: a hearing, a certification, a fitness ruling. `untranslatable_real primary="yes"` → the seat is a body, a practice, or a place, and what it became.

**`inherent_instrument`** — whether extraction runs through a certified measurement. If `yes`, the scored-Snare exception fires and the story may have a diegetic number, subject to Module 11 §6: the number appears only when a character with access acts on it in-scene, and the harm sits in the schema rather than the value. If `no`, the powerless position meets the constraint as consequence and sensation only.

**`verdict_scope`** — a field the contract needs and does not have, because without it a no-fit ruling cannot be made defensibly. Is the source's judgment *local* — a fact about an instance — or *global*, a fact about how one ought to live?

- **Local** → translates. The judgment survives the crossing intact and becomes the story's ruling (Module 3 §1c).
- **Global, terminal** → the judgment is a removable appendage. Subtract it and place what remains: a fairy who declares the toy Real, a narrator who scores the outcasts above the town.
- **Global, structural** → the judgment *is* the plot and cannot be subtracted without collapse. **This is the only genuine no-fit ruling**, and it is rarer than it looks. Test before ruling out: is the source's judgment about *this night, this body, this call*, or about what should be done generally? Most are the former and were merely phrased as the latter.

**`break_contract`** — replaces the ad-hoc "permitted break" this appendix previously proposed. Rule `prior_status` honestly. Most canonical sources are **DEAD**: their original violation is now the thing every reader arrives already knowing. A DEAD break means the resleeve must find a living expectation to violate instead, and *that* is where the adaptation's energy comes from — not from re-staging a twist the reader has known since school.

## 3. Mystery, permitted and gated

Local truths are recoverable (Module 3 §1b), so mystery is already legal. **What is forbidden is the mystery genre**, whose default moves each contradict something already in this bible. The gates are those rules, applied.

1. **No investigator.** No such role exists. Inquiry is always someone's second job, a visible deviation from the work they are paid for.
2. **The concealed thing is local and small.** A reading, a signature, a shipment, a date. Never the standard, the protocol, or the question underneath.
3. **Nobody is hiding it.** It was filed correctly, by a competent person, in an afternoon, for defensible reasons, and they have largely forgotten. No confession scene, because nothing would be refused.
4. **Finding it changes nothing procedurally.** Three outcomes, all available: the record is **held** (marked pending, Module 5 §6); the record is **absorbed** (entered, correct, and the arrangement adjusts around it); or the record **disperses** — cited in something that is cited in something that fails, leaving language in circulation and nothing else (Module 11 §4). **This is the gate that makes the procedural unwritable**, and everything interesting lives after it.
5. **The finder is implicated.** Long observation is long contact (Module 9 §9b). The inquiry turns up the inquirer's own trace.
6. **Asking costs someone.** Inquiry is a status assignment, and status assignment is the setting's central political act (Module 8 §3).
7. **No solution scene.** Nobody assembles the account aloud. The reader assembles; the characters carry pieces.

**The engine that replaces the reveal:** *you now know, and knowing gives you no standing, no forum, and no remedy, and you must decide how to live in the same room as the knowledge.*

**On spending a break here.** If the collection wants one confirmed reveal that genuinely lands, author it through the break contract rather than as an exception: `prior_status: DEAD` for the mystery form itself (readers arrive expecting revelation to resolve), `target_prior` = the expectation that a confirmed truth changes an arrangement. Then the story establishes the truth completely, it is accepted and entered, and nothing shifts — because nothing about the arrangement ever depended on the concealment. One story does this. Every later story inherits it.

## 4. Four worked crossings

### The Old Man and the Sea — native

*Detector A:* an achievement whose reality is inseparable from having been lived through, and which cannot survive being brought back for assessment. *Floor:* the dock's measure of a day's work. *Primary:* untranslatable_real.

He goes past the last approved waypoint, off the manifest, outside the corridor. He does something real and unwitnessed. The return destroys the evidence — not by bad luck, but because no instrument was pointed at him and no form has a field for it. What comes back is the frame of the thing and a man who cannot be believed, and the people who measure what is left are not wrong.

*The world supplies:* the structural reason no witness was possible, which the source had to arrange.

### Medea — native, and sharpened

*Detector B primary:* standing is conferred by a jurisdiction that has withdrawn it, and there is no ground beneath the withdrawal. *Seat:* a determination about a child.

She is outside certification, facing repatriation, holding no claim any forum recognises, and her partner has secured his standing by an arrangement she cannot contest. And the world hands her a worse instrument than the source's: irreversibilities are the only real property (Module 4), so **where a child is raised is unadministered, unrecoverable, and aimed with precision at the other parent.** She need harm no one. She makes a decision about gravity that no institution can name as a wrong, that cannot be undone, that might be correct — and neither of them will live long enough to find out.

### Hamlet — gated, and the break is DEAD

*`original_break`:* that a supernatural accusation would prove true and vengeance would be righteous. *`prior_status`:* **DEAD** — every reader arrives knowing the ghost was honest. *`target_prior`:* that establishing the truth confers any standing to act on it.

So run §3. He inquires, as a second job, visibly, at cost. **He finds out** — the record is there, the signature legible, the filer competent and unbothered and largely unable to recall it. He assembles it correctly and completely. And it gives him nothing: no forum will hear it, the filing was procedurally sound, and the man will be sincerely bewildered to be accused. The delay everyone reads as irresolution is the accurate perception that there is nothing the knowledge is for.

*Discard:* the confirming device, the villain who knows, the duel that settles anything.

### Oedipus — the pure test of the affordance

*Detector A:* a fact about a life that determines its meaning and can only be known by having lived it. *`inherent_instrument`:* no.

**Inverted (free) — and told from the wrong side.** The inquiry is competent, exhaustive, honest, and never closes; he cannot establish whether the thing he fears is true, and the not-knowing is not a reprieve. But do not give him the camera, or this becomes the Hamlet story again on a different seat.

**Put the POV on someone who has to live beside the inquiry.** A person who knows what he is asking, who may or may not be the answer, who cannot stop him and cannot help him, and whose own standing depends on the question staying open. They watch a decent man work toward something that would destroy them both and cannot prefer either outcome. Four-handed, so the fracture is wide (Module 11 §3). Terminal: cost changes, structure holds — he does not stop asking, and the household arranges itself around the asking, and by the last page it takes less out of them than it did.

**Straight (spends the break per §3).** He finds out entirely. The city does not fall. Nothing was contingent on the concealment. Then the blinding is not a response to knowledge but to learning that knowledge was never the thing in the way.

# Appendix B — The Generative Set

*The prohibitions, restated as what they buy. Twelve generators, each absorbing three or four rules.*

**Governing law:** every prohibition in this document exists because a cheap route was available and would have been taken. The rule closes the route; the generator is what the closure makes reachable. **A rule with no generator is either a guardrail or hygiene, and should be marked as one rather than presented as a principle.**

---

## 1. Vocabulary arrives through misuse

*Closed:* the explainer, the anthropological aside, the character who defines a term for another character, the outsider who diagnoses correctly.

*Open:* a word is learned from the gap between what it does and what someone thinks it means. A newcomer uses *carried* to mean transferred and is corrected by a silence. A child says a thing at the wrong volume and the room's reaction teaches the reader its weight. Someone confidently explains a custom they have misunderstood and nobody bothers to fix it. **The reader learns faster from a confident error than from an accurate account**, and the error is characterisation while the account is furniture.

## 2. The body is the only instrument anyone owns

*Closed:* narration denominated in the system's numbers, counting of any kind, measurement standing in for sensation.

*Open:* the stairs got worse. A cut takes longer than it used to. Food stops tasting like much and you salt it until someone comments. And because narration contains no numbers, **a single number becomes an event**: someone with access reads it aloud, disputes it, forges it, refuses to enter it. The scarcity is what makes the instrument a weapon when it finally appears.

## 3. Unavailable cause makes blame structural

*Closed:* the diagnosis, the solved case, the investigator, the reveal that establishes what happened.

*Open:* five candidate causes and no procedure that separates them, which means blame goes where the arrangement already points — and the person doing the blaming is kind about it. A competent clinician says *I can't tell you that*, means it, and is disbelieved, because the alternative to a cause is unbearable. **You get the scene where consolation and accusation are the same speech**, delivered by someone with no bad intent, and there is no version of it where anyone finds out.

## 4. Nobody is hiding anything, so everyone is available

*Closed:* the confession, the interrogation, the antagonist who knows what they are, the villain.

*Open:* you can put the person who caused the harm in the room and let them be helpful. They filed it correctly, in an afternoon, for defensible reasons, and have largely forgotten. **The most persuasive speech in the story can go to them.** Every character can be approached, questioned, liked, and none of them can be caught, because there is nothing to catch.

## 5. Position supplies the blindness, so warmth is safe

*Closed:* the winking beneficiary, the beneficiary who feels sorry for anyone, authorial signalling that a character is really wrong.

*Open:* write a genuinely lovely person doing damage, at full warmth, with no hedging, and the reader does the work. Their first move on an anomaly is diagnosis — *this is an impropriety, a misallocation* — not wonder. **The permission this buys is enormous:** you may make the reader enjoy a perspective they will be ashamed of enjoying, and the shame is theirs rather than the text's.

## 6. Nothing ends, so the small adjustment is the largest available event

*Closed:* revolution, reform that works, growth, transcendence, the protagonist who resolves.

*Open:* the ceiling on consequence is set so low that a hand taken under the other side of a carrier is legible as enormous. Matching pace instead of racing. A mark made to land on everyone. **The seventh ending — the structure holds and the cost changes — is only available because the sixth was closed**, and it is the ending most readers will remember.

## 7. Distance dissolves combination, so the room is the whole polity

*Closed:* movements, organising that survives separation, solidarity at scale.

*Open:* everything political happens between four people who see each other daily. A watch, a bay, a bunk row is a complete political system with its own factions, and it is the only scale at which anyone can act. **Every workplace scene is a constitutional scene**, and the reader never has to be told the stakes because the stakes are in the room.

## 8. Nobody may name the theme, so the gap becomes the medium

*Closed:* the analytical narrator, theme-naming dialogue, the line that could serve as the blurb, meta-commentary.

*Open:* dramatic irony as the primary instrument rather than an occasional effect. The reader assembles what the character cannot, and **that gap is where the feeling is kept.** A sentence can be generous, sincere, and lethal at once — the reader watches it land while the speaker watches it succeed. Nothing else in the toolkit does that.

## 9. The record is complete, which makes accuracy the weapon

*Closed:* the falsified document, the cover-up, the exposé.

*Open:* harm with no lie anywhere in it. One reading was taken when the protocol asked for one; something else was true four seconds later; the form has one field. **Nothing was hidden and nothing was wrong**, and any auditor will find the record complete and correct, and it will be. This is the only form of institutional violence that leaves nothing to expose, which is why it is the one that keeps working.

## 10. Nothing can be dated, so grief must be invented

*Closed:* the deathbed, the funeral, the moment, closure.

*Open:* a culture that has had to build its own apparatus — a grammar for stages of absence, a full goodbye that functions as a verdict, a point where writing stops that nobody agrees on, letters that outlive their senders, the dead worked into the shielding. **All of it is invention rather than inheritance**, which means every custom is contested, locally variant, and available to be got wrong by a character in front of the reader.

## 11. The author doesn't know either, so specify the shape of the gap

*Closed:* the withheld answer, the private solution, the note that says what really happened.

*Open:* author the mechanism space instead of the fact — which causes are candidates, why nothing separates them, what was never raised to compare against. **A complete specification containing no answer.** This is more work than deciding privately and it produces prose without coyness, because there is no locked door for a reader to press against.

## 12. Every device has a budget, so the next story must find another mechanism

*Closed:* the recurring image, the second use of the record-as-harm, the third hand-on-object ending.

*Open:* the budget forces the search. Harm has four routes and only one is worked — a body, a person, the physics, a room. Endings can land in speech, an absence, a document, another body, a sound, or mid-motion. Load can travel through smell, sound, taste, proprioception. **What the budget buys is the story you would not have written**, and it is the only rule in this document that improves the writer rather than the writing.

## 13. Nothing global can be settled, so a local ruling is the largest act available

*Closed:* the answered question, the verdict on how to live, the study that comes back, the authority that adjudicates.

*Open:* a person establishes one small true thing, at their own cost, in front of people, and it holds — and it changes no arrangement whatsoever. **The ceiling on consequence is what makes the act legible.** Because nothing can be settled generally, settling one particular is enormous, and the reader feels the size of it in a way no reform ending would permit.

*Distinct from #6, and they blur if you let them.* #6 is **arrangement**: people rearranging themselves so the same weight costs less, and nothing becomes true. #13 is **establishment**: one thing becomes true and stays true, and nothing gets lighter. A story can do both. Most should do one.

## 14. Everyone is right from where they stand, so the same room is a tragedy or a farce

*Closed:* the villain, the fool, the character who is simply mistaken, anyone whose position cannot be defended in full.

*Open:* a room in which six people reason correctly from incompatible premises is the engine of both registers, and the variable is whether the cost enters a ledger. Every instrument here accumulates with no operation for subtraction, which makes the default ledger tragic — so comedy is denominated in the one non-monotonic currency available: **standing in the moment, as against standing on the record.** Spend it freely and recover it by the last page. **Nothing entered, everything visible, and the identical scene is comedy** — the clause still read correctly, the queue still never refusing, the blindness still supplied by position, and everyone walks out. The rigor is the joke: the pleasure is watching the machinery run correctly to an absurd place, which is unavailable to any world whose institutions are merely stupid.

## Guardrails (buy nothing; prevent a collapse)

Marked as such so they are not mistaken for principles: reproductive licensing stays one polity's local arrangement; the incident is never established; the invariant never acquires an administrator; no naive outsider diagnoses correctly.

## Hygiene (craft rules, no world content)

Belong in a checklist, not in exposition: 350 words after the decisive scene; register modulates under pressure; no counting; no device more than three times within a story.
