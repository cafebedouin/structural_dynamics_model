# The Eighth Commentary

The examination hall at Wei-lin holds four hundred cells, each the width of a door, and a man sits in his cell for three days with the Code and his ink and nothing else, and at the end of it the province knows what he is worth. I sat it three times. The third time I passed, eleventh in a list of forty, and my mother wept, and the levy-men crossed our orchard off their rolls that same autumn, because a degree-holder's land pays no levy, and that, if you want the truth before the story, is what the list is for. The rice the levy does not take from us it takes from the farms of men who did not pass. Nobody says this in the hall. The hall says: the Code must be administered by those who know it, and here is how we know who knows.

Here is how we know who knows. A question is set — a flooded boundary, a disputed inheritance, a magistrate's clerk caught selling grain-weights — and the candidate writes his judgment, and two examiners read it against the Seven Commentaries, which are the sanctioned readings of the Code, bound in yellow, unchanged in my lifetime. An answer is correct when the examiner can find its sentences in the margins of the Seven. That is the whole art. There are tutors in every market town who have never judged so much as a chicken theft and who can recite which turns of phrase the yellow margins reward, and my father mortgaged the orchard's next nine harvests to buy me three years of such a man, and this is not a corruption of the examination. I must be exact here, because I spent my first two failures believing it was a corruption, and the belief cost my family two more years of the tutor. It is the examination working. The hall is not asking whether I can judge. The hall is asking whether my hand can be held still against the margins, for three days, in a cell the width of a door. It measures that. It measures it very well.

I failed the first time because of the flood. I was nineteen, and the question that year was a flooded boundary — two farms, the stones carried off, the silt lying new over both men's fields — and I had stood in that question in my own boots, in my uncle's village, the year the Kan overran its banks. I had watched the district magistrate, an old man with cracked nails, walk the drowned ground for a morning, and then set the boundary not where the old stones argued but where the two families could still draw water without crossing each other at the well at dusk, because, he said to no one in particular, a line that sets two houses at the same well at dusk is not a boundary, it is a slow knife. Both families cursed him for a season and kept the peace for thirty years. So I wrote what I had seen the ground require. I wrote it well; I can still recite it. The examiners could find my sentences in no margin, and they were right: the sentences were not there. I had answered the question as if there were people in it. In the hall there are never people in it. That is not an oversight. A case with living people in it cannot be graded twice the same way, and what cannot be graded twice the same way cannot rank four hundred men on a list, and the list, remember, is what the levy reads. The hall does not fail to measure judgment. Judgment is the thing that happens when a live person stands in front of the rule and the rule must be read for something. Put that in a cell and it is no longer itself; it has become an answer. The hall can only ever receive answers.

So I learned. That is the polite word. My tutor put it less politely: the hall is a scale, he said, stop bringing it weather. I brought it stillness instead. The second failure was narrow; the third sitting, I wrote three days of sentences with yellow margins behind every one of them, precise, obedient, and dead, and I felt the live thing in me stand aside to let them pass, the way you stand aside on a narrow stair. Eleventh of forty. The orchard free of the levy. My mother wept.

They gave me Ho-yang district, and in my second year the widow Fan came to the yamen over a field her husband's brother had enclosed, and I rode out and walked the ground, and it was the flood question, my flood question, the one I had failed with — silted line, two households, one well — and I knew what the ground required as surely as I have ever known anything with my hands or eyes. And I want you to see what I did, because it is the only thing in this account that I ask you to see. I set the line where the old magistrate would have set it, well apart from the well. And then I sat up two nights with the Seven Commentaries and built the judgment backward, sentence by sentence, so that every clause of the just thing wore a yellow margin over its face, so that the prefect's reviewers — who read my judgments exactly as the hall read my answers, for stillness against the sanctioned readings — would find nothing alive in it anywhere. It stands in the district records now. It reads as if no one ever stood in front of me. That is what it cost to make it stand.

You are waiting for the turn, so here is the turn: there is no eighth commentary. I looked. For years, in the way a man looks who cannot admit what he is looking for — some yellow-bound margin where the walked ground and the dusk-well and the cracked-nailed old man might sit as precedent, so that the next clerk in the next flood could cite the live thing instead of smuggling it. It does not exist, and I have come to understand, slowly, at my desk, that it cannot. Write that margin and it becomes the Seven's brother; the tutors would sell its turns of phrase by the following spring; my son would learn to hold his hand still against it. The thing the old man did on the drowned ground survives being done. It does not survive being sanctioned. There is no way to keep it except one clerk at a time, at night, dressing it in the margins of the dead.

My son begins with a tutor in the new year. The mortgage is long paid; the orchard's levy falls on farms I could name; I have bought him the same three years my father bought me. He has, I think, the eye — he watches the ground when men argue, not the men. If he is lucky, the hall will take that from him for a list and an exemption, and give it back to him disguised, later, at a desk like this one, at night. I do not know a better bargain on offer. That is not a defense of it. It is the figure of what we pay, entered by the man who paid it, in the margin where the reviewers never look.
