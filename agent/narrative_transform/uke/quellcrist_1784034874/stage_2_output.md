# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## Setting

The Sanitary Magistracy of the River Compact, a confederation of grain-port cities along a great inland delta, in the decades after the Long Drownings — a sequence of floods that killed a third of the basin's population and left the surviving cities dependent on a single interlocking system of levees, granaries, and quarantine law. The Compact's founding charter, written in the aftermath, established the **Ledger of Standing**: every resident is entered at birth or arrival as either *Kept* (a full claimant on flood-shelter, grain-ration, and burial rite) or *Counted* (a body the Compact tracks for labor and contagion purposes, owed shelter only as capacity permits, owed no burial rite, and legally disposable in declared emergency). This is not prejudice administered badly. It is the founding math of the charter itself — the Compact survived the Drownings by deciding, once, permanently, whose survival was structural and whose was contingent. The magistrates who apply the Ledger today did not invent the partition; they inherited a zero they cannot audit, only read from.

Three generations on, the port-city of Ostrehaven runs a functioning bureaucracy of Assessors, Wardens, and Notaries who administer the Ledger with genuine procedural rigor — filed appeals, witnessed hearings, recorded votes. The instrument is honest. Every reading it produces is a true reading. What no Assessor's hearing can ever produce is a *recalculation of the zero* — the founding decision that some bodies are ends and some are means was made once, by the charter, and the entire apparatus of hearings and appeals operates strictly downstream of it, never upon it.

## Constraint Reference Table

**C1 (invariant, Missing Floor) → The Ledger of Standing itself: the founding entry, Kept or Counted.**
Not naturalized as a Snare or Rope — naturalized as *physics*, the ground the city's law stands on. It has no administrator in the sense of an author; the Assessors read it, they do not write it. A Counted dockworker (X₁) experiences it as the fact beneath every other fact of his life: the reason his wages are half, his housing is riverside where the levee is thinnest, his name is recorded in a different book with different ink. A Compact Notary (X₂) experiences the same Ledger as the reason the city has survived three more floods without total collapse — the coordination that lets triage happen at all, fast, without deliberation, in an actual emergency. Both readings are true. Neither can see the other's zero.

**C2 → The Doctrine of Sanitary Necessity: the standing legal justification that Counted bodies may be conscripted into flood-defense labor, quarantine exposure, and levee work ahead of Kept bodies, "in proportion to the Compact's continuance."**
Locals call it *the Necessity*, or just *the Compact's arithmetic*. For a Counted laborer (X₁), the Necessity is a pure trap: it is invoked every flood season to send him onto the levee crown in the worst weather, into the fever wards first, and it offers him nothing back — no vote in how it's invoked, no ceiling on how often. For a Notary (X₂) who authors Necessity findings, it is indispensable coordinating law — the mechanism that lets the Compact mobilize labor during a levee breach without a citywide debate that would drown everyone while it was being held.

**C3 → The Culling Writ: the terminal instrument, invoked only in declared emergency, by which a Warden may order Counted residents of a flooding ward relocated last, fed last, or in the gravest declared emergencies, left. **
Called *the Writ* by everyone, spoken of by Counted families the way other people speak of weather that has already killed someone they loved. This is where C1's classification and C2's Necessity resolve into their final form: the moment the partition is not administered but executed. For a Counted family (X₁), the Writ is death dressed as procedure. For the Compact magistracy (X₂), the Writ is the tool that lets the city choose, quickly, which quarter to save when the levee cannot hold everywhere — statecraft, not cruelty, from where they stand.

## The Instrument, Honestly

The Ledger, the Necessity finding, and the Writ are all produced through real, witnessed, filed procedure — the Court of Standing hears petitions, the Notaries record votes, the tally of who is Kept and who is Counted in a given ward is public and consistently applied. **The numbers are true.** A Counted man's ration-weight, his labor-hours owed, his position on a flood-ward manifest — these are honestly measured, honestly recorded, and can be read aloud in the hearing room without distortion. What the Court cannot do, in any hearing, however fairly conducted, is put the *founding partition itself* to a vote. That was set once, in the charter, by people three generations dead, and the entire apparatus — however scrupulous — operates on a zero it inherited and cannot re-derive. A better Notary, a fairer Warden, an uncorrupted hearing: none of these touch the floor. This is the invariant's home in the world's physics.

## Characters as Positions

**Mireva Tesh** (X₁) — Counted, dockside cordage-splicer, Ostrehaven's lower wharf ward. Third generation Counted; her grandmother was entered at the founding charter's first census as a "surplus body, war-year," and the entry has propagated down the family line as if it were blood. χ = 0.675 at C1 (Tangled Rope: the Ledger genuinely coordinates flood response and genuinely extracts her labor and safety asymmetrically). At C2, χ = 1.05 (Snare: the Necessity has no coordination surplus for her at all, only cost). At C3, χ = 1.50 (Snare, maximal: the Writ is the terminal mechanism that could, in the next flood season, order her ward abandoned last or not at all).

**Notary Aldous Ferrent** (X₂) — Institutional. Third-generation Notary of the Court of Standing, author of two Necessity findings in the past decade, currently drafting procedural language for Writ invocation criteria. χ negative across all three constraints — he is not evil, not even unusually cold; he experiences the Ledger as the coordination that let his grandfather's generation survive the Second Drowning, the Necessity as the only tool fast enough to matter in an actual breach, and the Writ as the unbearable last resort he hopes never to sign, but whose *existence* he defends as the only alternative to total loss.

## Linguistic Strategy: Direct Translation + Naming

Base register: formal Compact legal-administrative English (Court of Standing, Notary, Warden, Writ, Ledger, Necessity) laid over a working-class riverine dialect for Counted speech — clipped, present-tense-heavy, oriented around water levels and shift hours rather than abstractions. Kept/institutional speech is procedurally dense, full of passive constructions ("it was found necessary that...") that erase the deciding agent.

Concepts with no clean English equivalent, carried in translation:
- ***Standing-water***: the Counted term for a family whose Ledger entry has never once, in any generation, been successfully appealed — used the way people elsewhere might say "dead broke," except it means dead-certain, structurally, not financially.
- ***To be read***: Counted idiom for having one's name called at a Ledger hearing — carries dread, since being *read* is the precondition for being *written into* a Necessity finding or a Writ.
- ***Charter-deep***: something so foundational no living authority can reach it — "that's charter-deep" is what a Notary says when a petitioner asks him to reconsider the Kept/Counted partition itself, and it is not evasion, it is the truth of the world's physics.

**Naming:** Kept families carry old Compact-founder surnames (Ferrent, Voss, Callenreigh) recorded in the founding charter's witness list — names that are, themselves, proof of original Kept status, since the charter only names its Kept signatories. Counted families carry ward-names — assigned at the founding census by wharf or quarter (Tesh, from "Third Eastern Shoal House") — functional, geographic, replacing whatever name the person's ancestors held before the Drownings erased the prior records. A Counted person's name literally *is* a filing address. This is not decoration; it's the floor made audible every time someone says who they are.
