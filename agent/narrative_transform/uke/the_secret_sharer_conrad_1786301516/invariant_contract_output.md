# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant.** The world contains a real — the wordless recognition between two people who share a fundamental grain, which arrives before any oath, license, or law and answers to none of them — that the system's own instruments cannot read, own, or reduce to a value. **Falsifier:** if the finished story contains a recoverable "true value" that the tribunal or the guild merely measured wrong — a correct verdict some fairer judge could have reached — the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**The missing floor.** Beneath the visible system of licenses, oaths, and tribunals there is a founding choice with no neutral ground under it: the assumption that any body of judges, however constituted, is competent to weigh an act done in the grip of the reef, the storm-hour, the debt no one else saw come due. There is no such competent body; the tribunal's confidence in its own jurisdiction is the founding fiction, not a fact about the world. **Falsifier:** if the story frames the tribunal's error as a local mistake a wiser or better-informed tribunal could have avoided, the floor invariant was lost.

**The substrate.** In the salt-marsh chartered towns of the Drowned Coast, the thing that cannot be measured is called **the grain of a diver** — a way of moving through black water, holding breath, reading current, that either matches another diver's grain or does not, and no license examiner, no depth-gauge, no guild ledger has ever been built that can certify it. Divers say a grain is "read," never "scored." Two divers either move as one body in the dark or they don't, and every diver alive knows within one dive which is true, while the Chartered Guild's entire apparatus of bells, badges, and seniority rolls has no column for it at all.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence.** *You press your palm flat against the hull of a stranger's boat before a dive because your hands already know, before your mind does, whether this is a grain you can trust your held breath to — and the Guild's badge on his coat has never once told you that, and never will.*
