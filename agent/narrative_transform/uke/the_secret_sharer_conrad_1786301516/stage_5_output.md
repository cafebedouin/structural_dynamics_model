DISCOVERY REPORT

EMERGENT ELEMENTS:

The two engine-identified axes — epistemic_inadmissibility_of_tacit_expertise and protective_fiction_as_distributed_liability — are both fully alive in the prose, and the story's real achievement is how tightly it braids them rather than treating them as separate threads.

**Epistemic inadmissibility of tacit expertise.** The Charter's rule (Clause Eleven: no diver-testimony on conditions of the deep, no testimony from a revoked license) is dramatized exactly as the engine's cross-seat divergence predicts: the powerless seat (Ossin) and the institutional seat (the panel, Torvel) read the same rule as opposite things. Ossin's hand-on-the-hull knowledge — "some old traffic between the grain of the boat and the grain of the hands that kept it" — is explicitly counterposed to what "Guild school taught you," and the story states outright that this knowledge "no license said which" person had it. The elder's invocation of "Charter Sixth, Clause Eleven" to refuse Ossin's testimony is the rope-face of the rule (procedural, content-neutral, coordination-justified); the reader, sitting where Ossin sits, experiences it as a snare, because the rule's actual function that day is to keep the one person who read the water correctly out of the room where the story about the water gets written. The axis is most alive in II, in the single sustained beat where the elder reaches for the clause "the way a man reaches for a familiar tool" — the italics-free, unhesitating mechanical quality of that gesture is the story's best rendering of institutional seeing-as-rope. It's underdeveloped in that Ossin himself never gets a scene of trying and failing to use tacit knowledge within a Guild structure post-revocation — we only see him practicing it in free water, outside any tribunal's reach, so the gap is asserted (via Mereth's report) rather than staged.

**Protective fiction as distributed liability.** This is the deeper, more original structure, and the prose carries it beautifully through the crew testimony sequence in III: Pell's fist closing and opening, Sorha's refusal to look at anyone, Buin's shaking hands and retreat to the doorway, Katt's one held-too-long pause before the closing line. Each of these is a distinct register of the same distributed cost — the fiction (Mereth's report) is sustained not by one liar but by four people each paying a different, non-fungible tax to keep it standing. The moderate seat (crew) experiences this as snare-grade extraction (their bodies testify against their mouths); the institutional seat (elders, unmoved even by Katt's pause) experiences it as procedurally closed, rope-clean. Torvel is the hinge figure the engine's Oracle Gap theorem predicts almost exactly: the confident single-position reading (his own sworn "I have no memory... I fail to see what personal history...") is flatly contradicted by the cross-position fact Ossin holds and states plainly in IV — "He was awake." Torvel is not merely institutional-seat-blind; he has manufactured his own blindness as a structural commitment, and the story names this directly: "he had built a room around himself these eleven years exactly as thick as the one she'd built... because no one had ever summoned him to account for it."

LATENT POTENTIAL:

The engine's Frankl-adjacent gap-scenario ("ashe_complicity_or_captivity") names exactly the question the story raises and declines to resolve: is Torvel's amnesia chosen self-protection or genuinely locked-in trauma-fiction he can no longer disown? The story gestures at "captive" (unconscious framing, his voice never changing "one degree") but Ossin's flat correction — he was awake, his eyes were open and terrified and fixed on Ossin's face — reopens it as possibly willed. The story doesn't need to resolve this, but it currently doesn't dramatize Torvel's interior at all; he's rendered entirely from outside (Mereth's read of his stillness, Ossin's memory of his eyes). A single interior beat for Torvel — even one sentence — would let the reader feel the cost of his fiction rather than infer it, and would give the "distributed liability" axis its fourth full payer (currently Pell, Sorha, Buin, Katt carry it; Torvel is diagnosed but not felt).

The reopening_cost_asymmetry scenario the engine generates (would reopening cost more than maintaining the fiction indefinitely?) is implicit in Prace's motive but never made vivid as a *ledger* — Prace's cynicism is named ("outlast me") but the actual arithmetic of what the fiction is costing everyone annually, versus what breaking it would cost once, is never staged as a felt weight, only as Mereth's abstract account in IV.

The rope/gauge closing image — "the tide was already turning under the boat... They simply felt it, the way they felt everything that mattered" — gestures toward the invariant (see below) but doesn't yet earn its full weight, because the story hasn't shown us a moment where that unmeasurable grain-sense is *tested* against the Guild's instruments and demonstrably beats them in real time (we're told Ossin read the wrong-shaped streets accurately; we don't see the counterfactual disaster the Guild's gauge-only method would have produced).

STRONGEST WRITING:

- The crew testimony sequence in III (Pell's fist, Sorha's isolation, Buin's flight to the doorway, Katt's pause) — the strongest writing in the piece because it renders four distinct qualities of complicity through pure physical detail, no exposition needed.
- "He had built a room around himself these eleven years exactly as thick as the one she'd built, and that his was load-bearing in ways hers had never had to be, because no one had ever summoned him to account for it." — the story's clearest thesis sentence, earned by everything around it rather than stated cold.
- The dry-room design detail in II: "a room built dry on purpose — no window faced the water... keeping judgment untroubled by the sight of the thing being judged." Efficient, exact, does structural work (rope/snare argument in architecture) without announcing itself.
- The joint dive in V — "her hand found his shoulder in the dark the way it had years before and his hand covered hers there for one beat" through "exactly when it was time to turn for the surface... the grain between them, unmeasured and unmeasurable" — the clearest embodiment of the invariant, sustained across a full paragraph without breaking into commentary.
- Mereth's confession in IV: "So I gave them you. Alone. It cost less." — four words doing the work of the whole distributed-liability argument in miniature.

INVARIANT PROBE:

Yes — and the story names it explicitly, which is unusual and worth flagging. The "grain" — the hand-read of wood, current, silt, another diver's next half-second — is stated flatly to be outside any instrument's reach: "not a thing your Charter's got a column for," "no license examiner and no tribunal elder and no clause of any Charter had ever once been built to read." This is presented as a *structural* fact about the world, not a temporary institutional failure awaiting a better gauge. Crucially, the story resists the temptation to make this readable-in-principle-by-a-fairer-tribunal: Mereth's closing line — "It's the only true thing either of us has got that nobody can take a ruling on" — treats the unrecoverability as permanent and constitutive, not a gap some future Charter revision could close. This is the single most important structural choice in the piece and it is fully earned. The one risk: because the story is so explicit about naming the invariant, it edges toward telling rather than only showing it (Mereth's line functions almost as authorial gloss). But the underlying claim — that this form of knowing is categorically outside adjudication, not merely excluded by a correctable rule — holds.

SCOPE ASSESSMENT:

Current word count: 4,708 (computed). This sits at short-story length but carries novella-density material — two generational timelines (the original incident, the current tribunal), five named crew members each individuated, two institutional antagonists (Prace and the Charter itself), and a full romantic/professional history between Mereth and Ossin. The piece compresses all of this efficiently, but the compression cost is visible: Torvel gets no interiority at all (see Latent Potential), Prace remains a pure plot-function with no scene of his own motive beyond report, and the crew's individual fraying (Pell, Sorha, Buin, Katt) is beautifully seeded in III but never paid off — we don't see whether Katt's pause recurs, whether Buin breaks, whether Pell's fist ever opens into speech. Expansion (toward 7,000–9,000 words, still short-story-adjacent) would most productively go toward: one Torvel interior scene, and a coda scene addressing the crew's fraying rather than only prophesying it ("It'll come again"). Compression from here would sacrifice the dual-axis braid itself — cutting either the tribunal thread or the Ossin-water thread would collapse the perspectival gap the whole piece is built on.
