# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant.** The world contains a real — the wordless recognition between two people who share a fundamental grain, which arrives before any oath, license, or law and answers to none of them — that the system's own instruments cannot read, own, or reduce to a value. **Falsifier:** if the finished story contains a recoverable "true value" that the tribunal or the guild merely measured wrong — a correct verdict some fairer judge could have reached — the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**The missing floor.** Beneath the visible system of licenses, oaths, and tribunals there is a founding choice with no neutral ground under it: the assumption that any body of judges, however constituted, is competent to weigh an act done in the grip of the reef, the storm-hour, the debt no one else saw come due. There is no such competent body; the tribunal's confidence in its own jurisdiction is the founding fiction, not a fact about the world. **Falsifier:** if the story frames the tribunal's error as a local mistake a wiser or better-informed tribunal could have avoided, the floor invariant was lost.

**The substrate.** In the salt-marsh chartered towns of the Drowned Coast, the thing that cannot be measured is called **the grain of a diver** — a way of moving through black water, holding breath, reading current, that either matches another diver's grain or does not, and no license examiner, no depth-gauge, no guild ledger has ever been built that can certify it. Divers say a grain is "read," never "scored." Two divers either move as one body in the dark or they don't, and every diver alive knows within one dive which is true, while the Chartered Guild's entire apparatus of bells, badges, and seniority rolls has no column for it at all.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence.** *You press your palm flat against the hull of a stranger's boat before a dive because your hands already know, before your mind does, whether this is a grain you can trust your held breath to — and the Guild's badge on his coat has never once told you that, and never will.*

# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

**Setting.** The Drowned Coast, a chain of salt-marsh harbor towns roughly two centuries after their founding by charter-fleeing refugees, now grown prosperous, bureaucratic, and self-satisfied. Their wealth comes from pearl-diving the flooded ruins of an older, larger civilization that drowned when the coastal shelf subsided — cathedral spires and counting-houses now three fathoms down, thick with black pearl oysters that grow nowhere else. Diving them is lethal work: narrow drowned stairwells, silt-outs, the bends, and a current called the Undertow that runs unpredictably through the ruin-streets. The Chartered Pearling Guild licenses every diver, sets every quota, and — critically — sits in judgment whenever a dive goes wrong and someone dies, since diving deaths are legally disputes over contract and negligence, not accidents. Guild tribunals convene in dry, paneled rooms on land, adjudicating events that happened in black water none of the judges have ever entered.

**Constraint reference table.**

- **C1 (Structural_Authority_Assignment) → "Wardenship."** Every dive-crew answers to a Warden, a Guild-certified senior diver with authority to call dives, assign partners, and testify at tribunal. Wardenship looks like bedrock — "the Warden decides" is treated as a fact of nature, like tide tables. It is not: Wardenship is a badge issued by the Guild, revocable, and a Warden who stops diving personally, who never again risks the black water himself, keeps the badge anyway. Agent_A and Agent_B are both Wardens, structurally identical — each commands a crew, each answers only upward to the tribunal, each has fused "Warden" so completely into who they are that neither can imagine unbecoming one, though nothing physical prevents it.

- **C2 (Legitimacy_Deficit_Under_External_Scrutiny) → "Standing before the Tribunal."** When a dive goes fatal, the Warden must stand before a tribunal of dry-land Guild elders and account for the call he made underwater — a call the elders cannot verify except through testimony, salvage logs, and the word of the crew. A Warden's authority, rock-solid on the boat, turns to vapor the instant it must survive cross-examination by people who have never dived. Agent_A, standing alone, feels this acutely — one contradicted witness from disgrace. Agent_C's crew, when they close ranks and testify identically, barely feels it at all; the crowd absorbs what the individual cannot.

- **C3 (Concealed_Reciprocal_Obligation) → "The Debt Under the Water."** Years before, in a stairwell collapse, Agent_D — now a senior Guild magistrate who sits on tribunals — was pulled from a silt-out by Agent_A, who broke three Guild diving protocols to do it, and by rights should have reported the improvised, unlicensed rescue-work of a diver everyone now calls an outlaw: Agent_B, who has since had his badge stripped and dives outside the Charter entirely, illegally, for smaller cuts, because he trusts his own grain more than any tribunal's judgment. Agent_A has never told the Guild that Agent_B saved the dive, saved the boy who became Magistrate Agent_D, and that the "reckless disregard" written into the incident report is a lie Agent_A has maintained for years to keep her own Wardenship. Agent_D knows the truth and has never surfaced it either, because surfacing it would validate an outlaw diver over Guild procedure and unravel the magistrate's own legitimacy along with it. Agent_C's crew, who were on the boat that day, know enough to be dangerous and say nothing, holding a silence that costs each of them something different: Agent_A carries it as a noose that tightens with every tribunal season; Agent_D, robed and dry and presiding, carries it as leverage that costs him nothing at all; Agent_C's crew carries it as a shared, exhausting weight that never quite breaks any one of them because they share it.

**Character positions.**

- **Agent_A → Mereth Cail**, Warden of the *Halcyon*, twelve years chartered, the diver everyone assumes is destined for the magistracy — except for one report she has never let anyone reread closely.
- **Agent_B → Ossin Thray**, once a Warden, badge stripped after the stairwell incident's official version took hold; now an outlaw pearler working the deep ruin-streets outside Guild boundary-buoys, trusted by no institution, trusted completely by the handful of divers who've ever shared black water with him.
- **Agent_C → the *Halcyon* crew**, four divers bound to Mereth by contract and by the stairwell debt, referred to collectively and individually — Pell, Sorha, young Buin, and old Katt — whose collective silence is a Rope, not yet frayed.
- **Agent_D → Magistrate Torvel Ashe**, seated on the Guild tribunal, the boy Ossin pulled from the silt-out, now a dry-land judge who has built a legitimate, gleaming career on an incident report he knows to be false.

**Linguistic strategy: Code-switching, dual-register.**
Two registers govern speech on the Coast: **Guild-tongue**, the clipped, Latinate, procedural language of licenses, quotas, and tribunal testimony ("the diver of record affirms compliance with depth protocol Sixth Charter, Clause Nine"); and **marsh-cant**, the older, tidal, kin-based speech divers use to each other underwater-adjacent, on the boat, in the shanty bars — thick with breath-idiom and current-idiom.

- Guild-tongue example: *"Warden Cail, the tribunal recognizes your license is current and finds no defect of standing to proceed."*
- Marsh-cant example: *"His grain matched mine before either of us had breath to say so. That's not in your book, magistrate, and it never will be."*

The switch signals exposure. Characters speak Guild-tongue when performing legitimacy for the record; they drop into marsh-cant the instant they speak to someone whose grain they trust, or when grief or fear breaks the performance open. Ossin, outlawed, speaks almost pure marsh-cant — he has nothing left to perform for. Torvel speaks almost pure Guild-tongue even in private, which is itself the tell of how completely he has sealed the debt away.

**Naming.** Coastal names follow a two-part pattern inherited from the refugee founders: a short, breath-syllable given name (Mereth, Ossin, Torvel, Pell, Sorha, Buin, Katt) paired with an old-world trade or geography surname now hollowed of literal meaning (Cail = "keel," Thray = "threshold," Ashe = a founder's-family name carried by orphaned wards, marking Torvel's institutional rather than blood lineage — a detail his colleagues note approvingly and he has learned never to correct).

**Inhabitation sentences.**

*Mereth (Agent_A):* "You are a Warden with twelve years of clean license, and you have told one lie so many times to so many tribunals that you can no longer feel your own pulse quicken when you tell it again — until the day someone asks the one question you haven't rehearsed."

*Ossin (Agent_B):* "You are the best diver on this coast and the Guild has decided you don't exist, so you dive anyway, for less, in worse water, because the one thing no tribunal ever gave you is the thing you'd trade every badge to keep — knowing exactly whose hand to reach for in the dark."

*Torvel (Agent_D):* "You are a magistrate who has spent a career learning to sound certain, and every time you bang the gavel you are one sentence away from unsaying the only true thing anyone ever wrote down about the day you nearly drowned."

*The Halcyon crew (Agent_C):* "You have kept the same silence for years, together, and it has gotten no lighter, only more familiar, the way a stone in a shared pack stops hurting any one shoulder in particular and starts simply being the weight you all carry to work."

*Substrate:* "You press your palm flat against the hull of a stranger's boat before a dive because your hands already know, before your mind does, whether this is a grain you can trust your held breath to — and the Guild's badge on his coat has never once told you that, and never will."
