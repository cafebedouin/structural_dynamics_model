# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## Step 0 — Invariant Recovery

**The untranslatable real:** The world contains a real — the felt, granular, moment-by-moment texture of a person's lived days — that the system's own instruments cannot read, own, or reduce to a value. What the instruments record is only the categorical shadow the days leave behind, never the days themselves.

**Falsifier:** If the finished story contains a recoverable "true value" of a life that the system merely tallied incorrectly — a correctable ledger error — the invariant was lost.

**The missing floor:** Beneath the visible system there is a founding choice with no neutral ground under it — the world was sorted into its categories of person *before anyone was born into them*, and every subsequent reckoning, however precise, only ever refines a first cut nobody chose and nobody can see behind.

**Falsifier:** If the story frames the sorting as a bureaucratic mistake a fairer census could fix, the floor invariant was lost.

**Inherent instrument (from Stage 0, source-sighted, licensing the Scored-Snare exception):** The constraint is not the past itself but a perfect external instrument that displays the past — an objective burden, not a private memory. This licenses a naturalized *reading*, subject to the narration rule below: the number appears only when a positioned hand acts on it in-scene.

## Step 1 — Setting

**1911, the Salt Compact of Kesh Varana**, a desert protectorate on the inland trade spine between two empires, neither of which fully governs it. Three generations back, the Compact's founding cadastral survey — the *Varsat*, ordered by the first Resident-Administrator to settle grazing and salt-pan disputes — divided every family into one of four hereditary *ledger-castes* (*vansh*) before a single person alive today was born: Panhold, Waterkeep, Roadborn, and Ashborne. The survey was meant to last one season. It has lasted eighty years, because the instrument it produced — the **Kesh Ledger-Stones**, a wall of fired clay tablets kept in the Compact's Reckoning House — cannot be re-surveyed without dissolving the water and grazing rights currently distributed by caste, and no faction with power to order a re-survey has an interest in doing so.

Each Ledger-Stone is inscribed, once a year at the Accounting, with a single number for every living adult: the *tally* — a compressed figure derived from witnessed labor, debts settled, water drawn, oaths kept or broken, computed by the Reckoners according to a method no living person outside the Reckoners' guild fully understands, and which the guild itself insists is inherited procedure, not invention. The tally is not your life. It is what the stone can hold of your life. Everyone knows this and no one can act as though it isn't the only thing that follows you when you leave a room.

The Compact survives on **salt-pan labor** (Panhold), **irrigation-channel maintenance** (Waterkeep), **caravan portage** (Roadborn), and **the disposal and accounting of the dead** (Ashborne) — a caste whose work is literally to close out the tallies of the deceased, reconciling a life's final entry before the stone is sealed.

## Step 2 — The Affordance Test

*Could a better instrument measure the invariant's "true value" correctly?* No — and this must be designed in, not asserted. The tally is not a mismeasured biography waiting for a sharper Reckoner. It is categorically incapable of holding the texture of a day — the smell of the pan at dawn, the specific fear of a particular debt, the exact quality of a particular kindness — because it was never built to hold texture, only footprint. A perfect Reckoner produces a perfect footprint, and a footprint is still not the foot. This is why the setting is admissible: no reform of the instrument closes the gap, because the gap is definitional, not technical.

**Scored-Snare gate:** Stage 0's `inherent_instrument: yes` flag is honored. The tally is real and diegetic — but it appears on the page only when a character with positional access reads it aloud, disputes it, forges an entry, or feels its physical weight in a specific transaction (a debt called in, a marriage refused, a grazing right denied). Narration itself is never denominated in tallies. Extraction is *felt* — the closing of a door, the specific hunger of a specific season — never *tallied* in prose.

**Substrate — what holds the invariant:** The **desert itself**, specifically the **salt-pan floor beneath Kesh Varana**, which the Compact's founding myth holds was "read" by the first Resident-Administrator to produce the four castes, as though the ground itself contained the sorting. This is false in the sense that matters (a person made the choice) and true in the sense that matters more (no one now living can find the seam where the ground ends and the choice begins). The pan is queried by no instrument that returns anything but salt.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You kneel on ground that has outlasted every empire that named it, and the Reckoners can weigh what the ground gives up in a season, right down to the grain, and never once tell you what it was to kneel there.*

## Step 3 — Linguistic Strategy: **Direct Translation**

Source register: a Sanskritic-Persianate administrative Hindustani/Rajasthani trade-tongue, rendered in English but carrying its grammar and untranslatable concepts forward.

- ***Vansh*** — caste-ledger lineage; used instead of "caste" to keep the *record-based*, not blood-based, origin audible. "What vansh do you carry" ≠ "who are your people," it means "which stone were you first written into."
- ***Tally* / *lekha*** — the yearly number. Characters never say "my score." They say "what the stone says of me this year" — the phrasing keeps the gap between the number and the person grammatically present.
- ***Kacha* vs. *pakka*** — "raw/unfired" vs. "fired/settled" — used for anything provisional (a debt, a promise, a relationship) versus anything entered into the Ledger-Stones and therefore permanent. A *kacha* promise can still become something; a *pakka* entry cannot be unmade without breaking the stone.
- ***Varsat*** — the founding survey. Spoken of the way one speaks of a flood or a birth — an event, not a decision. No character in living memory says "the Varsat was wrong." They say "the Varsat was long."

**Naming:** Given names follow regional Rajasthani-Persianate convention (Kesar, Nandram, Bhairon, Zeenat) layered with caste-surnames that are literally the ledger-office names (Panhold-children take *-sut* patronymic suffixes tied to the Reckoning House register, e.g., "Devsut," "Amrasut") — surnames are not family names but *ledger designations*, renewable, which is itself a texture of the floor invariant: your name is administrative before it is personal.

## Step 4 — Power Differential Through Material Conditions

- **Physical space:** Ashborne quarters sit downwind of the pan, where the dead are processed; walls are unplastered clay. The Reckoners' Reckoning House has cool stone, shade, water piped in first.
- **Daily rhythm:** Panhold and Roadborn families plan by the season and the caravan calendar — will the next tally be enough to marry off a daughter, redeem a *kacha* debt before it goes *pakka*. The Reckoners plan across Administrator terms, decades, treaties with the empires on either side.
- **Possessions:** A Waterkeep family owns the channel-rights passed at marriage; an Ashborne family owns almost nothing transferable, because disposal work is considered ledger-adjacent, half-outside the caste economy entirely — the group closest to the dead is treated as barely inside the world of the living.
- **Deference:** Roadborn caravan-masters speak first in any mixed gathering by trade-custom; Ashborne speak last, or are addressed through a Reckoner intermediary, never directly, at the yearly Accounting.

## Step 5 — Constraint Reference Table

```
C1 (invariant) → THE PAN'S TEXTURE: the granular, unrepeatable feel of a lived
  day/season on the salt flats — sweat, grief, a particular mercy, a particular
  cruelty — which no tally, however precisely reckoned, can hold. ε=0.0: it extracts
  nothing from anyone because it is not a mechanism, it is the ground everything
  else stands on. No administrator, no enforcement. It is simply what is true
  of a day that a number cannot be.

C2 → THE YEARLY ACCOUNTING (accumulation): each season's witnessed labor, debts,
  oaths — gathered, weighed, and entered by the Reckoners into that year's
  provisional (kacha) record before it is fired into the Stone.
  Agent_A (the central POV, moderate-power position — see below) experiences it
  as: the slow, almost companionable ritual of being seen and recorded — useful,
  even comforting, because an accurate tally protects you from false accusation
  at trade — WHILE simultaneously the record strips out exactly the details that
  made the season survivable, keeping only what the Stone can use. Real
  coordination value (protection, trade legibility) genuinely coexists with real
  cost (the asymmetric loss of everything that made the labor a life rather
  than a figure).

C3 → THE ACCOUNTING'S CLOSE (convergence): the moment, once every few years, when
  the Reckoners fire the kacha tallies into the permanent Ledger-Stones, converging
  every family's scattered seasonal record into one unified, objective, and public
  number that will be read at every future negotiation, marriage, and dispute.
  Agent_A experiences it as: dread and relief at once — dread, because a private
  texture of struggle becomes a public, unchangeable figure; relief, because for
  the first time the whole scattered mess of a hard year resolves into something
  that can be *used*, defended, pointed to. This is the fusion the story's break
  depends on: an unguided, personal, meandering season of choices converges,
  without anyone intending it to, on a single number that is treated by everyone —
  even Agent_A — as the objective truth of that year.
```

## Step 6 — Characters as Positions

- **Kesar Devsut** (X1, moderate power / biographical time / identity-locked exit / universal scope) — a mid-tier **Waterkeep channel-warden**, thirty-four, neither poor enough to be crushed by the tally nor powerful enough to escape needing it. Identity-locked: her *vansh* is fixed at birth and cannot be renounced or purchased out of — the Compact recognizes no mechanism for caste-change, only marriage-linked caste-adjacency, which she has already used once. She is the character who tests the Accounting's meaning most directly, because she has enough standing to question it and not enough to ignore it.
- **Amrasut the Elder**, Reckoner-Second — institutional position, sees the Ledger-Stones as a Rope (functional, protective, necessary) with total sincerity, because from the Reckoning House, unrecorded trade is chaos and violence, and the tally is what lets a stranger be trusted at the pan's edge.
- **Bhairon Ashborne**, a closer-of-tallies for the dead — occupies the exit-locked, scope-universal position that would, under a powerless-index counterfactual, tip C2/C3 toward Snare: for him the yearly Accounting is not protection but a machine that fixes his family permanently at the bottom of a category none of them chose, three generations deep, with no *pakka* record ever letting them out.

**Inhabitation sentences:**

*Kesar:* "You are a channel-warden in a country where a number fired into clay outlives every season you actually lived, and the only hour you are ever fully yourself is the one before the Reckoner arrives to write you down."

*Amrasut:* "You are the hand that steadies the whole Compact's trust in itself, one tally at a time, and you have never once let yourself ask what the stone doesn't say."

*Bhairon:* "You close the dead's accounts for a living, and every year the stone confirms, in your own family's unchanging line, that you were born already spent."

**Substrate (repeated per protocol):** "You kneel on ground that has outlasted every empire that named it, and the Reckoners can weigh what the ground gives up in a season, right down to the grain, and never once tell you what it was to kneel there."
