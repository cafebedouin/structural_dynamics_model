REVIEW ASSESSMENT

STRENGTHS:
The sensory grounding is genuinely strong — the salt on the palms, the ash-grey ground into Bhairon's hands, the cool stone floor of the Reckoning House against bare feet after the heat outside. The Bhairon threshold scene is the emotional center of the piece and earns its stillness; the narrator's refusal to imagine his answer, and her honest naming of her own trespass in even picturing it, is the most alive writing here. The final withholding (not reporting the repaid debt) gives the piece a real ambiguity rather than a tidy close.

BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The story leans hard on telling us what the record *cannot* hold ("None of that went into the clay," "What the record did not ask," "he believed the correction *was* the truth restored") rather than trusting the scenes to demonstrate it — the insight gets stated at the end of nearly every section, so the piece reads as a thesis about record-keeping illustrated four times rather than one thing happening.

I'll note as a reader: the story does not present a hidden "true value" waiting to be recovered by a fairer instrument — Farrukh's forgiven debt, Kesar's own uncounted labor, and the final unrecorded repayment all stay outside the Stone permanently, by choice or by the nature of *kacha* speech never fired. Nothing here reads as a mismeasurement correctable by a better Reckoner. That's consistent with an invariant holding, but I can't confirm against a contract I don't have.

BREAK:
From the text alone, I expected a system of measurement to be either vindicated or exposed as corrupt — the classic ledger-story arc. Instead the story violates that by refusing verdict: the record is shown as simultaneously a real protection (the wall Kesar stands behind) and a real erasure (Bhairon's labor, the taste of midnight water), and no synthesis or correction is offered — she chooses silence at the end, an individual, non-generalizable act. Checking against the contract's target_prior (expectation that unguided choice produces chaotic/personal result, not convergence on universal truth): the story's ending is Kesar's own personal, private non-disclosure — it does *not* converge into a stated universal truth, it stays personal and irresolute. This looks like it undershoots the target_prior rather than fulfilling it: the contract wants the piece to arrive at something universal despite no predetermined goal, and this draft instead lands on private ambiguity. Possible mismatch — worth flagging for a strategy pass to confirm whether the ending needs to gesture toward a more legible convergence, or whether "no universal truth reached" is itself the intended violation read backwards.

READINESS:
Close but not there — strong scene-level craft undercut by a persistent explanatory reflex at section-ends that keeps restating the theme instead of letting the last scene alone carry it.
