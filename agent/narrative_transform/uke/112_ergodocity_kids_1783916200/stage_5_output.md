DISCOVERY REPORT

EMERGENT ELEMENTS:
The story enacts a system of total accounting — the Ledger-Stone bureaucracy — set against bodies that keep their own, unwritten ledgers. No enhanced Prolog reports were supplied, so the axes below are drawn directly from the prose.

The central constraint is inscription itself: what can be fired into clay and what cannot. Every character operates under the same rule (the Reckoners record witnessed, weighable events) but experiences it through a different vansh-position:

- Kesar (narrator, Waterkeep-by-marriage) experiences the record as a *wall to stand behind* — protective when Nazneen's cousin accuses her, thinning when it can't hold the midnight kneeling, the cold water, the goat's cry. Her gap is the one the whole piece turns on: the record's mercy and the record's theft are "the same mark, drying on the same clay."
- Amrasut the Elder experiences the same system with no gap at all — his unmarked hands are the tell. He believes correction *is* restoration ("as far as the stone can say it," delivered "without any irony"). He is the character who has fused instrument and reality; the story's clearest instance of an oracle who cannot see that his confidence is itself a position, not a vantage above position.
- Bhairon Ashborne experiences the system as a caste floor that inscription cannot move — three generations pinned to the bottom mark regardless of the meticulous, grief-smoothed labor he performs closing everyone else's accounts. His ash-stain (permanent, off-Ledger, on the body) is the exact structural mirror of Kesar's salt-crust (seasonal, off-Ledger, on the body) — same category of unrecorded evidence, opposite trajectories: hers fades and returns, his never leaves.
- Farrukh experiences the system as adjudication of memory itself — a spoken forgiveness, unfired, becomes "nothing at all" the moment the debtor who could vouch for it dies. The system doesn't just fail to record the past; it retroactively un-happens it.

The axis is most alive in the doorway of the Reckoning House: stone floor, piped cold water, Amrasut's unmarked hands versus Kesar's salt-white knuckles — the temperature and texture differential doing the work of class difference without naming it.

It is present but underdeveloped in the Ashborne thread. Kesar's near-question to Bhairon is the story's most charged unexploited moment (see Latent Potential) — the axis is *gestured at* through his glance at her hands rather than *enacted* through exchange.

LATENT POTENTIAL:
The strongest latent seam is the aborted conversation at Bhairon's threshold. Kesar wants to ask whether it "sits differently" to close others' accounts while his own line cannot move — a Frankl-shaped question (what meaning is available inside an unmovable constraint) that the story raises and then, by its own custom-logic, refuses to let be spoken. This refusal is thematically justified (custom forbids direct address) but it also means the story's most promising cross-position comparison — narrator whose position moved sideways once vs. a man whose position has never moved in three generations — never actually occurs on the page. We get her looking at his hands and him looking at hers; we don't get one word between them. That silence could be the story's climax if developed, or it could remain, deliberately, an unbreachable gap — the discovery report flags it as underdeveloped rather than resolved.

The unfired debt in the final section (Nazneen's cousin's private repayment) gestures at a genuine Frankl-territory answer: meaning that exists *because* it will never be weighed, not despite it. But the story states this conclusion fairly directly in the closing paragraph rather than letting the scene's own specifics (what was said, what wasn't, the quality of the silence between the two of them at dusk) carry the weight the midnight-water and goat-cry images carry earlier. It's the one place where the story tells its thesis instead of enacting it.

The larger unexploited context: we never learn what happens to a family whose Stone-line sits at "the bottom mark" for generations, beyond Bhairon's individual case — the caste mechanics of vansh are implied (Roadborn speaks first, Ashborne addressed last, only through intermediary) but never dramatized as a system with its own history, texture, casualties beyond one man's stained hands.

STRONGEST WRITING:
- The opening: "The salt came up through my palms before I ever saw it... like something had been written on them and then erased, leaving only the ghost of ink." This image seeds the entire story's argument in the first paragraph and earns every later echo.
- "Which meant I was already thinking about the record, wasn't I. Even in the dark hour. Even before anyone had come to ask." — the self-catching turn, the narrator implicating her own consciousness in the system before any external agent appears.
- The Amrasut correction scene: "clay does not forgive that way, but adding, a small notch that any Reckoner reading it after would understand as *disputed, resolved thus*." Precise, physically grounded, and it does the philosophical work (correction vs. erasure) without announcing it.
- "the way a shadow is a true record of your shape and no record at all of your weight" — the single best compression of the story's whole argument.
- Bhairon's hands folding the dead man's cloth: "grief has been worn smooth out of the doing of it, the way a step-stone wears smooth under repeated feet" — this and the ash-stain paragraph are the richest physical writing in the piece, dense with implication about labor, caste, and time that the story doesn't over-explain.
- The final gate scene: "the water running cold under my hands, the salt already starting its faint white climb along my knuckles for another season" — the cyclical image returning with the private, unrecorded debt folded into it.

ANCHOR CHECK:
The "good figure" read aloud by Amrasut at the Accounting's close functions as a numeric moment, but the story pointedly withholds the actual number ("The number went through me... felt it, actually felt it") — it never gives us the arithmetic itself, only the bodily reaction to it. This is a deliberate evasion of the counting-anchor, not an instance of it. No descending scores, countdowns, or tallies-as-inevitability appear anywhere in the piece. None found.

INVARIANT PROBE:
Yes — there is a real here the instruments cannot read, and the story is explicit and consistent about this, which is its central achievement. The midnight kneeling at the gate, the taste of diverted water, the widow's goat crying at dawn, Bhairon's grief-smoothed hands, the unwitnessed grain-debt repaid at dusk — these are named repeatedly as categorically unrecoverable, not merely unrecorded-so-far. The story insists clay "does not forgive that way," that correction is addition not erasure, that even the Ledger-Stones (which can theoretically break) are less permanent than the ash ground into Bhairon's skin. Crucially, the story does *not* offer a "better instrument" or "fairer authority" that could someday read these things — no appeal to a future audit, no suggestion that a wiser Reckoner would capture what Amrasut misses. The final line makes the invariant explicit: what the ground "consents to give up... is never, no matter how carefully anyone weighs it, the thing itself." This is the report's most important finding: the unrecoverable is asserted as a structural feature of the world, not a gap awaiting a better measurer.

SCOPE ASSESSMENT:
Current word count: 3,034 (computed). This sits at short-story length, and the piece is well-scaled to that length — it does not read as compressed material struggling to fit, nor as a novella's premise left undercooked. The five-section structure (dawn ritual / Reckoner's visit / Reckoning House / Ashborne threshold / Accounting's close / private coda) is complete and proportionate.

Expansion would require either (a) dramatizing the Kesar–Bhairon exchange directly, which would need new scene-work and risks resolving a silence the story currently uses productively, or (b) widening to a second Accounting cycle to show the salt-crust/ash-stain contrast compounding over more than one season — this could deepen the invariant probe's claim about permanence vs. cyclicality but would cost the tight single-season frame.

Compression would sacrifice the Amrasut/Reckoning House section, which is the piece's clearest articulation of the perspectival gap (confident instrument-user vs. instrument-marked subject) — cutting it would leave the Bhairon and closing sections without their conceptual counterweight.
