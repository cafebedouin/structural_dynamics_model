## ANALYST COMMENTARY

This document is **exceptionally well-constructed** from an evidence and constraint analysis perspective. The author clearly understands Mountains (instrument-object identity is genuine), distinguishes them from Ropes (coordination mechanisms that can be reformed), and identifies potential Nooses (extraction masquerading as necessity).

**However, the recommendations don't fully respect the power dynamics the analysis reveals.** This is the classic pattern: brilliant diagnosis, fantasy prescription.

**The workplace recommendation (#2) is the clearest failure.** The document correctly identifies that current self-evaluation emphasis serves management interests (reduces assessment burden, creates plausible deniability). But then recommends federal mandate requiring multi-source assessment—which directly conflicts with those management interests. Employers have effective veto through lobbying. No compensation mechanism is viable (you can't buy off the loss of plausible deniability). The recommendation is **BLOCKED** but presented as viable.

**The therapeutic recommendation (#3) is borderline.** It could work with insurance company pressure (financial incentive overrides professional resistance), making it **ASPIRATIONAL** rather than blocked. But the document doesn't explicitly frame it as contingent on that trigger, creating false impression of near-term viability.

**The educational (#1) and research (#4) recommendations are sound.** No significant veto points, clear authority, aligned incentives. These could happen in 2-5 years with normal institutional friction.

**The deeper issue:** This document demonstrates what happens when constraint analysis is rigorous but power analysis is incomplete. The author sees the Mountains clearly (instrument-object identity), maps the Ropes accurately (coordination mechanisms), suspects the Nooses (extraction dynamics)—but doesn't fully account for **who has veto power and what they lose**.

**Recommended revision strategy:**
1. Keep educational and research recommendations as-is (viable)
2. Reframe workplace recommendation as voluntary certification (market mechanism respects power constraints)
3. Add explicit contingency framing to therapeutic recommendation (viable after trigger event)
4. Strengthen "Unresolved Questions" with specific data requests and accountability mechanisms

**After revision, this document would be audit-ready.** The underlying analysis is sound. The recommendations just need to respect the power dynamics the analysis reveals.
