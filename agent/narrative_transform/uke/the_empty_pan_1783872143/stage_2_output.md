# STAGE 2: NATURALIZATION OUTPUT

## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant (commitment + falsifier):**
There exists a state — the body's own knowledge of itself — that cannot be owned, measured, or made legible to any external instrument without ceasing to be what it is. The instant proprioception becomes a reading, it becomes something else: a number that can be disputed, traded, or used against you. **Falsifier:** If the finished story contains a "true" proprioceptive measurement that the system merely got wrong — a correct balance score the authorities could have read accurately — the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**The substrate that holds it:**
Inner ear equilibrium. The body's sense of where it stands in space. You know when you are falling before any instrument registers tilt. This knowledge is pre-linguistic, pre-numeric, owned by no one because it cannot be extracted from the body that holds it. It exists only in the present continuous of sensing.

**The substrate inhabitation sentence:**
You stand on a platform above the sorting gulf, and your body knows — in the meat of your inner ear, in the weight distributed across the soles of your feet — whether you are stable, but the moment the Equilibrium Registry writes that knowledge into a Posture Index, it becomes a score that decides whether you fall or stand, and the score is never what your body knew.

**The missing-floor invariant + falsifier:**
Beneath the Posture Index there is no neutral measurement. The zero-point — "correct standing posture" — was set forty years ago by the Margraviate's first Equilibrium Officiant, who calibrated the sensors to the standing posture of healthy soldiers in the Margrave's own regiment. Bodies that grew up carrying water on their heads, or bent over rice paddies, or holding children on their hips, register as "deviant" not because they are unstable but because they are measured against a standard that was never theirs. **Falsifier:** If the story frames the Posture Index as a neutral instrument that merely needs better calibration, the floor invariant was lost.

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

### Setting

**2091 CE, Plateau District, Margraviate of Tethys**

The Margraviate occupies a high plateau ringed by mountains, where the only traversable routes between settlements require crossing suspension bridges over the Sorting Gulf — a rift two hundred meters deep, carved by glacial melt over ten thousand years. The bridges are narrow: single-file, swaying, built from reclaimed carbon-lattice and synthetic hemp. They are the only way to reach the Central Registry, the medical depot, the grain cooperative, the secondary school.

Forty years ago, the Margraviate instituted the Posture Index as a public health measure. All residents over the age of twelve must pass an annual Equilibrium Assessment to receive a bridge-crossing permit. The assessment measures "standing stability" through pressure sensors embedded in platform floors at Registry stations. A passing score (PI ≥ 6.5) grants a year's access. A failing score results in bridge restriction: you may not cross unaccompanied. If you fail three consecutive years, you are relocated to Cliffside, the settlement at the bottom of the gulf, where all services are rudimentary and all commerce flows through brokers who live above.

The system was designed to prevent bridge accidents. In its first decade, fall deaths dropped by sixty percent. The Margraviate celebrated this as evidence-based governance. But the Posture Index was calibrated to a standard — "correct standing posture" — derived from young soldiers in the Margrave's regiment, who had grown up on the plateau, who had never carried weight asymmetrically, whose bodies had been drilled into uniformity.

Bodies shaped by other labor — by fieldwork, by childcare, by the hauling trades — register as "unstable" even when their bearers cross bridges daily without incident. The system does not measure falling risk. It measures deviation from a standard that was someone's posture, once, and became everyone's measure.

Three generations have lived under the Posture Index. It is taught in schools as a fact of physiology. "Good posture" is a virtue. "Instability" is a personal failing, a thing you fix through exercises the Equilibrium Officiant prescribes — exercises that, for many, do not change the score, because the score was never measuring what it claimed to measure.

### Constraint Naturalizations

**C₁ → Inner Ear Equilibrium (the unownable real)**

Your body knows when it is falling. This knowledge lives in the semicircular canals of your inner ear, in the weight of otoliths shifting across hair cells, in the proprioceptive map your brain builds from a thousand signals every second. It is immediate, pre-reflective, and cannot be transferred. You cannot give this knowledge to another person. You cannot write it down. The instant you try to make it legible — to translate the body's knowing into a number, a graph, a certified reading — it becomes something else. It becomes a representation that can be disputed, adjusted, or used as evidence against you.

The Equilibrium Registry does not measure your body's knowledge. It measures how your body's pressure distribution compares to a stored template. The template is not you. The template is not anyone, anymore — it is a composite average of soldiers who stood on the first platforms forty years ago, their postures normalized and encoded. When you stand for assessment, the system does not ask, "Is this person stable?" It asks, "Does this person's standing pattern match the template?"

The divergence is the score. The score is what crosses the bridge, or doesn't.

**Locals call it:** *Grounding* (the body's sense of standing) vs. *the Reading* (what the platform records). "I know my grounding" means: my body is stable, I trust my feet. "The Reading is off" means: the number does not match what I know, but the number is what decides.

**C₁ experiences (invariant — same for all, because it is physics):**

- **X₁ (powerless, trapped):** You press your feet into the platform and feel your weight settle — left heel, right ball, the small adjustments your ankles make without thought. You know you are stable. You have crossed the bridge a thousand times, carrying twice your weight in rice sacks, and never stumbled. But the Reading blinks red: PI 5.8. The Officiant says, "Unstable. Remediation required." You say, "I know my grounding." The Officiant says, "The platform does not lie."

- **X₃ (analytical, observer):** You are a graduate researcher from the Margraviate's Kinesiological Institute, here to study gait patterns in high-altitude populations. You stand on the platform as part of your onboarding. The Reading comes back PI 7.2 — well above threshold. You are surprised, because you have a slight scoliosis and your left leg is shorter than your right. You ask the Officiant, "What is the platform measuring?" The Officiant says, "Standing stability." You ask, "Against what reference?" The Officiant pauses. "Against correct posture." You ask, "Whose?" The Officiant does not answer.

**Brief example in use:**

> Mirae stands barefoot on the cold platform, her daughter's weight still phantom-pressed into her left hip where she carried her all morning. The Officiant watches the screen. Mirae feels her feet: stable, grounded, the micro-sway her body learned to correct when she was twelve and her mother taught her to carry water on her head. She knows she will not fall. The screen blinks. PI 6.1. The Officiant frowns. "Borderline. You're favoring your left side. Exercises recommended." Mirae says, "I carry my daughter on my left hip. I've done it for three years. I don't fall." The Officiant says, "The platform measures standing posture, not carrying posture. If you want to pass next year, correct the deviation."

**C₂ → The Posture Index (the extractive standard)**

A score from 0 to 10, generated by pressure sensors in the platform floor, compared against a reference template stored in the Equilibrium Registry's core database. The score determines bridge-crossing access. PI ≥ 6.5: unrestricted. PI 5.0–6.4: accompanied crossings only. PI < 5.0: Cliffside relocation after three consecutive fails.

The system was built to prevent accidents. It does prevent accidents — fall deaths are down sixty percent from the pre-Index era. But it also does something else. It extracts mobility from bodies that do not match the template. And the template was never neutral.

The first Equilibrium Officiant, appointed in 2051, calibrated the sensors using soldiers from the Margrave's own regiment: men aged 18–25, who had trained in formation drill, whose bodies had been shaped by the same exercises, the same nutrition, the same daily rhythms. Their average standing posture became the reference. The system has been recalibrated twice since then — in 2063 and 2078 — but both recalibrations used data from residents who had *already passed* the assessment. The template drifts toward the template. Bodies that fail do not enter the reference set.

Who fails? Fieldworkers whose spines curve from decades of bent-over labor. Haulers whose shoulders sit asymmetrically from carrying uneven loads. Mothers whose hips tilt from years of holding children. The elderly, whose posture compresses with age. Anyone whose body was shaped by work the soldiers never did.

The Posture Index is enforced by bridge access restriction and by social pressure. "Instability" is treated as a personal failing, a lack of discipline, a refusal to do the exercises. Families pressure failing members to "fix their posture" so they don't lose access. Employers prefer workers with high PIs — it signals reliability, self-care, respectability. The system has its own momentum now. It is not questioned, because it is framed as objective measurement, and because it genuinely does reduce bridge accidents.

But the reduction comes at a cost the system does not count: the mobility and economic access of everyone whose body does not match a forty-year-old template built from soldiers' postures.

**Locals call it:** *The Index* or *the PI*. "What's your Index?" is a common question, like asking someone's age. A high PI is a point of pride. A failing PI is a source of shame.

**Constraint reference table:**

- **X₄ (powerless, trapped) experiences it as:** A trap. You fail the assessment year after year. Your body is stable — you cross the practice bridge behind your house every day, carrying loads, never stumbling — but the platform reads you as deviant. You cannot afford the remediation exercises (they cost a week's wages for a single session with a certified trainer). You cannot relocate to Cliffside (your family is here, your work is here). You are trapped between a body that works and a system that says it doesn't.

- **X₂ (moderate, constrained) experiences it as:** A hybrid. You are an Equilibrium Officiant, one of fifteen in the Margraviate. You administer the assessments. You see the same people fail year after year — fieldworkers, haulers, mothers — and you see that their bodies are not unstable, only different. But you also see the accident reports from the pre-Index era: twenty, thirty fall deaths a year, mostly among the elderly and the drunk. The Index works. It saves lives. You believe in it. But you also see the way it sorts people, and you are not sure the sorting is just. You do the exercises yourself. Your PI is 7.8. You pass every year. You tell yourself the system is fair, because it is the same platform for everyone. But you know the platform was built for bodies like yours.

- **X₅ (institutional, arbitrage) experiences it as:** A tool. You are the Margrave's Director of Public Health, appointed to oversee the Equilibrium Registry. The Posture Index is your signature achievement. Bridge accidents are down. Insurance costs are down. The system is popular among plateau residents, who see Cliffside relocation as a fair consequence of negligence. You do not cross bridges yourself — your office is accessible by ground-level passage. You have never stood on the platform. When critics argue that the Index extracts mobility from the poor, you point to the accident data. When they argue that the reference template is biased, you point to the recalibrations. You are proud of the system. It is evidence-based governance. It works.

**Plot shift (in the world's language):**

The system was designed as a safety measure and became a sorting mechanism. The shift happened gradually, over forty years, as the template drifted and the social meaning of the score changed. Now the Index is not only about bridge safety. It is about respectability, employability, worthiness. A failing PI marks you as deficient. The shift is invisible to most residents, because they have lived inside it their whole lives. It feels like nature.

**C₃ → The Doctrine of Personal Calibration (justified compliance)**

The official narrative: your Posture Index is a measure of your body's stability, and stability is something you can improve through effort. If you fail the assessment, it is because you have not done the work. The Equilibrium Registry provides free educational materials on "posture hygiene." The schools teach children exercises to "build good standing habits." The Officiant prescribes remediation plans: ten minutes of wall-standing daily, weight distribution drills, core strengthening.

The doctrine is: your body is your responsibility. If your PI is low, you fix it. If you don't fix it, you accept the consequences.

This narrative serves two functions. First, it provides genuine value: some people do improve their scores through exercise, especially younger residents whose bodies are still plastic. The exercises work for them. Second, it masks the extraction: it frames bridge restriction as a consequence of personal failure, not systemic bias. If you fail, it is because you did not try hard enough.

The doctrine is enforced socially. Families pressure failing members to do the exercises. Neighbors gossip about people who "let themselves go." Failing three times and getting relocated to Cliffside is seen as a moral failure, a refusal to take responsibility for your own body.

But the doctrine is a lie for many people. Fieldworkers in their fifties whose spines have curved from thirty years of labor cannot "correct" their posture through ten minutes of wall-standing. Mothers whose hips have permanently tilted from carrying children cannot drill their way back to the template. The exercises do not work for them, because the template was never built for bodies shaped by their labor.

The doctrine survives because it is partly true. Enough people improve their scores that the narrative holds. The people for whom it does not work are dismissed as non-compliant, lazy, or physiologically deficient.

**Locals call it:** *Calibration* or *doing the work*. "Have you been doing your Calibration?" is a common question directed at people with low PIs, carrying the implication that failure is a choice.

**Constraint reference table:**

- **X₂ (moderate, constrained) experiences it as:** A hybrid. You are an Officiant. You teach the Calibration doctrine because you have seen it work. You have seen young people raise their PIs from 6.0 to 7.5 through disciplined exercise. You believe in personal responsibility. But you also see the people for whom it does not work — the elderly woman who does the drills every day and still scores 5.9, the hauler whose shoulders will never symmetrize because his work requires asymmetric load-bearing. You tell them to keep trying. You do not know what else to say. The doctrine is your job. It is also your belief. You experience it as both true and insufficient.

- **X₄ (powerless, trapped) experiences it as:** A lie that blames you for your own exclusion. You do the exercises. You have done them for three years. Your PI has not changed. The Officiant says you are not doing them correctly. You say you are doing exactly what the manual shows. The Officiant says you must not be doing them consistently. You say you do them every morning. The Officiant says your body is not responding, and that means you need to try harder. You stop arguing. You know the exercises do not work for you. You know the system is not measuring what it claims to measure. But you cannot say this, because saying it marks you as a complainer, someone who makes excuses. The doctrine makes your exclusion your fault.

### Power Differential Through Material Conditions

**X₄ (powerless, trapped):**
Mirae lives in a two-room stone house on the plateau's western edge, fifteen minutes' walk from the nearest bridge. The house is cold in winter — the Margraviate's heating credits are allocated by household income, and Mirae's fieldwork wages are low. She wakes at dawn, eats rice and pickled vegetables, walks to the communal field where she works bent over rows of quinoa and amaranth. Her back aches by midday. Her daughter, three years old, is cared for by Mirae's mother, who lives in the next house over. Mirae's mother also fails the Index (PI 5.4, chronic fail). Mirae crosses the bridge once a month to visit the medical depot for her daughter's vaccinations. She must be accompanied by someone with a passing PI. She asks her neighbor, a young man who works at the grain cooperative. He is kind about it, but she feels the weight of the asking every time.

**X₂ (moderate, constrained):**
Davin is an Equilibrium Officiant. He lives in a four-room apartment near the Central Registry, provided by the Margraviate as part of his employment. The apartment is warm. He has a private kitchen, a study, a bedroom for his son. He earns enough to buy meat twice a week. His work is indoors, seated or standing at the assessment platform. He administers fifteen assessments a day, records the scores, prescribes remediation plans. He believes in the work. He sees the accident reports. He knows the Index saves lives. But he also sees Mirae, year after year, scoring 6.1, 5.9, 6.0 — always borderline, always struggling. He sees her daughter in the waiting area, playing with a wooden toy. He wants to tell Mirae that the system is not measuring what it claims, but he does not have the language for it, and he is not sure it would help. He prescribes exercises. He believes they might work. He needs to believe they might work.

**X₅ (institutional, arbitrage):**
Director Sokol works in the Margraviate's administrative tower, a climate-controlled building with ground-level access — no bridges required. She has never stood on an assessment platform. She reviews aggregate data: accident rates, PI distributions, remediation compliance. The data is good. Accidents are down. The system works. She receives complaints — letters from residents who argue the Index is biased, petitions from Cliffside families who want reassessment — but the complaints are anecdotal, and the data is statistical. She trusts the data. When the Margrave asks if the system should be recalibrated, she says no: recalibration would destabilize the reference template, and the current template is evidence-based. She is proud of the Index. It is her legacy. She does not see Mirae. She does not see Davin's hesitation. She sees numbers, and the numbers justify the system.

### Linguistic Strategy: Code-Switching (Plateau Creole + Institutional Margravian)

**Base languages:**
- **Plateau Creole:** A hybrid of Tibetan, Nepali, and Mandarin, developed over three centuries of trade and migration across the plateau. Verb-final syntax, agglutinative morphology, extensive honorific system based on age and kinship rather than institutional rank. Used for intimacy, family, local commerce, private complaint.
- **Institutional Margravian:** A standardized register of Mandarin, used for all official interactions with the Registry, schools, medical depot, grain cooperative. Analytic syntax, formal pronouns, technical vocabulary for the Posture Index and Calibration doctrine.

**Mixing rationale:**
Code-switching signals who you are speaking to and whether you are performing. Plateau Creole is the language of the body, the home, the field. Institutional Margravian is the language of the platform, the score, the official record. When Mirae speaks to the Officiant, she switches to Margravian, because the Officiant represents the system. When she speaks to her daughter, she uses Creole, because her daughter is hers and the system is not.

**Core vocabulary with etymologies:**

- *Grounding* (Creole: *thampa*, from Tibetan *gdengs* "standing firm"): The body's felt sense of stability. Used only in private speech. Never appears in official documents.
- *The Reading* (Margravian: *dúshù*, from Mandarin "reading/measurement"): The score the platform generates. Used in all official contexts.
- *Calibration* (Margravian: *jiàozhǔn*, from Mandarin "to calibrate/correct"): The exercises and doctrine of personal responsibility. Always in Margravian, because it is a system term.
- *Cliffside* (Creole: *lung-ma*, "below-place"): The settlement at the bottom of the gulf. In Creole it carries connotations of exile, poverty, abandonment. In Margravian (*Xiágǔ Qū*, "Lower Valley District") it is neutral administrative terminology.

**Grammatical features carried into English:**

1. **Evidentiality marking (Creole):** Speakers mark whether they know something from direct experience (*-tsi*) or from report (*-yin*). "I know my grounding" would be *nga thampa shes-tsi* (I-grounding-know-EXPERIENCED). "The Officiant says I'm unstable" would be *jangchup-la nga mi-thampa yin-yin* (officiant-ERG I not-grounded say-REPORTED). This distinction is lost in Margravian, which does not mark evidentiality.

2. **Honorific inversion (Creole):** Younger speakers use respectful forms for elders, but elders use intimate forms for the young. Mirae addresses her mother with the honorific *ama-la* (mother-HON), but her mother addresses her as *bu-nga* (daughter-INTIMATE). This system breaks down in Institutional Margravian, which uses formal pronouns (*nín*, *tā*) based on institutional rank, not age or kinship.

3. **Verb-final syntax preserved in emotional speech:** When Mirae is upset, her Margravian syntax drifts toward Creole verb-final order. "I know I am stable" in standard Margravian: *wǒ zhīdào wǒ wěndìng* (I know I stable). In Mirae's stressed code-switching: *wǒ wěndìng zhīdào* (I stable know) — grammatically incorrect Margravian, but phonologically closer to Creole *nga thampa shes*.

**Example in use:**

> Mirae stands on the platform. The Officiant, Davin, watches the screen. He speaks Margravian: "Nín de dúshù shì liù diǎn yī. Biānyuán. Nín xūyào jiàozhǔn." (Your Reading is six point one. Borderline. You need Calibration.)
>
> Mirae responds in Margravian, but her syntax slips: "Wǒ thampa shes-tsi." (I grounding know-EXPERIENCED — mixing Creole evidential into Margravian frame.) She catches herself, reverts to full Margravian: "Wǒ zhīdào wǒ wěndìng." (I know I am stable.)
>
> Davin does not acknowledge the slip. He continues in Margravian: "Píngtái bù huì sāhuǎng." (The platform does not lie.)
>
> Mirae switches fully to Creole, because she is no longer performing for the system — she is speaking to Davin as a person: "Nga mi-lungma 'gro-gi yin-tsi. Nga'i bu-nga 'di-na yod." (I not-Cliffside go-will EXPERIENCED. My daughter here is.) *I will not go to Cliffside. My daughter is here.*
>
> Davin does not respond in Creole. He cannot. He is the Officiant. He says, in Margravian: "Nín kěyǐ zài shìyī cì." (You can try again next year.)

## SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

### RESOLVED

**Ω_E01: What caused the sixty percent reduction in bridge accidents after the Posture Index was implemented?**
- **Resolution:** Combination of (a) genuine screening effect (the Index does identify some people at high fall risk, particularly the elderly and those with neurological conditions affecting balance), (b) behavioral change (people are more cautious on bridges when they know they are being monitored), and (c) infrastructure improvement (bridge maintenance was increased at the same time the Index was implemented, but this is not widely known).
- **Impact:** The Index works, but not only for the reasons the system claims. This justifies X₅'s faith in the data while allowing X₃ to discover that the effect is overdetermined.

**Ω_E02: How is the Posture Index enforced for people who fail?**
- **Resolution:** Bridge access is controlled by turnstiles at each bridge entrance. Residents swipe an ID chip (embedded in a wristband, issued at birth) to unlock the turnstile. If your PI is below threshold, the turnstile displays "ACCOMPANIED ACCESS ONLY" and will not unlock unless a second person with passing PI swipes within thirty seconds. If you fail three consecutive years, your chip is flagged for Cliffside relocation, and the Margraviate's Social Services office contacts you to arrange transfer. Relocation is not legally mandatory, but bridge access is fully revoked, which makes plateau residence functionally impossible.
- **Impact:** Enforcement is automated and impersonal, which reinforces the system's claim to objectivity. No human decides whether you cross; the platform and the turnstile decide.

**Ω_E03: What is Cliffside like?**
- **Resolution:** A settlement of ~3,000 people at the bottom of the Sorting Gulf. Accessible by a single steep road (two hours' walk from the plateau) or by cargo lift (expensive, infrequent). Services are minimal: one medical clinic (no surgical capacity), one school (primary only), no grain cooperative (residents buy from brokers who transport from the plateau at markup). Most Cliffside residents work in salvage, recycling, or low-wage service jobs for plateau businesses that outsource labor. Cliffside has its own culture — less formal, more communal, skeptical of the Index — but it is economically dependent on the plateau. Relocation is survivable but represents a sharp drop in material conditions and social status.
- **Impact:** Cliffside is not a death sentence, but it is a punishment. This allows the system to claim it is humane (no one is killed or imprisoned) while still extracting mobility as a consequence of failing the Index.

**Ω_C01: Is the Posture Index inherently instrument-mediated, or could the constraint exist without the platform?**
- **Resolution:** Inherently instrument-mediated. The constraint is *bridge access restriction based on a certified measurement of standing posture*. Without the platform and the score, there is no constraint — only the natural risk of falling, which is C₁ (the Mountain). The platform is the mechanism of extraction. This justifies the Stage 0 flag `inherent_instrument: yes`.
- **Impact:** Numbers (PI scores) may appear diegetically, but only when a character with positional access acts on them in-scene (Davin reads the score aloud, Mirae sees it on the screen, Kiran analyzes the distribution). Narration is never denominated in scores. Mirae's experience of the platform is felt and enforced, never tallied.

**Ω_C02: What is the Margraviate's political structure?**
- **Resolution:** A technocratic autocracy. The Margrave (a hereditary title, currently held by Margrave Tenzin IV) appoints all directors and officials. There is no elected legislature. Policy is made by appointed experts (like Director Sokol) and justified through data. The Margraviate is small (~50,000 plateau residents, ~3,000 Cliffside residents) and geographically isolated, which makes centralized control feasible. The system is popular among plateau residents, who see it as competent and evidence-based. Dissent exists but is marginal.
- **Impact:** X₅'s power is real but derived. She serves at the Margrave's pleasure. This makes institutional reform (TR₃) possible but difficult — it requires convincing the Margrave, not winning an election.

**Ω_P01: What is the Margraviate's relationship to the outside world?**
- **Resolution:** The Margraviate is part of a larger federated region (the Plateau Confederacy), but it is semi-autonomous. It trades quinoa, amaranth, and rare-earth minerals with neighboring states. It imports medical supplies, electronics, and fossil fuels. The Posture Index is unique to the Margraviate — other Confederacy states do not use it, which creates a natural comparison for X₃'s research. The Margraviate is proud of its "evidence-based governance" and sees itself as more advanced than its neighbors.
- **Impact:** Alternative systems exist (X₃ can reference them), but the Margraviate is not forced to change by external pressure. Reform must come from within.

**Ω_P02: What is the tone of the setting — dystopian, mundane, or something else?**
- **Resolution:** Mundane with creeping wrongness. The Margraviate is not a dystopia. Most people live ordinary lives. The plateau is beautiful. The bridges are functional. The system works for most residents most of the time. But there is a quiet, pervasive injustice that people have learned not to see. The tone is *normalized extraction* — the horror is not that the system is cruel, but that it is reasonable, and that reasonableness is the mechanism of the harm.
- **Impact:** The story does not signal "this is a dystopia" through aesthetic markers (surveillance, propaganda, overt violence). It signals "this is a place where people live" and lets the wrongness emerge from the structure.

### UNRESOLVED

**Ω_E04: What happens to people who refuse Cliffside relocation after three fails?**
- **Recommendation:** They lose all bridge access and become plateau residents without mobility. They must rely entirely on others for anything requiring bridge crossing (medical care, school, commerce). This is rare, because it is functionally unlivable. Most people relocate. A few are supported by family networks. A very few become hermits on the plateau's edge, living off subsistence farming and barter.
- **Awaiting decision:** Does the story need to show this, or is it sufficient to leave it as background? If Mirae is approaching her third fail, this becomes urgent. If not, it can remain implicit.

**Ω_P03: Does Kiran (X₃) publish the research, or keep it private?**
- **Recommendation:** This is a narrative choice that determines the attractor. If Kiran publishes, the story moves toward Negotiated Equilibrium (knowledge becomes public, pressure for reform builds). If Kiran keeps it private (or publishes in an obscure journal that the Margraviate ignores), the story stays in Seeded Possibility (knowledge exists but does not propagate). The Stage 1 spec selected Seeded Possibility, which suggests Kiran's research should be completed but not widely disseminated — preserved as an underground artifact, not a catalyst for immediate reform.
- **Awaiting decision:** Confirm attractor selection before finalizing Kiran's arc.
