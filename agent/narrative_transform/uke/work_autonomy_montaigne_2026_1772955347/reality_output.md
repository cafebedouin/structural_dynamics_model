[CONSTRAINT-ANALYSIS #2]

Recommendation: "Mandate annual 'decision-frequency audits' for employers with 50+ employees, measuring how often workers make consequential decisions about work direction."

CONSTRAINT-MAP:
- Mountain:
  * Measurement problem: "Consequential decisions about work direction" is subjective and gameable
  * Enforcement: Requires inspectors, penalties, appeals process (resource-intensive)
- Rope:
  * OSHA precedent for workplace regulation (functional coordination)
  * Department of Labor enforcement infrastructure (exists but underfunded)
- Noose:
  * Current employment structure concentrates decision authority (documented: 2.8/5.0 employee autonomy vs. 4.2/5.0 self-employed)
  * Routine-as-control mechanism (Inference 2, moderate confidence)
- Zombie: None detected

FEASIBILITY-ASSESSMENT:
Classification: BLOCKED (current political climate) / ASPIRATIONAL (post-labor-movement-revival)
Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH
Reasoning:
This is a novel workplace regulation with no precedent. It faces:

1. **Definitional Quicksand**: What counts as "consequential decision about work direction"? 
   - Choosing which task to do first? (Trivial)
   - Choosing which project to prioritize? (Managerial)
   - Choosing company strategy? (Executive)
   - Every employer will game toward trivial definition

2. **Business Opposition**: Chamber of Commerce, NFIB, and industry groups will oppose as "job-killing regulation." They have veto power via congressional allies.

3. **Enforcement Impossibility**: DOL is already underfunded for existing mandates (wage theft, safety violations). Adding subjective "decision frequency" audits requires:
   - Training thousands of inspectors in new methodology
   - Developing audit standards (3-5 years)
   - Legal framework for penalties (subject to court challenges)
   - Appeals process (clogs system for years)

4. **Perverse Incentives**: Employers respond by:
   - Documenting trivial decisions ("employee chose which pen to use")
   - Restructuring to fall below 50-employee threshold
   - Outsourcing to avoid employee classification
   - Moving operations to states with lax enforcement

HOWEVER: This could become VIABLE after major labor movement revival or workplace catastrophe (mass burnout crisis, etc.). The mechanism is sound—making invisible subordination visible—but requires political conditions that don't exist.

IMPLEMENTATION-MAP:
Required Preconditions:
  - Political: Pro-labor congressional majority + sympathetic president (possible 2028-2032 window)
  - Economic: $5-10B annually for DOL enforcement expansion
  - Technical: Audit methodology development (3-5 years)
  - Social: Cultural shift recognizing "routine-as-hazard" (requires advocacy campaign)
  - Temporal: 5-7 years from political opening to full implementation

Energy Cost:
  - Person-hours: ~200,000 (regulation drafting, methodology development, inspector training)
  - Political capital: HIGH—requires spending labor movement capital on untested regulation
  - Opportunity cost: Same energy could strengthen existing labor protections (overtime, misclassification)

Timeline:
  - Optimistic: 5-7 years (requires pro-labor trifecta + no court challenges)
  - Realistic: 10-15 years (includes court battles, methodology refinement)
  - Catastrophe-contingent: 2-3 years IF workplace burnout crisis creates urgency
    * Without crisis: Blocked by business lobby
    * With crisis: Rapid implementation as emergency measure

Veto Points:
  1. Business Lobby (Chamber of Commerce, NFIB)
     - Status: LOSE (increases compliance costs, reduces managerial authority)
     - Compensation: Not viable (regulation is the cost)
     - Bypass: Possible if labor movement strong enough to override
  
  2. Congressional Republicans + Moderate Democrats
     - Status: OPPOSE (business-friendly positioning)
     - Compensation: Possible (phase-in period, small business exemptions)
     - Bypass: Requires Democratic trifecta + willingness to use reconciliation
  
  3. Federal Courts
     - Status: UNCERTAIN (novel regulation, subject to "major questions doctrine")
     - Compensation: N/A
     - Bypass: Requires careful legal drafting to survive judicial review

SCAFFOLD-REQUIREMENTS:
Load-bearing: NO (adds new requirement, doesn't remove existing structure)

[CONSTRAINT-ANALYSIS #3]

Recommendation: "Provide tax advantages and regulatory streamlining for worker cooperatives, where workers collectively own and direct the enterprise."

CONSTRAINT-MAP:
- Mountain:
  * Coordination: Cooperatives face genuine collective action problems (decision-making costs scale with membership)
  * Market: Cooperatives compete with traditional firms that can raise capital more easily
- Rope:
  * Tax code (functional coordination, but currently disadvantages co-ops)
  * State incorporation law (functional coordination, but co-op statutes vary widely)
  * Mondragon precedent (proven coordination mechanism in different context)
- Noose:
  * Capital access concentrated in traditional ownership structures
  * Legal/regulatory framework designed for shareholder corporations
- Zombie:
  * Outdated co-op incorporation statutes in many states (written for agricultural co-ops, not modern firms)

FEASIBILITY-ASSESSMENT:
Classification: VIABLE (with caveats)
Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH
Reasoning:
This is the MOST FEASIBLE of the three recommendations because:

1. **Precedent Exists**: Credit unions, agricultural co-ops, and some worker co-ops already receive tax advantages. Expanding this is incremental, not revolutionary.

2. **Bipartisan Appeal Possible**: 
   - Left: Worker ownership, reduced inequality
   - Right: Market-based solution, no mandates, "ownership society"
   - Libertarian: Voluntary association, reduced state intervention

3. **Low Fiscal Cost**: Tax advantages cost less than direct grants, and co-ops generate tax revenue through business activity.

4. **State-Level Viable**: Model incorporation statutes can be adopted state-by-state without federal action. Several states (CA, CO, NY) already have pro-co-op legislators.

5. **Self-Testing**: If co-ops don't flourish even with advantages, that tells us something about their viability. Low downside risk.

HOWEVER, CAVEATS:
- **Efficiency Question Real**: Mondragon is impressive but operates in specific cultural context (Basque region, Catholic social teaching tradition). US has different business culture.
- **Capital Access Still Matters**: Tax advantages help but don't solve the capital formation problem—co-ops still struggle to raise investment capital.
- **Scale Limits**: Co-ops work well at small-medium scale but face coordination costs at large scale. Not a universal solution.

IMPLEMENTATION-MAP:
Required Preconditions:
  - Political: State-level legislative champions (already exist in several states)
  - Economic: Minimal fiscal cost (tax expenditure, not direct spending)
  - Technical: Model statute drafting (can adapt existing co-op law)
  - Social: Growing interest in alternative ownership models (documented trend)
  - Temporal: 1-3 years for state-level adoption; 3-5 years for federal tax changes

Energy Cost:
  - Person-hours: ~50,000 (model statute drafting, state-by-state advocacy, federal tax lobbying)
  - Political capital: LOW-MEDIUM (incremental change, bipartisan appeal possible)
  - Opportunity cost: Minimal—doesn't preclude other labor reforms

Timeline:
  - Optimistic: 1-2 years (state-level adoption in friendly states)
  - Realistic: 3-5 years (federal tax changes require congressional action)
  - Catastrophe-contingent: Not applicable (doesn't require crisis)

Veto Points:

     - Bypass: Focus on friendly states first, build momentum
