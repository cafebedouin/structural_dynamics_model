# STAGE 2: NATURALIZATION OUTPUT

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

### Setting

**Bandar Seri Begawan, Negara Brunei Darussalam, 1968-1973**

The oil boom has transformed the Sultanate. British Petroleum engineers work alongside Malay technical staff in the offshore platforms. The city swells with new wealth—government housing blocks rise near kampung settlements, air-conditioned offices overlook traditional water villages. Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III has abdicated to his son, but the old structures of authority remain: the palace, the mosque, the petroleum ministry.

Dr. Rashid bin Abdullah works in the National Petroleum Research Laboratory, a concrete building near the harbor where the smell of crude oil mixes with salt air. He is forty-three, educated at Imperial College London, returned to serve the Sultanate. His laboratory studies enhanced extraction methods—how to pull more oil from aging wells, how to process heavier crude grades. The work is technical, precise, documented in English for international journals and Malay for ministry reports.

His daughter Aminah is nineteen. She was born with a cleft palate, repaired in childhood but leaving visible scarring. In a society where marriage arrangements begin with family photographs, where beauty standards are discussed openly, where a daughter's marriage prospects determine family standing, she has learned to make herself small. She works as a filing clerk in the same petroleum ministry, a position arranged by her father. She speaks Malay at home, English in the office, and has learned to read the micro-expressions that tell her when to leave a room.

Rashid's colleague, Dr. Karim, runs the ministry's technical review board. He is fifty-six, trained in Edinburgh, married with four sons. He approves research budgets, assigns laboratory space, controls which papers get published under the ministry's name. He believes genuinely in petroleum science as national development—that technical expertise will secure Brunei's future when the oil runs out. He chairs committees, writes policy recommendations, speaks at regional conferences about sustainable extraction.

Rashid's younger brother, Hassan, is a foreman at the offshore Platform Seven. He never attended university—went straight to technical training, worked his way up from roughneck to supervisor. He lives in government housing with his wife and three children, sends money to their mother in the kampung, believes the system works because from where he stands, it does. Good workers get promoted. Lazy ones don't. The company provides housing, medical care, children's education. What more could a man want?

### Constraint Naturalizations

**The Pursuit** (C₁)
*What they call it:* "Chasing recognition" (mengejar pengiktirafan)

Rashid's research into heavy crude processing could revolutionize extraction from Brunei's aging fields. If successful, it would extend the Sultanate's petroleum independence by decades. The work consumes him—sixteen-hour days, weekend laboratory sessions, meticulous documentation. He submits papers to international journals, presents at conferences in Singapore and Jakarta, corresponds with researchers in Aberdeen and Houston.

But the breakthrough remains elusive. Each promising result reveals new complications. The process works in small-scale tests but fails at industrial volume. International researchers cite his early work but pursue their own directions. The ministry asks for practical applications; he provides theoretical advances. Dr. Karim's review board grows impatient—five years of funding, minimal commercial output.

From Rashid's position: This is legitimate scientific ambition. The problem is genuinely difficult. The solution would genuinely matter. The extraction is real—his health declining, his family seeing him only at meals, his marriage becoming administrative coordination—but the purpose feels sufficient. He can stop anytime. He chooses not to because the work matters. (χ = 0.56, Tangled Rope: moderate power, can technically abandon the pursuit, but exit is identity-locked—who is he if not the scientist chasing this solution?)

From Dr. Karim's position: The same pursuit appears as one research program among many. The board manages dozens of projects, allocates resources strategically, measures outputs against inputs. Rashid's work is promising but not unique. The ministry can redirect funding if needed. The extraction is barely visible—committee meetings, budget reviews, paper evaluations. The system works. (χ = 0.63, Tangled Rope: moderate power, larger scope makes verification harder, exit merely constrained not locked.)

**The Obligation** (C₂)
*What they call it:* "Father's debt" (hutang bapa)

Rashid knows what he created. When Aminah was born with the cleft palate, he was completing his doctorate in London. He returned for two weeks, saw the infant's face, felt the bottom drop from his stomach, and flew back to his research. His wife Zainab handled the surgeries, the feeding difficulties, the stares in the market. He sent money. He wrote letters. He told himself his career was for the family's future.

Now Aminah is nineteen, working in a filing room, making herself invisible. She has learned not to smile too widely. She has learned which conversations to avoid. She has learned that when men discuss their daughters' marriage prospects, the room grows quiet if she enters. Rashid arranged her job—a favor called in, a position created. He knows it is not enough. He knows he should have been there. He knows he should help her now. He does not know how.

The obligation sits in his chest like a stone. It surfaces when he sees her eating alone in the ministry canteen. It surfaces when Zainab mentions, carefully, that Aminah has no friends her own age. It surfaces when he works late in the laboratory and realizes he is avoiding going home. The guilt is physical—nausea, insomnia, the sense of having broken something that cannot be repaired.

From Rashid's position: The obligation is crushing but unclear. What does a father owe? He provided surgery, education, employment. What remains is his absence—how does one repay that? The extraction is high (guilt, shame, avoidance) but the coordination value exists (genuine moral framework saying fathers must care for daughters). He cannot fulfill the duty without confronting what he created, which means confronting who he was, which threatens the identity he has built as the dedicated scientist. (χ = 0.64, Tangled Rope: moderate power, identity-locked exit, genuine coordination value but high personal cost.)

From Aminah's position: The same obligation appears as abandonment. She needed a father; she received money and a job. The obligation exists in principle—fathers should care for daughters—but it is inaccessible to her. She cannot compel his presence. She cannot make him see her. She is trapped in the filing room he arranged, grateful for employment she did not choose, invisible in a building where he works three floors above. (χ = 0.96, Snare: powerless to compel care, trapped by economic dependence and social position, local scope, high extraction from unmet need.)

**The Boundary** (C₃)
*What they call it:* "The proper order" (susunan yang betul)

In Bandar Seri Begawan's professional class, beauty matters. Marriage negotiations begin with photographs. Families discuss their children's prospects openly—which daughter is clever, which son is handsome, which family has good bloodlines. The standards are specific: fair skin, regular features, proportionate build. Deviations are noted, discussed, weighed against other assets.

Aminah's scarring is visible. The surgeries repaired function but left marks—a slight asymmetry to her upper lip, a tightness when she speaks. In family photographs, she is positioned to minimize the visibility. At ministry social events, she is introduced briefly, then forgotten. When Hassan's wife mentions potential suitors for her own daughters, the conversation does not include Aminah.

The exclusion is not violent. No one is cruel. People are simply... realistic. Marriage is a family matter, a joining of lineages, a calculation of assets. Aminah's prospects are limited. This is acknowledged with sympathy, with regret, with the tone one uses to discuss unfortunate facts. She is included in family gatherings but not in the conversations about futures. She is employed but not promoted. She is visible but not seen.

From Aminah's position: The boundary is everywhere and nowhere. No rule explicitly excludes her. No person directly rejects her. But every social interaction confirms her position. She is powerless to force acceptance—beauty standards are not negotiable. She is trapped—her appearance cannot be changed, alternative communities do not exist. The extraction is total: denial of intimacy, partnership, social recognition, the future her cousins take for granted. (χ = 1.15, Snare: powerless, trapped, regional scope across professional Malay society, extreme extraction.)

From Hassan's position: The same boundary appears as natural social order. Some people are attractive, some are not. This affects marriage prospects. This is unfortunate but not unjust. The system works—his own daughters are pretty, will marry well, will have good lives. He feels sympathy for Aminah but also... practicality. What can be done? One cannot force attraction. The boundary maintains family standards, ensures good matches, protects lineage quality. (χ = 0.306, Rope: organized family/community power, mobile exit if system becomes burdensome, generational time horizon, sees coordination value in maintaining standards.)

**The Withdrawal** (C₄)
*What they call it:* "Keeping to oneself" (menjaga diri)

Rashid has learned not to discuss his research outside the laboratory. The enhanced extraction methods involve experimental catalysts, pressure systems that occasionally fail spectacularly, processes that produce toxic byproducts. Early in his career, he mentioned these challenges at a ministry reception. Dr. Karim's expression shifted—concern, calculation, the look of a man reassessing risk. After that, Rashid learned to speak in generalities: "Progress continues," "Results are promising," "We're refining the methodology."

The isolation protects him. If colleagues knew the full scope of the failures—the equipment damage, the safety incidents, the theoretical dead-ends—they might question his competence. If Dr. Karim knew how far from commercialization the research actually is, the funding would vanish. If his family knew how much time he spends replicating experiments that fail, they might ask why he bothers.

So he works alone. He documents carefully but shares selectively. He attends conferences but presents only successful results. He corresponds with international researchers but omits the catastrophic failures. The withdrawal costs him—no collaborators to catch errors, no colleagues to share burden, no intimacy with anyone who understands the work. But it also protects him from judgment, from interference, from the exposure of how much he has invested in a solution that may not exist.

From Rashid's position: The isolation is chosen protection. He has moderate power—could share more, could ask for help—but exit is constrained by what revelation would cost. The extraction is real (loneliness, lack of support, inability to process failure with others) but the protection value is also real (continued funding, maintained reputation, autonomy in research direction). (χ = 0.44, Tangled Rope edge case: self-imposed constraint with subjective coordination value, moderate extraction.)

From Aminah's position: The same withdrawal is forced exile. She is isolated not by choice but by exclusion (C₃). She cannot force social acceptance. She is trapped—no alternative communities exist for unmarriageable daughters. Her withdrawal looks like Rashid's but feels completely different. His isolation protects; hers imprisons. (χ = 0.74, Snare: powerless, trapped, regional scope, high extraction from involuntary isolation.)

**The Silence** (C₅)
*What they call it:* "Laboratory matters" (hal makmal)

In 1970, one of Rashid's pressure tests failed catastrophically. The experimental catalyst decomposed under heat, producing hydrogen sulfide gas. The laboratory filled with the smell of rotten eggs. Rashid sealed the room, activated ventilation, filed an incident report describing "unexpected exothermic reaction" and "equipment malfunction." He did not mention that he had bypassed two safety protocols to reach higher pressures. He did not mention that he had ignored warning signs in previous tests. He did not mention that he knew the catalyst was unstable but hoped higher pressures would force stability.

No one was injured. The equipment was replaced. The incident report was filed and forgotten. But Rashid knows what he did. He knows he prioritized results over safety. He knows he gambled with his technicians' lives. He knows that if he had been wrong in a slightly different way, people would have died.

The knowledge sits in him like poison. He cannot discuss it—admitting the safety violations would end his career, possibly face criminal charges. He cannot seek advice—any colleague would be obligated to report. He cannot confess to Zainab—she would not understand the technical details, would only hear that he endangered people. So he carries it alone: the memory of the smell, the sound of alarms, the moment of realization that he had been catastrophically reckless.

The silence protects others—if the full details became public, it might trigger safety reviews that would shut down legitimate research across the ministry. But it also isolates him completely. The guilt compounds the isolation from C₄. The fear of exposure compounds the guilt from C₂. He works alone, speaks carefully, and knows he can never fully explain why.

From Rashid's position: The silence is crushing obligation. High extraction (isolation, guilt, fear) but genuine coordination value (prevents dangerous knowledge spread, protects ministry's research capacity). High suppression—both internal (guilt, horror at what he did) and external (fear of consequences). Identity-locked exit—revealing the truth means admitting he endangered lives for glory, which destroys the identity he has built. (χ = 0.60, Tangled Rope: moderate power, identity-locked exit, genuine coordination value but asymmetric burden on knowledge-holder.)

From Dr. Karim's position: The same silence appears as professional discretion. The ministry maintains confidentiality around research incidents. This is standard practice—protects intellectual property, prevents competitor advantage, maintains public confidence in petroleum operations. The board reviews incident reports, implements safety improvements, moves forward. The extraction is barely visible—occasional committee meetings about safety protocols. The system works. (χ = 0.30, Rope: organized institutional power, mobile exit, generational time horizon, national scope, low effective extraction despite high base extraction—classic naturalization pattern.)

### Linguistic Strategy: Code-Switching (Malay/English)

**Base languages:** Malay (Bahasa Melayu) and institutional English

**Historical context:** 1960s Brunei exists in linguistic transition. The Sultanate's 
professional class is bilingual—educated in English (often abroad), conducting technical 
work in English, but living in Malay. The switch signals power and intimacy.

**Switching contexts:**

1. **Malay for:**
   - Family conversation
   - Private emotion
   - Cultural concepts without English equivalent
   - Moments of vulnerability
   - Addressing elders or intimates

2. **English for:**
   - Technical discussion
   - Ministry business
   - International correspondence
   - Professional performance
   - Maintaining distance

**Key terms with no direct English equivalent:**

- *Hutang* — Debt, but specifically moral/social debt that cannot be repaid with money
- *Malu* — Shame, but specifically the shame of failing social expectations
- *Rezeki* — Provision/sustenance, but with connotation of fate/divine allocation
- *Susunan* — Order/arrangement, but specifically proper hierarchical order
- *Adat* — Custom/tradition, but specifically binding social practice

**Grammatical features carried into English:**

1. **Dropped articles:** "Father arranged position" (not "the position")
2. **Reduplication for emphasis:** "The work is slow-slow" (very slow)
3. **Lah/leh particles for softening:** Carried as "already" or "only"
   - "Can already" (boleh lah) = it's acceptable
   - "Like that only" (macam itu saja leh) = that's just how it is

**Example dialogue:**

```
Rashid (to Karim, in laboratory): "The catalyst decomposition rate exceeds our 
projections. We need to recalibrate the pressure parameters."

Rashid (to Zainab, at home): "Kerja lambat lagi. The work is slow-slow. Tak tahu 
bila siap." (Work is late again. Don't know when it will finish.)

Aminah (to herself, filing papers): "Susunan yang betul. Everything in proper order. 
Like that only."

Hassan (to crew, on platform): "Kerja kuat hari ini. Work hard today. Bonus bulan 
depan, can already." (Next month's bonus is possible.)
```

The code-switch itself performs power. Rashid speaking English to Karim maintains 
professional distance. Rashid speaking Malay to Zainab reveals (limited) intimacy. 
Aminah's mixed speech shows her split position—performing English competence at work, 
retreating to Malay for private thoughts. Hassan's Malay-dominant speech with English 
technical terms shows his position: working class but technically skilled.

### Inhabitation Sentences

**Rashid:** You are a scientist who believes your work matters enough to justify its 
cost, who carries the memory of hydrogen sulfide in your lungs and your daughter's

### RESOLVED

**Ω_E01: Historical accuracy of Brunei petroleum industry 1968-1973**
- Question: Did Brunei have domestic petroleum research laboratories during this period?
- Resolution: Yes. Brunei Shell Petroleum and government ministries operated research 
  facilities. The National Petroleum Research Laboratory is fictionalized but plausible 
  within documented infrastructure.
- Impact: Setting verified as historically plausible.

**Ω_E02: Cleft palate repair availability in 1960s Brunei**
- Question: Would surgical repair have been accessible to professional-class families?
- Resolution: Yes. Brunei's oil wealth funded modern medical facilities. Wealthy families 
  could access surgery domestically or in Singapore. Scarring from 1950s repair would be 
  visible by 1968.
- Impact: Aminah's medical history is historically accurate.

**Ω_E03: Language mixing patterns in 1960s Brunei professional class**
- Question: Would code-switching between Malay and English be natural for this demographic?
- Resolution: Yes. British colonial education system produced bilingual professional class. 
  Technical work conducted in English, family life in Malay. Code-switching marked class 
  and education level.
- Impact: Linguistic strategy verified as historically accurate.

**Ω_C01: Definition of "father's debt" (hutang bapa)**
- Question: Does hutang carry moral obligation connotation in Malay?
- Resolution: Yes. Hutang encompasses financial debt but extends to moral/social 
  obligations that cannot be monetarily repaid. Father's debt to child is culturally 
  recognized concept.
- Impact: C₂ naturalization uses culturally accurate terminology.

**Ω_C02: Beauty standards and marriage negotiation in 1960s Malay society**
- Question: Were appearance-based exclusions from marriage market socially normalized?
- Resolution: Yes. Marriage negotiations in professional Malay families involved explicit 
  discussion of physical appearance, family lineage, economic prospects. Visible 
  differences were acknowledged as limiting factors.
- Impact: C₃ naturalization reflects documented social practices.

**Ω_P01: Tone for Aminah's sections**
- Question: Should Aminah's experience be rendered with anger, resignation, or numbness?
- Resolution: Numbness with flashes of clarity. She has learned to make herself small—
  anger would be exhausting, resignation would be visible. Numbness with occasional 
  moments of seeing the structure clearly.
- Impact: Inhabitation sentence emphasizes learned invisibility and structural awareness.

**Ω_P02: Rashid's guilt presentation**
- Question: Should guilt be foregrounded or implied?
- Resolution: Physical manifestation (stone in chest, nausea) rather than psychological 
  narration. Show through body and avoidance behavior, not through internal monologue.
- Impact: C₂ naturalization uses somatic language.

**Ω_P03: Hassan's perspective presentation**
- Question: Should Hassan be portrayed as willfully blind or genuinely unaware?
- Resolution: Genuinely unaware. From his structural position, the system works. He is 
  not lying or self-deceiving—he simply cannot see what Aminah experiences because his 
  index is different. No moral judgment.
- Impact: Hassan's sections avoid villain framing, maintain structural analysis.
