# DISCOVERY REPORT

## EMERGENT ELEMENTS

The story enacts **extraction masking through procedural legitimacy**. The assayer's fee operates as the structural center: an 8% extraction that presents itself as neutral service provision. The prose tracks Túpac's journey from recognizing this as theft to defending it as "the price of doing business."

**The perspectival gap manifests between Túpac and Martín:**
- Túpac experiences the constraint (the fee system) as something to navigate successfully. By the second mita, he has learned to optimize within it—choosing rich ore, timing sales, understanding which assayers are "honest."
- Martín experiences the same constraint as a trap that becomes invisible through competence. His margin notes track what Túpac can no longer see: that Mendoza's honest scale doesn't reduce extraction, that working faster only increases the percentage-based fee, that survival-through-adaptation is itself the mechanism of capture.

**The axis is most alive** in the scene where Túpac explains the fee structure to Sami. The prose captures the moment of transmission—Túpac using the same patient tone the older mitayo used on him three years earlier, explaining the system "correctly" while Sami looks at him "like I had struck him." The gap between Túpac's steady hands and Sami's turned back contains the entire structural dynamic.

**Where it's present but underdeveloped:** The vara measurement scene gestures toward this same dynamic (indigenous measures replaced by Spanish standardization, precision as colonization) but doesn't fully integrate with the assayer throughline. Martín's comment about measures being "ours" versus "precise" opens territory the story doesn't occupy.

## LATENT POTENTIAL

**The Frankl question—what meaning is available in this constraint that the story hasn't yet found:**

The margin-writing itself. Martín writes in Quechua "where Spanish eyes cannot follow," creating a counter-archive in the spaces the official record doesn't see. But the story hasn't yet explored what this means beyond documentation. Who is this for? The story says "someone has to remember what he has forgotten," but it hasn't dramatized the possibility (or impossibility) of that remembering mattering.

**The invariant probe's territory:** Sami's death. The story notes that "the paper had outlived him"—the certification still exists, the ore still circulates, but Sami is gone. This is the moment where the system's instruments (the scale, the paper, the vara) encounter something they cannot read: a body crushed under rock. The story registers this but doesn't dwell in it. What does it mean that the paper continues to function after the person who broke the ore is dead? The story knows this matters but hasn't yet made it matter structurally.

**World elements implying larger context:**
- The ayllu system (communal land tenure) that sends mitayos in rotation
- The yungas (lowland refuge) where some flee
- The forty years of margin-logs on Martín's shelf
- The Cabildo's annual vara verification ritual

Each of these implies a fuller world, but they remain background. The story could expand by following any of these threads—particularly the ayllu's perspective (what does the community know about what happens to those it sends?) or the accumulated margins (forty years of Quechua counter-archive—has anyone ever read them?).

## STRONGEST WRITING

**The assayer's shop, first visit:**
> "He took a piece of ore from my sack, no bigger than my thumb. Placed it in a clay crucible, put the crucible in the furnace. I watched the ore glow, melt, the dross burning away. The smell made my eyes water. When it cooled, he poured the silver into a small mold, weighed it on the scale. The needle swung, settled."

The prose slows to match Túpac's attention. Each action is a discrete sentence. The sensory detail (smell making eyes water) grounds the abstraction about to arrive. The needle settling is the moment before the number appears—before value becomes legible.

**Túpac explaining the fee to Sami:**
> "'The eight percent is of the certified value,' I said. 'Not of what you brought. You brought one hundred marcos of ore. But the ore was only seventy-nine percent silver. So the value was seventy-nine marcos. The fee is eight percent of that.'
>
> Sami stared at me. 'That is theft.'
>
> 'That is the *tasa*.'
>
> 'It is the same thing.'
>
> 'No,' I said."

The dialogue does the structural work. Túpac's patient explanation, Sami's equation of tasa with theft, Túpac's flat "No"—the gap between them is the gap the story is about. The prose trusts the exchange to carry the weight without commentary.

**Martín's final margin note:**
> "*Ya no ve la tasa como robo. La ve como el precio de hacer negocios. Esto es lo que el sistema hace: te enseña a cargar tu propia cadena y llamarla una herramienta.*"
>
> He no longer sees the fee as theft. He sees it as the price of doing business. This is what the system does: it teaches you to carry your own chain and call it a tool.

The metaphor (chain/tool) lands because the story has earned it. We've watched Túpac learn to carry the weight. The margin note names what the prose has shown.

**The plaza conversation with Martín:**
> "'You have forgotten what you knew in your first year.'
>
> 'I have learned what works,' I said."

The entire dynamic in two lines. Martín's "forgotten what you knew" versus Túpac's "learned what works"—both true, both describing the same process from opposite sides of the perspectival gap.

## ANCHOR CHECK

**Counting passages identified:**

1. "Eighty-two percent *ley*" / "Eighty-four percent *ley*" / "Seventy-nine percent" / "Eighty-one percent" / "Eighty-six percent"—the recurring assay percentages
2. "One hundred marcos" / "Seventy-nine marcos" / "Eight percent"—the fee arithmetic
3. "Fifteen *varas* to silver" / "Twenty-three *varas* to the new vein"—shaft measurements
4. "Three years ago" / "Second *mita*" / "Forty years of logs"—temporal markers
5. "Six marcos and change" / "Seven marcos" / "Seventy-eight marcos"—transaction amounts

**What the numbers stand in for:**

The percentages and marcos are standing in for **the transformation of labor into extractable value**. The story needs to show how ore becomes certified silver becomes coin, but the precision of "eighty-two percent" versus "eighty-four percent" creates a false sense of exactitude—as if the system's instruments are reading something real rather than constructing what counts as real.

The vara measurements are standing in for **imposed standardization**—the replacement of indigenous measures (rikra, sikya) with Spanish precision. But "fifteen varas" versus "twenty-three varas" doesn't dramatize this; it just reports depth.

The temporal counting (three years, second mita, forty years) is standing in for **cyclical entrapment**. The story wants to show Túpac returning, Martín writing for decades, the system reproducing itself. But "three years" and "forty years" read as inevitability because arithmetic always does.

**Strategy pass note:** These passages are vivid because they're where the extraction happens—the moment the scale settles, the fee is calculated, the value is certified. But the vividness comes from the *transformation* (ore to number to paper), not from the numbers themselves. Revision should preserve the transformation while reducing the arithmetic's false precision. Show the needle settling, the paper being written, the coin changing hands—but let the exact percentages blur except where they're structurally necessary (e.g., Túpac explaining to Sami requires specific numbers to show how the fee calculation works).

## INVARIANT PROBE

**The unreadable real:** Sami's body under the rock.

The system's instruments—the scale, the certification, the vara, the official log—can read ore quality, shaft depth, daily wages, production totals. They cannot read (and do not attempt to read) what it means that Sami is crushed, that his poncho becomes his shroud, that no priest comes because none of them have coin, that the old words are said instead.

The story registers this clearly: "The paper had outlived him." The certification continues to function. The ore circulates. But Sami is gone, and the system has no register for that absence except as a gap in production.

**Crucially:** This is unreadable *in principle*, not merely hidden. A better scale, a fairer assayer, a more honest foreman—none of these would make Sami's death legible to the system, because the system's instruments are designed to read value-extraction, not human cost. The cost is externalized by design.

The story knows this. Túpac thinks about writing to the ayllu and realizes he has nothing to say that the system's language can hold: "Your son is dead, but his ore tested at eighty-one percent? The paper is still good?" The gap between what matters (Sami is dead) and what the system can read (eighty-one percent ley) is unbridgeable.

**What this means structurally:** The story has found its invariant. There is a real here (human life, human death, the meaning of crushing stone until you are crushed by stone) that the system cannot and will not read. Martín's margins are an attempt to create a counter-archive, a place where this unreadable real can be recorded. But the story hasn't yet explored whether that recording can ever become legible, or whether it's doomed to remain in the margins, unread, until Martín dies.

## SCOPE ASSESSMENT

**What the piece became:** A long short story that wants to be a novella.

**Current scope:** 3,337 words (actual count per orchestrator).

**Target range for current form:** 4,500-6,000 words would let the story breathe without requiring new structural elements.

**What expansion would require:**

1. **Dramatize the margins' function.** Right now Martín writes in Quechua "where Spanish eyes cannot follow," but we never see what happens to those writings. Does anyone read them? Does Martín hope they'll be read, or is the writing itself the point? A scene where someone (another foreman? a literate mitayo? Túpac himself, years later?) encounters the margins would give the counter-archive dramatic weight.

2. **Develop the ayllu perspective.** The community that sends mitayos in rotation is present only as background. What do they know about what happens in Potosí? What do they tell themselves about the ones who don't return? A section from the ayllu's viewpoint (perhaps the meeting where Túpac's name is called for the second mita) would show the system's reach beyond the mountain.

3. **Follow Sami's thread further.** His death is the story's clearest encounter with the unreadable real, but it's handled in a single section. Expanding this—perhaps showing Túpac trying and failing to write to the ayllu, or showing what happens to Sami's certification after his death—would deepen the invariant probe.

4. **Give the vara scene structural integration.** Right now it's a beautiful moment (indigenous measures replaced by Spanish precision) that doesn't fully connect to the assayer throughline. Either cut it or develop it—show how standardization and extraction are the same process, how the vara and the scale are both instruments of the same system.

**What compression would sacrifice:**

The story's current length lets it track Túpac's transformation across two mitas with enough detail to make the change feel earned. Compression to standard short story length (2,500-3,000 words) would require cutting either:
- The first mita entirely (start with Túpac already returned, already defending Mendoza), which would lose the before/after contrast that makes the perspectival gap visible, OR
- The second mita's development (compress the Sami subplot, the plaza conversation, the final margin notes), which would lose the dramatization of how the gap operates.

Either sacrifice would weaken the structural work the story is doing.

**Recommendation:** Expand to 5,000-6,000 words. The story has found its spine (Túpac's journey from seeing the fee as theft to defending it as legitimate, tracked against Martín's margin-archive of what's being forgotten). It needs space to develop the margins' function and the ayllu's perspective, and to deepen the encounter with the unreadable real that Sami's death represents. The current draft is overlong for a short story but undercooked for the novella it's reaching toward. Expansion would let it become what it's trying to be.
