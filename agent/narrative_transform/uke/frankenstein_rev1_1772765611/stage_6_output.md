# STAGE 6: STRATEGY BRIEF

## THE FIVE QUESTIONS

**1. Who is the implied author?**

Someone who notices systems before individuals, who sees compulsion as phenomenology rather than moral failure, who understands that clarity doesn't enable action. This author watches people work—the steady hands, the counting, the procedural thinking—and sees how competence becomes complicity. They care about precision: fourteen seconds to lung collapse, sixty seconds to be sure, one hundred eighty seconds of transformation. They skip psychological interiority for behavioral observation. The register is clinical without being cold—the prose counts because the characters count, because precision is how you survive in systems that will kill you.

**2. Who is the audience?**

Readers who recognize institutional capture from the inside. Who have filed reports that disappeared, seen patterns they couldn't break, understood their own complicity without knowing how to exit it. Who don't need villains explained or systems diagrammed—they've lived in them. Who can handle violence shown rather than explained, endings that close without resolving, characters who see clearly and do nothing. This audience doesn't need hope or catharsis; they need recognition.

**3. What is the scope?**

Short story pushing toward novella length. Current: 7,847 words. Natural length: 9,500-11,000 words. The material needs room for:
- The Drive scene (already added, working)
- The three minutes (already added, working)
- The hunt sequence (already added, working)
- Park's concrete stakes (partially present, needs dramatization)

Target: 10,000 words. This gives space to show Park's life (what twenty years built, what deletion protects) without expanding beyond the story's natural boundaries.

**4. What creative deepening is available?**

The Prolog reports identify this as Snare (χ₁: compulsion) + Tangled Rope (χ₂: complicity). The discovery report notes: "The story's power is in making complicity feel like breathing—not a choice but a condition of continued existence."

The deepening available: make Park's deletion feel like breathing. Not a dramatic choice, not a moral failure—a continuation of what he's already doing. Show the life that deletion protects: the routines, the relationships, the identity built over twenty years. Then show the deletion as maintenance of that life, not abandonment of principle. The horror isn't that Park chooses wrong; it's that he chooses correctly given the constraints he lives inside.

This requires one additional scene: Park's morning routine before the deletion. What does twenty years of service look like as daily practice? Who does he nod to in corridors? What reports does he file? What does "Security Chief" mean as lived identity rather than job title? 400 words, placed before the deletion sequence, showing what the deletion protects.

**5. Where does this material flinch?**

**FLINCH POINT (remaining):**

**Location:** Part Four, deletion sequence (currently lines 847-891)

**What it's doing:** Stating Park's stakes abstractly ("reassignment to inner system work, loss of human status") without showing what those stakes mean as lived experience. We know he'd lose something; we don't feel what.

**What honesty requires:** Show Park's life before showing his choice. What does twenty years build? Not career advancement—daily existence. The commissary table where he sits. The officers who report to him. The routine of filing reports, maintaining order, being the person who sees patterns and documents them. Show this as texture, not explanation. Then show the deletion as protection of that texture—not moral failure but survival instinct.

**PATTERN CHECK:**

The story's default is quiet resolution through system maintenance. This is correct for the material—Park's paralysis *is* quiet, *is* systemic, *does* maintain order. The pattern check isn't whether the story chooses quiet resolution (it should) but whether it earns that choice by showing what it costs.

Current status: The Drive scene earns Okonkwo's compulsion by showing it happen. The three minutes earn Tala's transformation by counting every second. The hunt sequence earns the murder by showing patience as inevitability. The deletion sequence doesn't yet earn Park's paralysis—it states the cost without showing it.

The fix: one scene showing what Park protects. Then the deletion becomes earned rather than asserted.
