# REVIEW ASSESSMENT

## STRENGTHS:

The story's architecture is precise and earned. Each of the four parts builds a distinct transformation—Okonkwo's compulsive brilliance, Tala's three-minute recognition, the hunt as inevitable consequence, Park's twenty-year survival instinct—and the pacing matches the psychology: crisis moves fast (execution, chase), recognition moves methodically (the counting, the investigation), deletion moves quietly (the morning routine, the file marked "pending"). The prose is clean. The 180-second count works as both structure and experience. The ending lands because Park's shaking hands and morning coffee have been established as what he's protecting, not abstractions about complicity.

## BIGGEST WEAKNESS:

The chase sequences (Part Three, lines 290-358 and 360-410) still feel slightly mechanical despite the pacing compression. The problem isn't tempo—it's that we're watching Okonkwo experience fear without accessing what makes that fear specific to her. She runs, glances back, her hands shake, but we don't get the question that would make this *her* terror rather than generic pursuit. Does she recognize the footsteps? Does she know who's following? Is she running from consequence or from not-knowing? The scene works functionally but doesn't achieve the psychological precision of the other three parts.

## READINESS:

The story is structurally sound, thematically disciplined, and 95% of the prose is alive. One sequence needs a final pass to match the precision of the rest.

## ROUTE: STRATEGY

The biggest weakness is specific and addressable: the chase sequences need one more editorial pass to give Okonkwo's fear the same psychological specificity that Tala's counting and Park's morning routine achieve. This is a refinement problem, not a structural problem. The story is close—one more iteration will get it there.
