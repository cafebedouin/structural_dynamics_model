## 2. VOICE ARCHETYPE SELECTION

**Selected Archetype:** The Condemned

**Justification:**

Miguel's structural position maps precisely to the Condemned archetype:
- **Inside the constraint logic:** Miguel experiences *ang utang* as natural obligation, not constructed extraction
- **Cannot see systemic shape:** The debt feels like family responsibility, not institutional design
- **Acts rationally given limited information:** Saying no to organizing twice is rational self-preservation, not systemic critique
- **Experiences constraint as inevitable:** The contract is "what you do for family," not "what the company does to workers"
- **Voice is immediate, experiential:** Miguel lives in his body—the vibration in his hands, the metal smell, the exhaustion

**Attractor Compatibility:** ✓ Deterministic Tragedy
The Condemned voice allows tragedy to unfold through lived experience without analytical distance. Miguel cannot see the trap until he's in it. The reader sees the cascade coming; Miguel doesn't.

**Power/Rationality Requirements:** ✓ PIR model, powerless/moderate position (π ≥ 1.0)
Miguel is moderate power in C₁ (π = 1.0), powerless in C₂/C₃ (π = 1.5). PIR ensures no negotiation opportunity.

**POV Selection:** First person, close present tense
Miguel's voice must be immediate—no retrospective framing, no analytical register. The reader experiences the constraint tightening in real-time.

**Tone:** Tragic, somber, embodied
The darkness comes from what Miguel cannot see, not from what he explains. His love for his family is genuine. His exhaustion is real. The extraction is invisible to him until it's total.

**Alternative Archetypes Rejected:**

- **The Builder:** Would require Miguel to test for leverage points, learn from failures. But C₂ gives no feedback—you don't know you've been entrapped until it's over. Builder voice would promise agency that doesn't exist.

- **The Witness:** Would require distance from the constraint. Miguel is inside it. A Witness voice would collapse the immediacy that makes the tragedy felt.

- **The Survivor:** Would require temporal distance (narrating from after). But C₃ is total extraction—there is no "after" for Miguel. Survivor voice would require him to escape, which violates the attractor.

## 3. INDEXICAL REVELATION STRATEGY

**Selected Strategy:** Gradual Realization (Single POV, slowly discover others experience differently)

**Implementation:**

Miguel's POV throughout. The reader sees the constraint network complete; Miguel sees only his immediate situation. Revelation occurs through collision with other indices:

**Act 1 (C₁ activation):** Miguel experiences *ang utang* as coordination. The debt is real, but so is the house. He speaks to his family via video message—they're happy, grateful, proud. The reader sees the identity-lock; Miguel feels obligation.

**Act 2 (C₂ activation):** Lena approaches Miguel. She speaks Filipino. She shares stories about her time in the outer rings. Miguel believes her because she's like him—she came from the same place. The reader sees the entrapment; Miguel sees solidarity. The revelation comes when he gives names and sees the reassignments. He realizes too late. But he still doesn't see the system—he thinks he made a mistake, not that he was targeted.

**Act 3 (C₃ activation):** The tribunal. Miguel finally sees Torres—a man who's never been in the shafts, who speaks only English, who reads from a tablet. Twenty minutes. The verdict is predetermined. Miguel realizes the system isn't personal—it's mechanical. But this realization comes at the moment of maximum powerlessness. He can see it now, but he can't act on it.

**Indexical Variance Revealed Through:**
- **Lena's dialogue:** She genuinely believes she's helping. Her explanations reveal her index (powerful, biographical, arbitrage exit). Miguel hears her words but can't process the gap—she sounds sincere because she is sincere from her position.
- **Torres's procedural language:** Clinical, legal, distant. Miguel hears the words but they don't connect to his reality. Torres is describing a system; Miguel is living a catastrophe.
- **Physical markers:** Miguel notices things Torres doesn't see—the way the tribunal room is carpeted, the way Torres's hands are clean, the way the air smells different in the central core. These details reveal the indexical gap without Miguel naming it.

**Why This Strategy:**
Single POV maintains the Condemned voice's immediacy. Gradual revelation allows the reader to see the trap closing while Miguel experiences it as a series of rational choices. The collision with other indices (Lena's genuine belief, Torres's procedural distance) exposes the constraint network without Miguel achieving analytical perspective.

## 4. EDITORIAL DECISIONS

**LENGTH:** Novelette (7,500-10,000 words)
*Justification:* Three-act structure with constraint cascade requires space. Act 1 must establish *ang utang* as felt experience (not exposition). Act 2 must build the entrapment slowly—Lena's approach, Miguel's deliberation, the moment he gives names. Act 3 must show the tribunal and the 72-hour processing period's approach without rushing. Flash or short story would compress the tragedy into plot summary. Novelette allows the constraint to tighten at lived pace.

**POV:** First person, Miguel Santos
*Justification:* The Condemned voice requires first person. Miguel must be inside his experience, not observed from outside. Third limited would create distance; third omniscient would collapse the structural blindness that defines the Condemned archetype.

**TENSE:** Present tense
*Justification:* Immediacy. Miguel cannot see the future; the reader shouldn't either (even though the attractor is deterministic). Present tense eliminates retrospective framing—no "I didn't know then" or "If I had understood." Miguel is always in the moment of not-knowing.

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 3 primary (Miguel, Lena, Torres) + 2 secondary (Rosa, Dante)
*Justification:*
- **Miguel:** POV character, the Condemned
- **Lena:** The intelligence officer, represents the Beneficiary index (experiences C₂ as Tangled Rope)
- **Torres:** The administrator, represents the System (experiences C₃ as Rope)
- **Rosa:** Miguel's wife (Earth-side), voice messages only. Represents the family obligation that creates the identity-lock
- **Dante:** Fellow worker, outer ring. Miguel gives his name to Lena. Represents the cost of Miguel's entrapment—Dante is reassigned, avoids Miguel afterward. The friendship's collapse is the first sign Miguel misread the situation

**NAMING:** Cultural (Filipino given names + Spanish surnames)
*Justification:* Grounds the setting in Philippine labor migration patterns. Names signal cultural identity, which is central to the identity-lock mechanism (*utang na loob*, *hiya*). 

**Primary names:**
- Miguel Santos (POV)
- Lena Reyes (intelligence officer)
- Captain Torres (tribunal administrator)
- Rosa Santos (Miguel's wife, Earth-side)
- Dante Cruz (fellow worker)

**LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:**

**Code-switching occurs:**
1. **Miguel ↔ other workers (Filipino):** Private speech, moments of intimacy or frustration. Signals: this is real, I am not performing.
2. **Miguel ↔ supervisors/Lena (English):** Required for official interactions. Signals: I am compliant, I understand my position.
3. **Lena ↔ Miguel (Filipino, strategic):** Lena switches to Filipino to signal false intimacy. Miguel hears it as solidarity; the reader should feel the manipulation.
4. **Torres (English only):** Never switches. Represents institutional monolingualism—the system doesn't speak your language.

**What the switch signals:**
- **Filipino → English:** Shift from private to surveilled space. Miguel's internal monologue is Filipino-inflected English (aspect markers, honorific stress). His dialogue with supervisors is formal English.
- **English → Filipino:** Lena's switch signals "I'm one of you." Miguel believes it. The reader should feel the trap in the language choice itself.

**Grammatical features in Miguel's narration:**
- Aspect marking: "Already I am drilling" (completion emphasis)
- Honorific stress: "Yes, ma'am" with Filipino "po" stress pattern
- Plural inclusive/exclusive: "We all" (including reader/other workers) vs. "we" (excluding supervisors)

**Core vocabulary (used without translation after first introduction):**
- *Utang na loob* (debt of gratitude)
- *Hiya* (shame)
- *Pakikisama* (smooth interpersonal relations)
- *Walang hiya* (shameless—worst insult)

**EMOTIONAL CORE:** (from constraint dynamics, not imposed)

Miguel's love for his family is genuine. The exhaustion is real. The house being built is real. The debt isn't a metaphor—it's concrete, material, photographable. The tragedy isn't that Miguel is tricked into a false obligation. The tragedy is that the obligation is real, and the system uses that reality as a weapon.

**Emotional through-line:**
- **Act 1:** Pride. Miguel is doing what a man does for his family. The exhaustion is bearable because the purpose is clear.
- **Act 2:** Confusion. Miguel thought he was building solidarity; he's built complicity. He doesn't understand what happened. The friendship with Dante collapses. The promotion feels wrong.
- **Act 3:** Recognition without power. Miguel finally sees the system—Torres's clean hands, the carpeted floor, the predetermined verdict. He sees it at the moment he can't act on it. The recognition is the cruelty.

**ENDING STRATEGY:** Attractor reached (Deterministic Tragedy)

The story ends as Miguel is led to the processing facility. The 72-hour period is beginning. The reader knows what's coming (from the context description, from the structural inevitability). Miguel knows something is coming but not what. The last line is Miguel's body noticing something—a physical detail that reveals his fear without naming it.

**Not shown:** The processing itself, the return to Earth, the aftermath. The story ends at the threshold. The tragedy is complete when Miguel enters that room.

## 5. INHABITATION SPEC

**Miguel Santos (POV Character):**

**Phenomenological register:** Hypervigilance to physical labor + numbness to systemic position

**Mode of attention:**
Miguel notices:
- **The body's limits:** His hands shake during sleep from drill vibration. He counts the hours until the shift ends by how much his shoulders ache. He knows he's dehydrated when his tongue sticks to the roof of his mouth.
- **Material details of extraction:** The way lithium deposits glow faintly in the shaft. The sound the drill makes when it hits a vein. The weight of the hauling cart. The smell of metal and recycled sweat.
- **Signs of home:** Rosa's voice in video messages. The photos of the house—concrete walls, tin roof, his brother in school uniform. These are the only things that feel more real than the shaft.

Miguel does NOT notice:
- **The contract structure as extraction:** He experiences it as obligation, not design. The debt is what you owe, not what was done to you.
- **The indexical variance:** He doesn't see that Lena experiences the system differently. When she speaks Filipino, he hears solidarity, not strategy.
- **The tribunal as constructed:** When it happens, it feels like natural law—inevitable, impersonal, like gravity.

**What the constraint feels like in Miguel's body:**

*Ang utang (C₁):* A weight in the chest that's also a purpose. The exhaustion is yours; the benefit is theirs. You can endure because you know what it means. The debt isn't in your head—it's in your hands, your shoulders, your lungs. You feel it every shift, and you feel it as *right*.

*Ang patibong (C₂):* The moment you realize you misread everything. Your stomach drops. Your hands go cold. You gave names—Dante, others—and now they won't look at you. The promotion badge feels like a brand. You can't undo it. You can't explain it. The complicity is in your body now—you wear it.

*Ang hustisya (C₃):* Twenty minutes and you're still trying to breathe normally. Torres is reading from a tablet. The room is carpeted. Your boots leave dust prints. You notice these things because you can't process what's happening. The verdict is words you hear but don't understand. Then they say "72 hours" and your body understands before your mind does. Your legs don't want to move.

**Anti-pattern check:**
- ✓ Not a metric (no numbers changing over time)
- ✓ Describes sensation, not measurement
- ✓ Gives Stage 4 a mode of attention (hypervigilance to body + numbness to system), not a device to repeat

**Lena Reyes (Secondary Character, Beneficiary Index):**

**Phenomenological register:** Confidence + strategic attention to others' vulnerabilities

**Mode of attention:**
Lena notices:
- **Who's frustrated:** She watches for workers who complain, who gather others during breaks, who ask questions about contract terms. She's good at reading body language—the way someone's shoulders tense when a supervisor walks by, the way they look away when asked about their family.
- **When to switch languages:** She uses Filipino strategically. She knows when it signals intimacy, when it signals manipulation. She doesn't think of it as manipulation—she thinks of it as connection.
- **Her own success:** She came from the outer rings. She completed her term. She proved herself. The system works because she's proof it works.

Lena does NOT notice:
- **That she's entrapping people:** She genuinely believes she's identifying agitators and helping good workers advance. The recording is intelligence work, not betrayal.
- **That her index is different:** She experiences *ang patibong* as a functional tool (Tangled Rope, χ = 0.486). She doesn't see that Miguel experiences it as a trap (Snare, χ = 1.08). Both are true, but she can only see her own.

**What the constraint feels like in Lena's body:**

*Ang patibong (C₂):* A tool she's good at using. When she approaches a worker, she feels calm, focused. She's helping them—identifying who has leadership potential, who's just complaining. The promotion she offers is real. The advancement is real. She's not lying. From her position, she's not lying.

**Captain Torres (Secondary Character, System Index):**

**Phenomenological register:** Procedural distance + comfort in institutional space

**Mode of attention:**
Torres notices:
- **Contract terms:** He's read them hundreds of times. He knows every clause, every penalty structure, every disclosure requirement. They're clear. They're fair.
- **Efficiency metrics:** Retention rates, productivity numbers, incident reports. The system works because the numbers say it works.
- **His own comfort:** The carpeted floor, the climate control, the wooden desk. He's earned this. Twenty years with TPMC. He's good at his job.

Torres does NOT notice:
- **The bodies:** He's never been in the shafts. He's never seen a worker after processing. He doesn't need to. The reports are thorough.
- **The indexical gap:** He experiences *ang hustisya* as a necessary enforcement mechanism (Rope, χ = -0.20). He doesn't see that Miguel experiences it as total extraction (Snare, χ = 1.20). He doesn't need to see it. The system works without him seeing it.

**What the constraint feels like in Torres's body:**

*Ang hustisya (C₃):* A procedure he executes. He reads from the tablet. He asks the required questions. He deliberates for five minutes (the manual requires five minutes). He delivers the verdict. His body is calm throughout. This is his job. He's good at his job. When he goes home, he sleeps fine.
