### Minor Fractures Identified:

**F16: Ambiguity - "Compounding" Definition Drift**

The essay uses "compounding" in three distinct senses:
1. **Mathematical compounding:** Exponential growth via multiplication ($1.10^{10}$)
2. **Knowledge compounding:** Later insights reinterpret earlier ones
3. **Institutional compounding:** Accumulated organizational knowledge

**Issue:** The essay assumes these are analogous, but the analogy is never explicitly defended. Mathematical compounding is well-defined; knowledge compounding is metaphorical. Does knowledge *actually* compound multiplicatively, or is this a suggestive analogy?

**Repair:** Add explicit bridging argument in § "The Compounding Illegibility Problem":

> "The claim that knowledge compounds *like* capital requires justification. Three features suggest the analogy is structural, not merely metaphorical: (1) **Reinterpretation multiplies value** - later insights don't just add to earlier ones, they change their meaning retroactively, creating multiplicative rather than additive growth. (2) **Non-linear returns** - documented learning curves in complex domains (language acquisition, mathematical expertise) show accelerating rather than linear progress, consistent with compounding. (3) **Path dependence** - the order in which insights are acquired matters, suggesting multiplicative interaction rather than simple accumulation. However, this remains an *analogy* requiring empirical validation—see § Unresolved Question: Domain Specificity."

---

**F12: Hedging Fog - Rhetorical Padding**

Several instances of unnecessary hedging that don't reflect genuine epistemic uncertainty:

- "This could reflect ordinary human impatience" → "This *might* reflect..." (The essay goes on to reject this explanation, so "could" is false uncertainty.)
- "The alternative hypothesis examined here" → "The hypothesis examined here" (No need for "alternative" qualifier in the introduction.)
- "If temporal position creates genuine epistemic constraints" → "If temporal position creates epistemic constraints" (The word "genuine" is doing no work—either they're constraints or they're not.)

**Repair:** Cut rhetorical hedges that don't reflect actual uncertainty. Preserve hedges that do (e.g., "likely reflects," "suggests," "may indicate").

---

**F07: Non-Sequitur - Evolutionary Argument Overreach**

In § "The Perceptual Immediacy Constraint":

> "Natural selection optimized for reproductive success within organism lifespans. For most of human evolutionary history, planning beyond a few years provided minimal fitness advantage—you might not survive that long, and conditions would change unpredictably."

**Issue:** This is plausible evolutionary psychology, but it's presented as if it follows necessarily from the neural architecture evidence. It doesn't. The neural architecture could have decade-scale limitations for *many* reasons (developmental constraints, metabolic costs, etc.). The evolutionary story is *one possible explanation*, not the only one.

**Repair:** Reframe as hypothesis rather than conclusion:

> "One evolutionary explanation: Natural selection optimized for reproductive success within organism lifespans. If planning beyond a few years provided minimal fitness advantage during most of human evolutionary history, selection pressure for decade-scale temporal reasoning would have been weak. However, alternative explanations (developmental constraints, metabolic costs of maintaining long-term temporal models) could also account for the observed neural architecture. The evolutionary story is plausible but not uniquely determined by the evidence."

---

**F04: Cherry-Picking - Missing Denominator (PhD Abandonment)**

The essay cites PhD abandonment rates peaking in years 3-5 as evidence for the "abandonment at inflection point" pattern. But it doesn't address the *base rate* question: What percentage of PhDs who reach year 3 eventually complete? If 80% complete, the "abandonment peak" is a small minority. If 40% complete, it's a major pattern.

**Repair:** Add context in § "Project Abandonment Timing":

> "Analysis of PhD dissertation completion rates (Council of Graduate Schools, 2008) shows abandonment peaks in years 3-5... **[VERIFICATION NEEDED: What is the overall completion rate? If most students who reach year 3 eventually complete, the abandonment peak represents a minority pattern. If completion rates are low, this is a majority pattern. The strength of the evidence depends on this denominator.]**"
