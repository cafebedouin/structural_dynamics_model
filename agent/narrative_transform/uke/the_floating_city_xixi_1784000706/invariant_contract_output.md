## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant:** This world stands on a founding violence — a deliberate suspension of the return-law that governs the rest of the land — committed once by named founders for reasons that made survival sense, then folded into ritual and soil until it reads as the first fact of nature rather than the first crime. If the story ever resolves into a correctable policy error awaiting a wiser hand, the invariant has been lost.

**Substrate:** The Long Winter Line — a walked, sung, re-consecrated boundary below which the land does not return what is given to it the way land is supposed to. Its instruments (soil-readers, planting-priests, the annual walking) report faithfully and cannot see that the Line was drawn, not discovered.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You walk the Line every spring with your neighbors, singing the words that make it holy, and in the cellar under the granary there is a book, older than the song, that remembers the Line being drawn.*

**Missing-floor invariant, restated with falsifier:** A society founded on an arbitrary suspension of natural law, normalized as permanent condition. Falsifier: if a wiser authority could simply undo it and restore the "true" order, the floor was lost.

**Subordinate grain (present but not primary):** A shared groundlessness — felt below the Line by everyone, powerful and powerless alike, as a private vertigo, never a public fact. Falsifier: if any official diagnosis of this feeling survives contact with the feeling itself, the grain was lost. *This grain gets one on-screen beat where it recedes and the floor-question replaces it — see MarASz's confession scene below.*

## Setting

The year is reckoned locally as **the two hundred and eleventh spring since the Drawing** — no other calendar is kept below the Line. This is not medieval Europe, not feudal Japan, not any fishing village: it is an inland river-delta polity of grain-tenant farmers and canal-wardens, displaced to a wholly invented geography — the **Tallow Delta**, a silt basin fed by a single, slow, brown river called the **Ashmother**, whose floods are the only wealth the Delta has ever had. The culture draws loosely and combinatorially from Low-Countries polder engineering, Mesopotamian canal-bureaucracy, and West African griot-lineage record-keeping — a blend specific enough that no single real-world analogue maps onto it. Governing institution: not a church, not a corporation, not a monarchy — a **Ledger-Council of Wardens**, hereditary custodians of the Line's song and the canal locks, who are neither aristocrats nor elected officials but something closer to a guild of ritual engineers.

Two hundred eleven years ago, a catastrophic flood-failure killed most of the founding population. The council that survived it — seven households, remembered now only as **the Seven Hands** — made a choice: they broke the old inheritance-law, which said land returns to common redistribution every generation, and declared instead that whoever held land *at the moment of the flood* would hold it and pass it directly to blood-heirs, forever, because redistribution mid-catastrophe would have meant renegotiating every field boundary while people starved. It was a triage decision. It saved lives that spring. It has never been undone. Two centuries of song, liturgy, and soil-science now insist that land simply *is* inherited this way below the Line — that the river itself "chose" the holding families — and that above the Line, in the old redistributive country, the soil is different, poorer, un-blessed. The soil is not different. The Line is a fence around a decision, wearing the face of a fact.

Tenant-workers below the Line — the **silt-hands** — work land they can never own, owned by **holding-families** whose claim traces to the Seven Hands, under the administration of the Ledger-Council, whose wardens genuinely maintain the locks and channels that keep the whole Delta from drowning. The locks are real. The flood-math is real. The coordination is not a lie. But the holding-families' grip on the best silt terraces, passed hand to hand for two hundred years while silt-hands are re-leased their own worked plots at rising toll each spring, is the asymmetric cost riding inside the genuine coordination.

## Constraint Naturalizations

### C₁ → **The Toll of the Fields** (foundational_precarity)

What it actually is: the base condition of being born a silt-hand — you owe a rising annual toll on the plot your family has worked for generations, calculated by the Ledger-Council's soil-readers according to "what the river gives," and the toll is never survivable to refuse. This is not enforced by soldiers; no one polices it. It is simply how much is left after the toll that determines whether you eat through winter, and everyone below the Line has always known this the way they know rain is wet.

Local term: **"the standing debt"** — not owed to a person, owed to the ground itself, as everyone below the Line understands it.

In use: A silt-hand named **Odrun Vask** (X₁) watches the soil-reader chalk the season's toll-mark on the granary door and does not ask why it rose again. His daughter asks. He tells her: *"The ground gives what the ground gives, and takes back its own after. Don't quarrel with the river."*

Per-character experience:
- **Odrun Vask (X₁, χ=0.72, Snare):** identity-locked — he has never imagined himself as anything but a silt-hand; the toll is not a policy to him, it is the shape of being alive. He cannot see it as separable from himself.
- **Marasz Ilendrë (X₂, χ=0.83, Snare):** the sharpest reader among the silt-hands, apprenticed briefly to a soil-reader before being sent back to the fields for asking too many questions about the flood-year records. She *sees* the standing debt is arithmetic, not weather — and seeing it does not lower it by one measure of grain.
- **Kest Vask (X₃, χ=0.72, Snare):** Odrun's cousin, young, still able to imagine leaving for the river-ports upstream — but leaving means forfeiting the family's claim entirely, which is its own kind of death to people whose whole worth is bound to a plot.

### C₂ → **The Lock-Rite and the Warden's Cut** (compensatory_production)

What it actually is: the canal-lock system genuinely prevents the Ashmother from drowning the Delta every spring — this is real, necessary, coordinated labor, requiring the Ledger-Council's engineering knowledge and the silt-hands' physical maintenance labor together. But the toll for lock-maintenance is assessed asymmetrically: silt-hands pay in labor-days that count against their own planting season, while holding-families pay in stored grain they have surplus of regardless. The coordination is real. The cost is not shared evenly, and the unevenness is dressed as tradition ("the Wardens' Cut has always been taken this way").

Local term: **"the Wardens' Cut"** — spoken of by silt-hands with a specific, exhausted, non-metaphorical bitterness; spoken of by holding-families as simply "upkeep."

Per-character experience:
- **Odrun Vask (X₁, χ=0.48, Tangled Rope):** genuinely needs the locks to hold — his own fields would drown without them — and cannot separate the real protection from the toll it costs him. He maintains the locks himself, calloused hands, because the alternative is worse.
- **Marasz Ilendrë (X₂, χ=0.55, Tangled Rope):** understands precisely how the labor-day assessment is weighted against silt-hands versus holding-families, and can describe it exactly — and still shows up to lock-duty every spring, because refusing collapses the whole system on everyone, herself included.
- **Kest Vask's holding-family cousin, warden-apprentice Thessaly Marrow (X₃, χ=0.29, Naturalized):** a holding-family daughter training into the Ledger-Council, genuinely believes the Wardens' Cut is fair because she has never once carried a labor-day quota that competed with her own planting. Her low personal cost isn't innocence — it's the asymmetry doing its work quietly on her, too, deflecting the extraction she never has to notice.

### C₃ → **The Drawing and Its Heirs** (authority_inversion)

What it actually is: the founding suspension itself, now generations deep. The original Seven Hands broke the old redistribution law once, in genuine crisis. Their direct descendants — the holding-families — now sit in the Ledger-Council not through active tyranny but through inherited position so old it has stopped looking like position at all. One silt-hand family, trapped at the bottom of two centuries of compounding toll, faces total loss of their plot this generation. One holding-family heir, meanwhile, has begun quietly buying up defaulted plots at "fair Council price" from families exactly like the first — a legal, sanctioned, generationally-inherited maneuver that requires no wrongdoing on her part at all, only position.

Local term: no name for the mechanism as a whole — silt-hands call the compounding failure **"going under the toll"**; holding-families call their own accumulating advantage simply **"holding true."**

Per-character experience:
- **Odrun Vask (X₁, χ=0.84, Snare):** this spring, the toll finally exceeds what the Vask plot can produce. There is no exit. He is not being punished by any person — the numbers simply arrived at this point, as they were always going to, two hundred years on a compounding schedule no one alive designed.
- **Thessaly Marrow (X₃, χ=-0.11, Rope):** inherits the Ledger-Council seat her mother is retiring from this same spring, and with it the standing right to acquire forfeited plots at Council-set price — a right she did not create, does not feel as extraction, and will very likely exercise on the Vask plot specifically, because it is adjacent to her family's holding and the Council price is, after all, fair by the only measure she's ever been taught.

## Character Roles (Positions)

**Odrun Vask** (X₁) — silt-hand, fourth-generation tenant of the Vask terrace, forty-one years old, wife dead of river-fever, one daughter (Sennet, twelve). Owns: a hand-cart, two seed-chests, a single iron toll-scale inherited from his grandfather that he is not permitted to use to check the soil-reader's figures. Material daily rhythm: rises before light, works the terrace and the locks in the same season, has never traveled further than the next lock-station upstream.

**Marasz Ilendrë** (X₂) — silt-hand, mid-thirties, briefly a soil-reader's apprentice as a girl (sent back after "asking about the flood-year books"), now works a family terrace and unofficially does sums for half the silt-hands in her lock-district, for grain rather than coin. Sharp, exhausted, watched.

**Thessaly Marrow** (X₃) — holding-family heir, twenty-six, warden-apprentice completing her final rite before taking her mother's Council seat. Has genuinely worked the locks alongside silt-hands every spring of her apprenticeship, believes this makes her fair-minded, has never once faced a toll she could not pay.

**Sennet Vask** — Odrun's daughter, the questioning child, positioned to inherit either the standing debt or, if things go badly this spring, nothing at all.

## Linguistic Strategy: **Direct Translation** (with embedded Naming stratum)

Source register: an invented but internally consistent Delta-dialect rendered in plain, granular English — agricultural, hydraulic, liturgical vocabulary, no contractions in ritual speech, heavy contractions in field speech (marking register-switch between sacred and mundane registers of the *same* language, rather than code-switching between two languages).

Concepts with no clean English equivalent, translated directly and defined in use:
- **"holding true"** — to possess something by right so old it no longer requires justification; used with pride by holding-families, with weary irony by silt-hands ("the river holds true to no one, but the Council holds true to itself")
- **"going under the toll"** — the specific, terminal state of a family's plot debt exceeding what the plot can ever again produce; spoken as a diagnosis, almost medical, never as blame
- **"the standing debt"** — owed to the ground, not to any person; grammatically it takes no possessive ("I owe the standing debt," never "I owe *him* the standing debt")

Naming: silt-hand families carry short, hard, single-syllable-rooted surnames tied to old riverbed features (Vask, from a word for silt-bar); holding-families carry surnames commemorating the Seven Hands' founding acts (Marrow, from "one who first marked the ground"). This distinction is load-bearing and instantly legible within the world without ever being explained by the narration.

Example of register-switch in use:
> Ritual (Line-walking day): *"We do not ask the river its reasons. We ask only that it hold to what it has always held."*
> Field speech (same character, an hour later): *"Gods, my back. Tell Sennet I'll be at the west channel till dark, and if the toll-mark's up again this year I don't want to hear it till I've eaten."*

**Ω_E01:** Should the Line be a literal geographic boundary or purely administrative? → Resolved: literal, walked, physically marked — a levee-ridge that also functions as the real flood-boundary, so the founding lie is welded to genuine hydraulic necessity and cannot be dismissed as pure superstition. → *Impact: strengthens the affordance test — the "true value" here is never a recoverable number, because the Line's realness (it does mark real flood risk) and its falseness (the inheritance-law bolted onto it) are inseparable in the world's own physics.*

**Ω_E02:** Does the cellar ledger (proof of the Drawing) survive into the story, or is it destroyed/mythologized before the narrative present? → Resolved: it survives, illegally, in Marasz's family's keeping — this is what she was "sent back" for finding as a girl. → *Impact: gives the floor invariant a concrete, discoverable object without making it a solvable mechanism — the ledger proves the founding choice was made, but proving it changes nothing about this spring's toll, which is the break-contract requirement: the problem stays psychologically live even when the historical fact is confirmed.*

**Ω_C01:** Is the Wardens' Cut asymmetry legally codified or purely customary? → Resolved: customary, never codified — no single document says silt-hands owe more labor-days proportionally; it has simply always been assessed this way, which is precisely why Thessaly can believe it's fair and Marasz can prove it isn't, using the same facts. → *Impact: preserves indexical variance as structural rather than a matter of one side lying.*

**Ω_C02:** Does Thessaly know about the cellar ledger? → Resolved: no, not yet — but her mother, the outgoing warden, does, and has kept it from her deliberately, believing ignorance makes a fairer administrator. → *Impact: seeds a future collision (Thessaly discovering what her inheritance actually rests on) without resolving it — consistent with Seeded Possibility.*
