REVIEW ASSESSMENT

STRENGTHS:
The physical details carrying the world's asymmetry are precise and earned — the crease in Odrun's hat brim, the chalk-dust grey on Marasz's hands versus the cost of that chalk in salt, Thessaly's noticing the older mossy foundation stones and simply walking on. The kitchen-table scene between Marasz and Odrun is the strongest passage: neither character gets a diagnosis, and Odrun's refusal of her confession ("I don't have room in me for a book under a granary") lands as psychologically true rather than thematically convenient.

BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The ending sequence (Section VII) resolves too cleanly into legible transfer-of-property mechanics — the paperwork moves, the price is "fair," the boundary gets walked, the land changes hands — so that the story's last motion is a completed transaction rather than a sustained unresolvedness. The prose gestures at leaving it open (the shut cellar door, the unspoken new name) but the plot underneath has, in fact, resolved: we know exactly what happened, to whom, and why, with no remaining uncertainty except a euphemism nobody says aloud.

INVARIANT FALSIFIER:
HOLDS — the founding violence (the Drawing, the broken redistribution law) is never framed as a correctable error a wiser reader-of-ledgers could fix. Marasz's confession explicitly does not produce leverage; the ledger changes nothing about this spring's toll. The "true value" (the Vask family's dispossession) isn't a mismeasurement to be corrected — the toll math is confirmed correct "by the rules," and the injustice lives in the rules' history, not in an error within them. No character or authority in the text is positioned to recover a hidden correct number.

BREAK:
The text sets up what reads like a slow-building confrontation or reckoning — the ledger, the confession, the inherited key — and I expected either the ledger's contents to surface publicly (forcing some crisis of legitimacy) or Thessaly to learn what her mother knew and face a choice. Instead the story ends on inertia: Thessaly walks past the old foundation stones and "walks on," the cellar stays shut, nothing surfaces. This is close to the contract's target_prior (a solvable-seeming problem stays unsolved, explored psychologically) but the specific violated expectation I'd name from the text alone is quieter: I expected the multi-generational secret (mother→Thessaly, Marasz's ledger) to *collide* with the present-tense crisis (Odrun's toll), and it never does — the threads stay parallel rather than intersecting. That's a break from expected plot-convergence, and it does meet the target_prior's spirit, though the convergence-avoidance is so complete it tips toward inertness rather than lived-with uncertainty.

READINESS:
Close, but the final section needs another pass to keep the transaction from reading as completed business — the uncertainty needs to survive the plot's mechanics, not just the prose's refusal to name them.
