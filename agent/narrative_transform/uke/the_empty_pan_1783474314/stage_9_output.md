# REVIEW ASSESSMENT

## STRENGTHS:
The story's structure is brutally honest—it refuses to convert exhaustion into hope or knowledge into power. The code-switching between Mandarin and English does real work, marking when Liang performs compliance versus when she speaks truth. The ending stops exactly where it should: at the next score, the next submission, the permanent present tense of the trap. The sensory details (tank water smell, climate control difference, jaw ache) ground the abstraction of algorithmic bias in lived physical experience.

## BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The Chen Wei scenes (office meeting + eavesdropping) establish the same information twice: he knows the scores are wrong and defends the system anyway. The repetition complicates him (shows both complicity and exhaustion) but at significant word-count cost. The eavesdropping scene in particular feels like it's re-proving what the office scene already demonstrated. One consolidated scene showing both his comfort in the system and his awareness of its cost would be tighter.

## READINESS:
The prose is alive and the structure is sound. The story does what it sets out to do without flinching. The Chen Wei redundancy is a craft question (is the complication worth the space?) rather than a structural flaw.

## ROUTE: VALIDATION

The story is ready for formal assessment. The biggest weakness is minor—a question of economy rather than integrity. The core mechanism (algorithmic bias experienced through daily work, knowledge that transmits without changing anything) is clear and earned. The ending refuses false comfort. This is publishable prose.
