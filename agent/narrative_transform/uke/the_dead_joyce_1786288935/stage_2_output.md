# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant.** This world contains a real — a grief held privately, without transaction or display, for someone irrecoverably gone — that the instruments of social life (reputation, performance, comparison, the currency of being seen to feel) cannot read, price, or borrow against. A person may carry such a grief and never once put it on the table, and its worth does not diminish for going unspent.

**Falsifier:** if the finished story contains a recoverable "true value" of that grief — a correct account of how much the dead should matter, which some character merely gets wrong or under-performs — the invariant was lost. The story must not resolve into *he should have grieved better/more/differently*. It must resolve into the discovery that grief held that way was never available for comparison in the first place.

**The substrate.** In this world it is called **the kept weight** — a private practice of carrying the memory of the dead by a fixed, wordless discipline (an object touched, a route walked, a silence observed at a specific hour) that confers nothing on anyone who sees it and *loses its nature entirely* the moment it is described, confessed, or offered as evidence of feeling. The instant it becomes visible as performance, it stops being the kept weight and becomes something else — a story about grief, not grief.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You touch the cold latch of the box every night at the hour she died, and you have never told anyone it is there, because the moment you did, it would become a thing you did for someone instead of a thing that is true.*

**The missing-floor invariant.** Beneath the marriage as an institution in this world sits an unexamined founding assumption: a wife's interior life is presumed to be held in trust for her husband, the way a household's accounts are held in trust for its head — not hers to keep sealed, but his to eventually audit. There is no neutral floor beneath this; no one asks by what right an interior life became property. **Falsifier:** if the story frames this presumption as one man's personal failing, correctable by his becoming a better, more attentive husband, the floor invariant was lost. It must remain visible as the ground the marriage stands on, not a defect in this particular building.

# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

**Setting.** A spa town in the Kingdom of Bohemia, late August 1898 — the last weeks of the "season" at a mineral-water resort favored by minor Austro-Hungarian gentry, retired officers, provincial lawyers, and their wives, who arrive each year to drink prescribed glasses of bitter water at the Colonnade, promenade at fixed hours, and conduct the year's engagements, rivalries, and courtships in the space of six weeks before the baths close and everyone returns to provincial obscurity for the winter. The town runs entirely on visibility: who walks with whom at the eleven o'clock promenade, who is received at whose villa, who is seen leaving the reading-room in animated conversation. It is a closed system of a few hundred people who will spend the next ten months describing this season to those who weren't there.

Among them: a retired cavalry officer of modest means and inflated bearing, his wife of twelve years, a widowed apothecary's widow who keeps a small pension-house near the upper spring, and the town's resident physician-of-fashion, who prescribes the waters and adjudicates, informally, half the town's status disputes through his waiting-room seating order.

**Constraint reference table.**

**C1 — the kept weight (invariant, not a Snare).** Naturalized as: the private discipline of grief described above. It is not enforced by anyone; it is simply true, the way the mineral spring's source is true whether or not the Colonnade attributes healing properties to it. No character administers it. It is discovered, not imposed.

**C2 — the Season.** Naturalized as: the fixed daily choreography of promenade, pump-room attendance, calling hours, and dinner seating that assembles the resort's entire social world twice a day. For the officer (moderate power, local host of his wife's performance, identity-locked to his rank), the Season is a genuinely coordinating structure — it is how introductions happen, how his standing is maintained, how the year's business gets done — but it also costs him constant, exhausting self-presentation he cannot set down. For the apothecary's widow, who runs the pension-house and depends on the officer's circle for her season's income and her only local respectability, the same Season is a trap with no slack in it: one misstep in performance, one whispered irregularity, and her livelihood for the year is gone. She smiles at the pump-room at seven every morning whether or not she has slept.

**C3 — the marriage.** Naturalized as: the presumption, unstated and unquestioned by anyone including her, that the officer's wife's inner life is his ledger to keep. From his position this is simply what marriage *is* — a coordinating institution, unremarkable, even generous, since he provides her standing, her house, her place at the table. From her position it is a Snare with almost nothing left outside it: she has no income, no name apart from his, and no legal or social exit that does not cost her everything the Season has built. When she is later discovered to hold something of her own — a private, wordless grief for a man who died before her marriage, never spoken of, never used — it does not renegotiate her situation. It only reveals, retroactively, that what he called knowing her was never anything but an inventory taken from outside a locked room.

**Character positions (variable mapping).**

- **X1 → Rittmeister Anton Vesely**, retired cavalry officer, mid-fifties, moderate power in the town's status economy, host of the season's central performance, identity-locked to a rank and reputation he can no longer materially afford but must continue to display.
- **X2 → Klára Vesely**, his wife, late thirties, powerless within the marriage's economy, trapped by identity and dependency, carrying a private grief for a young officer she loved and lost before her marriage — a grief she has never once mentioned to her husband in twelve years.
- **X3 → Frau Немцová (Ludmila Nemcová)**, the widowed pension-house keeper, powerless and trapped economically within the Season's rhythm, moderate-power host's counterpart on the service side — her livelihood depends entirely on continued good performance within a system she does not control and cannot exit without ruin.
- **G1 → Dr. Reiner**, the resort physician, moderate power, institutional host of the Season's rituals (he prescribes the schedule the whole town obeys), identity-locked to his professional role, genuinely providing a coordinating function (health, order, occasion) while also extracting constant deference and unpaid social labor from those who depend on his goodwill for their standing.

**Linguistic strategy: Historical register (late Habsburg spa-town German-Czech bilingual gentility, rendered in English).**

Formal address dominates public space — third-person constructions, professional titles used even in private thought ("the Rittmeister felt," never "Anton felt," in his own interior monologue when performing). Grammatical feature: a proliferation of conditional and subjunctive hedges in dialogue ("One might suppose," "It would perhaps not be improper to mention") that thin to direct statement only in the rare private scenes — a marker readers learn to feel before they can name it. Czech surfaces only in service exchanges (Frau Nemcová with her cook, a porter's aside) — never in gentry dialogue, which stays in a stiffly correct German-inflected English — marking who performs for whom.

*Example, public register:* "The Rittmeister's lady was, one understood, much occupied this season with her correspondence." (Nobody asks with whom.)

*Example, private register — the only place grammar goes plain:* "She touched the latch. Eight o'clock. She did not think his name. She had stopped needing to."

**Inhabitation sentences.**

- **Anton:** "You have built twelve years of standing on being a man whose wife is content, and it has never once occurred to you to ask her, because asking would mean admitting there was ever a question."
- **Klára:** "You smile at the pump-room at seven and at his dinner table at eight, and at nine, alone, you touch a cold brass latch and are, for one minute, entirely unowned."
- **Ludmila:** "You have thirty more mornings to be liked well enough that you eat this winter, and liking costs more than anyone who has never had to be liked for money will ever understand."
- **Substrate (Klára, closest to it):** "You touch the cold latch of the box every night at the hour she died, and you have never told anyone it is there, because the moment you did, it would become a thing you did for someone instead of a thing that is true." *(reused verbatim from Section 0 — the seed both sections must carry identically.)*

# SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

**RESOLVED**

- **Ω_E01:** Did an 1898 Bohemian spa season plausibly run on daily fixed-hour choreography (promenade, pump-room, calling hours) rigid enough to carry C2's coordination-plus-enforcement structure? → Resolution: yes, well-documented feature of Habsburg-era spa towns (Karlsbad, Marienbad, Franzensbad); the "season" ran on published schedules with real social penalties for absence or misstep. → Impact: setting adopted without strain; no invented mechanism needed.

- **Ω_E02:** Would a pension-house keeper's economic survival plausibly depend on a single season's social performance to this degree? → Resolution: yes — seasonal resort economies concentrated a full year's income into six-to-eight weeks; off-season the town emptied. → Impact: confirms X3's Snare-classification (χ=0.84) as materially inevitable, not authorially imposed.

- **Ω_C03:** Should the kept weight (C1) be given any physical enforcement or discovery mechanism (a diary, a locket found by another)? → Resolution: no — any external discovery mechanism turns the invariant into evidence, i.e., a value someone could have read correctly with better instruments. The kept weight must be disclosed only by Klára's own choice, and even then, disclosure ends the practice rather than confirming its content. → Impact: TR1's precondition ("disclosure must be involuntary/unprompted by C2's social performance logic") is honored — she must choose the one moment C2's logic is not watching.

- **Ω_C04:** Does Dr. Reiner's role risk over-instrumentalizing C2 into something numerically scored (patient counts, prescribed glasses of water as a quantified regimen)? → Resolution: keep the water-cure prescriptions present but never denominated on the page — no glass-counts, no dosage figures recur in narration. Reiner's authority is social (whom he receives, whom he keeps waiting), not arithmetic. → Impact: Scored-Snare gate respected; no `inherent_instrument` flag was passed, so no numeric Snare is permitted regardless of setting plausibility.

**UNRESOLVED**

- **Ω_P05:** Should Anton's terminal state (the TR2/TR3 ambiguity — genuine re-indexing versus aestheticized absorption of Klára's grief into a new performance) be left visibly open on the page, or should the prose lean toward one reading while preserving technical ambiguity? → Recommendation: leave visibly open — write his final scene so that his silence is compatible with both a man who has finally gone quiet enough to feel something outside himself, and a man already composing the story he will tell about having felt it. Do not resolve for the reader. → Awaiting Stage 4/5 decision, as this is the story's terminal ambiguity and belongs to execution, not to worldbuilding.

- **Ω_P06:** Should Ludmila (X3/C2's Snare-index) and Klára (X2/C3's Snare-index) ever directly compare positions on the page, given they occupy structurally analogous traps under different constraints? → Recommendation: at most one brief, oblique scene (a transaction at the pension-house desk) where each recognizes something in the other without naming it — full comparison risks flattening two distinct constraints into one generic "women trapped" theme. → Awaiting Stage 3/4 judgment on scene economy.
