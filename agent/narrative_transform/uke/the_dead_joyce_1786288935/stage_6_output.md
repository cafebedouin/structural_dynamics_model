STRATEGY BRIEF

IMPLIED AUTHOR: A close, formal ironist in the vein of late-period Trevor or early Wharton — someone who trusts the physical detail (cold brass, blue glass-light, a maid finding an unaccustomed glass by a cold hearth) to carry the weight the narration currently insists on stating. This author notices thresholds and temperatures, tracks two competing ledgers (social capital, private feeling) without editorializing on either, and believes an ending should close the ritual, not annotate its meaning.

AUDIENCE: Readers of quiet literary fiction who enjoy Trevor, Pym, or Brookner — comfortable with restraint, alert to irony that is never underlined, willing to sit with an ending that withholds resolution rather than explains it.

SCOPE: Short story, target 3,200–3,900 words (trim, don't expand). Ceiling well inside the 11,468-word feasible range. World-building density stays exactly where it is — the spa-town social machinery is sufficient and shouldn't be built out further. Ending resolution stays irresolute (Klára not knowing if she'll open the drawer again) — this is correct and should not be pushed toward either hope or despair.

CREATIVE DEEPENING: The story's real subject is not the dead man or the marriage but *what happens to a private practice once it acquires an audience* — the way naming a ritual changes its ontological status even though no fact about it has changed. This is already present in the latch's failure at the end; it simply needs to be trusted rather than restated. Additional deepening available: Anton's own half-formed impulse to *tell* the story to Reiner (turning his wife's grief into social currency that reflects well on him) is a second, smaller instance of the same corruption-by-exposure the main plot dramatizes, and could be sharpened as a quiet echo rather than left as a passing aside — this doubles the theme without adding a word of explanation.

FLINCH POINTS:
1. Klára's confession speech ("I touch the latch at a fixed hour and that is the whole of it... because the moment I did, it would stop being what it is"): This states the story's thesis in the character's own mouth, pre-empting the ending's dramatization of the same idea. Honesty requires trusting the confession to be partial, halting, and incomplete — she should say enough to break the ritual without articulating *why* breaking it matters; the mechanism should be demonstrated only once, at the drawer, not explained in dialogue first.
2. The narration's gloss immediately after ("felt the discipline of all those years turn, mid-sentence, from a private fact into an account of a private fact"): This is the same insight restated in authorial voice. Honesty requires cutting this sentence outright or replacing it with a physical/behavioral correlate (what her hands do, what she doesn't say next) rather than a second abstraction.
3. The closing paragraph's final line ("It was she who had changed the terms, simply by having once, in a doorway, said the thing aloud"): A third restatement. The scene of the ordinary, warmed brass already *is* this insight — dramatized, complete, correctly withheld from comment. Honesty requires ending on the sensory fact (or one beat past it) and cutting the explanatory clause entirely.
4. Minor: Anton's interior paragraph about "no entry for this in any ledger he had ever kept" risks being a fourth iteration of the same move applied to his side of the story — worth checking against the cut pass, though it's doing slightly different work (his blindness, not the mechanism itself) and may survive.

PATTERN CHECK: Concern, but not the usual one. This isn't a case of the pipeline reaching for systemic-evil-resolved-through-organization — there's no villain and none is needed, and the review confirms the invariant holds (no correct amount of grief is ever assigned). The failure mode here is explanatory redundancy, not thematic softening: the story already achieves the harder, more honest thing (irresolution, cost without correction) and then undercuts its own nerve by explaining it three times, as if it didn't trust the dramatization to land. The fix is subtractive, not structural.
