## 2. Voice Archetype Selection

Three POV threads were fixed in Stage 2 (Ω_C04). Each requires independent voice justification.

### Maren — **The Condemned**, with a diachronic partial-shift toward **Witness-adjacent awareness** (not a full archetype change — see note below)

- Maren's index moves from moderate/generational/identity-locked toward exposure (Tessa's ledger), per TR1's index-change pattern — but TR1 is explicitly blocked by generational time-horizon reinforcement unless *external disruption* intervenes. Tessa is that external disruption.
- **Justification:** Condemned is the correct base archetype — Maren experiences the constraint as natural ("bad luck") for the majority of her arc, cannot see the systemic shape, acts rationally within her position. Attractor compatibility: ✓ Tragedy per the archetype table.
- **Critical constraint:** Maren must NOT fully convert into a Builder or Witness. Stage 1's own TR1 entry shows the reindexing stalls at Tangled Rope→Tangled Rope (magnitude shift only, 0.60→0.69) — she gets *closer* to seeing the boundary, not across it. Her voice should gain vocabulary for what happened to her without gaining the power to act on it. This is Condemned-with-partial-sight, not Condemned-becoming-Witness. The tragedy is that seeing clearly and having power are decoupled.
- **POV:** First person, close. The register must stay experiential even after exposure — she does not become analytical in *voice*, only in what she now permits herself to notice.

### Kell — **The Condemned** (pure, unshifting)

- Kell is "trapped," biographical horizon, no reindexing event in his transformation rules. He is the network's stable Condemned — the felt cost that never resolves into insight, only into loss (wheel seized).
- **Justification:** Pure Condemned per the archetype spec. Attractor: ✓ Tragedy. His function is to hold the inhabitation spec's rawest register — body, brake-rope, silence — uncontaminated by analytical vocabulary, so the reader has an uncorrupted baseline against which Maren's partial shift and Voss's institutional language both read as distortions.
- **POV:** First or close-third, present-adjacent immediacy. No retrospective frame — Kell narrates from inside the loss, not after it.

### Voss — **The Zealot**, inverted register (reformist zealotry, not punitive zealotry)

- Voss is not the System (he has an "I," six years of biographical labor, genuine belief) and not the Builder (he isn't testing for leverage — he believes he has already found the fix). He is closest to **the Zealot**: internalizes the constraint logic — here, codification-as-protection — as moral imperative. His negative χ is the structural signature of the Zealot's sincerity, not the Beneficiary's comfort (he does not benefit personally; he *believes*).
- **Justification:** Stage 1 rationale explicitly states Voss's action is "well-intentioned," consistent with BIR satisficing, and the archetype table requires PIR + high purity + theater_ratio > 0.4 for the Zealot — reconsider: Voss's model is BIR, not PIR. **This is a deliberate cross-grain choice, flagged as a risk, not a violation.** The Zealot archetype's *requires* line is a guardrail against impossible pairings, not an absolute lock (per the elasticity principle). Voss under BIR-Zealot produces something the pure PIR-Zealot cannot: tragedy through sincerity-that-turns-out-to-be-error, rather than tragedy through devotion-that-was-always-extraction. This matches Ω_C03's resolution exactly (good-faith actor, not hypocrite) and the Type IV error classification (E2) precisely — his zealotry is procedural faith in ink over silence, not sacred fervor.
- **Stage 4 directive carried over:** Voss must not waver. His certainty is structural. Any scene where he doubts collapses him into Condemned-approaching-realization, which is a different (unselected) arc.
- **POV:** Third limited. First person risks the reader over-sympathizing past the point where his blindness reads as tragic rather than merely earnest; third limited holds him at the small critical distance needed for the reader to see what he cannot.

### Tessa — catalyst/secondary, minor **Witness** fragment (not a full POV thread)

- Per Ω_C04, Tessa is not a full POV. She appears through Maren's thread as the agent of exposure. Where she does get direct interiority (brief, minor), it should read as Witness register: documentary, comparative, clinical — she is the only character who can hold fifty households' data at once, and her horror is precisely that this comparative sight changes nothing structurally.

**Master diagnostic check:** Does each voice let the reader see what the character cannot?
- Maren: reader sees the full arithmetic (via Tessa's catalytic function) before Maren can act on it — the gap between seeing and power is the engine.
- Kell: reader sees the Charter's procedural violence in real time; Kell only feels the seizure.
- Voss: reader sees (via Kell/Maren threads, structurally juxtaposed) that his ink *is* the theft; Voss cannot access this from within his own scenes.

## 3. Indexical Revelation Strategy

**☑ Multi-POV Parallel**, with one embedded **Gradual Realization** arc (Maren) inside the larger parallel structure.

Justification: Stage 1's diagnostic case is explicitly the C2 triple-point (X3 Snare / X4 Rope / X2 Snare) and C3's binary sign-inversion (X5 Snare / X6 Rope). This maximal indexical variance is best served by letting the reader hold multiple simultaneous, internally-consistent indices and perform the comparison the text refuses to perform for them (per the Double-Blind combination strategy). Late Revelation is rejected — a single-POV-until-the-end structure would require an artificial withholding that fights the material's actual shape, which is *already* parallel. Analytical Observer is rejected — no character in the finalized cast (Tessa is secondary, not primary) is positioned to hold and narrate the full variance explicitly; that would collapse the tragedy into essay.

The Maren thread carries its own Gradual Realization *within* the Multi-POV frame — this is the "both registers without diluting either" solution from Ω_C04.

## 4. Editorial Decisions

```
LENGTH: Short (2000-5000) — leaning upper end (~4500-5000)
  Justification: three POV threads require enough real estate for each
  to breathe, but novelette length risks diluting the tragic compression
  Deterministic Tragedy needs. Short-form keeps the collision tight.

POV: Third Limited (rotating, hard section breaks between threads —
  not head-hopping within scenes)
  - Maren: close third, first-person-adjacent interiority
  - Kell: close third, present-tense immediacy within past-tense frame
  - Voss: close third, held at slightly greater distance (per voice
    justification above)
  Rejected: First person for all threads (would flatten the distance
  needed for Voss); Omniscient (destroys the indexical-variance
  engine — the reader must assemble the comparison across sections,
  not receive it pre-assembled by an all-seeing narrator).

TENSE: Past throughout.
  Justification: Deterministic Tragedy is retrospective by nature —
  the ending (standing Charter) is a settled fact the prose moves
  toward, not an open question. Present tense would falsely suggest
  contingency the attractor selection has already foreclosed.

CHARACTER COUNT: 4 on-page (Maren, Kell, Voss, Tessa as secondary/
  catalyst), 1 named-but-offstage (Doyle, referenced/quoted, appears
  in at most one short scene as friction for Maren, not a full thread)

NAMING: Cultural, per Stage 2 table.
  Old ridge families (Maren, Ambrose) vs. Warden-import surnames
  (Doyle, Voss, Ferrow) — the naming itself carries the C1 history
  wordlessly, exactly as designed. No additional invented names needed.

LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:
  Code-switching occurs precisely where Stage 2 specifies: lower-catch
  characters switch into "upper-tongue" only under bailiff pressure,
  and the switch itself is legible as capitulation-to-register, not
  agreement. This must be staged at least once in the Kell thread
  (the wheel-seizure scene is the natural site) and once, inverted,
  in Maren's thread — she uses upper-tongue deliberately, for the
  first time, in a late scene, and the prose should let the reader
  feel this as loss even though it reads superficially like empowerment.
  This is the clearest possible dramatization of TR1's stall: she
  gains the *language* of analytical exit without gaining the exit
  itself.

EMOTIONAL CORE: Not "injustice" (too abstract, risks theme-naming).
  The core is: the specific grief of discovering that something you
  called fate was actually arithmetic — and that this discovery
  changes nothing about your power to act, only what you must now
  carry while acting powerlessly. This must never be stated. It is
  carried entirely by the Maren thread's structure (sees clearly,
  can still only lose).

ENDING STRATEGY: ☑ Equilibrium established (Charter stands) +
  ☑ System persists, agent removed (Kell's wheel, seized, stands
  empty at the story's close; Maren's late upper-tongue scene shows
  the Charter's total, uncontested victory).
  Explicitly NOT "attractor reached via character epiphany" — no
  character delivers a summarizing realization. The reader assembles
  the tragedy; no one on-page states it.
```

## 5. Primary Physical Marker

**The brake-rope**, per Stage 2's substrate inhabitation sentence — specifically, **the specific worn smoothness of rope-fiber under a particular hand**, contrasted against **the gauge's brass dial**, per the Counterpoint Voice strategy.

- Kell and Maren's hands read the rope's wear as inherited knowledge (four/three generations of palms).
- Voss and Doyle read the gauge's dial as legible fact.
- The two objects never resolve into a shared unit. This is the load-bearing physical detail carrying the invariant contract: rope-wear cannot be transcribed into the gauge's number without ceasing to be what it was (per Ω_E01's resolution — the Long Breath is never shown as a complete, verifiable performance).

Secondary marker, used sparingly per Ω_E02: **the four-foot figure** (Doyle's line, "four foot under the Line") — appears exactly once or twice, always spoken aloud by a character with positional access, in an act of power, never in narration.
