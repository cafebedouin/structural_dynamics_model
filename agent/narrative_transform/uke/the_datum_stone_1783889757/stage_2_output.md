# STAGE 2: NATURALIZATION OUTPUT

## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant, as commitment + falsifier:**
The world contains a real — the shared, unspoken rhythm by which people take turns drawing on a common necessity — that no gauge, ledger, or certified instrument can read without destroying it. The instant anyone measures it, marks it, or writes it into a right, it stops being the mutual thing it was and becomes property. **Falsifier:** if the finished story contains a recoverable "true value" for how the turning *should* go — a correct schedule the system merely botched — the invariant has been lost.

**The substrate that holds it, in the world's own terms:**
*The Long Breath* — the wind community's name for the unwritten sequence by which the great cliffside sailwheels are allowed to catch air, one after another, in an order nobody wrote down and everybody nonetheless knows. It lives in the body — in the timing of a grandmother's hands releasing a brake-rope, in the specific quiet before a neighbor's wheel groans awake. It has no register. The moment the Ridgewardens built a gauge to "confirm" it, the Breath became a schedule, and the schedule became theirs.

**The substrate inhabitation sentence:**
*You stand at the brake-line at dawn with your palm on rope your mother's palm wore smooth, waiting for a silence that has never once needed a bell to tell you it has arrived — and forty feet above you, a brass gauge is already writing down a number that will replace your waiting with a debt.*

**The missing-floor invariant + falsifier:**
Beneath the whole apparatus of "fair wind-shares" sits a single arbitrary act: someone, once, drove a stake into the ridge and called the air above it *elevation zero*, and everything since has been measured as height *above that stake* — as though the stake were a fact of geology rather than a choice made by whoever happened to be standing there with a hammer. **Falsifier:** if the story frames the injustice as a bad measurement from an otherwise legitimate zero-point — correctable by moving the stake or refining the gauge — the floor invariant has been lost.

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT

### Setting Description

The Kessary Verge is a spine of coastal highlands where the wind is the only reliable wealth. For nine generations, households have built their sailwheels — timber-and-canvas turbines lashed to the cliff-face — at whatever height their family plot happens to occupy on the ridge. Height was never supposed to matter. The old households insist the wind belongs to everyone in equal turn, governed by the Long Breath, a sequence of unwritten deference passed down through the hands, not the mouth.

But forty years ago, the Ridgewardens — the regional authority that licenses timber, arbitrates land disputes, and collects the highland tithe — installed a certified wind-gauge on the escarpment called the Verge Line. The gauge measures elevation above a stake driven into the rock at the Verge's founding, back when the first surveyor simply chose a boulder as "sea-height zero" because it was convenient to his tripod. Wheels above the Verge Line are classified *upper-catch*; wheels below are *lower-catch*. Upper-catch households were granted, by the Wardens' charter, first right to any wind surge — a formal, licensed, tithe-backed priority. This is called Verge-right.

Before the gauge, height didn't determine turn order at all. The Long Breath did — an intricate, memorized sequence of deference in which lower wheels caught the gusts that upper wheels, by unspoken agreement, let pass. Nobody enforced it. It required no enforcement. It simply worked, the way a hundred people crossing a doorway without collision works, because everyone had learned the rhythm since childhood.

Verge-right did not replace the Long Breath cleanly. It layered on top of it, and it is winning. Upper-catch households now invoke their charter openly when a surge comes; lower-catch households still try to hold the old sequence, and where the two collide, the Wardens' bailiffs enforce Verge-right with fines, wheel-seizure, and in bad years, forced relocation below the tithe-line. The Long Breath is dying exactly where it collides with the gauge.

### Constraint Reference Table

**C1 (invariant substrate) → The Verge Line stake:** The founding choice — a surveyor's convenient boulder called "zero" — treated by everyone now as simply where height begins, rather than as a decision someone made for reasons no one remembers or questions.

**C2 → Verge-right (height-based wind priority):**
- *Old Maren* (X1, moderate power, generational horizon, tied to family plot by inheritance obligation, local scope) experiences it as: the wheel she inherited sits at "lower-catch" by four feet of rock she did not choose, and every strong wind season now, she watches the surge pass over her canvas into her neighbor's, and she has always called this bad luck, not policy.
- *Old Maren, reindexed* (same agent, having learned the gauge's history and audited its records) experiences it as: the same four feet of rock, but now she can see the Wardens' ledger, see that the stake was placed by a man being paid by the upper households in 1780, and the bad luck resolves into an arrangement.
- *Tessa Ferrow* (X2, an itinerant tithe-clerk, analytical exit, biographical horizon, local scope but trained to compare cases across the whole Verge) experiences it as: the sharpest version of the wrongness, because her ledger work lets her set fifty households' gauge-readings side by side and see the pattern Maren can only feel — that lower-catch households cluster below the tithe-line generation after generation, never by chance.
- *Warden-Clerk Assen Doyle* (X4, institutional power, identity-locked to his post, local scope) experiences it as: simply the charter working as designed — a clean, legible allocation rule that ended decades of dockside brawling over gusts, and he can point to the brawl records to prove it.

**C3 → The Verge Charter of Recorded Turns (codification of the Long Breath into law):**
- *Kell Ambrose* (X5, powerless, biographical horizon, trapped — his household cannot relocate without forfeiting ancestral tithe standing, local scope) experiences it as: the year the bailiffs came to enforce a "turn schedule" that claimed to be the Long Breath but wasn't — a paper version that happened to favor upper wheels at every disputed hour — and when he tried to hold the old sequence anyway, his wheel was seized for "unlicensed catch."
- *Warden-Surveyor Priam Voss* (X6, institutional power, immediate horizon, analytical exit, local scope) experiences it as: his life's proudest reform — he spent six years interviewing elders, transcribing the Long Breath into the Charter of Recorded Turns so that it could finally be *enforced fairly*, protecting lower households from being simply muscled aside by upper ones in disputes the old system had no teeth to settle.

### Linguistic Strategy: Direct Translation + Naming

The Verge has its own working vocabulary, translated but not explained:

- **"Catch"** — a household's turn to draw wind. Never "share," never "allocation." *"It's my catch"* not *"my percentage."*
- **"The Breath holds"** / **"The Breath's gone thin here"** — said of whether the old deference sequence is still respected in a given stretch of cliff.
- **"Reading the stake"** — slang for citing the Verge Line gauge in an argument; carries faint contempt, as in *"Don't read me the stake, Doyle, read me the wind."*
- **"Upper-tongue"** — the clipped, ledger-inflected way Wardens' clerks talk, full of clause and precedent. Lower-catch households code-switch into upper-tongue only when forced to argue with a bailiff, and everyone recognizes the switch as capitulation to the register, not agreement with its content.
- Names carry three generations of settlement history: old ridge families (Ambrose, Maren-family patronymics dropped generations back, now just "Maren" as both given and inherited) versus Warden-appointed administrative families with import surnames (Doyle, Voss, Ferrow) — marking, without a word of exposition, who arrived with the charter and who was there before it.

**Example in use:**
> "The gauge says you're four foot under the Line," Doyle said, not unkindly. "That's not a rule I made."
> "No," said Maren. "Somebody made it before you were born. You just read it to me like weather."

## SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

**RESOLVED:**

**Ω_E01:** Should the "informal system" (C4, deferred) be shown directly on-page or only through memory/testimony?
→ Resolution: Shown only through testimony and residual practice (Maren and Kell still attempting to hold it), never fully depicted as a functioning scene — this preserves its unrecoverability. If we showed the Long Breath working cleanly on-page, we'd implicitly certify a "correct" version of it, which reintroduces a measurable true value.
→ Impact: Scenes involving the Long Breath must always be partial, interrupted, or remembered — never a complete, verifiable performance.

**Ω_E02:** Does the Verge-right charter (C2/C3) need an explicit numeric threshold (feet above the stake) visible on-page?
→ Resolution: A number may appear, but only when a character with positional access (Doyle, Voss, or a bailiff) cites it in-scene as an act of power ("The gauge says you're four foot under"). It is never used in narration to describe height, wind, or fairness generally.
→ Impact: Satisfies the Scored-Snare gate's narration rule under the confirmed inherent_instrument flag — numbers are wielded, never ambient.

**Ω_C03:** Is Priam Voss a hypocrite or a tragic good-faith actor?
→ Resolution: Good-faith actor. His χ is negative at his own index; the story's tragedy requires that codification be undertaken by someone who believes it is purely protective. Making him cynical collapses the Type IV inversion into simple villainy.
→ Impact: Voss's scenes must show genuine belief and genuine six-year labor, not concealed self-interest.

**Ω_C04:** Does Tessa Ferrow (analytical index) need to be a fully separate POV, or can her function be folded into Maren's arc diachronically?
→ Resolution: Kept as a separate, minor POV rather than folded in — the specification's maximal indexical divergence at C2 (sign inversion between X4 and X2/X3) is best carried by two live consciousnesses in dialogue, not by one character's before/after. However, Maren's own arc (moderate → exposure to the ledger) will ALSO carry a diachronic shift, giving the story both registers without diluting either.
→ Impact: Three POV threads: Maren (diachronic shift within one person), Kell (trapped, unchanging, the felt cost), Voss (institutional, tragic good faith). Tessa appears as a catalyst/secondary rather than a fourth full POV, to avoid diffusing intimacy.

**Ω_P05:** Tonal register — should the prose lean elegiac (mourning the Long Breath as already lost) or procedural (following the mechanics of enforcement)?
→ Recommendation: Elegiac in the Maren/Kell threads, procedural-bureaucratic in the Voss/Doyle threads, with the collision between registers doing structural work — the reader should feel the Charter's paper-neatness grinding against the Breath's wordlessness as a texture, not a stated theme.
→ Awaiting: Confirmation at Stage 4 draft review; flagged as stylistic, not structural, so it does not block handoff.

**UNRESOLVED (flagged for downstream awareness, not blocking):**

**Ω_P06:** Whether the story's ending should show the Charter still standing (Tragedy fully realized, per Deterministic Tragedy selection) or show a rupture (wheel-seizure resistance escalating to open breach) as the terminal image.
→ Both are compatible with Deterministic Tragedy per the Stage 1 selection; the break contract requires that the ending NOT resolve into "the gauge gets fixed" — either a standing Charter or an open rupture satisfies this, but they produce different final tones (numb permanence vs. violent foreclosure). Left open for Stage 3/4 to decide based on chapter economy.
