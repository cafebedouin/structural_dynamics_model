STRATEGY BRIEF

IMPLIED AUTHOR: A patient, procedural sensibility — one who trusts bureaucratic texture (drawers, clauses, rotations, ledgers) to carry moral weight without editorializing. Notices the gap between institutional self-satisfaction and lived cost; skips melodrama in favor of flat administrative diction used as an instrument of violence. Ends on image, not statement — the dog whose restlessness "passed unremarked into the wind" is the register this author trusts. This sensibility must now hold its nerve through Section V rather than granting a working fix.

AUDIENCE: Readers of literary fiction attentive to systems and institutions — the kind of reader who enjoys Ferrante's bureaucratic textures or Ishiguro's compliant narrators, who wants injustice rendered through mechanism rather than confrontation, and who will notice (and feel cheated by) a plot-level contradiction between theme and event.

SCOPE: Target 3,800–4,600 words (current: 3,465; feasible ceiling 11,468). This is a revision, not an expansion — most sections hold. The needed work is concentrated in Section V and possibly a light touch to the Coda; growth should come from deepening what's already earned, not new material.

CREATIVE DEEPENING: The story's real subject is the difference between a parameter and a zero-point — between a system that can be petitioned within its own logic and a theft that predates and structures that logic. Every other section already dramatizes this correctly: Voss cannot see the stake because he only ever measured the room; Tessa's report is absorbed without a hole; Kell's rope hangs uncut-down because "there is no clause that requires it." Section V must join this pattern rather than break it. The deepening available: show Maren getting *exactly* what the Charter can give her — a real, signed, functioning allocation — and have the story hold steady in showing that this changes nothing about the zero-point. Not "it worked, but it felt hollow" (which concedes the falsifier and only adds regret on top), but "it worked, and here is precisely what winning within these terms cannot touch." The old knowing — the dog, the quiet, the pressure in the knees — should not almost-return-but-fail; it should be demonstrably a different kind of instrument than the gauge, one that cannot be adjudicated by Doyle at all, so that Maren's win and her loss are not sequential (win then vertigo) but simultaneous and incommensurable.

FLINCH POINTS:
1. **Doyle's "You're right. Deferred catch applies."** — The draft resolves the four-section indictment of the Charter by having the Charter, applied more attentively, correct itself for Maren. This dignifies the instrument the rest of the story discredits. Honesty requires that Maren's invocation of section three either (a) succeed narrowly and visibly on its own narrow terms — more canvas, more water, a real number — while the story refuses to let that success be legible as restoration of anything the ridge lost, or (b) be shown succeeding only because Maren has become fluent in a language that cannot express the thing she actually lost, so that the "win" is denominated in a currency that was never the right currency to begin with. Either way, the text must stop saying or implying "it worked" as a resolution beat — it should read as a transaction closing, not a wrong righted.

What honesty requires: the vertigo needs to be simultaneous with the transaction, not its aftermath — woven into the scene with Doyle itself, so the reader never gets a clean beat of triumph to later retract. The two should be inseparable in the same paragraph, even the same sentence.
