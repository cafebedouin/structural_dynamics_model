# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The floor invariant (primary):** In this world, a person's value and reality are defined entirely by their position within a formal hierarchy — there is no ground of selfhood outside the hierarchy's own reckoning, and the system that reckons rank was never built on a neutral zero; it was built on someone's founding decision about who counts. **Falsifier:** if the finished story frames the hierarchy as a correctable misjudgment of some prior, truer worth the characters already possessed — a soul the ledger merely mis-totals — the floor invariant was lost.

**The instrument stays honest.** This is a source where the missing floor is *inherently instrument-mediated*: the certified marker of rank is not a corrupt gauge misreading a real underneath it. The marker **is** the real, for every purpose the world transacts in. Remove the marker and the hierarchy does not persist in some purer form — it simply stops functioning. The scale weighs true. There is no truer weight underneath it to recover.

**The substrate that holds it:** the world's rank-markers — badges, seals, weighted braid, whatever the setting instantiates — are not disguises over a person. They are the only ground a person stands on. Selfhood without a marker is not hidden selfhood; it is no legible self at all.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You hold the seal that says what you are, and when it is not on you, you are not anyone the world's ledgers can find — not disguised, not hidden, simply absent from the only reckoning that exists.*

**Break address (not executed here, left open):** the world must not be built so that its rule-breaking automatically resolves into a moral lesson about a "recognizable human world" underneath the strangeness. The substrate must be strange enough, and functionally load-bearing enough, that a story set here could break its own physics purely for the vertiginous pleasure of the break — the door to allegory-refusal stays unlocked; this stage does not walk through it.

# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## Setting

The Sortworks of Anhedge Cistern — a vertical archive-city built into the shaft of a drained aquifer, four hundred years after the surface wars poisoned open ground and drove the surviving bureaucracies underground into the old water infrastructure. Nine hundred feet of spiraled stone gallery, wet-black with old mineral seep, lit by fish-oil lamps hung on iron brackets bored straight into the aquifer wall. The Cistern was never designed to hold people. It was designed to hold water, and now holds the last intact filing hierarchy on the continent, because the filing hierarchy is what didn't drown when everything else did.

Every soul in the Cistern wears a **seal-cord** — a braided collar, dyed and knotted according to registered rank, fastened by the Wardens of Tally at the Cistern's single Certifying Table on the third gallery. The cord is not a symbol of your rank. It is your rank, in the only sense the Cistern's law recognizes a rank to exist. Strip a person of the cord and every gate, ration-window, water-right, and marriage-registry in the Cistern treats them not as demoted but as *unfiled* — a category with no procedure attached, because no one ever wrote a procedure for a person the ledgers cannot find. The unfiled do not get punished. They get skipped, the way a scribe's eye skips a smudged line.

The Cistern runs on the **Tally**, a nine-tier cording system inherited from the pre-collapse Reclamation Authority, whose original purpose — verifying which engineers were certified to touch which failing machinery, so untrained hands didn't detonate the last pressure-locks — has calcified over four centuries into the entire structure of who eats first, who sleeps dry, who may petition whom. Nobody remembers the Reclamation Authority. Everybody remembers the Tally, because the Tally is still, today, in daily technical use: certain gallery locks genuinely require a ninth-tier cord to open safely, and opening them wrong still kills people. The coordination function is real. That is what makes it impossible to simply refuse.

## Constraint Naturalizations

**C₁ — the Sump.** Deep beneath gallery nine, past the last lamp-bracket, the aquifer's original bedrock is still bare: cold, granular, seeping stone that no cord, no ledger-entry, no rank touches or changes. Everyone in the Cistern eventually goes down to stand in it — to grieve, to be born near it, to die near it if they're lucky enough to choose where. It has no gate. It asks nothing of anyone and gives nothing back that can be filed. Locals call it **the Sump**, and they say of a person who has stopped scheming for a better cord, "she's gone down to the Sump in her thinking" — meaning she's found the one place rank doesn't reach, and it cost her the will to climb.

**C₂ — the Tally / seal-cords.** The nine-tier cording system. Naturalized as the literal collar every resident wears, re-braided and re-certified at the Certifying Table whenever status changes. It is genuinely load-bearing (certain locks require certified tiers to operate without killing people) and genuinely extractive (tier determines ration order, sleeping-gallery dryness, who may address whom directly, whose testimony is entered in the Cistern's dispute-ledger at all).

- *Agent_A's position (moderate tier, identity-locked):* a fifth-tier Lock-Warden, cord-bound to their tier by a *marriage-record fusion* — decades ago they wed into their spouse's tier-line, and Cistern law fused the two cords into one filing identity. Reacquiring a separate cord now requires proving, to the Certifying Table, that the fused identity was ever separable — a proof the Table itself refuses to process without a witnessed anomaly-event, which routes straight into C₃.
- *Agent_B's position (powerful, mobile):* an eighth-tier Reclamation Engineer, senior enough that the Tally mostly works *for* them — they feel its cost mainly as tedious deference-ritual, not extraction, though the same cording rule that annoys them at their tier crushes someone three tiers down.
- *Agent_C's position (powerless, trapped, local scope):* an unrated cistern-child, born in the wet-gallery to a mother whose cord was contested at the time of birth, so the child was never entered on any tier at all — surviving on scavenged ration-chits, invisible to every water-right in the Cistern, held immutably at the bottom not by cruelty but by the simple fact that no procedure exists for un-filing someone back in.

**C₃ — the Admittance Filter.** Every dispute, petition, or anomaly in the Cistern must pass through the **Reading**, a formal hearing where a claim is checked against the ledger's existing categories before anyone will act on it. If your claim doesn't match a category the ledger already has — if you are, say, a person whose birth-cording was contested and who therefore doesn't fit "born of tier X" or "born of tier Y" — the Reading does not deny your claim. It simply cannot find a slot to enter it in, and an unenterable claim has, procedurally, never been made.

- *Agent_C* brings exactly this kind of claim (an anomalous birth-cording, evidence that doesn't fit the Table's categories) and the Reading returns nothing — not rejection, silence. High suppression: the Reading is actively built to not-hear this.
- *Agent_D*, the Reading's presiding Notary, experiences the identical filter as protective infrastructure — the Reading is what keeps the Table from being flooded with unverifiable grief-claims every time someone dies badly, and Agent_D genuinely believes (correctly, from their seat) that an unfiltered Table would collapse under petition volume within a season.

## Character Positions

- **Agent_B → Instructor-Engineer Prel Kastade**, eighth-tier, oversees the ninth-gallery pressure locks, genuinely necessary to the Cistern's survival, genuinely insulated from the cording system's worse costs.
- **Agent_C → the girl called Nine-and-Nothing** (a nickname, not a filed name — residents call her that because she was born on the ninth gallery and holds no tier), a cistern-child living unrated at the aquifer's lowest dry ledge.
- **Agent_D → Notary Ceth Ambrail**, presiding officer of the Reading, protecting the Table's function from a volume of claims it was never built to individually adjudicate.

## Linguistic Strategy

**Direct Translation**, rooted in a constructed bureaucratic-liturgical register — the Cistern's language treats filing verbs as sacred verbs. Key untranslatable-into-plain-English concepts, carried in with their own grammar:

- ***seated*** (v.) — to have a claim formally entered in the ledger; distinct from merely "true." A grief can be true and never seated. *"It doesn't matter that she's his daughter. It was never seated."*
- ***the unfiled*** (n., plural-only, no singular form permitted in Cistern grammar) — persons with no tier; grammatically, Cistern speech cannot construct "an unfiled person" as a countable individual, only "some of the unfiled," mirroring the ledger's inability to individuate them.
- ***to skip*** (v., transitive with no object-recovery) — what the system does to the unfiled at every gate. Not "deny." *"The gate skipped her again"* — no agent named, because procedurally none acted.
- ***going down*** — the euphemism for visiting the Sump; carries connotations of both pilgrimage and defeat, deliberately ambiguous.

Bureaucratic Cistern-formal for filed interactions ("By what tier do you address the Table"); a stripped, verb-heavy demotic among the unfiled themselves, who have built their own grammar of mutual recognition precisely because the official one cannot see them — this second register is warm, contracted, and elides rank-address entirely, since among each other rank is not the currency.

## Inhabitation Sentences

**Ostrun Vey (Agent_A):** *"You wear a cord that says who your dead wife was as much as who you are, and the one door that could finally separate the two of you keeps opening onto a room where no one is permitted to answer."*

**Instructor-Engineer Prel Kastade (Agent_B):** *"You know the locks would kill people if anyone without your cord touched them, and you have stopped noticing that the same rule which keeps you righteous keeps someone else unfed."*

**Nine-and-Nothing (Agent_C):** *"You are not being punished; you are being skipped, over and over, by a ledger that has no page with your name's shape on it, and the only place that has ever held you without asking first is the cold stone at the bottom no one else wants."*

**Notary Ceth Ambrail (Agent_D):** *"You keep the Table from drowning in every grief anyone has ever wanted heard, and you are right that it would drown — and you have never once had to be the grief that goes unheard to know you're right."*

**Substrate sentence (repeated for continuity):** *"You hold the seal that says what you are, and when it is not on you, you are not anyone the world's ledgers can find — not disguised, not hidden, simply absent from the only reckoning that exists."*
