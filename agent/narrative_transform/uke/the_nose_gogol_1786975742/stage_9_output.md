REVIEW ASSESSMENT

STRENGTHS:
The institutional refrain ("The Table thanks you for bringing this forward... It has not been seated at this time") does real work by landing identically on two very different griefs, dramatizing procedure's indifference through repetition rather than commentary. The parallel structure across three sections — petitioner, notary, aquifer floor — lets the story make its case about the ledger's limits without a single character diagnosing it aloud. Ostrun and the unnamed girl's wordless exchange in Section III earns its silence; the knot-tying is concrete, unglossed, and doesn't resolve into false intimacy.

BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The story leans hard on a genuinely moving humanist register (dead mothers, cold throats, a child who "learned very young exactly how far a room's attention could reach") that keeps pulling toward exactly the recognizable-human-world allegory the break contract says must stay closed off. Hara's "Managing isn't the same as being seen" and Ceth's private aside about "the leftover any filing makes" both gesture at a moral clarity — the unfiled *are* people the Table fails to see — that sits uneasily against the invariant's claim that there is no truer self underneath the marker. The story wants readers to feel the tragedy of unrecognized personhood, but the invariant insists there is no personhood to recognize outside the ledger. These two pulls are not fully reconciled.

INVARIANT FALSIFIER:
HOLDS, narrowly. No character recovers a "true value" the Table merely miscalculated — Ostrun's marriage, the girl's parentage, none of it resolves into a legible fact a fairer notary could file correctly. The claims fail because no category exists, not because the wrong category was applied. But the prose's emotional weather (Hara's dignity, the girl's cold throat, Ceth's private ache) keeps implying a person underneath the absence of a page, which brushes against the falsifier without crossing it. The closest risk is Hara's line "You don't have a form for that either" — it stops just short of claiming a truer thing exists beyond forms, framing the unfiled life as its own separate mode of being rather than a recoverable value, so it holds.

BREAK:
The text sets up an expectation that Ostrun's petition-strand, Ceth's procedural-strand, and the aquifer-floor strand will braid into a shared moral resolution — some scene where recognition finally occurs. It violates this by ending in continued, unresolved absence: no petition is seated, no name is exchanged, the cord is fixed but nothing else is. This matches the target_prior's shape (readers expect an allegorical "recognizable human world" payoff — found family, quiet triumph of decency) and the story declines to deliver it, ending instead on two people sitting in cold and dark with nothing solved. The break is present and meets the target_prior, though it is muted rather than vertiginous — this is a quiet refusal, not a strange or physics-breaking one, so the "unlocked door to allegory-refusal" is used cautiously rather than swung wide.

READINESS:
Close, but the humanist warmth in Sections II and III needs one more pass to ensure it dramatizes the ledger's totality rather than smuggling in a "real self underneath" the reader is meant to mourn.
