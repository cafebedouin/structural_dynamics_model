STRATEGY BRIEF

IMPLIED AUTHOR: A close observer of bureaucratic grammar who trusts the physical object (cord, throat, knot, stone) to carry meaning the narration must not state. This author believes restraint is not withholding but a form of respect for the reader's ability to complete a thought unassisted. The author cares about the exact mechanics of how systems fail people politely, and refuses — almost as a matter of ethics — to let a closing paragraph do the thesis-work that the whole story has otherwise earned through accumulation. Where the draft breaks this discipline (the "same kind of wound" passage), the implied author's task is to trust the earlier craft and cut the summarizing voice, not add more of it.

AUDIENCE: Readers of literary short fiction attuned to secondary-world bureaucratic/procedural settings (comparable to readers of Kafka, or of Ted Chiang's more administrative pieces, or China Miéville's bureaucratic weird) — an audience that wants texture and implication over resolution, that will feel insulted by explanation, and that has the patience for a story which ends in stasis rather than turn. They do not need worldbuilding exposition; they need the ledger-logic to remain internally consistent and the emotional weight to arrive through withheld naming.

SCOPE: Short story. Current length 3,141 words. Target range: 3,000–4,200 words. This is a compression/precision task, not an expansion task — the material does not want more incident, it wants the existing ending re-embodied without narration doing the connecting work. Ceiling of 11,468 words is far above what this piece needs; do not pad toward it. Any added material should be in service of replacing tell with show at the close, and possibly deepening one earlier beat (see below), not increasing scope.

CREATIVE DEEPENING: The richest available material is the asymmetry between Ostrun's petition (a fused thing he wants split, a wound of excess connection to a dead woman) and the girl's condition (a wound of no connection at all, never entered). The story already has the physical vocabulary to let these sit unresolved next to each other — the cord-knot Ostrun undoes for her is the one moment where his competence (Lock-Warden, someone who reads faults in mechanisms) crosses into her world without the Table's grammar attached. That scene is the load-bearing hinge and should be trusted more, not less. The "same kind of wound" narration at the close should be cut and replaced by allowing the physical details already established (bare throat, doubled cord, cold stone) to sit in proximity without the seam being pointed at. Ceth's unresolved crack — asking a question with no page for it — is correctly left unresolved; do not be tempted to give Ceth a redemptive action in the second pass. The story's power is that the crack stays a crack.

FLINCH POINTS:
1. Closing paragraph ("No one was standing far enough back to see they were the same kind of wound. Not tonight."): This is the draft explaining its own thesis at the exact moment restraint has been earned and is most needed. What honesty requires: trust the accumulated physical detail (cord, throat, knot, stone, cold) to carry the parallel without the narrator naming it as parallel. Cut the interpretive frame; let the image of the two of them in the dark be the last thing, undiagnosed.
2. Ceth's held thought after Hara's exit ("an unfiltered Table would drown in every grief that wanted hearing, and I have made myself the wall... and the wall is a true and necessary thing"): This is close to earning its keep — it's Ceth's private grammar, not the narrator's — but it edges toward self-justifying explanation right where the crack should stay mute longer. Consider whether the thought needs its final clause ("and the wall is a true and necessary thing") or whether cutting that clause leaves the crack rawer and less resolved into self-forgiveness.
3. Hara's exit lines ("You don't have a form for that either... Managing isn't the same as being seen, but nobody down where we live mistakes the two anymore"): This is right at the edge of stating the theme through a mouthpiece character but earns it because it's delivered as lived fact, not narratorial commentary, and Ceth doesn't get to absorb it into insight. Leave as is — this is earned bluntness, not flinch, but flag it as adjacent to the pattern so the rewrite pass doesn't over-correct by cutting the story's one moment of direct address and losing all bluntness along with the narratorial overreach.

PATTERN CHECK: Concern, but narrow and already identified correctly by the Review. The constraints (unfileable claims, procedural death-by-politeness, the girl's total exclusion) are not being resolved through system or organizational hope — the Table does not bend, Ceth does not act, nothing is fixed. The one place the pipeline's default flinch shows up is not in plot resolution but in *narratorial* resolution: the ending reaches for authorial synthesis ("same kind of wound") as a soft substitute for the harder discipline of leaving the parallel inert and unclaimed. This is a smaller, more local version of the pattern than full plot-resolution, and the fix is subtractive (cut telling), not additive (no new redemptive beat needed).
