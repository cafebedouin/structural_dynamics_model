## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The untranslatable real (Detector A — low-ε Mountain recovered):**
A weaver's hands know the tension of silk threads in ways no loom specification can capture. Decades of work develop a tactile vocabulary — the give of warp under humidity, the whisper before a thread breaks, the rhythm that prevents repetitive injury. This knowledge keeps production flowing and bodies intact, but it dies the instant anyone tries to write it down, measure it, or teach it through manual. The Textile Standards Bureau's certification exams test only what can be enumerated: thread count, defect rate, output speed. The real skill — the thing that separates a surviving weaver from a broken one — registers as zero on every instrument the Bureau recognizes.

**Falsifier:** If the finished story contains a recoverable "true skill metric" that the certification system merely measured incorrectly, the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**The missing floor (Detector B — from Stage 0 contract):**
The Textile Standards Bureau does not certify weavers because weaving requires certification. It certifies weavers because the Imperial procurement system refuses to purchase cloth from uncertified workshops. The refusal creates the necessity. There is no neutral ground beneath this: before the Bureau existed, cloth circulated through kinship networks and guild vouching. The current system is not a correction of chaos — it is the replacement of one coordination mechanism with another, chosen by those who profit from the replacement.

**Falsifier:** If the story frames the certification requirement as a necessary correction of a pre-existing market failure, the floor invariant was lost.

**The substrate that holds the invariant:**
The body itself — muscle memory, callus patterns, the specific deformation of finger joints from decades of shuttle work, the lung capacity developed to blow dust from finished cloth. The substrate is biological and sensory. It cannot be photographed for a manual, cannot be transferred except through years of apprenticeship, and becomes illegible the moment it is reduced to a Bureau checklist.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:**
You are a master weaver whose body has become the instrument, and the only language that speaks this mastery is the one the certification examiners cannot read.

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT

### Setting Description

**Chang'an Prefecture, Shu Province, 1821 CE — Eighteenth year of the Jiaqing Emperor**

The Imperial Textile Standards Bureau arrived in Shu Province six years ago, following the consolidation of procurement under the Ministry of Revenue's regional offices. Before the Bureau, cloth moved through the province along kinship lines and guild networks — a weaver's reputation traveled by word of mouth, and quality was vouched for by the guild elder who had trained you. Now, all workshops selling to Imperial procurement (military uniforms, official robes, tributary gifts) must employ only Bureau-certified weavers. The certification exam tests output speed, defect rate per bolt, and conformity to the Bureau's standard weave patterns.

The exam does not test — cannot test — the things that keep a weaver's body intact through thirty years of work: how to read thread tension by sound, when to slow the rhythm to prevent tendon inflammation, which humidity levels require which adjustments, how to blow dust from finished cloth without inhaling it. These skills have no names in the Bureau's vocabulary. They live in hands, lungs, and the specific architecture of scar tissue across a weaver's palms.

The city's weavers divide into three populations. The **workshop weavers** — young, recently certified, employed in the expanding Bureau-compliant factories — work fast, meet quotas, and develop repetitive injuries by their late twenties. The **independent masters** — older, trained in the guild system, carrying decades of embodied knowledge — cannot pass the certification exam because their techniques deviate from Bureau standards, and the exam allows no space for "it depends on the thread" or "you have to feel it." They survive by selling to local markets the Bureau does not control: wedding cloth, funeral shrouds, temple donations. The **Bureau examiners** — drawn from the Ministry of Revenue's administrative class, trained in textile economics and quality metrics — design the standards, administer the tests, and genuinely believe the system produces superior cloth at lower cost.

The workshop system is expanding. Independent masters are aging out. The guild network is dissolving — not because it failed, but because the procurement system's refusal to recognize uncertified cloth starves it of the market access that sustained it.

### Constraint Naturalizations

**C₁: Bureau Certification Requirement**
**Local term:** 证书门槛 (zhèngshū ménkǎn, "certificate threshold")

In this world, C₁ is the Imperial procurement rule: only Bureau-certified weavers may work in workshops supplying government contracts. The certification exam is administered twice yearly in the provincial capital. It costs three months' wages to sit. It tests speed, defect rate, and pattern conformity using the Bureau's standard loom setup and thread grades.

The constraint has these properties because:
- **ε = 0.90 (high extraction):** The exam fee, travel costs, and three-day testing period extract heavily from workshop weavers who must take unpaid leave and often fail multiple attempts. The certification itself extracts ongoing compliance costs: Bureau-approved tools, mandatory record-keeping, annual renewal fees.
- **Supp = 0.80 (high suppression):** The procurement system actively enforces the partition. Workshops caught employing uncertified weavers lose contracts. Independent masters attempting to sell directly to government buyers are turned away at the procurement office gate.
- **Coord = false:** The certification provides no coordination value. It does not prevent fraud (guild vouching did that), does not improve quality (the exam cannot test the skills that produce durability), does not match weavers to appropriate work. It functions purely as a gate.
- **Asym = true:** Workshop weavers experience the requirement as a barrier to employment. Bureau examiners experience it as a revenue stream and administrative simplification.

**X₁ (workshop weaver) experiences it as:**
You passed the exam on your second attempt, at age nineteen, after borrowing the fee from your uncle. You work in the Dongcheng Workshop, which holds an Imperial uniform contract. The certification allows you to work, but it did not teach you to weave — your aunt taught you that, in the year before you tested. The exam tested whether you could match the Bureau's speed standard on their loom with their thread. You can. Your hands hurt by evening. The older weavers — the ones who never certified — tell you to slow down, to listen to the thread, but you have a quota and the foreman checks your output twice daily.

**X₃ (Bureau examiner) experiences it as:**
You administer the certification exam and design the standards. The system works: certified workshops produce consistent, measurable output. Defect rates have dropped eleven percent since certification became mandatory. The independent masters complain that the exam doesn't test "real skill," but they cannot define what that means in terms the Ministry of Revenue can audit. If a skill cannot be specified, it cannot be standardized. If it cannot be standardized, it cannot scale. The certification system is the foundation of reliable procurement.

---

**C₂: Output-Speed Quota System**
**Local term:** 计件工分 (jìjiàn gōngfēn, "piece-rate work points")

In this world, C₂ is the workshop payment structure: weavers earn by the bolt, with base rates determined by the Bureau's complexity tables. Faster weavers earn more. The quota system is anchored to the certification exam's speed standard — if you cannot maintain exam pace, you fall below subsistence income.

The constraint has these properties because:
- **ε = 0.80 (high extraction):** The piece-rate system extracts maximum output. Weavers who slow down to prevent injury earn less. The complexity tables — designed by Bureau economists analyzing average completion times — do not account for thread variation, humidity, or the embodied techniques that prevent long-term damage.
- **Supp = 0.70 (high suppression):** Workshop foremen monitor output. Weavers who fall below quota are moved to lower-paid auxiliary work (thread preparation, loom maintenance) or dismissed.
- **Coord = true:** The system does provide coordination value: it matches payment to output, enables production planning, and creates price predictability for procurement contracts.
- **Asym = true:** Workshop weavers experience the quota as a forced choice between income and bodily integrity. Bureau administrators experience it as an efficient payment mechanism.

**X₁ (workshop weaver) experiences it as:**
You weave eight bolts per day, six days per week. This is the speed that keeps you employed. It is faster than your body wants to work. Your right shoulder has developed a chronic ache that worsens in damp weather. The independent masters — your aunt among them — work slower, but their cloth lasts longer and their hands still work at fifty. You are twenty-three. You do not know if your hands will work at fifty. The complexity tables say your current piece is worth four hours. Your body says it is worth six. The gap between those numbers is the part of you the quota consumes.

**X₂ (independent master) experiences it as:**
You watch the workshop weavers and see your own past — the year you tried to work at Bureau speed, before you understood that speed breaks bodies. The quota system is not wrong about time — it is wrong about what time means. A bolt that takes four hours at the cost of cumulative tendon damage is not the same product as a bolt that takes six hours and leaves the weaver intact. But the complexity tables see only completion time, and the procurement system pays only for speed. You tried to explain this to a Bureau examiner once. He asked you to quantify "cumulative tendon damage." You could not. He marked you as non-compliant.

**X₃ (Bureau examiner) experiences it as:**
The piece-rate system is actuarially sound. It is based on measured completion times across three hundred certified weavers. The tables account for thread grade, pattern complexity, and loom type. They do not account for "intuition" or "feel" because those are not measurable variables. Some weavers claim the system forces them to work too fast, but output data shows certified weavers maintain quota for years. If the pace were unsustainable, we would see dropout rates. We do not. What we see is consistent production.

---

**C₃: Bureau Quality Inspection Protocol**
**Local term:** 验布程序 (yàn bù chéngxù, "cloth inspection procedure")

In this world, C₃ is the mandatory inspection system for all workshop output. Each bolt must pass Bureau inspection before payment is released. Inspectors check defect counts, measure thread density, verify pattern conformity. Weavers whose bolts fail inspection receive no payment for that piece and must re-weave.

The constraint has these properties because:
- **ε = 0.50 (moderate extraction):** Inspection catches genuine defects (coordination value), but the protocol is rigid. Inspectors cannot distinguish between defects that matter (structural weakness) and deviations that don't (aesthetic variation, adaptive technique). Re-weaving extracts unpaid labor.
- **Supp = 0.90 (very high suppression):** Failed inspection means zero payment. No appeals process. The inspector's judgment is final.
- **Coord = true:** The system does prevent fraud and ensures minimum quality standards. It protects buyers from defective cloth.
- **Asym = true:** Workshop weavers bear the cost of re-weaving. Bureau inspectors experience the protocol as a quality-control tool with no personal cost.

**X₁ (workshop weaver) experiences it as:**
You weave a bolt using a technique your aunt taught you — a slight tension adjustment that prevents puckering in humid weather. The inspector marks it as non-conforming thread density. You re-weave it to Bureau standard. The second version passes inspection but will pucker when worn in summer. You know this. The inspector does not. The protocol measures what can be measured and calls the rest defect. You have learned to weave for the inspector, not for the person who will wear the cloth.

**X₂ (independent master) experiences it as:**
The inspection protocol is where the system's blindness becomes visible. You brought a bolt to the procurement office once, before you understood the rules. The inspector rejected it for irregular selvage — the deliberate irregularity that prevents edge-curling over years of wear. You explained this. He showed you the specification: selvage must be uniform within two-thread tolerance. Your technique was four-thread variable. He marked it defective. The specification cannot see the future of the cloth, only its present conformity. You sell now to temples and families, where the buyer can see the irregularity and understand what it prevents.

**X₃ (Bureau examiner) experiences it as:**
The inspection protocol is the quality gate. It is based on Imperial standards developed by the Ministry of Revenue's textile specialists. The standards are clear, measurable, and consistently applied. Some weavers claim their "traditional techniques" produce superior cloth, but they cannot demonstrate this within the inspection framework. If a technique cannot be specified, it cannot be verified. The protocol works because it eliminates subjective judgment. Every inspector applies the same standards. This is what quality control means.

### Linguistic Strategy: Code-Switching (Mandarin Administrative + Shu Dialect)

**Base structure:**
- **Mandarin (官话, Guānhuà):** Used for all Bureau interactions, official documents, certification exams, inspection reports. Formal register. Technical vocabulary for textile standards.
- **Shu dialect (蜀语, Shǔyǔ):** Used for workshop floor conversation, family/apprentice teaching, independent master networks. Informal register. Contains vocabulary for embodied weaving knowledge that has no Mandarin equivalent.

**Code-switching signals:**
- **Switch to Mandarin:** Signals interaction with Bureau authority, performing compliance, official record. Creates distance, formality.
- **Switch to Shu dialect:** Signals intimacy, practical knowledge transmission, resistance to Bureau categories. Creates proximity, embodiment.

**Core vocabulary examples:**

*Mandarin administrative terms (Bureau language):*
- 合格率 (hégélǜ) — "compliance rate" (percentage of bolts passing inspection)
- 标准织法 (biāozhǔn zhīfǎ) — "standard weaving method" (Bureau-approved techniques)
- 缺陷密度 (quēxiàn mìdù) — "defect density" (defects per square chi)

*Shu dialect embodied terms (workshop/guild language):*
- 线性 (xiàn xìng, but pronounced with Shu tone) — "thread temperament" (how a specific thread behaves under tension; no Mandarin equivalent)
- 手感 (shǒugǎn) — "hand-feel" (tactile knowledge of when to adjust; Bureau examiners use this word but mean something different)
- 老布 (lǎo bù) — "old cloth" (cloth woven with techniques that prioritize longevity over speed; marked as defective by Bureau standards)

**Example code-switch moment:**
> Mei Xiang's hands move across the warp threads. "这个线性不对," she mutters in Shu dialect — *this thread's temperament is wrong*. Her fingers adjust tension by feel, a micro-correction her aunt taught her. The foreman passes behind her. "梅香,你的合格率怎么样?" he asks in Mandarin — *Mei Xiang, how is your compliance rate?* She switches registers without pause: "今天八匹,全部合格。" *Eight bolts today, all compliant.* The Shu dialect knowledge (thread temperament, hand-feel) has no space in the Mandarin answer.

## SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

### RESOLVED

**Ω_E01: What specific textile products were produced in Shu Province during the Jiaqing period?**
- **Resolution:** Shu Province (modern Sichuan) was historically known for silk brocade (蜀锦, Shǔ jǐn) and ramie cloth. For narrative purposes, focusing on silk weaving for Imperial procurement (uniforms, official robes, tributary gifts) provides the necessary market structure.
- **Impact:** Grounds the Bureau certification system in historical plausibility (Imperial procurement did require quality standards) while maintaining displacement from any specific historical certification system.

**Ω_E02: Did the Qing Dynasty have a centralized textile standards bureau?**
- **Resolution:** The Qing had Imperial Textile Factories (织造局, Zhīzào jú) and procurement systems, but not a "Textile Standards Bureau" as naturalized here. The naturalization invents the Bureau as a plausible extension of Qing administrative rationalization.
- **Impact:** Maintains historical plausibility (Qing did centralize procurement) while ensuring displacement (the specific certification system is invented).

**Ω_E03: What was the actual guild structure for weavers in Qing-era Sichuan?**
- **Resolution:** Textile guilds (行会, hánghuì) existed and did function through reputation/vouching systems. Specific structure varied by region and period. For narrative purposes, the guild system is naturalized as the pre-Bureau coordination mechanism — sufficient detail for contrast, not ethnographic precision.
- **Impact:** Provides the "before" state (guild vouching) that the Bureau system replaces, making the "missing floor" invariant visible.

**Ω_C01: How to naturalize "embodied knowledge" without making it mystical?**
- **Resolution:** Ground it in specific physical phenomena: callus patterns, muscle memory, lung capacity, joint deformation, humidity sensing, sound-based tension reading. These are real, biological, and materially verifiable — but not measurable by the Bureau's instruments (which test only output speed, defect rate, pattern conformity).
- **Impact:** The invariant (knowledge that dies upon measurement) remains materially grounded, not magical. The substrate is the body itself.

**Ω_C02: What makes the certification exam specifically unable to test embodied knowledge?**
- **Resolution:** The exam tests discrete, measurable outputs (speed, defect count, pattern match) using standardized conditions (Bureau loom, Bureau thread, controlled environment). It cannot test adaptive response to variable conditions (humidity, thread temperament, long-term injury prevention) because those require years of experience and cannot be evaluated in a three-day exam.
- **Impact:** The exam's limitation is structural, not a flaw. It is designed to test what can be standardized. The thing it cannot test is the thing that requires non-standard response.

**Ω_P01: Should the narrative include successful certification exam passage or only failure?**
- **Resolution:** Mei Xiang passed on her second attempt. This is more structurally interesting than pure failure: she is certified and compliant, yet the certification did not capture what her aunt taught her. The exam tested one kind of knowledge (speed on standard equipment); her survival depends on a different kind (adaptive technique). Both are real. The exam's blindness is not about failing weavers, but about measuring the wrong thing.
- **Impact:** Avoids "exam as villain" framing. The exam works as designed. The problem is what it was designed to measure.

**Ω_P02: How much technical weaving detail is needed?**
- **Resolution:** Enough to ground the embodied knowledge in physical sensation (thread tension, shuttle rhythm, humidity effects, dust in lungs, shoulder pain), not enough to require the reader to understand actual weaving. The goal is felt experience, not technical instruction.
- **Impact:** Reader experiences the knowledge as real and specific without needing to verify its technical accuracy.
