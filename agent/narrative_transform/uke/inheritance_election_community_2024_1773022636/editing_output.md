## §5. OPEN QUESTIONS (Ω) & LOGIC GAPS

### Ω1: Duration Threshold (Epistemic)
**Question:** How many years of observation across how many contexts constitute "sufficient sampling" for character assessment?
**Source:** Essay acknowledges this (Unresolved Questions §1) but doesn't provide even rough heuristics
**Impact:** Medium - Readers may want *some* guidance even if precise thresholds are unknown
**Possible Resolution:** Add: "Preliminary research suggests 2-3 years across at least 3 distinct contexts (professional, personal crisis, power dynamics) as minimum threshold, but this remains contested."

### Ω2: Virtual Communities (Empirical Gap)
**Question:** Do online communities produce comparable character transformation to geographic ones?
**Source:** Listed as Tier 3 hypothesis but increasingly relevant given remote work/digital socialization trends
**Impact:** High - If virtual communities *don't* transform character, the essay's practical advice may be obsolete for digitally-native generations
**Possible Resolution:** Flag explicitly: "This analysis assumes face-to-face interaction. Whether virtual communities produce comparable effects remains unknown."

### Ω3: Involuntary Community Transitions (Ethical Boundary)
**Question:** If someone is *forced* into a new community (incarceration, military deployment, refugee resettlement), does the transformation mechanism still apply? And if so, what are the moral implications?
**Source:** Essay mentions "involuntary transitions" in Alternative Explanations but doesn't explore the ethical dimension
**Impact:** High - This is where the "choosing your character" frame breaks down most obviously
**Possible Resolution:** Add paragraph in "Exit Privilege" section: "The transformation mechanism operates regardless of voluntariness—incarceration, military service, and forced displacement all produce documented behavioral change. This suggests character is more environmentally contingent than we typically acknowledge, raising uncomfortable questions about moral responsibility for behaviors shaped by unchosen contexts."
