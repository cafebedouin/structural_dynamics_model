## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The Invariant (Untranslatable Real):**
There exists a state of genuine mastery — the moment when hand, mind, and material become one thing — that cannot survive the act of certification. The instant an examiner measures it, a buyer prices it, or an institution validates it, the state collapses. What the instruments read is always the afterimage, never the thing itself.

**Falsifier:** If the finished story contains a "true skill level" that better instruments could measure correctly, or if mastery becomes recoverable through fairer testing, the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**The Missing Floor (Founding Choice):**
Beneath every system of craft valuation lies an arbitrary declaration of zero — a choice about what counts as "beginning competence" that someone made and then buried. There is no natural ground under the baseline. The system treats its zero-point as discovered, but it was invented.

**Falsifier:** If the story frames the injustice as a local error a wiser authority could correct, rather than as rooted in an arbitrary founding choice with no neutral alternative, the floor invariant was lost.

**The Substrate:**
The substrate that holds the invariant is **the interval between breath and action** — the untimed, unobserved moment when a potter's hand knows the clay is ready, when a smith knows the iron will move, when a dyer's fingers read saturation in fiber that looks identical to every instrument. This interval exists only in practice. It cannot be photographed, demonstrated on command, or translated into procedure. The state is real — measurably real in its consequences (the pot that doesn't crack, the blade that doesn't shatter, the color that doesn't fade) — but the moment itself is constitutionally illegible to any observer not inside it.

**Substrate Inhabitation Sentence:**
You are a dyer's apprentice in a system that certifies mastery by examination, and the thing your hands know — the moment the madder root has given all it has to give — exists only while you are alone with the work, and dies the instant the inspector asks you to demonstrate it.
