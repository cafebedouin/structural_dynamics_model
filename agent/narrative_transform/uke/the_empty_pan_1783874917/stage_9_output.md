REVIEW ASSESSMENT

STRENGTHS:
The prose texture work is exceptional — the examination hall scene enacts the interval's collapse through fragmenting syntax and halting rhythm, creating a visceral contrast with the fluid, sensory-driven workshop passages. The economic weight is specific and accumulating: Wu's bent back, the moneylender he still pays, Master Feng's coins running out, the fine growing faster than Chen Shu can pay it. The story refuses to resolve the contradiction at its center: the interval is real (the silk proves it) AND the conditions are unsustainable (the debt proves it), both truths held simultaneously without reconciliation.

BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The ending repeats its core gestures too many times. The final three pages cycle through the same elements — hands knowing, debt growing, inspector coming, work continuing — without progression or intensification. The repetition feels like circling rather than dwelling, diluting the weight the story has built.

INVARIANT FALSIFIER:
HOLDS. The story never suggests a better examination could measure *shoujue* correctly, or that fairer officials would see what Chen Shu's hands know. The interval remains constitutionally illegible to observation — it exists in the pre-dawn workshop and dies in the examination hall, not because the examiners are incompetent, but because the act of observation collapses the state. The real stays unreadable to the system.

READINESS:
Close. The prose is alive, the economic consequences are specific, the invariant holds. The ending needs tightening — fewer repetitions, more pressure — but the story's core is sound.
