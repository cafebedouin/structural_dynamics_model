# STAGE 2: NATURALIZATION OUTPUT

## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The invariant (commitment + falsifier):**
A form of keeping exists only in its direct performance between people who know each other's names. The moment it is written into a number, assigned a value to be compared or traded, it stops being keeping and becomes a different thing entirely — a claim, a debt, an account. The substrate that holds this real is **the practice of direct reciprocal care** — meals shared, tools lent, labor given when someone's roof needs mending, children minded, grief witnessed. This practice cannot be tallied without destroying what makes it work: the fact that no one is counting.

**Falsifier:** If the finished story contains a recoverable "true value" of care that the ledger merely measured incorrectly — if better accounting could capture what was lost — the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**The missing floor (second invariant):**
Beneath the visible system is a founding choice with no neutral ground under it: the decision to assign each person a single number representing their worth to others, and to treat that number as a fact rather than a verdict. The system presents this quantification as neutral record-keeping, but it is a constitutive act — it creates the reality it claims only to measure.

**Falsifier:** If the story frames the injustice as a local error a better authority could correct (a fairer algorithm, less biased administrators), the floor invariant was lost.

**The substrate that holds the invariant:**
The old practice of mutual aid — what people in the stilt-houses call *timbangan*, "the keeping-between-us." It operates through memory, witnessed acts, and reciprocal obligation that no one writes down. The substrate is **unwriteable care**: the fact that you fed someone's children when the nets came up empty, that someone else repaired your boat without asking payment, that grief and celebration are attended because presence matters, not because attendance is logged.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:**
You know who kept you alive through the dry season not because anyone recorded it, but because you remember their hands and their constancy, and the moment someone tries to write that knowing into a number to be traded or compared, it stops being the thing that kept you.

**The affordance test (applied):**
In this world, could a better instrument measure *timbangan*'s "true value" correctly? **No.** The substrate — direct reciprocal care performed between people who know each other — is structurally unreadable to any measuring apparatus. A ledger can record transactions (fish given, labor performed), but it cannot record *keeping* because keeping is constituted by its unaccountability. The act of measuring converts care into debt, which is a different ontological category. The world's physics has a home for the invariant: a practice that holds real value while remaining constitutionally illegible to any instrument.

**Scored-Snare gate (applied):**
The central Snare (C₃: ledger) is inherently instrument-mediated per Stage 0's flag (`inherent_instrument: yes`). The extraction runs through certified numerical scores. Therefore the instrument may exist diegetically, BUT:
- (i) Narration rule: numbers appear only when a character acts on them in-scene (reads a score aloud, disputes it, pays against it). Narration is never denominated in the ledger's numbers.
- (ii) Extraction is felt and enforced, never tallied, in every POV passage. The score is the mechanism of the taking; it is not the texture of the telling.
- (iii) The orchestrator's deterministic numeric meter will govern every number that reaches the page.

**Break affordance (applied):**
The Break Contract targets the prior: "A system that is technically accurate and internally consistent can still be fundamentally destructive to human value." The naturalization must not foreclose this violation. Test: In this world, can a ledger be perfectly accurate in its recording and still destroy what it measures? **Yes.** The ledger can record every transaction with zero error and still annihilate *timbangan* by converting unaccountable keeping into accountable debt. The substrate affords the break.

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

### Setting

**Teluk Mawar, 2187 CE.** A coastal settlement in the Riau Archipelago, built on mangrove stilts where the Malacca Strait meets the South China Sea. The old world drowned a century ago; what remains are the stilt-villages, the fish, and the memory of when the coast was solid ground. Teluk Mawar sits at the intersection of three trade currents: the Thai trawler fleets to the north, the Javanese protein processors to the south, and the offshore arcology platforms that buy everything and sell nothing the villagers can afford.

The settlement is three generations old, built by climate refugees who carried boat-building traditions from a dozen drowned coastlines. Two hundred people live here year-round; another hundred cycle through with the monsoon fishing seasons. Structures are hybrid: bamboo and reclaimed carbon-fiber, solar sheeting and woven palm, whatever the tide brings in and hands can make hold. The village sits in legal gray space — not formally recognized by any nation-state, not formally prohibited, sustained by the fact that no authority wants to pay for relocating people who survive without subsidy.

Power comes from tidal generators and salvaged panels. Protein comes from fish, kelp, and the offshore farms the villagers are not permitted to own but sometimes work. Fresh water comes from atmospheric condensers and rain collection, rationed carefully. Medical care comes from a traveling clinic boat once a month and from Pak Rashid, who learned field medicine in the archipelago wars and keeps a pharmacy of what he can synthesize or trade for.

The material rhythm is subsistence with occasional surplus. Most days: net-mending, boat repair, kelp harvest, fish cleaning, water management, the endless maintenance of a settlement that lives on the boundary between sea and air. Some days: a good catch, a salvage find, a bulk trade with the processor boats. Rare days: nothing, and the village draws on stored protein and collective memory of leaner times.

The social structure is flat by necessity. No one has enough surplus to establish permanent hierarchy. Leadership is contextual: Pak Rashid for medical decisions, Ibu Salmah for weather prediction and net-craft, Encik Haron for trade negotiation with the processor boats. Disputes are settled by consensus or by the person with the most relevant expertise. The structure works because everyone knows everyone's capacity, and because defection means exile to open water.

### Constraint Naturalizations

**C₁: The Ontological Divide (invariant-Mountain)**

*What it is in this world:* The fundamental incompatibility between *timbangan* (direct reciprocal care, unaccounted-for keeping) and *hutang* (quantified debt, recorded obligation). These are not two ways of measuring the same thing; they are two different kinds of reality. *Timbangan* exists in performed memory and witnessed reciprocity — you remember who kept you, and you keep them in turn, and no one writes it down because writing it down converts keeping into debt. *Hutang* exists in recorded claims — what you owe, what is owed to you, enforceable and tradeable. The moment *timbangan* is written into *hutang*, it ceases to be *timbangan*. The ledger cannot measure care; it can only replace it with debt.

*Why it has Mountain properties:* Zero extraction, zero suppression. This is not a rule anyone enforces; it is a fact of ontology. No one chose to make care and debt incompatible. The incompatibility emerges from what they are: one is constituted by unaccountability, the other by accountability. No action within a biographical horizon can make them commensurable. You cannot design an instrument that measures *timbangan* without destroying it, because measurement itself performs the conversion to *hutang*.

*What locals call it:* "The divide between keeping and counting." Or more plainly: "You cannot write down who loves you."

*Example in use:* Mina's mother died in the monsoon floods three years ago. Pak Rashid sat with the body, prepared it, spoke the prayers, stayed through the night. Ibu Salmah brought food for the mourning week. Encik Haron repaired Mina's boat so she could fish again. No one recorded this. No one said "you owe us." When Pak Rashid's daughter fell sick, Mina stayed awake two nights helping monitor fever. When Ibu Salmah's net tore, Mina mended it. This is *timbangan*. If someone had written "Pak Rashid: funeral services rendered, value 2000 credits" and "Mina: nursing services rendered, value 1500 credits, net debt 500 to Rashid," the keeping would have died. The care would still have happened, but it would no longer be care — it would be a transaction awaiting settlement.

**C₂: The Ideology of Objective Record-Keeping**

*What it is in this world:* The belief system that quantified measurement is neutral, that writing things down makes them fair, that a number cannot lie. This ideology is carried by the **Verification Cooperative** — the regional entity that certifies trade weights, arbitrates disputes, and (recently) began offering "reputation services" to formalize creditworthiness. The ideology presents ledger-keeping as moral progress: instead of relying on memory and favor (which can be biased, selective, corrupt), record everything objectively. Let the numbers speak. This is sold as transparency, as protection against exploitation.

*Why it has these properties:* High extraction (ε=0.75) because the ideology justifies a system that takes. High suppression (0.7) because it requires active normalization to maintain — people have to be taught that numbers are neutral, that recording is not an act of power. Coordinating (yes) because the ideology genuinely provides legitimacy to institutions that solve real problems (trade verification, dispute resolution). Asymmetric (yes) because it distributes costs unevenly: those with power benefit from formalization (their claims become enforceable), those without power pay the cost (their informal keeping becomes unrecognized).

*What locals call it:* "The Verification faith" or "number-trust." Pak Rashid, skeptical, calls it "the belief that writing something down makes it true."

*Example in use:* When the Verification Cooperative first arrived, they offered to certify fish weights for trades with the processor boats. This was genuinely useful — it stopped the processors from shorting the villagers on weight. People saw the value: objective measurement protects the weak. Then the Cooperative expanded: they would record not just weights but transactions, not just trades but favors. "Build a complete record," they said. "Then everyone knows who contributes, who free-rides. Fair and transparent." Ibu Salmah, who has lived seventy years on *timbangan*, said: "The number does not know why I gave the fish." But her daughter, who learned arithmetic in the traveling school-boat, said: "That is exactly the point, Ibu. The number does not play favorites."

**C₃: The Reputation Ledger**

*What it is in this world:* A centralized system operated by the **Verification Cooperative** that assigns each resident a numerical score (0-1000) based on recorded transactions: fish sold, labor performed, debts settled, favors logged. The score determines access to credit (for boat repair, medical supplies, passage to the mainland), eligibility for Cooperative services, and — increasingly — social standing. The system is voluntary in theory (no one forces you to register), coercive in practice (unregistered people cannot access formal credit, cannot trade with certified partners, are excluded from Cooperative arbitration).

The ledger is maintained by **Ahmad**, grandson of one of the original Verification founders, who operates the system from a floating platform anchored between Teluk Mawar and the neighboring village of Tanjung Seri. Ahmad is not corrupt in the classical sense — he does not falsify scores, take bribes, or play favorites. He believes genuinely in the system's fairness. He is also the system's primary beneficiary: he extracts a percentage of every scored transaction as a "verification fee," and his structural position (arbiter of scores, gatekeeper to credit) gives him power no one in the flat village structure previously held.

*Why it has these properties:* High extraction (ε=0.8) because the system takes a percentage of every transaction plus the conversion of *timbangan* into *hutang* (unaccounted care becomes unrecognized, therefore valueless). Low suppression (0.1) because the system relies on voluntary participation, not overt force — people adopt it because it solves real coordination problems (access to credit, trade certification). Coordinating (yes) because it genuinely provides services: credit access, reputation signaling, dispute resolution. Asymmetric (yes) because costs distribute by power position: those with high scores benefit, those with low scores or no scores are excluded, Ahmad extracts from everyone.

*What locals call it:* "The Cooperative's book" or "the number-name." People distinguish between "book-debt" (what the ledger says you owe) and "keeping-debt" (what you actually owe because someone kept you).

*Naturalization of the Snare (per Scored-Snare gate):* The ledger exists as a diegetic instrument (it is the mechanism of extraction), but the story's narration is never denominated in its numbers. When a character with positional access acts on a score in-scene — Ahmad reads it aloud, Mina disputes it, someone pays against it — the number may appear. Otherwise, extraction is felt and enforced through consequence and sensation: Mina cannot get credit for boat repair, so she patches with salvage and hopes it holds. Pak Rashid's daughter needs medicine the traveling clinic does not carry, and the Cooperative will not extend credit to someone unscored, so he synthesizes a substitute that works less well. The ledger's presence is constant; its arithmetic is rarely visible.

*Example in use (X₂ powerless index, Snare):* Mina fishes. She sells to the processor boats, which require Cooperative certification. To get certified, she must register with the ledger. Once registered, every transaction is scored: fish sold (+), supplies bought (-), favors logged (±). Her score is 340. This is low — not because she contributes less, but because most of what she contributes is *timbangan* (unrecorded care), which the ledger does not see. Low score means: no credit for a new net when hers tears, no passage-money to the mainland when her cousin's child is sick, no access to the bulk-purchase cooperative that gets better prices. She is trapped: cannot fish without certification, cannot get certification without scoring, cannot score well because her keeping is invisible.

*Example in use (X₂ post-entry, moderate index, Tangled Rope):* After two years, Mina's score is 680. She learned to log favors, to convert *timbangan* into recorded transactions. When she helps mend Pak Rashid's roof, she records it. When Ibu Salmah gives her fish, she records it. Her score rises. Now she has: credit for a new net, access to the bulk-purchase co-op, social standing as a "good contributor." She also has: the habit of counting what she used to keep, the need to maintain her score (which means sometimes declining to help when it would not log well), and the knowledge that her standing depends on a system that excludes her neighbors. She experiences real coordination value (credit, access, standing) at high extractive cost (the conversion of keeping into counting, the exclusion of others, the 8% verification fee on every transaction).

*Example in use (X₃ institutional index, Rope):* Ahmad operates the ledger. His score is 920 — highest in the region. He pays no verification fees (he is the verifier). He has: unlimited credit, arbitration authority, structural power no one in the village previously held. From his position, the system is pure coordination: it solves real problems (credit access, trade certification), it is objectively fair (he does not falsify scores), it benefits everyone (look at Mina's rising score — the system works!). He does not see the extraction because he is the extraction's beneficiary. The system takes from others and gives to him, but from his index it appears as value creation, not value transfer.

### Character Positions (Material Circumstances)

**X₁: Pak Rashid** — 58 years old, field medic and pharmacist. Analytical power position naturalized through: (i) physical space: maintains a pharmacy-hut with salvaged medical equipment, organized supplies, reference materials; cleaner and more orderly than most village structures because contamination kills; (ii) daily rhythm: diagnostic thinking, triage decisions, synthesis protocols — work requires analysis, not just execution; (iii) social role: people defer to his medical judgment, ask questions, wait for his assessment; he speaks second in disputes (after relevant facts are established) because his framing often clarifies; (iv) material possessions: owns medical supplies, synthesis equipment, reference library (digital archive on salvaged tablet) — these are high-value, specialized, not fungible.

Constrained exit (not trapped, not mobile): Cannot leave the village because he is the only medical provider, and leaving would mean people die. Not physically trapped (could board a processor boat tomorrow), but structurally bound by role obligation and moral weight. Engages with the Cooperative because he must access certified medical supplies; the traveling clinic carries only basics.

**X₂: Mina** — 31 years old, fisher. Powerless position (initial state) naturalized through: (i) physical space: lives in a small stilt-hut, one room, shared with her daughter; cramped, humid, smells of fish and salt; no privacy, no quiet; (ii) daily rhythm: survival labor — fishing, net-mending, fish-cleaning, water-hauling, constant maintenance of boat and gear; no time for strategic planning, only execution; (iii) social role: defers to elders in consensus decisions, speaks last or not at all; her labor is essential but her voice is not weighted; (iv) material possessions: owns a small boat (shared inheritance), one good net, basic tools; everything else is borrowed, salvaged, or improvised; one mechanical failure from catastrophe.

Trapped exit (initial state): Cannot leave because she has no savings, no skills that transfer to mainland economy, no contacts outside the village, and a daughter to feed. The processor boats do not hire women for deck work. The arcologies do not hire anyone without certification she does not have.

Moderate power position (post-entry state, after two years in the ledger): (i) physical space: still the same hut, but now she has a newer boat (bought on credit), better nets, a small supply cache; (ii) daily rhythm: still survival labor, but with slack — she can plan a week ahead, can afford to take a rest day, can help others without immediate cost; (iii) social role: her voice is weighted more in consensus decisions because she has "proven contribution" (high ledger score); people ask her advice on how to score well; (iv) material possessions: owns better equipment, has access to credit, has a small savings buffer.

Identity-locked exit (post-entry): Can physically leave (has money for passage), but cognitively fused to the system. Her success is legible only within the ledger's logic. Leaving would mean abandoning the score she worked two years to build, the standing she earned, the proof that she is a "good contributor." The system has become her identity.

**X₃: Ahmad** — 34 years old, ledger operator and Verification Cooperative administrator. Institutional power position naturalized through: (i) physical space: lives on a floating platform anchored between two villages; the platform is large, clean, solar-powered, climate-controlled; has a dedicated office with computing equipment, communication arrays, filing systems; his space is a node of information and authority; (ii) daily rhythm: administrative work, not survival labor — he reviews scores, adjudicates disputes, certifies transactions, manages the ledger database; his work is strategic, not tactical; (iii) social role: people come to him; he does not go to them; they wait for his judgment; he speaks with institutional authority, not personal opinion; (iv) material possessions: owns the platform, the ledger system, computing equipment, communication access; has wealth in the form of accumulated verification fees; has formal education (attended Cooperative training on the mainland).

Arbitrage exit: Can play alternatives against each other. If Teluk Mawar rejects the ledger, he moves to Tanjung Seri. If the Cooperative tries to reduce his fees, he threatens to go independent. Structural mobility.

### Linguistic Strategy: Code-Switching (Malay-English)

**Base languages:** Bahasa Melayu (Malay) and institutional English.

**Mixing rationale:** The settlement's population carries Malay as the language of kinship, intimacy, the body, and the old ways of living. English is the language of the Cooperative, formal transactions, the ledger, and the institutional world that arrived after the drowning. Code-switching signals: (i) who you are speaking to (kin vs. institution), (ii) what you are speaking about (keeping vs. counting), (iii) whether you are performing compliance or expressing selfhood.

**Grammatical features carried into English:**
1. **Reduplication for intensification:** "The number-number people" (the Cooperative administrators), "waiting-waiting" (prolonged waiting with frustration).
2. **Topic-comment structure with fronting:** "This keeping, cannot write down" (topic: keeping; comment: cannot write down).
3. **Aspect markers from Malay grammar:** "Already I tell you" (perfective aspect), "still he don't understand" (continuative aspect).

**Social contexts:**
- **Malay** for: private speech, family, intimate relationships, *timbangan* (keeping), emotional expression, the body, memory, grief, care.
- **English** for: Cooperative interactions, ledger discussions, formal disputes, trade negotiation, anything that will be recorded.
- **Code-switching mid-sentence** signals: (i) emotional intensity breaking through formal register, (ii) irony or skepticism about institutional language, (iii) the friction between two worlds.

**Examples:**

*Mina speaking to her daughter (pure Malay):*
"Jangan risau, nak. Ibu akan cari jalan." (Don't worry, child. Mother will find a way.)

*Mina speaking to Ahmad about her score (English with Malay substrate):*
"My score, you say is low. But this keeping — the fish I give to Pak Rashid when his daughter sick, the night I sit with Ibu Salmah when her husband die — this one you don't write. So how the number can be true?"

*Pak Rashid speaking to another elder about the Cooperative (code-switching):*
"They say the ledger is 'objective, transparent, fair.' Objective, ha! The number does not know *kenapa* (why). Does not know *siapa* (who). Just counting-counting, like we are all the same."

*Ahmad speaking to a villager (institutional English):*
"Your score is currently 340, which places you in the lower quartile. To improve your standing, I recommend logging all transactions and participating in certified exchanges."

*Ahmad speaking to his wife (Malay, private):*
"*Mereka tak faham* (They don't understand). I am helping them. The old way — too easy to cheat, too easy to forget. The ledger is *adil* (fair)."

**Naming traditions:**

- **Malay naming:** Given name + respectful title. "Pak" (father/uncle, for older men), "Ibu" (mother/aunt, for older women), "Encik" (Mr., formal), "Cik" (Ms., formal). Family names are not emphasized; kinship terms and given names dominate.
  - Pak Rashid: given name Rashid, title Pak (respected elder).
  - Ibu Salmah: given name Salmah, title Ibu (respected elder woman).
  - Mina: given name, no title (younger, less formal standing).

- **Institutional naming (Cooperative context):** Family name + given name, English order. Ahmad bin Ismail becomes "Ahmad Ismail" in ledger records. This is a forced formalization — the ledger requires a "surname" for database sorting, so kinship patronyms (bin/binti) are converted into family names.

- **Nickname structures:** Diminutives and descriptive names used in intimate contexts. Mina's daughter is called "Adik" (little sister/younger one) by most of the village. Pak Rashid is sometimes called "Pak Ubat" (medicine father) by children.

### Inhabitation Sentences

**X₁ (Pak Rashid, analytical position, constrained exit):**
You are the one who keeps people alive, and you see clearly that the system which writes down their worth cannot see the thing that makes them worth keeping, and you cannot leave because leaving means they die, and you cannot destroy the system because it also brings the medicine you need, so you stay and you watch the numbers replace the names.

**X₂ (Mina, powerless position, trapped exit):**
You are a woman who fishes in water that is not yours, selling to boats that pay what they choose, and the only way to make the payment fair is to let someone write a number next to your name, and the number does not see the care you gave or the nights you sat with the dying, so the number says you are worth less than you are, and you cannot leave because your daughter needs to eat.

**X₂ (Mina post-entry, moderate position, identity-locked exit):**
You worked two years to make the number say you are good, and now the number opens doors, and you have a better boat and people respect your voice, and you know the number does not see everything but it sees enough, and you know the system takes but it also gives, and you cannot leave because leaving would mean admitting the number was never you, and you cannot bear that.

**X₃ (Ahmad, institutional position, arbitrage exit):**
You are the one who keeps the book, and the book is fair because you do not lie, and the book helps people because it gives them credit and proves their worth, and you do not see that the book takes from them because the book gives to you, and you could leave tomorrow because you have money and skills and the next village would hire you, but you do not leave because you believe the book is good.

**Substrate (the invariant, from the position of whoever stands closest to sensing it — Ibu Salmah, village elder):**
You remember every hand that kept you through seventy years, and you know their constancy not because it was written but because you lived it, and you know that the moment someone writes it into a number to be compared or traded, the keeping stops being keeping and becomes a debt, and you cannot explain this to your daughter who learned arithmetic and believes the number is fair, so you watch the keeping die and you do not know how to say what is being lost because the words for it are in a language the number does not speak.

## SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

### RESOLVED

**Ω_E01: What specific medical supplies does Pak Rashid need from the Cooperative that he cannot synthesize locally?**
- **Resolution:** Antibiotics (broad-spectrum, for resistant infections), antivirals (for dengue and emerging fevers common in coastal zones), immunosuppressants (for the one villager with an autoimmune condition), and diagnostic reagents (for blood testing). These require industrial synthesis or cold-chain supply that the village cannot maintain.
- **Impact:** Establishes Pak Rashid's structural dependence on the Cooperative despite his skepticism. He cannot exit because exit means people die of treatable infections.

**Ω_E02: What is the demographic composition of Teluk Mawar? (age distribution, gender ratio, family structures)**
- **Resolution:** ~200 permanent residents. Age distribution: 30% under 18, 50% working-age (18-60), 20% elderly (60+). Gender ratio approximately 1:1. Family structures: mostly nuclear families (parents + children), some multigenerational households, a few single-parent households (widows/widowers from the drowning and the archipelago wars). High childhood mortality in the first generation (pre-Pak Rashid); lower now but still present.
- **Impact:** Establishes the village as a young settlement with living memory of the drowning. Elderly characters (like Ibu Salmah) carry *timbangan* as lived practice; younger characters (like Mina's daughter) learn the ledger as normal.

**Ω_E03: What is the verification fee percentage Ahmad charges, and what is his annual income from it?**
- **Resolution:** 8% fee on all certified transactions. Annual income: approximately 45,000 credits (regional currency), which is 15x the median village income (~3,000 credits/year for a fisher). This is not extravagant wealth by mainland standards, but it is massive disparity within the village context.
- **Impact:** Establishes Ahmad as structurally extractive (high income from fees) while not cartoonishly villainous (he is not a oligarch, just a local administrator with asymmetric position).

**Ω_E04: How does the traveling clinic boat operate, and why doesn't it provide the supplies Pak Rashid needs?**
- **Resolution:** The clinic is run by a regional health NGO, visits once a month, provides: vaccinations, basic wound care, prenatal checkups, and common medications (pain relief, anti-parasitics, oral rehydration). It does NOT provide: advanced antibiotics (too expensive, require cold storage), antivirals (same), chronic disease management, or surgical intervention. The clinic's model is "basic preventive care for uninsured populations," not comprehensive treatment.
- **Impact:** Establishes the gap that forces Pak Rashid to engage with the Cooperative (which has cold-chain supply agreements with mainland distributors).

**Ω_C01: Is *timbangan* a real Malay word, or should it be replaced with an actual term?**
- **Resolution:** *Timbangan* is a real Malay word meaning "balance/scale" (the physical object). In this context it is being used metaphorically for "the balance kept between people" — a poetic extension, not standard usage. Alternatively, could use *jaga-menjaga* (mutual care/watching-over) or *tolong-menolong* (mutual helping). Decision: keep *timbangan* because the metaphor of "balance" (unwritten, unmeasured, held in memory) directly contrasts with the ledger's written balance. The slight poetic license is acceptable for a future coastal creole.
- **Impact:** Establishes the linguistic substrate for the invariant.

**Ω_C02: What is the ledger's technical implementation? (digital database, blockchain, paper records, etc.)**
- **Resolution:** Distributed digital database, encrypted, backed up to mainland servers, accessible via local mesh network. NOT blockchain (too energy-intensive for a settlement on tidal power). The system is centralized in terms of authority (Ahmad controls access and adjudication) but distributed in terms of data storage (redundancy against equipment failure). Villagers with tablets or phones can view their own scores; only Ahmad can edit.
- **Impact:** Establishes the ledger as technically robust (not easily destroyed or falsified) but institutionally centralized (Ahmad is gatekeeper).

**Ω_P01: Should the story include a scene of the Cooperative's initial arrival, or begin in medias res with the ledger already established?**
- **Resolution:** Begin in medias res (ledger already operating for 2-3 years). The arrival can be told in flashback if needed, but starting with the system already normalized creates immediate tension: characters are already trapped, already converting *timbangan* to *hutang*, already experiencing the consequences. Frontloading the arrival would delay the central conflict.
- **Impact:** Story begins with Mina at powerless index (score 340, trapped) and Pak Rashid at analytical index (constrained, watching the conversion happen).

**Ω_P02: What is the narrative POV structure? (single, dual, rotating third-person, etc.)**
- **Resolution:** Rotating third-person limited, three POVs: Pak Rashid (analytical index, constrained exit), Mina (powerless → moderate index, trapped → identity-locked exit), Ahmad (institutional index, arbitrage exit).
