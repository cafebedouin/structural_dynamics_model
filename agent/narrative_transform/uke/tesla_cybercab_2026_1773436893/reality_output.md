- **Rope:** Regulatory approval sequences solve coordination problem (safety validation before deployment)

**FEASIBILITY-ASSESSMENT:**
Classification: **BLOCKED**

Reasoning:
- **Veto holder analysis reveals fundamental problem:**
  
  1. **Tesla/Manufacturers**
     - Status: Lose massively (stranded capital if denied)
     - Compensation: Not viable (cannot compensate for $100M+ stranded assets)
     - Bypass: No (they are the regulated party)

     - Bypass: No (but have political influence)
  
  3. **Workers/Communities**
     - Status: Lose (job losses if production halted)
     - Compensation: Politically difficult (requires government intervention)
     - Bypass: No (but create political pressure)
  
  4. **NHTSA/Regulators**
     - Status: **Lose either way**
       - Deny exemption → blamed for economic harm, job losses
       - Grant exemption → blamed if safety incidents occur
     - Compensation: N/A (cannot compensate for reputational damage)
     - Bypass: No (they must make decision)

**The Structural Trap:**

Once vehicles are manufactured at scale, denying exemptions creates concentrated, visible harm (stranded capital, job losses, investor losses) while granting exemptions creates diffuse, invisible harm (safety risk distributed across public).

Political economy strongly favors granting exemptions because:
- Harm from denial is immediate and attributable
- Harm from approval is probabilistic and delayed
- Concentrated interests (manufacturers, investors, workers) lobby effectively
- Diffuse interests (public safety) lack organized advocacy

**This is not a solvable problem through regulatory action alone.** The fait accompli has already succeeded by the time regulators must decide.

- Political: **Impossible** - requires regulators to accept blame for economic harm

- Temporal: N/A (decision point already passed once production began)

- Opportunity cost: N/A

- Optimistic: N/A

Veto Points:
- **Everyone loses** (see analysis above)

## [CONSTRAINT-ANALYSIS SUMMARY]

**Pattern detected:** Document correctly diagnoses fait accompli strategy but implicitly assumes regulators can deny exemptions after production. This assumption violates political economy constraints.

**Core insight:** The strategy succeeds not because regulators are captured, but because concentrated harm (stranded capital) creates political pressure that diffuse harm (safety risk) cannot match. This is a **Mountain** (political economy coordination problem), not a **Noose** (regulatory capture).

**Recommended reframe:**
- FROM: "Regulators should deny exemptions to prevent this pattern"
- TO: "Regulators must establish preventive framework requiring approval before production, because denial after production is politically infeasible"

**Viable recommendations:** All four explicit institutional actions (public registry, testing clarification, disclosure requirements) are **VIABLE** and should proceed.

**Blocked recommendation:** Implicit suggestion that regulators can/should deny exemptions after production is **BLOCKED** by political economy. Document should acknowledge this explicitly.

**Document strength:** Tier 1 evidence is excellent. Constraint analysis is sophisticated. Only weakness is implicit assumption that regulatory denial is viable option post-production.
