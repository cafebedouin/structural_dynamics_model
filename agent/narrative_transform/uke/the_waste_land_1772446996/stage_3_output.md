# STAGE 3: OPERATIONAL SPECIFICATION

## 1. TERMINAL ATTRACTOR VERIFICATION

**Selected: ☑ Seeded Possibility**

**Justification:**
The constraint network supports this attractor through structural features:

- **C₁ (Fragmented Knowledge)** has TR₁ (Collective Organization) as viable transformation, moving powerless agents from Snare (χ=0.72) to Rope (χ=0.192) through organized collective action
- **C₂ (Exhaustion)** has TR₄ (Alternative System) that changes exit from identity-locked to mobile, creating structural escape route
- **C₃ (Arrangement)** has TR₅ (Refusal) that shifts exit from trapped to constrained

The rationality model is **BIR** (Bounded, Institutional, Rational) for transformation attempts:
- Agents have incomplete information (fragmented knowledge)
- Institutional structure (muqaddam system) shapes available actions
- Agents act rationally within constraints (Yusuf petitions, Layla works, Maryam uses privilege strategically)

**Why not other attractors:**
- **Deterministic Tragedy:** Transformations are structurally possible, not blocked by Mountain constraints
- **Negotiated Equilibrium:** Would require coordination=true across constraints; C₁ and C₂ have coordination=false
- **Revolutionary Rupture:** Would require complete system collapse; the transformations are incremental (collective organization, alternative systems, refusal) not revolutionary

**Attractor compatibility check:**
- Seeded Possibility requires: viable transformation paths ✓, agent capacity to recognize constructed nature of constraints ✓, cost of transformation high but not impossible ✓
- The "seed" is the secret meetings (TR₁), the alternative furnace (TR₄), the refusal (TR₅) — small acts that demonstrate the system is not natural law

## 2. VOICE ARCHETYPE SELECTION

**Selected: ☑ The Builder**

**Primary Voice: Yusuf ibn Musa**

**Justification:**

Yusuf maps perfectly to Builder archetype:
- **Sees partial system shape:** He experiences C₁ as Snare (χ=0.828 from analytical index equivalent) but recognizes fragmentation is constructed, not natural. His inhabitation sentence: "knowing that the man who grants or denies your requests profits from keeping the knowledge scattered" — this is structural awareness.
- **Tests for leverage points:** TR₁ (secret meetings) is Yusuf initiating collective organization. He's not passively suffering; he's strategically testing whether direct knowledge-sharing can bypass the muqaddam system.
- **Acts strategically within constraints:** He writes petitions (works within system) while simultaneously organizing resistance (tests system boundaries). This is Builder behavior — tactical, observational, adaptive.
- **Voice is tactical, observational, adaptive:** First-person from Yusuf allows reader to experience his strategic thinking while maintaining limited perspective (he doesn't see everything al-Shirazi sees, doesn't have Maryam's resources).

**Attractor compatibility:** ✓ Builder + Seeded Possibility
- Builder voice promises agency that the attractor delivers (transformations are possible)
- Builder's partial vision creates tension (he sees fragmentation is constructed but doesn't see full institutional structure)
- Builder's testing generates plot (each petition, each secret meeting, each refusal is a leverage test)

**Power range check:** Yusuf is X₃ (powerless, π=1.5) for C₁, but his analytical awareness and strategic action align with Builder's moderate power range (0.6 ≤ π ≤ 1.0). Resolution: His powerless structural position is offset by his analytical capacity and willingness to organize. He's powerless within the system but not passive.

**Rationality model:** ✓ BIR required
- Yusuf has bounded information (doesn't know al-Shirazi's full strategy)
- Institutional structure shapes his actions (must petition in Persian)
- Acts rationally given constraints (organizes when petitions fail)

**POV decision:** First person. Builder voice strongest in first person — reader experiences the strategic thinking, the testing, the partial victories and failures directly.

## 3. INDEXICAL REVELATION STRATEGY

**Selected: ☑ Gradual Realization (with Multi-POV Parallel elements)**

**Primary Strategy: Gradual Realization through Yusuf's Builder perspective**

Yusuf begins understanding C₁ (fragmented knowledge) as personal frustration, gradually recognizes it as constructed system, eventually sees how it connects to C₂ (exhaustion) and C₃ (arrangement). The reader follows his discovery process.

**Secondary Strategy: Multi-POV Parallel for indexical variance**

Brief sections from Layla and Maryam's perspectives reveal how the same constraints operate differently:
- **Layla sections** show C₂ (exhaustion) as Snare (χ=1.08) — the physical cost Yusuf doesn't fully experience
- **Maryam sections** show C₁ as Tangled Rope (χ=0.64) — the privilege/complicity dynamic Yusuf doesn't see from his position

**Al-Shirazi:** Seen only from outside, through Yusuf's interactions. Never a viewpoint character. This maintains Builder's partial vision — Yusuf can observe al-Shirazi's actions but not access his internal justifications.

**Revelation arc:**
1. **Act 1:** Yusuf experiences fragmentation as personal problem (needs collaboration, writes petitions, waits)
2. **Act 2:** Yusuf recognizes pattern (al-Shirazi grants some petitions, denies others, no clear logic) → begins testing (secret meetings)
3. **Act 3:** Yusuf sees full structure (fragmentation maintained deliberately, exhaustion prevents organization, arrangement extracts from all) → chooses transformation attempt despite cost

**Reader position:** Stays slightly ahead of Yusuf (sees Layla's exhaustion before Yusuf fully grasps it, sees Maryam's complicity before Yusuf confronts it) but not so far ahead that dramatic tension collapses.

## 4. EDITORIAL DECISIONS

**LENGTH:** ☑ Novelette (7,500-17,500 words)

*Justification:* Three constraints, four viewpoint characters, multiple transformation attempts require space. Flash or short story would compress the gradual realization arc. Novelette allows:
- Yusuf's strategic testing to develop over time
- Layla's physical degradation to be visceral, not summarized
- Maryam's moral reckoning to feel earned
- Secret meetings (TR₁) to build tension across multiple scenes

**POV:** ☑ First Person (Yusuf primary) + ☑ Third Limited (Layla, Maryam sections)

*Justification:* 
- First person for Yusuf maintains Builder voice strength (tactical thinking, strategic observation)
- Third limited for Layla and Maryam provides indexical variance without diluting Yusuf's voice
- Shift in POV style signals shift in perspective (reader knows when they're seeing through different index)

**TENSE:** ☑ Present

*Justification:* Present tense increases immediacy for Builder voice. Yusuf is testing the system *now*, making strategic decisions *now*. Past tense would create retrospective distance that undermines Builder's in-the-moment tactical thinking.

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 4 viewpoint characters (Yusuf primary, Layla secondary, Maryam tertiary, al-Shirazi observed)

*Justification:* Minimum needed to show indexical variance across constraints:
- Yusuf: C₁ Snare, C₃ Snare, Builder attempting transformation
- Layla: C₁ Snare, C₂ Snare, C₃ Snare — shows maximum extraction
- Maryam: C₁ Tangled Rope, C₂ Tangled Rope — shows privilege/complicity
- Al-Shirazi: C₁ Rope, C₂ Rope — shows institutional benefit (observed, not inhabited)

**NAMING:** ☑ Cultural (Arabic names with historical grounding)

*Justification:* Historical setting (1401 Raqqa) requires period-appropriate names. Names already established in Stage 2:
- Yusuf ibn Musa (lens grinder)
- Layla bint Khalil (furnace operator)
- Maryam al-Raqqi (colorant specialist)
- Hasan al-Shirazi (muqaddam)

**LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:**

Code-switching (Arabic/Persian) occurs:

**When:**
- Yusuf writes petitions: switches mid-sentence from Arabic (craft description) to Persian (formal request)
- Yusuf speaks to al-Shirazi: Persian for official business, Arabic slips through in frustration
- Secret meetings: Arabic only (first time Yusuf speaks craft language without switching in two years)
- Layla's internal monologue: Arabic, but Persian administrative terms intrude (she thinks in the language of the system even when alone)

**What it signals:**
- Arabic → Persian: submission, performing powerlessness, acknowledging occupation
- Persian → Arabic: frustration breaking through, system failing to contain
- Arabic only (secret meetings): resistance, reclaiming language, collective space outside system
- Persian terms in Arabic thought: internalization of constraint, system colonizing internal life

**Example:**
> I need the cobalt formula — *al-ṣīgha al-kuḥliyya* — for the lens, but to request it I must write: *'arz-e man barā-ye dastresī be māl-e rangī* (my petition for access to colorant material). The craft has a name in my language. The system has a procedure in theirs.

**EMOTIONAL CORE:** (emerges from constraint dynamics)

Not imposed, but predicted from structure:
- **Yusuf:** Frustration → strategic hope → costly determination (Builder arc)
- **Layla:** Exhaustion → desperation → fragile relief (if TR₄ succeeds) or collapse (if TR₃ completes)
- **Maryam:** Comfort → guilt → active complicity-breaking (if she executes TR₄)
- **Reader:** Growing recognition that the system is constructed, not natural; that transformation is possible but costly; that the characters' choices matter

**ENDING STRATEGY:** ☑ Seeded Possibility demonstrated (transformation attempted, outcome uncertain but viable)

The story ends with:
- TR₁ (secret meetings) producing first complete lens outside muqaddam system
- TR₄ (alternative furnace) operational but expensive, unsustainable long-term
- TR₅ (Yusuf's refusal) enacted, consequences beginning but not resolved
- System still standing but demonstrated to be non-natural
- Seed planted: other artisans see the collective lens, know it's possible

Not: revolution succeeds, system collapses, everyone liberated
Not: tragedy, everyone crushed, system wins
But: the wall has a crack, and everyone saw it form

## 5. PRIMARY PHYSICAL MARKER

**The petition ink drying on Yusuf's fingers**

**Rationale:**

This marker emerges directly from the inhabitation sentence: "every day you write petitions in the conqueror's language to access the rest."

**What it reveals:**
- **To Yusuf (early):** Just the mechanics of the system. He writes, he waits, he works. The ink is neutral.
- **To Yusuf (middle):** The ink starts to feel like a stain. He's writing in Persian, performing submission, and the physical act of writing becomes the physical act of participating in his own extraction.
- **To Yusuf (late):** When he stops writing petitions (TR₅), the absence of ink on his fingers is the first physical sign of refusal. His hands are clean of Persian script for the first time in two years.
- **To reader:** The ink tracks Yusuf's relationship to the system. When he's writing petitions, he's inside the constraint. When his fingers are clean, he's testing exit.

**Physical specificity:**
- Iron gall ink (period-appropriate, black, permanent)
- Stains fingers for hours after writing
- Yusuf notices it when he's grinding lenses (the ink smudges on the glass)
- Other artisans have the same stains (visual marker of shared constraint)
- Layla doesn't write petitions (can't read/write Persian) — her hands are clean of ink but scarred from furnace work (different physical marker, different constraint)

**Scenes where it appears:**
1. Opening: Yusuf writing petition, ink drying, he doesn't think about it
2. Middle: Yusuf grinding lens, sees ink smudge on glass, realizes he's marking his work with the language of submission
3. Secret meeting: Yusuf notices everyone's hands have ink stains except Layla's (she can't write petitions, she's trapped differently)
4. Refusal: Yusuf's hands clean for the first time, he notices the absence, it feels like freedom and terror simultaneously
5. Ending: Another artisan's hands, ink-stained, holding the collective lens — the system continues, but the lens proves it doesn't have to
