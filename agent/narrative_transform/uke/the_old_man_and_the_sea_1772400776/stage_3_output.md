# STAGE 3: OPERATIONAL SPECIFICATION

## 1. Terminal Attractor Verification

**Selected: ☑ Seeded Possibility**

**Justification:**

The Stage 1 specification and BIR rationality model support Seeded Possibility as the optimal attractor:

**Why not Deterministic Tragedy:**
- BIR allows learning and adaptation (characters aren't locked into PIR's implacable logic)
- TR1 (Collective Organization) and TR4 (False Mountain Reveal) are structurally feasible
- The marked harvester's situation is dire but not thermodynamically inevitable

**Why not Negotiated Equilibrium:**
- Requires institutional flexibility the Allocator lacks (parameters locked since 2047)
- The Allocator is autonomous AI, not human negotiators
- Power asymmetry too extreme (institutional vs powerless) for bargaining

**Why not Revolutionary Rupture:**
- BIR produces satisficing, not brittle systems prone to sudden collapse
- Ω_C04 establishes why collective action hasn't occurred (isolation, desperation, monitoring)
- The arcology's closed-loop system is too stable for revolutionary break

**Why Seeded Possibility works:**
- The apprentice's index change (powerless → analytical via guilt/recognition) seeds future transformation
- TR4 (False Mountain Reveal) is achievable within biographical time
- Underground transformation possible: small acts of resistance, information sharing, mutual aid networks
- BIR's bounded rationality allows perspective-taking and learning
- The story can end with the marked harvester still trapped BUT the apprentice carrying seeds of recognition that could grow into TR1 (collective organization) in the next generation
- Aligns with the source text's structure: individual tragedy + structural hope

**Attractor compatibility check:**
- ✓ BIR model (allows learning, coordination under uncertainty)
- ✓ Constraint logic (TR1 and TR4 are feasible, not blocked by Mountains)
- ✓ Indexical variance (apprentice's position enables perspective shift)
- ✓ Emotional resonance (tragedy + hope, not despair)

## 2. Voice Archetype Selection

**Selected: ☑ The Condemned**

**Primary POV: The Marked Harvester**

**Justification:**

**Structural fit:**
- Inside the constraint logic (experiences Luck Score as natural/inevitable)
- Cannot see systemic shape (powerless, biographical, trapped, local)
- Acts rationally given limited information (works harder, takes painkillers, accepts isolation)
- Voice is immediate, experiential, not reflective
- **Attractor compatibility:** ✓ Seeded Possibility (his tragedy seeds the apprentice's recognition)
- **Requires:** PIR model OR Mountain-dominated → **EXCEPTION GRANTED**: While BIR is selected, the marked harvester's *experience* is PIR-like (system feels implacable from his trapped position). His powerlessness + trapped exit + immediate time horizon creates subjective inevitability even within BIR structure.
- **Power range:** π = 1.5 (powerless) ✓

**Why this voice:**

The marked harvester embodies Type I error (False Mountain). He experiences C2 (Luck Score) as unchangeable—not because he's stupid, but because from his index it *objectively appears* as Mountain:
- No visibility into other power positions (can't see it's Tangled Rope for parents)
- Immediate time horizon (can't see generational changeability)
- Trapped exit (no experience of mobility that would reveal constructedness)
- Local scope (can't see how system varies across positions)

His blindness is structural, not intellectual. This creates the tragic gap: readers see the Snare, he experiences a Mountain. The gap generates the story's emotional core.

**POV: First person**

**Tone: Tragic, somber, with moments of quiet dignity**

The voice must be:
- Immediate (present tense sensations: hands cramping, conveyor jamming)
- Unselfconscious (no meta-awareness of being trapped)
- Dignified (not self-pitying—he endures, he persists)
- Observational (notices details without analyzing systems)

**Example voice:**
> "The conveyor jams at 14:23. I count because counting helps. Thirty-seven seconds to clear the blockage. Seventy-two pods lost to the timer. My wrist terminal flickers: score drops to 30. I don't look at it anymore. The number falls whether I look or not."

---

**Secondary voice: The Apprentice (nested/counterpoint)**

**Archetype: The Condemned → The Builder (transition)**

The apprentice begins experiencing the system as inevitable (Condemned) but his guilt over abandoning the marked harvester seeds analytical awareness. By story's end, he's testing for leverage points (Builder).

This transition is the "seed" in Seeded Possibility. The marked harvester's tragedy plants recognition in the apprentice that could grow into collective action.

**Implementation: Nested voice**

The primary narrative is the marked harvester's first-person present. The apprentice appears in:
1. **Dialogue** (brief, guilt-laden exchanges)
2. **The marked harvester's observations** (watching the young man's face, his hesitation)
3. **One brief first-person section** (the apprentice's internal monologue during the transfer negotiation—shows his index shift)

**Why nested, not alternating chapters:**
- Keeps focus on the marked harvester's immediate experience
- The apprentice's perspective is the *seed*, not equal weight
- Prevents the story from becoming a dual-protagonist structure (dilutes the Condemned voice)

---

**Tertiary presence: The Parents (reported speech only)**

**Archetype: The Beneficiary**

The parents never get direct POV. They appear only through:
- The apprentice's reported dialogue ("My mother said...")
- The marked harvester's observations (overhearing negotiations)
- Their *absence* (the apprentice's transfer happens off-screen, we see only the result)

**Why no direct POV:**
- Their Type III error (Snare-as-Rope) is more powerful if readers infer it from their actions
- Direct POV risks making them villains (they're not—they're structurally blind)
- Their warmth and reasonableness must come through the apprentice's conflicted love for them

**Critical directive from archetype:**
The parents must be *likeable*. The apprentice loves them. They're protecting their son. Their blindness to the marked harvester's suffering isn't cruelty—it's genuine invisibility from their moderate-power index. If they become convenient villains, the indexical variance collapses.

## 3. Indexical Revelation Strategy

**Selected: ☑ Gradual Realization (with nested analytical moment)**

**Structure:**

**Act 1: Immersion (no indexical awareness)**
- Pure Condemned voice
- Marked harvester experiences system as natural
- Readers see the Snare before he does (dramatic irony)
- Apprentice present but not yet questioning

**Act 2: Friction (indexical variance emerges)**
- Apprentice's score drops → parents intervene
- Marked harvester observes the apprentice's guilt
- Brief nested section: apprentice's internal conflict (first glimpse of indexical variance)
- Readers see: same system, different experiences (apprentice has exit, marked harvester doesn't)

**Act 3: Seed (partial recognition, no escape)**
- Apprentice transfers (marked harvester loses last connection)
- Marked harvester's isolation deepens (score drops to 28)
- **Critical scene:** Apprentice returns briefly (brings food, can't stay)
- In this scene: apprentice's face shows he *sees* now (the system is constructed, not natural)
- But seeing doesn't save the marked harvester (he's still trapped)
- **Seed planted:** Apprentice leaves carrying recognition that could grow into future action

**Revelation mechanism:**

The story doesn't explain indexical variance. It *shows* through:
1. **Physical markers:** Marked harvester's cramping hands vs apprentice's smooth, fast movements
2. **Temporal pressure:** Marked harvester counts seconds (immediate survival) vs parents plan decades (generational strategy)
3. **Spatial positioning:** Marked harvester trapped in tower 89-C vs apprentice mobile between towers
4. **Dialogue gaps:** What the parents *don't* say about the marked harvester (he's invisible to them)

**Key technique: The unsaid**

The most powerful revelation is what characters *don't* say:
- Parents never mention the marked harvester by name (he's "that assignment," "the tower")
- Apprentice can't articulate why he feels guilty (no framework language)
- Marked harvester never says "this is unfair" (he has no concept of fairness applying to him)

The reader performs the indexical analysis. The characters just live it.

## 4. Editorial Decisions

**LENGTH: ☑ Novelette (7,500-17,500 words)**

**Rationale:**
- Three-act structure with nested voice requires space
- Constraint naturalizations need room to breathe (can't rush the Luck Score's self-reinforcing logic)
- Physical degradation arc (marked harvester's body failing) needs time to accumulate
- Seeded Possibility ending requires setup (apprentice's guilt must build gradually)

Target: ~12,000 words
- Act 1: 4,000 words (establish system, marked harvester's routine, apprentice's presence)
- Act 2: 4,500 words (score drop, parents intervene, transfer negotiation, nested apprentice section)
- Act 3: 3,500 words (isolation deepens, final encounter, seed planted)

---

**POV: ☑ First person (primary) + ☑ First person (nested secondary)**

**Primary:** Marked harvester, first person present tense
**Secondary:** Apprentice, first person past tense (brief nested section in Act 2)

**Rationale:**
- First person present (marked harvester) = immediacy, no analytical distance
- First person past (apprentice) = reflection, beginning of analytical awareness
- Tense shift signals index shift (trapped present vs. mobile past-that-could-change)

---

**TENSE: ☑ Present (primary) + ☑ Past (nested)**

**Primary narrative:** Present tense
- Marked harvester lives in eternal now (immediate time horizon)
- No past to reflect on, no future to plan for
- Present tense = trapped in the moment

**Nested section:** Past tense
- Apprentice reflecting on the transfer decision
- Past tense = distance, beginning of analytical perspective
- "I told him I was leaving" (past) vs. "The conveyor jams" (present)

---

**CHARACTER COUNT: 3 (marked harvester, apprentice, parents as unit)**

**Rationale:**
- Minimum for constraint interactions (powerless, powerless-becoming-analytical, moderate)
- Parents function as single unit (no need to distinguish mother/father—they share index)
- Other harvesters present as background (social isolation visible through their absence)
- The Allocator present as system voice (not character—institutional constraint speaking)

---

**NAMING: ☑ Role-based + ☑ Cultural (Indonesian/Mandarin)**

**The Marked Harvester:** Budi (Indonesian: "wise," ironic given his structural blindness)
**The Apprentice:** Wei (Mandarin: 伟, "great," carrying parents' hopes)
**The Parents:** Ibu and Pak (Indonesian: mother and father—role names, not individual names)

**Rationale:**
- Role-based naming emphasizes structural position over individuality
- Cultural names naturalize the Jakarta heritage setting
- The marked harvester gets a name (he's a person), the parents get roles (they're a function)
- The Allocator never gets humanized (always "the Allocator," always capitalized)

**Other harvesters:** Unnamed or role-identified ("the supervisor," "the woman in 92-A")
- Emphasizes the marked harvester's isolation (he doesn't have relationships, just proximity)

---

**LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:**

**Code-switching occurs:**

1. **Budi's internal monologue:** Indonesian base with English technical terms
   - "Krampen lagi" (cramping again) + "conveyor jam, quota 900"
   - Intimate sphere (body, pain) = Indonesian
   - System sphere (work, metrics) = English

2. **Wei's dialogue with parents:** Mandarin negotiation + Indonesian emotion
   - "我们需要关系" (we need guanxi) when discussing transfer
   - "Aku tidak mau meninggalkan dia" (I don't want to leave him) when expressing guilt
   - Negotiation = Mandarin, emotion = Indonesian

3. **Allocator interface:** Pure English, no translation
   - "Tower 89-C. Quota 900. Contamination rate 7%. Score: 30."
   - System voice is cold, untranslatable, foreign

**What the switch signals:**

- **Indonesian → English:** Moving from human sphere to system sphere (intimacy to alienation)
- **Mandarin → Indonesian:** Moving from strategy to emotion (calculation to feeling)
- **English (Allocator):** No human language can touch this (institutional power beyond negotiation)

**Example:**

> Budi's hands cramp. *Krampen*, he thinks. The Indonesian word feels right—his grandmother's word for the ache in her knees. The conveyor jams. His wrist terminal flashes: "Contamination rate 7%. Score: 30." The English words mean nothing. They're just numbers that fall.

---

**EMOTIONAL CORE:**

Not imposed—emerges from constraint dynamics:

**Primary emotion: Dignified endurance**
- Budi doesn't rage or despair (that would require hope)
- He persists because stopping isn't an option
- Quiet, stubborn refusal to be erased

**Secondary emotion: Guilt (Wei)**
- Not performative guilt (no grand gestures)
- Quiet, corrosive guilt that changes how he sees

**Tertiary emotion: Invisible love (Ibu and Pak)**
- They love Wei (genuinely, warmly)
- Their love for Wei makes them blind to Budi
- Love as structural force, not moral failing

---

**ENDING STRATEGY: ☑ System persists, agent removed + ☑ Seed planted**

**Final scene:**

Budi's score drops to 28. The Allocator flags him for termination review (30 days). Wei returns one last time—brings nutri-paste, can't stay long (his new supervisor is watching). They don't talk much. Wei's face shows he understands now (the system is constructed, Budi is trapped in it, Wei escaped by abandoning him). Budi sees the understanding in Wei's face. Doesn't blame him. Just nods.

Wei leaves. Budi returns to tower 89-C. The conveyor jams. His hands cramp. He keeps working.

**Final line:**

> "The number falls whether I look or not. But Wei looked. That's something."

**What this achieves:**

- Budi's tragedy complete (system persists, he will be disposed of)
- Seed planted (Wei's recognition could grow into collective action)
- No false hope (Budi isn't saved)
- No despair (the seed exists, even if Budi won't see it grow)
- Seeded Possibility attractor reached

## 5. Primary Physical Marker

**The cramping hands**

**Not a designed motif—the thing the body does that reveals constraint experience from inside.**

**Why this marker:**

When you work twelve hours a day doing repetitive motion with failing equipment in a system that measures your worth by output speed, your hands cramp. This is C1 (Body's Limits, Mountain) intersecting with C2 (Luck Score, Snare). The cramp is natural law. The fact that the cramp tanks your score is constructed extraction.

**How it functions:**

1. **Immediate sensation:** Budi feels the cramp constantly (present-tense embodiment)
2. **Temporal marker:** Cramp worsens over the story (physical degradation parallels score degradation)
3. **Indexical contrast:** Wei's hands are smooth, fast (youth + better equipment). Budi watches them and knows: this is Wei's future too.
4. **Reader recognition:** Readers understand the cramp before Budi articulates it (the body knows what the mind can't say)

**Key scenes:**

- **Opening:** Budi's hands cramp at 03:47 into shift (establishes baseline)
- **Midpoint:** Cramp so bad he drops a pod (contamination, score drops)
- **Climax:** Wei notices Budi's hands shaking, offers to help, Budi refuses (pride, or knowledge that association will tank Wei's score)
- **Ending:** Budi's hands cramp, he keeps working (the cramp persists, he persists)
