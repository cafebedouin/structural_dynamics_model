## 2. VOICE ARCHETYPE SELECTION

**Primary Voice:** The Condemned (Ah-Yong, X₁)

**Justification:**
- **Inside constraint logic, cannot see systemic shape:** Ah-Yong experiences C₁ (contract), C₂ (bodily degradation), C₃ (fumigation) as inevitable. She has no analytical register to name extraction or recognize the transformation occurring within her.
- **Acts rationally given limited information:** Her decisions (signing contract, accepting *fei-gong* status, remaining during fumigation) are rational from her structural position (powerless, trapped, immediate time horizon).
- **Voice is immediate, experiential, not reflective:** Close-third limited POV keeps reader inside her physical experience without analytical distance.
- **Attractor compatibility:** ✓ Seeded Possibility. The Condemned's blindness to her own transformation is structural — she cannot see what C₂ has made her because she has no framework for ontological shift. The seed grows in darkness.

**Secondary Voice:** The Witness (Dr. Morrison, X₃)

**Justification:**
- **Observes constraint dynamics without direct stake:** Morrison sees the pattern (C₁→C₂ coupling, systematic bodily destruction) but cannot intervene.
- **Can see multiple indices simultaneously:** Documents worker experience (X₁), owner rationalization (X₂), institutional logic (X₄).
- **Voice is documentary, analytical, clinical:** Provides structural context Ah-Yong cannot access.
- **Attractor compatibility:** ✓ Seeded Possibility. Morrison sees the surface trajectory clearly but cannot detect the underground transformation. His analytical capacity stops at the material level.

**Voice Combination Strategy:** Nested Voice
- **Primary:** Ah-Yong (Condemned) — close-third limited, present tense, immediate experience
- **Frame:** Morrison (Witness) — third limited, past tense, retrospective documentation
- **Structure:** Morrison's medical records frame Ah-Yong's immediate experience. He documents what he can see (bodily degradation, protocol enforcement). She experiences what he cannot measure (the transformation occurring beneath the metrics).

**Compatibility Check:**
- ✓ Condemned + Seeded Possibility + close-third limited = seed visible but unstated (voice has no future tense, no analytical register to name transformation)
- ✓ Witness framing prevents essay collapse (Morrison documents, does not explain)
- ✓ Gap between voices generates story engine: Morrison sees pattern, Ah-Yong experiences transformation, neither can name what is actually happening

## 3. INDEXICAL REVELATION STRATEGY

**Selected Strategy:** Late Revelation with Nested Witness Frame

**Mechanism:**
- **Act 1:** Morrison's frame establishes pattern (C₁→C₂ coupling, systematic degradation). Ah-Yong's immediate experience shows constraint activation.
- **Act 2:** Ah-Yong's POV dominates. Reader experiences C₂ (bodily transformation) and C₃ (fumigation) from inside. Morrison's frame provides periodic structural context but cannot access Ah-Yong's interior state.
- **Act 3:** Morrison's frame returns. He documents outcome (survival/death under protocol). Final beat reveals what neither voice could see: the transformation that occurred during C₂ made Ah-Yong's body unreadable to C₃'s logic.

**Revelation Timing:**
- **Gradual (Acts 1-2):** Reader sees indexical variance between Morrison (analytical, π=1.15) and Ah-Yong (powerless, π=1.5) experiencing same constraints differently.
- **Late (Act 3):** The ontological transformation is revealed retrospectively. Neither Morrison nor Ah-Yong could detect it while it was occurring. Reader discovers it through outcome, not explanation.

**Anti-Pattern Prevention:**
- ✗ No character explicitly aware of indexical variance (would collapse into analytical observer)
- ✗ No multi-POV parallel (would make variance too visible too early)
- ✗ No gradual realization arc (Condemned voice cannot achieve analytical awareness)

## 4. EDITORIAL DECISIONS

**LENGTH:** Short story (4,000-5,000 words)
- Justification: Three-act structure with nested frame requires space for both Morrison's documentation and Ah-Yong's immediate experience. Constraint cascade (C₁→C₂→C₃) needs room to develop without rushing.

**POV:** 
- **Primary:** Third limited (Ah-Yong) — close, present tense
- **Frame:** Third limited (Morrison) — past tense, retrospective
- Justification: Close-third for Ah-Yong maintains immediacy without first-person's analytical demands. Morrison's frame provides structural context without omniscient collapse.

**TENSE:**
- **Primary (Ah-Yong):** Present tense — immediacy, no future horizon
- **Frame (Morrison):** Past tense — retrospective documentation
- Justification: Tense shift marks voice transition. Present tense traps reader in Ah-Yong's immediate experience. Past tense signals Morrison's analytical distance.

**CHARACTER COUNT:** 4 primary (Ah-Yong, Morrison, Patterson, Harding)
- X₁ (Ah-Yong): Condemned voice, primary POV
- X₃ (Morrison): Witness voice, frame POV
- X₂ (Patterson): Beneficiary (visible in background, no POV)
- X₄ (Harding): System voice (visible through protocol enforcement, no POV)
- Justification: Minimum necessary for constraint interactions. Patterson and Harding appear in scenes but do not hold POV — reader sees them through Morrison's and Ah-Yong's perspectives.

**NAMING:**
- **Cultural naming:** Ah-Yong (Cantonese given name, no surname — marks her social position)
- **Western naming:** Patterson, Morrison, Harding (full surnames, marks institutional authority)
- **Role-based terms:** *fei-gong* (廢工), consular police, workshop manager
- Justification: Naming strategy naturalizes power asymmetry. Ah-Yong has no surname in the text because she has no institutional identity. Western characters have surnames because they operate through institutional roles.

**LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:**

**Code-switching occurs:**
1. **Worker-to-worker dialogue:** Cantonese (rendered in English with Cantonese grammatical features)
   - Example: "The smoke, cannot breathe through cloth. Three day, maybe we die here."
   - Topic-prominent structure, aspect markers, measure words retained

2. **Worker-to-authority dialogue:** Halting institutional English
   - Example: "Yes, sir. I understand protocol. I stay in workshop."
   - Grammatically correct but stripped of personal markers

3. **Morrison's internal voice:** Shifts between languages depending on who he's thinking about
   - Thinking about patients: Cantonese-inflected English ("The *fei-gong* workers, their lungs already ruined")
   - Writing to authorities: Formal institutional English ("I must object to the fumigation protocol on medical grounds")

**The switch signals:**
- **Cantonese → intimacy, shared vulnerability, private speech among powerless**
- **English → performance of compliance, interaction with power, official discourse**
- **Morrison's code-switching → his position between worlds, able to see both but change neither**

**EMOTIONAL CORE:** (Emerges from constraint dynamics, not imposed)
- **Primary:** The gap between what Morrison can document and what Ah-Yong experiences
- **Secondary:** The transformation occurring beneath both their awareness
- **Tertiary:** The institutional logic that reads bodies as data points

**ENDING STRATEGY:** 
- ☐ Attractor reached (transformation revealed retrospectively)
- ☐ System persists, agent removed (protocol completes, outcome ambiguous until final beat)
- **Selected:** Seeded Possibility attractor reached through late revelation. Morrison documents outcome. Reader discovers transformation through what the protocol could not detect.

## 5. INHABITATION SPEC

**Ah-Yong (X₁) — Phenomenological Register:**

**Mode of attention:** Hypervigilant to breath, heat, and time remaining.

What kind of noticing does this character do?
- Counts breaths when climbing stairs to the attic
- Tracks the quality of air (coal smoke vs. steam vs. clean morning air from the river)
- Measures time in shifts, contract years, days until fumigation ends
- Notices which other workers have developed the cough, how long until they transition to *fei-gong*
- Feels heat as weight on the chest, not just temperature

What does the constraint feel like in her body?
- C₁ (contract): A tightness in the throat when speaking English to Patterson. The weight of the contract is in her voice — she cannot say what she means in the language that binds her.
- C₂ (bodily degradation): The cough is not just a symptom. It is a clock. Each coughing fit marks time passing, body changing, contract approaching its end while the body becomes unemployable.
- C₃ (fumigation): Sulfur smoke is not just choking — it is the feeling of the protocol entering her lungs, the institution making itself physical inside her body.

**Anti-pattern check:**
- ✓ Not a metric: "Breath count" is not a number that changes over time. It is a mode of attention — she notices breath because breath is uncertain.
- ✓ Mode of attention, not device: The spec does not tell Stage 4 to repeat "she counted her breaths." It tells Stage 4 that this character pays attention to breathing in a way that reveals her structural position.

---

**Dr. Morrison (X₃) — Phenomenological Register:**

**Mode of attention:** Clinical observation layered with moral distress.

What kind of noticing does this character do?
- Sees bodies as both patients and data points
- Tracks patterns across populations (five years → respiratory failure)
- Notices what his medical training cannot fix (the pattern is clear, the intervention is blocked)
- Feels the gap between diagnosis and treatment as professional failure

What does the constraint feel like in his body?
- C₂ (bodily degradation): He sees it in others, documents it, cannot stop it. The constraint is not in his body — it is in the gap between his knowledge and his power.
- C₃ (fumigation protocol): He experiences it as institutional override. His medical objection is heard and dismissed. The constraint is the feeling of being correct and irrelevant simultaneously.

**Anti-pattern check:**
- ✓ Not a metric: "Pattern recognition" is not a number. It is a mode of attention shaped by medical training and moral position.
- ✓ Mode of attention, not device: Morrison's clinical gaze is not a technique to deploy. It is how he sees the world, and the story emerges from what that way of seeing cannot change.
