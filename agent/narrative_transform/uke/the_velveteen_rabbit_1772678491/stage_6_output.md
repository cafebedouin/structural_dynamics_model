# STRATEGY BRIEF

**IMPLIED AUTHOR:**

A documentary sensibility that trusts accumulation over interpretation. This author notices: medical measurements that fail to explain, economic calculations that reveal themselves, the specific weight of smoke in damaged lungs, the gap between protocol language and bodily experience. Skips: moral commentary, psychological interiority beyond what breath-counting reveals, explanations of what scenes mean. The prose lives in ledgers, journals, and physical sensation—categories that claim to contain experience but cannot.

**AUDIENCE:**

Readers who recognize institutional violence operates through procedure, not malice. Comfortable with epistemic failure as outcome rather than problem to solve. Expect historical fiction to show how power works without resolving into contemporary moral lessons. Will tolerate medical and economic detail if it does structural work. Need the impossible recovery to remain impossible—explanation would be failure.

**SCOPE:**

Novella. Current 11,200 words can compress to 9,500-10,000 through Part V tightening and micro-level efficiency gains. The thirteen-month span, multi-perspectival structure, and documentary form require this length. Ending opens rather than closes: Ah-Yong walks toward something the story cannot see, carrying memory of being outside categories she's returned to living inside.

It refuses to resolve the tension between Morrison's documentation and his complicity. These refusals are the point—they show how institutional violence operates through people doing their jobs correctly.

Deepen by trusting these refusals more completely. Part V still hedges by having Liang explain what the scene shows. The final movement (Part VI) already demonstrates the deepening: Ah-Yong's phenomenological account ("something let go") resists Morrison's medical framework without replacing it with another explanation. Extend this trust backward into Part V.
