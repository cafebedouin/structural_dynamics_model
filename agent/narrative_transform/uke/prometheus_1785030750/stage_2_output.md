# STAGE 2: NATURALIZATION

## Step 0 — Invariant Recovery (worked, not shown in output)

**Detector A (untranslatable real):** Present but marked non-primary in the Stage 0 contract — a capacity for self-directed growth that, once granted, generates futures its grantor cannot predict or revoke. This is a Mountain a character *reaches for* (it is what the punished agent actually did, and what the whole system fears), so it is invariant, not backdrop — but it is subordinate to the floor per the dominance ordering.

**Detector B (missing floor) — PRIMARY, per Stage 0 contract:** The right to rule is assumed to belong to the current holder of power; this obscures that the position was seized by force, not derived from shared principle. This is the Tier-1 real. It must be the ground the world stands on, not a thing any instrument reads.

**Inherent instrument:** NO (Stage 0 flag explicit). Therefore the Scored-Snare gate applies at default: C3's punishment-Snare must NOT be naturalized as a score, index, or ledger entry. Extraction must be felt as consequence and sensation — a hand closing, a place made unreachable — never as arithmetic.

**Break contract:** Target prior — a rebellion against an unjust system is expected to end in the system's destruction; instead it is assimilated into compromise that preserves the system. The setting must leave this executable: there must be a real coordination function worth preserving (so that destroying the whole order is a live temptation the story can decline), not a pure tyranny with nothing worth saving.

**Affordance test on setting choice:** Reject any setting where "who has the right to rule" is a fact a sufficiently good historian, census, or lawyer could establish. The floor must be a *founding silence* — a thing the culture's own record-keeping apparatus was built to not-ask, because asking it would dissolve the apparatus's authority to keep records at all.

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

### Invariant Contract (carried forward verbatim)

**The floor invariant:** The world contains no answer, anywhere in its instruments, to *who first had the right to divide the water.* Every institution that measures, allots, and punishes water-use presupposes an original division whose legitimacy cannot be audited — because auditing it would require standing outside the division, and no such standing exists inside the world's own record. **Falsifier:** if the finished story reveals a "true" original allotment that a wronged party could point to and say *this was always rightfully mine, the record simply lied*, the floor invariant has collapsed into a correctable clerical error and the naturalization has failed.

**The subordinate grain (untranslatable real):** A person, once taught to read the deep channels — to sense where the water will go before it goes there — carries that reading in their body permanently. No river-warden, however senior, can revoke a trained eye once it has opened. This capacity generates predictions the teacher cannot control and did not license. It is present in the world, it matters to the ending, but it resolves and recedes before the floor's question is allowed to stand alone in the final movement.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You kneel at the head-gate at dawn with your palm flat on wood worn smooth by four generations of palms before yours, feeling for the give in the current that will tell you how much water passes today — and nowhere in the ledger, the oath, the shrine, or the warden's seal is there any account of who first had the right to put a gate here at all.*

---

### The Setting

The Kethrani Vale is a terraced river valley, three days' walk end to end, where eleven villages share a single snowmelt river through a network of stone head-gates, sluice channels, and timed release-rights inherited from a founding "Division" no living person witnessed and no surviving document describes in full. It is late in a long dry epoch — perhaps four centuries into settlement, in a highland culture with its own script, its own calendar of flood-months and starve-months, no likely resemblance to any real-world hydraulic civilization, its houses built of river-stone and packed loess, its wealth counted in irrigated terrace-days rather than coin.

The valley is governed by the Kesh-Ordinate, a hereditary river-tribunal seated at the valley's single natural chokepoint, the Throat, where the river narrows enough that one structure — the Greater Gate — can, in principle, starve every terrace below it. The Kesh-Ordinate does not claim to have built the Greater Gate. It claims custody: that it *received* the Division from the "First Wardens," whose names are recited but whose actual founding act — whether conquest, negotiation, theft, or gift — is not recorded in any surviving text, only asserted in liturgy that begins *"as it was measured, so it remains."* This is the floor: the assumption that whoever currently holds the Gate holds it by right, when the record of how the holding began does not exist and the liturgy is structured precisely so that no one can ask.

Beneath the Kesh-Ordinate sit the Sluice-Wardens, hereditary gate-keepers for each of the eleven villages — people of real local standing, literate in the release-schedules, empowered to open and close their own village's channel, answerable to the Ordinate but not interchangeable with the powerless. Below them, the terrace-workers: families who hold no gate, no schedule-authority, no vote in the twice-yearly Reckoning, and whose terraces simply receive what the schedule allots or don't.

The valley's practice of *reading the channel* — training a young person to feel, by hand and eye alone, how the deep current will move before instruments confirm it — is the valley's oldest craft and its most destabilizing secret. A trained reader can anticipate drought, predict where a hidden spring will surface, sense a coming flood two days before the water-marks show it. The skill cannot be un-taught, revoked, or confiscated once it takes; it belongs entirely to the body that holds it. The Ordinate tolerates readers because their predictions keep the whole valley alive — but a reader who reads *against* the schedule, who tells a starved village where an unlisted spring runs, has done something no punishment can retroactively undo. The knowledge is already out, already in bodies, already spreading by word of mouth from terrace to terrace.

### Constraint Reference Table

**C1 → The Division** (naturalized as: the founding allotment of water-rights, recited but never re-derived)

The Division is the hereditary schedule itself — which village receives water on which of the twelve moon-turns, how many gate-hours per terrace-row, whose ancestor stood where when "it was measured." It is maintained through the twice-yearly Reckoning, a ritualized recitation-and-adjustment ceremony at the Throat, attended under compulsion by every Sluice-Warden.

- **Agent_A (X1) → Kesh-Ordinate, the Warden-Speaker.** Experiences the Division as pure coordination: without it, eleven villages would drown each other's terraces in flood-months and murder each other in starve-months. The Speaker genuinely believes — and is not wrong, locally — that the Division prevents war. From the Throat, the schedule is a Rope: a working thing you protect, not a weapon you wield.
- **Agent_B (X2) → the Sluice-Warden of the lowest terrace-village, Torn Hollow.** Experiences the Division as a Tangled Rope: the schedule genuinely keeps Torn Hollow from being wiped out by upstream villages taking everything in dry years, *and* genuinely gives Torn Hollow the shortest, latest, least reliable gate-hours of any village in the valley. The Warden defends the schedule in council and curses it in private, often within the same hour.
- **Agent_C / Agent_D (X3, X4) → terrace-workers of Torn Hollow, one young (a channel-reader in training) and one old (a parent who has worked the same allotment for thirty years).** Experience the Division as immovable weather — something you plan your whole life around, the way you plan around the length of winter. Neither has ever seen the schedule questioned successfully in living memory. It is, to them, simply how water is.

**C2 → Sealed Knowledge** (naturalized as: the prohibition on teaching the deep-channel reading to anyone outside a licensed Reader's own bloodline, and the specific prohibition on reading *for* an unlicensed village)

The Ordinate does not ban channel-reading outright — that would be impossible to enforce and would blind the valley to real drought. Instead, reading is licensed, bloodline-restricted, and its findings must be reported to the Ordinate before being acted on. A reader who senses an unlisted spring, or predicts a flood two days early, and tells their own unlicensed village directly — bypassing the Ordinate's confirmation — has broken Sealed Knowledge.

- **Agent_C (X3), the young Torn Hollow reader-in-training,** experiences Sealed Knowledge as identity-lock: the prohibition isn't a rule she resents from outside, it's woven into what a "proper" reader is supposed to be — trained to distrust her own certainty until the Ordinate confirms it, taught that reading-for-your-own-people-first is a kind of theft from the whole valley. She has partly become the rule.
- **Agent_A (X1), the Warden-Speaker,** experiences Sealed Knowledge as clean coordination: readers report centrally so that no village acts on a false or self-serving reading, so that drought response is valley-wide rather than a scramble.
- **Agent_B (X2), the Sluice-Warden,** sees it analytically and uneasily: he is powerful enough to notice that "report first, act second" reliably means his own dying terraces wait for confirmation while upstream villages, whose Wardens sit closer to the Throat, seem to get quiet early warnings anyway.

**C1 (recurring, in dominant register) → Terminal enforcement, felt not counted.**

**C3 → The Drowning-Silence** (naturalized as: the punishment for a Sluice-Warden who breaks Sealed Knowledge — not a fine, not a trial with a verdict anyone reads aloud, but the physical closing of that Warden's own village gate by Ordinate order, for a duration the Ordinate alone decides, with no published schedule of return)

This is C3, and it is built explicitly to fail the Scored-Snare exception (no `inherent_instrument` was granted) — so it is never a number. No one in the valley says "forty days." The Drowning-Silence is known by what it does: the gate is shut, the terraces below it visibly, physically dry day by day, the color of the loess changing, the smell changing, children sent to beg water from neighboring villages who are permitted — even encouraged — to refuse. It ends when the Ordinate decides it ends, and the ending is announced by the sound of the gate opening, not by any prior date anyone could have counted toward.

- **Agent_B (X2), if and when he breaks Sealed Knowledge** (see transformation sequence below), experiences the Drowning-Silence as absolute — the total collapse of every standing he held as Sluice-Warden into raw powerlessness overnight. The man who spoke at council now stands at the shut gate with everyone else.
- **Agent_A (X1)** experiences enforcing the Drowning-Silence as *maintenance*, not cruelty — the gate is shut the way you'd quarantine a sick animal, a regrettable but perfectly ordinary function of holding the Throat. This is the Naturalized register: the highest-extraction act in the valley, performed by the person to whom it feels most like paperwork.

### The Break, Addressed (not executed — left open for downstream)

The setting is built so the reader's sympathy can plausibly start with the Ordinate (someone must hold the Throat; the alternative is eleven villages fighting over one river) and be pulled, through Agent_B's arc, toward the transgressor — while leaving open, structurally, a *third* option the source's target-prior demands: not the Ordinate's total victory, not the Throat's destruction, but a forced widening of the Reckoning itself, so that Torn Hollow gets a seat at the recitation it never had, and the Division survives, altered, un-destroyed, its founding silence still unspoken. Nothing here forecloses that outcome; nothing here requires it either.

---

### Transformation Sequence (naturalized)

**TR1 — Agent_B, at the height of his standing as Sluice-Warden, reads (or has read for him) an unlisted spring during a starve-month, and tells Torn Hollow directly — gate-hours be damned — before reporting to the Throat.** He acts from real analytical power (he has stood in council, argued schedule revisions, been listened to) and real desperation (his own terraces are dying). The act is public within a day; every village in the valley hears that Torn Hollow drank water the Ordinate never allotted.

**TR2 — Agent_C, the young reader, having supplied the reading, finds her own position shift: no longer merely trained-into-silence, she is now visibly, undeniably a reader whose finding *changed something real.*** She is not free — the prohibition still stands over her — but the shape of her constraint has changed from *identity you were taught* to *identity you now have to actively navigate,* because everyone in Torn Hollow now knows what she can do.

**TR3 — The Ordinate closes Torn Hollow's gate. The Drowning-Silence begins.** Agent_B goes, within days, from Sluice-Warden to a man standing in his own dry terrace with everyone else, indistinguishable in the Ordinate's eyes from any powerless terrace-worker. His civilizational time-horizon — his years of standing, his family's decades of Wardenship — collapses to the biographical: *how many days can we last.*

**TR4 — Agent_B, with nothing left to lose and something the Ordinate cannot simply seize — the fact that Torn Hollow now knows an unlisted spring exists, and other Sluice-Wardens have started asking why their own readers never mention such things — organizes.** Not a village uprising with pitchforks at the Throat; a coalition of Sluice-Wardens, quietly, at the next Reckoning, refusing to open the recitation until Torn Hollow's gate is reopened and the unlisted spring is added to the Division. This is leverage, not liberation — Agent_B has something the Ordinate needs (the cooperation of the other Wardens, without which the twice-yearly Reckoning cannot proceed at all) rather than an escape route.

**TR5 — The Ordinate settles.** The gate reopens. The spring is added to the schedule — as an Ordinate grant, folded into the Division's language, recited from the next Reckoning forward as though it had always been accounted for. Torn Hollow's gate-hours improve, modestly. Agent_B is not restored to his old standing; he is permitted back into it, on terms the Ordinate sets. The Division persists — altered, its silence about the First Wardens' original right to divide anything at all completely undisturbed, the liturgy unchanged: *as it was measured, so it remains.*

### Linguistic Strategy: Direct Translation with Ritual Register

Source language: a constructed highland tongue (unnamed, no real-world cognates foregrounded), rendered in English with specific untranslated structural features:

- **The Division** is never called "law," "rule," or "policy" — always *"the measure"* or *"as it was measured."* This phrase is load-bearing: it appears at the Reckoning, in threats, in comfort, in complaint. Its grammatical passivity (measured *by whom* is never specified, in the language itself) is the floor invariant living in syntax.
- **Reading** (channel-sensing) takes a special verb aspect distinct from ordinary seeing/knowing — glossed here as *"to feel-forward"* — marking a permanent, uneraseable state-change in the one who has learned it, distinct from any temporary skill.
- **The Drowning-Silence** has no term of duration in the language — there is literally no word-form in Kethrani speech for "the sentence will last X days," because sentence-length is Ordinate discretion, not a category of grammar. Characters describe it by stage ("the third dry day," "the day the well-smell changed") never by countdown.
- Village register vs. Ordinate register: villagers speak in concrete, sensory compounds (*stone-throat*, *loess-crack*, *low-water fear*); the Ordinate speaks in recited fixed phrases, liturgical and unchanging, which do not bend even under argument — Agent_A's dialogue should feel *quoted* even in ordinary conversation.

### Naming

- **Agent_A → Ren Osar-Ked**, "Warden-Speaker" — Ked is a title-suffix denoting Ordinate office, worn like a second surname; personal name (Ren Osar) rarely used even by intimates.
- **Agent_B → Duvakh Sethrei**, Sluice-Warden of Torn Hollow — Sethrei is a hereditary Warden-clan name, four generations attached to that one gate.
- **Agent_C → Ila Sethrei-Nem**, Duvakh's daughter, reader-in-training — Nem is a craft-suffix marking active apprenticeship, dropped once (if) she is fully licensed.
- **Agent_D → Marn Kohl**, terrace-worker, no gate-name, no clan-suffix — surnamelessness itself is a marker of terrace-worker status in this culture, immediately legible to any local.

### Inhabitation Sentences

**Ren Osar-Ked (Agent_A):** *You have never once, in thirty years at the Throat, thought of the gate you hold as a weapon — it is simply the thing that stands between eleven villages and each other's throats, and closing it on Torn Hollow felt exactly like closing it on flood-season, an ordinary motion of an ordinary office.*

**Duvakh Sethrei (Agent_B):** *You argued schedule revisions in council for fifteen years and were listened to, right up until the morning your own gate went dry under Ordinate seal and you understood, for the first time in your life, that being listened to and having power were never the same thing.*

**Ila Sethrei-Nem (Agent_C):** *You were taught to distrust your own hands until the Throat confirmed what they felt, and then one dry morning you told your father where the water was before anyone confirmed anything, and there is no teaching that undoes a thing your hands already know how to find.*

**Marn Kohl (Agent_D):** *You have watched three Reckonings from the back of the crowd where terrace-workers stand, and you have never once believed the schedule could change, because in your whole life you have never seen anyone try and still be standing afterward.*

**Substrate (the floor itself):** *You kneel at the head-gate at dawn with your palm flat on wood worn smooth by four generations of palms before yours, feeling for the give in the current that will tell you how much water passes today — and nowhere in the ledger, the oath, the shrine, or the warden's seal is there any account of who first had the right to put a gate here at all.*
