# SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**The floor invariant (primary):** The world contains a founding assumption that is never audited because auditing it is unthinkable to those who hold it: that the household — the sealed, chosen, private space of a marriage — is by definition a refuge. This is not a belief characters argue for. It is the ground under every other belief, including the beliefs of people trying to help. The floor is not "the house might be dangerous and no one noticed." The floor is that *the category of sanctuary itself* has no test, no instrument, no outside — you cannot check whether a sanctuary is a sanctuary, because checking presupposes standing somewhere the sanctuary-status has already been decided. **Falsifier:** if the finished story frames the danger as something a smarter neighbor, a better doctor, or a more suspicious relative could have caught in time — a correctable failure of vigilance against a legible threat — the floor invariant was lost. The story must instead make visible that the vigilance had nowhere to stand, because the space it needed to doubt was the one everyone, including its occupant, was standing on.

**No untranslatable-real / grain is present** in this source (Stage 0 marked it absent). No subordination beat is required.

**The instrument stays honest.** Whatever measuring apparatus exists in this world (a physician's diagnostic reasoning, a family's read of a marriage, a neighbor's judgment of a household) must be built to weigh *correctly from a bought zero* — it gives true readings of everything except the one thing its zero-point forecloses it from questioning. The failure is never "the instrument lied." The failure is "the instrument was calibrated, honestly, against a floor no one is permitted to set the scale beneath."

**Substrate:** The marriage-house itself — not as metaphor, but as the actual physical structure whose walls, cellar, and marriage bed constitute the sealed unit the culture has designated unaudit-able. What the house *contains* (see Section 1) is real, physical, and lethal — and it is lethal precisely because the house's sanctuary-status is the one thing no one in this world, including its occupant, has the standing to test.

**Substrate inhabitation sentence:** *You live inside the one room the whole village has agreed, without ever saying so, that no one is allowed to walk into and ask what's wrong.*

# SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

## Setting

The Ostrogsk Marshes, 1841. A scattering of timber-and-turf steadings along a river delta in the Russian Empire's northwestern periphery, three days by cart from the nearest garrison town, populated by descendants of Old Believer exiles who fled Church reforms two centuries prior and never fully rejoined the surrounding Orthodox peasantry. The Old Believer settlements are endogamous, insular, and governed by a lay council of elder men (the *soborchane*) who interpret both scripture and civil obligation to the Crown, since no priest will serve unbaptized-by-new-rite communities. There is no resident physician within thirty versts. The nearest is a district *zemsky vrach* — a state-appointed doctor — who visits twice yearly if the roads hold and the Crown's budget does not lapse.

Marriage among the Old Believers of the marsh is arranged by household heads and sealed by a dowry-transfer that makes the wife's presence in her husband's house a matter of contract, not affection. Once married, a woman's blood-kin have no standing to enter her husband's house uninvited — doing so is treated as an insult to the husband's *sobor* standing, adjudicated by the council, not by the woman's family. The house, once sealed by marriage, is understood by everyone — council, kin, the woman herself — as a threshold across which outside scrutiny does not travel. This is not enforced by armed men. It requires no enforcement at all. It is simply what a house *is*, once a marriage has been contracted into it.

The marsh air carries a chronic wasting fever locals call *the damp* — swelling joints, night sweats, a slow greying of the skin — attributed by all available medicine to bad water, marsh vapors, and the moral temperature of the household. No one in the marsh, layman or trained doctor, possesses the means to distinguish a fever caused by vapor from a fever caused by a substance introduced deliberately into food or drink. The chemistry does not exist here. This is categorical, not personal, ignorance — the district doctor is as blind to it as the most superstitious grandmother.

## Constraint Reference Table

**C1 — The Damp (Medical Ignorance) → Mountain, χ≈0.10-0.12, uniform across index**
What it is: the total absence, in this world, of any diagnostic method capable of distinguishing environmental fever from poisoning. Locals call it simply *the damp*, and treat it as weather — something the marsh does to bodies, like frost does to crops.
- Agent_A (the wife) experiences it as: an explanation that closes every door. When her body worsens, *the damp* is the sentence spoken over her, and it forecloses the question she has no words for anyway.
- Agent_B (a woman of moderate standing — a healer/midwife figure) experiences it as: a professional ceiling. Her herb-lore and years of watching bodies fail her here; she reaches the edge of what marsh knowledge can diagnose and stops, correctly, because there is nothing past that edge to reach.
- Agent_C (the district doctor, visiting) experiences it as: the same wall, dressed in Latin. His training gives him better words for his ignorance, not less ignorance.

**C2 — The Sealed House (Gendered Passivity) → Snare at Agent_A's position (χ=0.72), Rope at Agent_B or council position (χ=-0.10)**
What it is: the marriage contract that makes a wife's house impenetrable to her own kin, and makes her presence inside it a matter of her husband's honor rather than her own claim. Locals don't name this as oppression — they call it *keeping the threshold*, and speak of it as a virtue: a well-kept house keeps its threshold; a woman who invites her mother past her husband's door has shamed her own marriage.
- Agent_A experiences it as: total identity-lock. She cannot leave, cannot summon her own mother past the door without dishonoring herself, cannot renegotiate what "wife" means without ceasing to be legible as a person in this community at all.
- The council / an institutional-standing figure (e.g., her husband's uncle, who sits *sobor*) experiences the identical threshold-keeping as pure coordination — the mechanism that lets marriages function without constant kin-interference, that lets a household hold its own affairs, that lets the community run without every domestic dispute becoming a clan war. He is not lying when he calls it good order. From his seat it *is* good order.

**C3 — The Lair (Hidden Predator) → Snare, χ=1.20, zero variance across all trapped agents**
What it is: the specific, physical, deliberate act of a husband (Agent_D, unformalized in Stage 1's body but structurally present as the extraction's source) poisoning his wife slowly, using the house's isolation and the damp's medical cover as his instrument. This is not metaphor. He is not "toxic" in the modern therapeutic sense — he is administering something, in the food, in the drink, behind the sealed threshold C2 built for him. The house that keeps outside eyes out is the same house that lets him do this without a single witness.
- Agent_A experiences it as: a worsening she has no vocabulary to route outside "the damp." Every wifely and every villager instinct she has been given tells her the house cannot be the thing that's wrong, because the house is where wrong things are *supposed to be kept out of*, not *where they come from*.
- Agent_B (the healer) experiences it as: a case that will not resolve no matter what marsh medicine she applies — and no data available to her distinguishes "this case is unusually stubborn damp" from "this case is not damp at all."
- A third trapped figure (an in-law, a servant-girl, or a second wife in a nearby household — whichever the drafting stage selects) converges to the *identical* index: powerless, immediate, trapped, local. Whatever standing she held elsewhere collapses to nothing once she is inside a lair.

The critical structural fact, carried directly from the Omega: **C3 does not care what any of these women were before they crossed the threshold.** Agent_A's cousin who married into wealth, Agent_B's own relative standing as a respected healer — none of it transfers. The lair erases prior position uniformly. This is not injustice distributed by rank. It is injustice that makes rank irrelevant the moment the door closes.

## Character Positions

- **Agent_A** — the young wife. Married two years into the household of a marsh trapper of modest but rising standing. No living mother nearby; her own kin are a day's cart-ride distant and cannot cross her husband's threshold without his invitation. Worsening health, attributed by everyone (including herself) to the damp.
- **Agent_B** — the settlement's *znakharka*, a lay healer-midwife of moderate communal standing, called to households the doctor cannot reach in time. Analytically sharp, professionally humble about the edge of her own knowledge.
- **Agent_C** — the district *zemsky vrach*, visiting twice yearly, formally credentialed, regionally scoped, and no less blind to the actual mechanism than Agent_B, for all his Latin.
- **Agent_D** (implicit; the extracting agent behind C3, present in the world though not centered as POV) — the husband. His position is never a Snare; it is the house's architecture working exactly as the community built it to work.

## Linguistic Strategy: Historical Register (19th-c. Russian Marsh-Old-Believer English)

Era: 1841, rendered in English prose carrying the syntax and lexical texture of Church Slavonic-inflected peasant Russian in translation — formal address between unequals, kinship terms doing structural work, biblical cadence in moral speech.

Grammatical/lexical features:
1. **Formal patronymic address even in intimacy** — a wife refers to her husband by name-and-patronymic in front of others, by a diminutive only alone; the switch itself signals who is present and whether the threshold is being "kept."
   > *"Foma Silovich bids you sit,"* she tells the healer at the door — then, once the door is shut and only Agent_B remains, *"Fomka says my blood is thin again."*
2. **The threshold as grammatical subject.** Sentences are built so that the *house*, not the woman, is the actor: "The house does not open to her mother this season," rather than "she cannot see her mother."
3. **"The damp" as weather-vocabulary**, declined like a natural phenomenon: one does not *have* the damp, one *is caught in* the damp, the way one is caught in rain — never a disease one contracts from a source.
4. **Kinship-obligation vocabulary for the sealed house**: *"keeping the threshold," "a well-kept door," "threshold-shame"* — the entire ethical vocabulary of wifely virtue is spatial, architectural, never named as isolation.

## Inhabitation Sentences

**Agent_A:** *You are worsening inside the one place everyone — your mother, the healer, God, and you yourself — has agreed cannot be where the wrongness comes from, because it is the place that is supposed to keep wrongness out.*

**Agent_B:** *You have spent fifteen years learning exactly where marsh medicine's knowledge ends, and you have just watched a case walk past that edge with a face you recognize and no name to give what's on the other side.*

**Agent_C:** *You carry a diploma that makes your ignorance sound like diagnosis, and you will write "wasting fever, of the marsh type" in a ledger tonight with complete professional honesty.*

**Substrate (repeated, carrier voice — Agent_A, the closest to it):** *You live inside the one room the whole village has agreed, without ever saying so, that no one is allowed to walk into and ask what's wrong.*
