STRATEGY BRIEF

IMPLIED AUTHOR: A patient ethnographer of complicity — someone who trusts the reader to sit with unresolved suspicion and refuses the consolations of both melodrama (a villain unmasked) and allegory (illness-as-metaphor for a bad marriage). This author notices the texture of small institutional failures — a doctor's honest ledger, a healer's priced-out silence, a husband's face caught off-guard at a washing-trough — and skips easy interiority in favor of what each character can afford to know given what knowing would cost them. The prose stays cool, close to the body (hands, nail-beds, buckets), and the ending must stay open on a structural level even as it closes a single life.

AUDIENCE: Readers of literary short fiction attentive to structural/institutional critique dressed as period social realism — comfortable with ambiguity, folk-medical texture, and multiple center-of-consciousness shifts (Marfa, Praskovya, Struve) without hand-holding. Not genre-mystery readers expecting resolution; not readers who need the marriage explicitly named as abusive.

SCOPE: Short story, staying in current family. Current length 3,358 words; target range **3,600–4,600 words**, well inside the feasible ceiling. Resolution should remain non-resolution — no new case should crystallize into evidence. World-building density stays exactly as spare as now; do not add new institutional apparatus (no additional doctors, no expanded folk-taxonomy) — deepen what exists rather than widening the cast.

CREATIVE DEEPENING: The richest untapped material is in the *asymmetry between the three parallel cases*, not their symmetry — the review correctly flags that Marfa/servant-girl/new-wife currently read as a demonstrated pattern rather than a discovered one. The deepening move is to make each case cost something structurally different to notice or not-notice (Marfa costs Praskovya her professional standing to voice; the servant-girl costs her the comfort of an available alternate explanation — the girl lives; the new wife costs her literally nothing since she isn't even summoned) so the repetition reads as escalating *cheapness of the excuse* rather than mechanical rhyme. The washing-trough moment and the grief-as-stagnant-water passage are the strongest existing material — both should be trusted, not multiplied. Consider whether Praskovya's final "filing" needs one more texture: not a new fact, but a sharper cost — what specifically does *not* going to the new wife's house buy her this time, now that she's old and near the end of her working life rather than young and building a reputation? The stakes of her silence should compound across the story, not repeat.

FLINCH POINTS:
1. **The servant-girl's recovery** ("felt something that was not quite relief and not quite disappointment — an unease she didn't examine too closely"): the draft names the flinch and then flinches from the flinch — it tells us Praskovya doesn't examine the feeling rather than making us feel the cost of her not examining it. Honesty would require either dwelling one beat longer in the specific shape of that unexamined unease (what exactly would she have to admit if the girl had *not* recovered?) or trusting the silence completely and cutting the narrator's gloss ("that this was the marsh being the marsh") which currently does Praskovya's rationalizing *for* her, softening her culpability into narratorial explanation.
2. **The final section's hearsay register** (new wife's cough "heard... the way one hears weather"): this is thematically correct (Praskovya's world of secondhand knowledge closing in on itself) but the draft uses distance to avoid staging the actual moment of choice. The review is right that tension drains here. Honesty doesn't require a scene — it requires the *reader* to feel Praskovya actively decline a specific, available action, not just report that no summons came. Right now the absence of a summons does the moral work that should belong to Praskovya's own inaction.
3. **Foma's washing-trough face**: currently the single sharpest ambiguous beat in the piece, correctly unresolved — but immediately narrated away ("A tired man scrubbing a pot was not evidence of anything"). This line risks over-explaining what the image already accomplished. Honesty may require trusting the image alone, or at minimum shortening the explanatory close so the ambiguity isn't tidied by authorial reassurance.

PATTERN CHECK: Concern, narrow. This is not a case of the pipeline defaulting to quiet systemic resolution against the material's wishes — the material *is* about the unnamed, unauditable nature of institutional complicity, so quiet non-resolution is correct and earned. The concern is subtler: within that correct quiet mode, the draft occasionally over-narrates its own ambiguity (explaining *why* a character doesn't know rather than just showing they don't), which is a milder version of the same flinch — converting the discomfort of true uncertainty into the comfort of narratorial control. The fix is compression and trust, not escalation toward darkness or toward resolution.
