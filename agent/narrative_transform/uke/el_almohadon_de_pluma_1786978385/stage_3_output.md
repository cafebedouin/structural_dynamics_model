## 2. Voice Archetype Selection

**Primary: The Condemned (Agent_A)**
**Secondary: The Witness (Agent_B, nested)**
**Structure: Nested Voice — Witness framing/bracketing Condemned's close-third sections**

**Justification:**

Agent_A's index (powerless, immediate, trapped, local) maps directly onto the Condemned's requirements (PIR model ✓, Mountain-dominated ✓, π ≥ 1.0 ✓). She experiences C2 as natural fact ("keeping the threshold" as virtue, not cage) and C3 as unnamed worsening routed through C1's vocabulary ("the damp"). Her voice must be immediate and experiential, never reflective — she has no analytical register to reach for, because C1 forecloses it and C2 forecloses trust in any analytical register that reached her anyway.

Agent_B satisfies the Witness requirements: analytical index relative to Agent_A, moderate standing, can observe indexical variance (her own professional ceiling vs. Agent_C's Latin-dressed identical ceiling) without possessing power to intervene. She is not omniscient — she is boundaried, which is the point. Her Witness function lets the reader see Agent_B *reach the edge correctly and stop*, which is the E3 error (Mountain Denial risk) inverted into its correct form: she does NOT misdiagnose C1 as remediable. She diagnoses correctly and is still powerless against C3, because C3 was never a medical question.

**Master Diagnostic check:**
- *Does the voice allow the reader to see what the character cannot?* Yes. Agent_A cannot see the house as anything but sanctuary-by-definition; the reader, given Agent_B's proverb-level unease ("some fevers come from the pot, not the marsh") and the accumulating physical detail, sees the shape of extraction Agent_A has no vocabulary to name.
- *Blindness cost:* Fatal, to Agent_A. Tone: tragic, somber — as specified by the archetype.
- **Blurb test:** The single most important thing Agent_A understands about her situation is — nothing nameable. She cannot articulate "my husband is poisoning me" because C1 forecloses the concept and C2 forecloses the trust required to seek outside verification even if the concept existed. Her voice archetype does not permit her to say the thesis aloud. This is confirmed structurally sound: the Condemned's blurb-test failure is a feature, not a bug, of this pairing.
- Agent_B's blurb-test: the most important thing she understands is "this is not damp" — but she has no name for what it *is instead*, only a proverb she herself dismisses. She cannot say the thesis either. Good.

**Rejected alternatives:**
- Beneficiary for Agent_A: wrong — she is not benefiting, she is dying. Reserved for a possible glancing use on the council/uncle figure if a scene requires his interior logic (which Ω_C01 already forbids for Agent_D, and I extend that caution to the uncle: no POV access, glimpsed only through what the sealed house permits Agent_B or Agent_A to observe of him).
- Prophet for Agent_B: rejected — she has no foreknowledge, only professional limits. Prophet requires justified prescience; nothing in the manifest grants it.
- Zealot for anyone: rejected — no character in this manifest performs devotion to the constraint as sacred; "keeping the threshold" is naturalized, not fervent. A Zealot voice would over-signal ideology where the constraint requires invisibility.

## 3. Indexical Revelation Strategy

```
☑ Multi-POV Parallel (constrained) — specifically: Nested/Chorus hybrid
```

Two narrating positions, alternating in marked sections: Agent_A (close third, immediate) and Agent_B (close third, retrospective-professional). The reader sees the variance between what Agent_A experiences (worsening with no name) and what Agent_B observes (a case that will not resolve, reaching a professional edge) *before* either character could articulate it as convergent evidence — because neither character ever fully articulates it. Agent_C's visit is rendered from Agent_B's observing position, not his own POV, per Ω_C01's logic extended: giving Agent_C interior access risks the same false bargaining-surface problem, and his diagnostic correctness is more devastating witnessed from outside than narrated from within his own professional confidence.

This is **not** Late Revelation — there is no final-act recontextualization that redeems earlier confusion, because Deterministic Tragedy forecloses a "one who saw it coming" epiphany at the human character level. The reader is the only position that assembles the full pattern. No character achieves it. This is the correct rendering of the floor invariant: the vigilance had nowhere to stand, for the reader also gets no confirming instrument — only accumulation.

## 4. Editorial Decisions

```
LENGTH: Short (4000-5000 words) — long enough for the fever's slow arc and Agent_C's twice-yearly visit structure to land without compression artifacts; short enough that no scene can afford to over-explain.

POV: Third limited, alternating — Agent_A / Agent_B, marked by section break (no headers naming them; the shift in register — Agent_A's sensory immediacy vs. Agent_B's professional-observational cadence — carries the transition).

TENSE: Past. (Chronicle-past appropriate to the historical register; present tense would import a modern interiority-signaling that fights the "biblical cadence in moral speech" linguistic strategy.)

CHARACTER COUNT: 4 — Agent_A (wife, full POV), Agent_B (healer, full POV), Agent_C (doctor, observed-only), Agent_D (husband, glimpsed-only, no interiority per Ω_C01). Servant-girl (Ω_P05) resolved below.

Ω_P05 RESOLUTION: Single-household servant-girl. Tightest fidelity to C3's local scope (σ=0.8) per Stage 2's own recommendation; village-wide pattern would risk diffusing the claustrophobia that makes the sealed-house floor invariant legible. She appears without POV access — glimpsed by Agent_B during a household visit, her condition registering as a second data point Agent_B cannot yet connect to Agent_A's case (they are in different households; Agent_B's professional traffic between households is what eventually, near the story's end, lets her notice the pattern without being able to name or act on it — this is the closest the story comes to a "seen but not actionable" near-miss, consistent with Deterministic Tragedy rather than Seeded Possibility, since Agent_B's noticing changes nothing and saves no one within the story's timeframe).

Ω_P06 RESOLUTION: Agent_B is granted exactly one proverb-level utterance ("some fevers come from the pot, not the marsh") — spoken once, early, to no one in particular, immediately self-dismissed as the kind of thing her own teacher used to say and which she associates with superstition rather than method. It recurs once more, wordlessly, as a felt unease rather than a repeated line, near the story's end, when she visits the servant-girl. It is never escalated into hypothesis. This satisfies the Ω_P06 directive precisely: dramatic irony seeded, C1's Mountain ceiling not breached.

NAMING: Cultural (period-authentic Russian Old Believer naming — patronymics doing structural work per Stage 2's linguistic strategy). Agent_A → given name + patronymic address pattern; diminutive reserved for private/unwitnessed moments. Agent_B → title-first (znakharka [name]), reflecting her professional/communal standing. Agent_C → title-first, formal (doctor's surname + rank), reflecting his institutional remove. Agent_D → almost always referred to by Agent_A via patronymic-formal even in her own interiority ("Foma Silovich," never a private diminutive for him) — this itself is a chilling structural detail: she has no unwitnessed name for her husband, because there is no private register in which he is not also the house.

LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:
  Code-switch occurs at the boundary of witnessed/unwitnessed space, per Stage 2 example. Agent_A's formal patronymic address for her husband NEVER relaxes into diminutive — this is the tell (absent code-switch, not present one) that the house's "privacy" is not actually private; there is no space, even alone, where she is not performing threshold-keeping, including inside her own head. This is the single most important structural-linguistic decision in the blueprint: withholding the diminutive is how the prose enacts the floor invariant without stating it.
  Agent_B code-switches normally (formal with patients/family, proverb-register alone or with trusted peers) — her code-switching is healthy and demonstrates, by contrast, what Agent_A has lost.

EMOTIONAL CORE: Not "she is being poisoned and no one notices" (too legible, risks the falsifier). The emotional core is: the total absence of a register in which Agent_A could doubt her own house, even to herself — grief without an object, because the object requires a category that does not exist for her.

ENDING STRATEGY: ☑ System persists, agent removed.
  Agent_A dies (or reaches the terminal stage of C3's progression — drafting discretion on exact endpoint, but no rescue, no naming, no reveal to any character). The house continues. Agent_D remarries or continues, off-page, unremarked, because nothing in the constraint graph was disturbed. Agent_B logs the case in her memory as an unusually stubborn damp. Agent_C's twice-yearly ledger entry (Ω_C02) stands uncorrected. The servant-girl's case remains unconnected. This is the correct terminal state for Deterministic Tragedy under PIR: no bargaining surface was ever available, so nothing resolves it, argues against it, or ruptures it.
```

## 5. Primary Physical Marker

**Selected: The greying of the hands, read differently by each index.**

Rejected candidates: swelling joints (too generically medical, risks becoming a counted symptom — a metric); night sweats (same problem, plus risks being staged as a countdown). 

The greying of the hands satisfies the inhabitation-not-metric test: it cannot be expressed as a number changing over time without losing everything that matters about it. It is witnessed differently by each position:
- Agent_A feels it first as *cold that does not leave even near the stove* — a displacement (Stage 3 register: Displacement), not named as symptom but as a private, almost embarrassing failure of her own hands to hold heat, which she reads as her own fault, her own thinning blood, before she reads it as illness at all.
- Agent_B sees it as color — the specific grey-going-blue at the nailbed she has catalogued in fifteen years of damp-cases, cross-referenced against every damp-case that resolved and didn't, and this one does not match the pattern cleanly, though it resembles it enough to file under it.
- Agent_C, on his twice-yearly visit, notes it in Latin-adjacent vocabulary in his ledger, correctly, as a documented sign consistent with marsh-fever's late presentation — his correctness is the trap.

**Irresolvable tension marked for Stage 4:** Agent_C's diagnostic correctness *is* the mechanism of the tragedy. The reader cannot resolve this comfortably by faulting his competence — his competence is real, his kindness is real, and both are precisely what let the ledger entry stand. Stage 4 must write toward this, not soften it into a portrait of professional negligence.
