Vex found the discrepancy during a routine archive scan.

She'd been cataloging atmospheric reports from Year 2847—the year the Engineering Council had implemented "temporary allocation optimization" during a processor failure—when the numbers stopped making sense. The failure had lasted four months. The repairs were completed in 2851, documented in triplicate.

But the allocation variance had never been reversed.

She pulled up the current distribution table. Level Five: 720 hours. Level Nineteen: 690 hours. Level Thirty-eight: 420 hours. Level Forty-two: 380 hours.

She cross-referenced the original design specifications. The system had been built for uniform distribution: 720 hours per level, variance within five percent. The current spread was forty-seven percent.

Vex sat back in her chair. The temperature held steady at twenty-two degrees. The air moved freely through the overhead vents. She'd worked here for six years and never once noticed her breathing.

She made a note in the file: *Historical allocation discrepancy. Investigate original intent.*

Then she moved on to the next catalog entry.
