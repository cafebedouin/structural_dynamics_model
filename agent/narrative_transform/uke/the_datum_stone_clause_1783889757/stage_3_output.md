```
☑ Deterministic Tragedy
```

**Verification against Stage 1:** Confirmed. The generation sequence C1→C2→C3 shows monotonic ε/Supp increase, TR2 (codification) fires unblocked, and TR3 (the only reform-path transformation) is structurally foreclosed by C3's own downstream effect — destroying the coordination substrate before organized resistance can access it. This is BIR-Tragedy: tragic outcome through cascading good-faith error (Ossit's belief in the Ledger's fairness, Dael's belief in the Charter's protection), not through implacable optimization. ## 2. Voice Archetype Selection

**Primary structure: Chorus Voice, three-part, with one Counterpoint pairing embedded.**

### Selected archetypes:

**Meret Sanne (X3) → The Condemned**
- Inside C2's constraint logic, cannot see systemic shape, experiences the Ledger as natural/inevitable weight rather than constructed asymmetry
- π = 1.5 (powerless), trapped exit, PIR-adjacent experiential position — qualifies cleanly under Condemned requirements (powerless/moderate π ≥ 1.0)
- Tone: tragic, somber, immediate
- Justification: Meret's arc doesn't require her to *understand* the Notch's construction. She needs to feel it. This is exactly the register the Condemned is built for — experiential, not analytical.

**Ossit Fenn (X4) → The Beneficiary**
- π = −0.2 (institutional), false CI_rope signature confirmed by Stage 1 (χ=−0.128, classified Rope from his index while Snare from Meret's and Iss's)
- Genuinely believes the Ledger is fairer than what preceded it; has never carried a bucket far enough to learn what the Ledger costs
- Tone: warm, assured, likeable — the reader must want to be inside his comfort to feel the dissonance
- Justification: This is the paradigm case the archetype was built for. Ossit is Stage 1's E1 (Type III error) made flesh. He must never wink at the reader. His voice should be the most *pleasant* voice in the story — reasonable, fair-minded, a little proud of his own competence — precisely so the gap between his certainty and Meret's two extra hours does the work.

**Iss Braddal (X2) → The Witness, constrained**
- Analytical index (π=1.15), can see multiple indices simultaneously, reads plats professionally
- Constraint per Ω_P06: she may *act* on her analytical position (advising Meret toward a doomed petition, noticing the plat's convenient placement) but must not deliver plaza-speech exposition. Her Witness function is exercised through selective attention and professional gesture, not monologue.
- Tone: distanced, controlled, privately grieving
- Justification: Iss is the structural bridge between Meret's felt Condemned experience and Ossit's blind Beneficiary comfort. She is the only character positioned to see both, which is precisely what makes her silence (per Ω_P06) load-bearing rather than a missed opportunity.

**Excluded from POV, present in text:** Coyt Braddal (notch-blind, background register only — his scenes are viewed by Iss, never narrated by him, to avoid a fourth full POV diluting the chorus); Sarn Vey and Dael Korrin (their C3 conflict is the *second* movement of the tragedy, visible through Meret's proximity to Sarn and referenced but not POV'd — reserving Sarn's interiority prevents the Turn-Feel subplot from becoming co-equal with the Notch/floor, per Ω_C03).

### Master Diagnostic check:

*Does this voice allow the reader to see what the character cannot?*
- Meret: reader sees the Notch's constructedness (via structural framing/Iss's proximity) that Meret's body only registers as weight and distance.
- Ossit: reader sees Meret's cost that Ossit's index cannot register at all. This is the central engine — dramatic irony without narrator pointing at it.
- Iss: reader sees that Iss sees, and sees her choosing silence. The gap here is not blindness but *withheld speech* — a different, quieter engine.

### Combination Strategy: **Double-Blind Counterpoint (Meret/Ossit) nested inside Chorus (add Iss)**

Meret and Ossit are the Double-Blind pair specified in the Advanced Combination list: two structurally blind voices in opposite directions, observing the same marker (the Notch, the Ledger reading) from opposite ends of the power scale, neither able to perform the extraction math the reader performs automatically. Iss is the Witness-adjacent third voice who *could* perform that math and chooses, per Ω_P06, not to say it aloud — which makes her chapters the tensest in the book, since the reader waits for a reveal that structurally cannot come.

## 3. Indexical Revelation Strategy

```
☑ Multi-POV Parallel (primary)
   + elements of Analytical Observer (Iss, constrained — see below)
```

Alternating close-third sections: Meret → Ossit → Iss → Meret → Ossit → Iss, with the pattern breaking only at the final movement (see Act 3). The reader assembles the indexical variance (Stage 1's C2: Snare/Rope/Snare triple-point) *before* any character does, because no character ever holds all three positions simultaneously. Iss comes closest — she is the Analytical Observer archetype's natural home — but per Ω_P06 she is explicitly constrained short of that archetype's full expository mode. She observes the variance; she does not narrate the theorem.

This satisfies the Structural Prevention checklist's blurb test: the single most important thing each character understands about their situation —
- Meret: *the water is heavier every year* (felt, not theorized)
- Ossit: *the Ledger is fairer than what it replaced* (believed, not tested)
- Iss: *the paper is honest and the honesty is the trap* (known, but unspoken to anyone who could act on it)

None of these, spoken aloud by their holder, constitutes theme-naming, because none of them is the thesis. The thesis (the floor invariant) is only visible in the *composite* — which is exactly what Multi-POV Parallel is for.

## 4. Editorial Decisions

```
LENGTH: ☑ Novelette (7500-17500) — three-POV chorus with Double-Blind counterpoint
        requires enough real estate for indexical variance to accumulate weight
        rather than feel schematic. Short would compress the chorus into gesture.

POV: ☑ Third Limited, rotating (Meret / Ossit / Iss), strictly single-consciousness
     per section — no head-hopping within a section. Omniscient is banned: it would
     let the reader access variance directly rather than assemble it, killing the
     Double-Blind mechanism.

TENSE: ☑ Past — the standard narrative distance for constraint fiction; present
       tense would falsely imply urgency/instability where the point is precisely
       that this arrangement has calcified into "just how it is."

CHARACTER COUNT: 3 POV (Meret, Ossit, Iss) + 3 non-POV load-bearing presences
                 (Coyt, Sarn, Dael) — six total, matching Stage 1's six-agent
                 formalization exactly.

NAMING: ☑ Cultural, per Stage 2 spec — compound consolidated surnames for
        high-rights families (Braddal, Fenn, Korrin) vs. single older clan names
        for low-pasture families (Sanne, Vey). The asymmetry in naming is load-
        bearing and must appear in the text's first roll-call moment (a water-court
        session, a licensing queue) before any dialogue explains why.

LINGUISTIC IMPLEMENTATION:
  Code-switching occurs at the register boundary between Compact/court procedural
  English ("the elevation stands as certified") and the low families' private
  vocabulary (turn-feel, notch-blind, cutting the water, tent-line). The switch
  signals point-of-view allegiance more than setting: Meret's sections default to
  the private vocabulary and treat court language as an intrusion (rendered as
  slightly stiff, quoted-feeling even in free indirect style); Ossit's sections
  default to procedural language entirely and never once reach for the low
  vocabulary — he doesn't know the words exist, which is itself characterization.
  Iss's sections are bilingual and the switching itself is diagnostic: when she
  is performing her job she uses court language; when she is alone or with Meret,
  the private vocabulary surfaces, and the seams between the two registers in her
  prose are where her suppressed analytical position leaks through without ever
  becoming a speech.

EMOTIONAL CORE: The specific ache of a cost that cannot be itemized — Meret's
  two extra hours are not a grievance she can bring to any court because no
  court measures hours, only elevation. The tragedy is not that she loses her
  case; it's that there is no mechanism by which her case could be a case.

ENDING STRATEGY: ☑ System persists, agent removed
  (Elaborated in Act 3 below — the Charter completes, the Turn-Feel is filed,
  Sarn's rotation becomes a fee schedule, and the Notch is re-certified the
  following spring exactly as it was, honestly, by a competent surveyor, forever.)
```

## 5. Primary Physical Marker

**The notch itself — the stone shelf on the valley's western wall — functions as the Counterpoint marker per the Advanced Combination spec.** Every POV character encounters it differently:

- **Meret** passes it on her two-hour detour and reads it as a wall she has already lost against — she does not need to touch it to know what it says.
- **Ossit** visits it once a year for the spring re-certification and experiences it as reassurance — proof the system is honest, precise, working.
- **Iss** is the only character who has ever asked, professionally, *why here* — and found nothing, because there is nothing to find. Her scenes at the notch are the closest the text comes to the floor invariant surfacing, and per Ω_P06 she must stop short of saying it.

**Secondary marker (Scavenger-adjacent sensory register, not a POV but present in Meret's world):** the weight of the water itself — not measured, never given a number in Meret's sections, felt as accumulating fatigue across seasons. This is the inhabitation register, detailed below.
