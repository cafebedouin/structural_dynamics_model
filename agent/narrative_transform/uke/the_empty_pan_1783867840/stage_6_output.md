STRATEGY BRIEF

**IMPLIED AUTHOR:**
A materialist observer who trusts bodily knowledge and structural analysis equally. Notices: the weight of ore in gloved hands, the specific tone a drill bit makes in good metal, the way institutions preserve themselves by refusing to see. Cares about: competence trapped inside flawed systems, knowledge that exists without instruments to verify it, the arithmetic of survival under constrained choice. Skips: villains, psychological interiority that isn't structural position, resolution through individual heroism or system reform. The prose lives in objects and procedures—what people do with their hands, what numbers say, what gets archived for later.

**AUDIENCE:**
Readers who work inside systems they suspect are lying to them. Assumes familiarity with: institutional non-response patterns, the gap between procedural correctness and actual truth, how economic constraint forecloses choice. Expects: patience with technical detail, comfort with unresolved endings, tolerance for characters who make compromises the reader might judge. Needs: enough sensory grounding to feel the work (drill bits, core samples, calibration rituals), enough structural clarity to see how the trap operates, an ending that acknowledges cost without offering escape.

**SCOPE:**
Current: 6,255 words. Target range: 8,000–10,000 words. The story has room to expand into its best possibilities—varying texture between perspectives, deepening the sensory register of Kaaro's sections, giving Yuki's refusal-to-look more dramatic weight. Feasible ceiling: 11,468 words (hard limit from downstream token budget). World-building density: medium (Belt economy, Mars colony, CSA bureaucracy—established through use, not exposition). Ending resolution: open but not ambiguous—the archives exist, the grain remains unmeasurable, the work continues.

**CREATIVE DEEPENING:**
The discovery report identified three latent potentials:

1. **Sensory divergence between perspectives**: Kaaro's sections should feel different in the body—the hum of the drill, dust in low gravity, the particular weight of good ore. Yuki's sections should live in procedural rhythm—calibration checks, confirmation tones, the satisfaction of protocol followed correctly. Amara's sections should carry archival patience—spreadsheets, backed-up files, the long view. Right now all three think in the same measured analytical voice. Varying sentence rhythm and sensory texture between them would make each section feel like a different mode of knowing.

2. **Yuki's refusal as dramatic event**: Section 8 contains the story's sharpest moment—Yuki actively choosing not to look up research that would invalidate her competence. Right now it's told efficiently but not dramatically. This deserves more space: the physical act of not-searching (hand hovering over terminal, closing it without typing), the specific shape of what she's protecting (not just her promotion but her sense of being good at something), the cost of that choice made visible in her body.

3. **The grain as physical presence**: The grain—ore's actual crystalline structure—is explained clearly but never made sensuously present. What would it mean to show the grain without naming it? Kaaro feeling something in the rock his instruments can't capture, Amara seeing it in smelting data as a pattern that shouldn't exist, the way it remains real precisely because it can't be measured. The story knows this conceptually; it could know it texturally.

**FLINCH POINTS:**

1. **Section 4, Kaaro's response to marginal certification**: The draft has him say "Then certify it wrong" and immediately regret it. This converts rage into composure too quickly. What honesty requires: let him stay in the anger longer—not as outburst but as sustained pressure. The rock is good, the number is marginal, his daughter needs medication, and the system that's stealing from him is represented by this one operator following protocol. The draft looks away from how that feels in the body.

2. **Section 8, Yuki's promotion**: The draft treats her promotion as earned competence—"She'd done everything right. She'd trusted the system. The system had rewarded her." This is true from inside her perspective, but the story knows she's being rewarded for procedural compliance that produces systematic theft. The draft dignifies her achievement without acknowledging its cost to others. What honesty requires: hold both truths simultaneously—she is competent at her job AND her job is stealing from miners. The promotion scene should carry that weight.

3. **Section 9, Amara's choice to archive rather than fight**: The draft has her explain this choice clearly: "I could go public... But if I go public, I lose my position." This is the right structural move, but it's too clean. What honesty requires: the specific texture of that compromise—what it feels like to choose documentation over action, to preserve knowledge while people are being robbed, to teach the standard curriculum knowing it's built on lies. The draft explains the choice; it should dramatize the cost.

**PATTERN CHECK:**
CONCERN. All three flinch points follow the pipeline's default pattern: convert cost into composure, dignify compromise, resolve through quiet archival hope. These aren't wrong moves—the story's power comes from characters trapped inside systems they can't escape. But the uniformity suggests the draft is choosing quiet resolution by default rather than because the material demands it. At least one of these moments should be allowed to stay harder, sharper, less resolved—probably Kaaro's response to the marginal certification, which is where the theft is most direct and the constraint most immediate.
