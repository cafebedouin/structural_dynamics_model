REVIEW ASSESSMENT

STRENGTHS:
The story's sensory grammar is precise and earned — the glassy-smooth dark bands, the resonance traveling through the contact pad into the chest, the way cold finds you through thermals. These textures build a vocabulary for what Lena knows before she has language for it, and the prose never explains what the sensations mean; it trusts them to accumulate into understanding. The constraint topology is clean: the SSA's sulfide-concentration standard is not a mistake to fix but a founding choice that can't be corrected without invalidating the entire system, and the story holds that distinction without flinching.

BIGGEST WEAKNESS:
The canteen debate (Chika proposing organizing, Ngozi explaining why it failed before) runs long and arrives at its conclusion — "there's always someone who'll cross the line" — through dialogue that feels like it's performing the argument for the reader rather than discovering it in real time. The scene would be tighter if it reached the same endpoint through fewer exchanges, letting the impasse land harder.

INVARIANT FALSIFIER:
HOLDS. The story never suggests a better instrument could recover the "true" reading the SSA missed. The formations Lena reads that sing — the ones with stable resonance but low sulfide — are not mismeasured; they're measuring something the standard was never designed to recognize. Dr. Okonkwo's email offers grammar for what the stone is saying, not correction of what the SSA got wrong. The real (the coherence in how stone holds memory) stays unreadable to the system throughout.

READINESS:
The prose is alive, the invariant holds, and the story executes its constraint topology without converting the problem into a solvable injustice. This is ready.
