## 2. VOICE ARCHETYPE SELECTION

**Selected Archetype:** The Condemned (primary) with Analytical Observer (secondary, nested)

**Justification:**

**Primary voice: Lena (X₁) as The Condemned**
- ✓ Inside constraint logic, cannot see systemic shape
- ✓ Acts rationally given limited information and structural position
- ✓ Experiences constraint as natural/inevitable, not constructed
- ✓ Voice is immediate, experiential, not reflective
- ✓ **Attractor compatibility:** Condemned + Seeded Possibility = ✓ (tragedy visible, seed invisible to narrator)
- ✓ **Power range:** Powerless (π=1.5), trapped exit → perfect fit
- ✓ **Rationality model:** PIR at individual level (Lena optimizes within constraints she can see)

**Why this works:** Lena experiences C₃ as Snare (χ=0.96) but cannot see it as *constructed*. She knows the rejection rate is high, she knows the fees are extractive, but she experiences this as **terrain** — the way things are. The reader sees what Lena cannot: that the 1883 baseline is arbitrary, that the SSA's calibration is biased, that collective organization (TR₁) could transform her χ from 0.96 to 0.256. The gap between Lena's experience and the reader's structural knowledge generates the tragic tone.

**Secondary voice: Amara (X₂) as Analytical Observer (nested)**
- ✓ Observes constraint dynamics with partial stake
- ✓ Can see multiple indices simultaneously (knows X₁ experiences Snare, X₃ experiences Rope)
- ✓ Voice is analytical, documentary, but **not omniscient**
- ✓ Identity-locked prevents full exit → maintains tension

**Why nested, not parallel:** Amara's sections provide the structural knowledge the reader needs to see what Lena cannot, but Amara herself is trapped (identity-locked, χ=0.736 Snare from her own index). The nesting prevents the story from becoming a simple "ignorant victim / enlightened observer" binary. Both are trapped; one sees the trap, one doesn't.

**Voice combination strategy:** **Nested Voice**
- Lena's first-person present-tense sections (primary, 70% of text)
- Amara's third-person limited past-tense sections (secondary, 30% of text)
- Amara's sections provide context for Lena's experience without Lena ever gaining that context herself

**Tone:** Tragic, somber (Condemned baseline) with clinical analytical passages (Analytical Observer)

**Anti-pattern check:**
- ✗ Hopeful Condemned: Lena does not gain agency the voice can't support
- ✗ Overexplaining Guide: Amara observes but does not pedagogically explain to reader
- ✓ Structural blindness maintained: Lena never realizes the baseline is arbitrary

**Narration rule:**
- **Lena's sections:** Numbers appear only when she ACTS on them in-scene:
  - Checks credit balance at terminal → number appears
  - Reads rejection notice → fee amount appears
  - Disputes rejection → cites resonance time from sensor log
  - **NEVER:** "She had 340 credits left" as ambient narration
  - **ALWAYS:** "She checked the terminal. 340 credits. Rent was due in three days."

- **Amara's sections:** Numbers appear when she ACTS on them:
  - Reviews rejection notice → adds to database count (1,847 → 1,848)
  - Writes report → cites calibration threshold from archived data
  - **NEVER:** "The SSA maintained 18% profit margins" as ambient fact
  - **ALWAYS:** "She opened the budget report she'd copied in 2154. Profit margin: 18.3%. Still."

**Test for every number in draft:**
- WHO read it? (Which character, with what access?)
- On WHAT instrument? (Terminal, rejection notice, sensor screen, archived report?)
- Doing WHAT with it? (Checking balance, disputing rejection, updating database, writing report?)
