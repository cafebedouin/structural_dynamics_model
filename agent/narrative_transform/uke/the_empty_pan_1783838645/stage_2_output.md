# STAGE 2: NATURALIZATION OUTPUT

## SECTION 0: INVARIANT CONTRACT

**Primary Invariant (Untranslatable Real):**
There exists a coherence in how stone holds memory — a rightness in the grain, a truth in the stratification — that any hand trying to claim it for proof destroys in the claiming. The geologists call it "structural integrity under non-invasive observation," but the miners who work the deep shafts know: the rock tells you what it is only while you're not trying to own what it says.

**Falsifier:** If the finished story contains a recoverable "true reading" of the stone that better instruments could measure correctly, the invariant was lost and the naturalization failed.

**Substrate:** The Pre-Cambrian shield formation beneath the Sudbury Basin — 2.4 billion years of metamorphic record, older than atmospheric oxygen. The stone holds a register of planetary formation that exists independent of anyone reading it. The Basin's institutions measure, certify, and monetize readings of the stone, but the stone's truth is not in the readings. It is in the *being-read-while-unclaimed* — a coherence that collapses the instant extraction begins.

**Substrate Inhabitation Sentence:**
You descend into stone that has been holding the planet's formation for two billion years, and the company's sensors read the formation every shift and never once translate what the rock is saying — only what the rock is worth.

**Missing-Floor Invariant:**
Beneath the Basin's certification system lies a founding partition: someone decided, without neutral ground to stand on, what counts as "ore-grade" versus "waste rock" — which readings matter, which strata have value, which formations get mapped. That baseline was set by the first colonial mining surveys in 1883, optimized for nickel extraction, and every certification since treats it as geological fact rather than interested choice.

**Falsifier:** If the story frames the injustice as a local error a better authority could correct — a miscalibrated standard, a corrupt inspector, a fixable bias — the floor invariant was lost.

## SECTION 1: CONTEXT DESCRIPTION

### Setting

**Sudbury Basin, Northern Ontario — 2157 CE**

The Basin is a 60-kilometer impact crater filled with the richest concentration of nickel, copper, and platinum-group metals on Earth. Three generations after the Last Boom (2091–2134), when automated extraction made surface operations obsolete, the real value moved underground: *certified stratigraphic readings* of Pre-Cambrian shield formations for planetary geology databases and off-world construction planning.

The certification system runs through the **Sudbury Stratigraphic Authority (SSA)**, a Crown corporation established in 2139 when the Ontario Geological Survey privatized. SSA certifies readings taken by licensed *strat-readers* — workers who descend into active and abandoned shafts, place resonance sensors against exposed formation faces, and upload readings to the central registry. Certified readings sell to university archives, off-world construction consortia, and planetary formation databases at rates indexed to depth, formation age, and reading clarity.

The city of Sudbury proper (population 47,000) sits on the crater's southern rim. The crater floor is a patchwork of flooded shafts, tailings ponds turned wetlands, and the ruins of twentieth-century smelters. The deep shafts — 3 to 7 kilometers down, cut through Archean and Proterozoic formations — are worked by **independent strat-readers** who live in shaft-towns: Creighton (pop. 890), Garson (pop. 1,200), and Levack (pop. 340).

The shafts are cold (8–12°C year-round), wet (constant seepage through fractured rock), and dark (helmet lamps only). Readers work 8-hour shifts placing sensors, waiting for resonance stabilization (15–45 minutes per placement), uploading readings via fiber-optic tether to surface stations. A good reader completes 6–10 placements per shift. Certified readings pay 40–180 credits depending on formation value, minus SSA certification fees (18–35 credits per reading), equipment rental (12 credits/shift), and shaft access fees (8 credits/shift).

The substrate is everywhere: you walk through it, you touch it, you read it. But the SSA certification process requires readings to pass through **standardized sensor calibration** (set in 2139 to match the original 1883 survey's nickel-optimized baselines) and **authorized placement protocols** (only certain formation faces qualify; most don't). The standard was designed for extraction economics, not planetary formation record. It sees nickel-bearing sulfides clearly and renders everything else as "background variance."

### Constraint Naturalizations

**C₁: The Stone's Integrity (Invariant — Mountain)**

The Pre-Cambrian formations hold a coherent record of planetary formation — metamorphic grades, isotopic ratios, crystal orientation, inclusion patterns — that exists independent of measurement. This is not "data waiting to be collected." It is a *state of being* in the rock itself: the way the formation holds its history.

The SSA's sensors detect signal. But the act of *certifying* a reading — extracting it for sale, indexing it to a buyer — requires collapsing the reading into the 1883 baseline's categories. The standard asks: "How much nickel? How much copper? What's the sulfide concentration?" It does not ask: "What does this formation say about planetary cooling rates in the Proterozoic?" That question has no price, so it has no certified answer.

The stone's integrity is **unownable**. A reading taken for love of knowing — placed carefully, interpreted slowly, shared freely — can approach the formation's truth. A reading taken for certification **cannot**, because certification requires forcing the stone's voice into the standard's mouth.

Locals call it **"the grain"** — the way the formation *wants* to be read, which you feel in your hands when the sensor settles and the resonance stabilizes. Readers talk about placements that "sing" (clean resonance, the sensor finds the grain immediately) versus placements that "fight" (the standard's calibration conflicts with the formation's actual structure, and you spend 40 minutes trying to force a reading that the stone isn't giving).

**What changed:** Nothing. The grain was here before the shafts, before the surveys, before the SSA. It will be here after. The constraint is **physics** — the rock's coherence under non-invasive observation — not a rule anyone enforces.

---

**C₂: The Baseline (Epistemological Foundation — Rope for those who understand it, invisible to those who don't)**

In 1883, the Geological Survey of Canada sent teams into the newly discovered Sudbury deposits to map "ore-grade" versus "waste rock." They drew the line at 1.5% nickel content by mass. Below that: waste. Above that: ore.

That partition was **arbitrary** in the sense that it could have been drawn differently (1.0%? 2.0%? stratified by depth? indexed to sulfide type?), but it was **necessary** in the sense that *some* partition was required for coordinated extraction. You cannot mine without deciding what counts as ore.

When the SSA inherited the certification system in 2139, it inherited the 1883 baseline. The standard's sensor calibration, the formation classification tables, the pricing indices — all built on the assumption that 1.5% nickel is the natural threshold between value and waste.

For **institutional agents** (SSA certification officers, university archive buyers, off-world construction planners), the baseline is a **coordination mechanism**. It allows them to compare readings, price formations, plan extraction. They experience it as **Rope** — functional, necessary, taken for granted.

For **analytical agents** (independent geologists, formation historians, some senior strat-readers), the baseline is **visible as choice**. They know it was set by interested parties (the 1883 survey was funded by mining companies). They know it optimizes for nickel extraction, not planetary formation record. But they also know that *some* baseline is necessary — you cannot certify readings without a standard. They experience it as **Rope** — arbitrary but functional, and changing it would require coordinating every database, every buyer, every sensor calibration in the system.

For **powerless agents** (shaft-town strat-readers), the baseline is **invisible**. They know the SSA certification fees are high, they know most of their placements get rejected as "insufficient clarity" or "non-standard formation," but they don't see the 1883 partition as a *choice*. They experience it as **terrain** — the way things are.

The baseline is **changeable** (it's a Rope, not a Mountain), but changing it requires collective coordination across the entire certification system. No single agent can shift it.

**What locals call it:** "The standard." SSA officers say "We certify to the standard." Readers say "It didn't meet standard" when a placement is rejected.

**What changed (in 2139):** The Ontario Geological Survey privatized, and the certification system shifted from public record-keeping to profit-indexed data sales. The 1883 baseline didn't change, but its **function** did: it went from coordinating extraction to **coordinating extraction AND certification revenue**. The SSA now has a financial interest in maintaining the baseline (changing it would invalidate 18 years of certified readings and collapse the market).

**C₃: SSA Certification (Institutional Standard — Snare/Tangled Rope/Rope depending on position)**

To sell a stratigraphic reading, you must get it **SSA-certified**. Certification requires:

1. **Licensed sensor equipment** (rental: 12 credits/shift)
2. **Authorized shaft access** (fee: 8 credits/shift)
3. **Placement on approved formation faces** (most faces don't qualify — "insufficient exposure," "non-standard lithology," "contaminated by modern drilling")
4. **Resonance stability within standard parameters** (calibration set to 1883 baseline)
5. **Upload via SSA-approved tether** (fiber-optic lines installed and maintained by SSA contractors)
6. **Certification review** (18–35 credits per reading, depending on depth and formation age)

A strat-reader in Creighton shaft-town pays **32 credits in fixed costs per shift** (equipment + access + tether) before placing a single sensor. A successful placement yields 40–180 credits gross, minus 18–35 credits certification fee. **Net per placement: 5–145 credits.** A good reader completes 6–10 placements per shift.

**Best case (senior reader, deep formation, clean placements):**
- 10 placements × 180 credits = 1,800 credits gross
- Minus 10 × 35 certification = 350 credits
- Minus 32 fixed costs = 32 credits
- **Net: 1,418 credits/shift** (177 credits/hour)

**Median case (mid-level reader, mixed formations):**
- 7 placements × 90 credits = 630 credits gross
- Minus 7 × 25 certification = 175 credits
- Minus 32 fixed costs = 32 credits
- **Net: 423 credits/shift** (53 credits/hour)

**Bad case (junior reader, shallow formations, rejections):**
- 4 placements × 40 credits = 160 credits gross
- Minus 4 × 18 certification = 72 credits
- Minus 32 fixed costs = 32 credits
- **Net: 56 credits/shift** (7 credits/hour)

The system **does provide coordination value**: certified readings are trusted by buyers, the central registry prevents double-sales, the standard allows price comparison. But the **extraction is asymmetric**: certification fees, equipment rental, and access fees flow to SSA and its contractors (who are institutional-position agents), while strat-readers (powerless-position agents) bear the risk of rejection, equipment failure, and formation variance.

**You experience C₃ as Snare** (χ = 0.96). The system takes almost everything. The coordination value (certified readings are trusted) is real, but from your position it is **dominated by extraction**.

## SECTION 2: OMEGA LOG

### RESOLVED

**Ω_E01: What is the actual sensor technology?**
- **Question:** Do strat-readers use seismic resonance, electromagnetic scanning, or some other technology?
- **Resolution:** **Resonance-based sensor arrays** — place sensor against formation face, emit low-frequency acoustic pulse, measure resonance patterns in crystal structure. Technology is real (ground-penetrating radar + seismic tomography), scaled to handheld use.
- **Impact:** Determines why placements "sing" or "fight" (resonance matches vs. conflicts with crystal orientation), explains 15–45 minute wait times (resonance stabilization), justifies rejection criterion "insufficient clarity" (unstable resonance = noisy signal).

**Ω_E02: How deep are the shafts, and is this physically plausible?**
- **Question:** Can humans work at 3–7 km depth?
- **Resolution:** **Yes, with pressure equalization and thermal management.** Deepest current mines (Mponeng, South Africa) reach 4 km. By 2157, shaft infrastructure includes pressure-equalized cages, thermal regulation, and breathable-atmosphere maintenance. Temperature at depth is 40–60°C (geothermal gradient), managed by cooling systems in work areas.
- **Impact:** Justifies 40-minute cage rides, explains why shifts are only 8 hours (thermal stress limits), grounds the "cold and dark" experience in managed work zones (8–12°C maintained).

**Ω_E03: Why would off-world construction firms buy stratigraphic readings?**
- **Question:** What is the economic driver for certified readings?
- **Resolution:** **Planetary formation databases for off-world construction.** Mars, lunar, and asteroid mining operations need predictive models of planetary geology. Earth's Pre-Cambrian formations are the only accessible record of planetary formation processes (Mars has no plate tectonics, so its early formations are preserved differently). Certified readings sell to construction firms planning deep excavations on other bodies.
- **Impact:** Justifies high prices for deep/old formations (Archean readings are rarest and most valuable), explains why the market exists, grounds the SSA's institutional power (they control the only trusted certification system).

**Ω_C01: Is the 1883 baseline historically accurate?**
- **Question:** Did the 1883 Geological Survey of Canada actually set a 1.5% nickel threshold?
- **Resolution:** **Conceptually accurate
