# The Parable Problem: How Epistemic Warnings Survive—Or Don't

## Pattern First: The Transmission Asymmetry

"Survivorship bias" appears across undergraduate statistics, business schools, and popular science. The story is always the same: Abraham Wald, during World War II, examined bullet holes in returning aircraft and recommended reinforcing areas *without* holes—because planes hit there didn't return. The parable compresses a complex statistical insight (selection effects in observational data) into a portable narrative that propagates across disciplines.

Meanwhile, formal frameworks for understanding epistemic failures—Kahneman and Tversky's heuristics and biases program, Kuhn's paradigm incommensurability, the replication crisis literature—remain largely confined to their originating fields despite equal or greater validity. The parable travels; the framework stays home.

This pattern repeats: Feynman's cargo cult science, Chesterton's fence, Goodhart's Law, Campbell's Law. Each compresses a mechanism-level insight into narrative form. Each crosses disciplinary boundaries that formal treatments cannot. The question is whether this transmission asymmetry reflects a fundamental constraint on how epistemic knowledge propagates—a "mountain" in the constraint taxonomy analyzed here—or merely a contingent feature of academic culture.

## The Proposed Structure: Two Substrates, Eleven Families

The taxonomy under analysis organizes epistemic failure modes into eleven families, each defined by an underlying error operator rather than surface symptoms. The key architectural claim: these failures partition into two distinct substrates.

**Empirical Substrate (Families 1-7):** Failures in reasoning about reality itself
- Survivorship Bias (Family 1): Selection effects in data
- Overfitting (Family 2): Mistaking noise for signal
- Streetlight Effect (Family 3): Searching where measurement is easy
- Legibility Trap (Family 4): Optimizing for what's measurable
- Goodhart's Law (Family 5): Metrics becoming targets
- Composition Fallacy (Family 6): Properties failing to survive scale transitions
- Chesterton's Fence (Family 7): Removing constraints without understanding their function

**Social Substrate (Families 8-9):** Failures in reasoning about what game is being played
- Intent Arbitrage (Family 8): Exploiting gaps between stated and actual objectives
- Game Misidentification (Family 9): Treating strategic contests as epistemic ones

The taxonomy claims these substrates are genuinely distinct—not just categories of convenience—with the deepest damage occurring when participants misidentify which substrate they're operating on.

## Evidence Framework

### Documented in Observable Patterns (Tier 1):

**Cross-disciplinary citation asymmetry:** Specific parables (Wald's bullet holes, Feynman's cargo cults) appear in textbooks, popular science, and professional discourse across fields from medicine to software engineering. Formal frameworks explaining the same mechanisms (selection bias correction methods, Bayesian model criticism) remain discipline-specific despite broader applicability.

**Narrative compression as transmission technology:** The parables that propagate share structural features: concrete historical moment, vivid imagery, counterintuitive insight, clear mechanism. Abstract formulations of the same insights (e.g., "observational data subject to selection effects requires survivorship correction") do not achieve comparable cultural penetration.

**Mechanism-based classification stability:** When epistemic parables are sorted by underlying error operator rather than surface domain, they cluster into stable families. The Deferential Realism diagnostic reports show this classification survives across different observer perspectives for some families (mountain classification for parable transmission itself) but fractures for others (tangled_rope classification for the substrate split).

**Adversarial vs. non-adversarial error structure:** Some epistemic failures involve no strategic behavior (survivorship bias, overfitting), while others fundamentally depend on adversarial intent (intent arbitrage, game misidentification). This structural distinction maps cleanly to the empirical/social substrate division.

### Reasonable Inferences from Observable Patterns (Tier 2):

**Narrative compression as physical constraint:** The transmission asymmetry between parables and formal frameworks suggests a genuine constraint on information propagation, not merely academic inertia. The Deferential Realism analysis classifies parable transmission as "mountain"—an immutable structural property—with high purity (0.96) and gauge-invariant classification across all observer perspectives. ## Alternative Explanations Considered

**Simpler explanation: Academic specialization creates artificial boundaries**

The transmission asymmetry between parables and formal frameworks could simply reflect disciplinary silos and academic incentive structures. Researchers are rewarded for technical depth within fields, not for translating insights across them. Parables succeed because they're short enough to escape these institutional barriers.

**Why insufficient:** This doesn't explain the *mechanism* of transmission. If brevity alone mattered, executive summaries of formal frameworks should propagate similarly. They don't. The parable's narrative structure—concrete moment, vivid imagery, counterintuitive reversal—appears to do specific cognitive work that abstract compression cannot replicate. The Deferential Realism analysis supports this: parable transmission shows "natural law" signature (extreme inaccessibility to change, minimal enforcement required), suggesting something deeper than institutional contingency.

**Competing explanation: The substrate split is observer-dependent, not mechanism-level**

Perhaps the empirical/social distinction reflects different analytical perspectives on the same phenomena rather than genuinely distinct error types. A "game misidentification" could be reframed as an empirical error about social reality.

**How evidence distinguishes:** The formal analysis detects "gauge-variant" structure—the substrate split classification changes across observer positions. Powerless observers see it as tangled_rope (coordination hiding extraction), institutional observers see it as scaffold (temporary framework), analytical observers see it as tangled_rope. This perspectival fracture is precisely what you'd expect if the split itself sits on a boundary between epistemic and strategic domains. The question isn't whether observers disagree, but whether that disagreement is itself evidence of a deeper structural property.

## Unresolved Questions

**What evidence would validate the substrate split?**

The empirical/social distinction needs independent verification beyond the taxonomy's internal logic. Specific tests:
- Do errors on each substrate respond differently to intervention? (Empirical errors should respond to better measurement; social errors should resist methodological reform)
- Can we identify "substrate confusion" events where treating a social error as empirical (or vice versa) produces predictable failure modes?
- Do the error families within each substrate share formal properties that distinguish them from the other substrate?

**Can flagship parables be minted for gap families?**

The Composition Fallacy family lacks a culturally transmissible parable despite clear mechanism-level distinctiveness (properties failing to survive scale transitions). Candidates exist—the square-cube law, Braess's paradox—but haven't achieved the cultural penetration of survivorship bias or Goodhart's Law. Is this because:
- The mechanism is genuinely harder to compress into narrative form?
- The right story hasn't been found yet?
- The family isn't actually distinct from others?

**What institutional structures make epistemic errors load-bearing?**

If errors persist despite documented awareness, what makes them functionally necessary? The formal analysis flags "coupling drift" (previously independent dimensions becoming entangled) and "extraction accumulation" (asymmetric benefit flows increasing over time). These suggest institutional arrangements where the error-generating capability serves purposes beyond its stated function—but the specific mechanisms remain unspecified.

**Is the framework itself falsifiable?**

The taxonomy claims to organize epistemic failures by mechanism-level structure. What would falsify this claim? Possibilities:
- If parables cannot be stably sorted into families (high inter-rater disagreement)
- If the substrate distinction doesn't predict differential response to intervention
- If "incubating families" never develop flagship parables despite clear mechanisms
- If the framework's own classification (as snare/rope/tangled_rope depending on observer) proves unstable

## Why This Matters

The transmission asymmetry between parables and formal frameworks isn't just an academic curiosity. It determines which epistemic warnings propagate through culture and which remain trapped in specialized literature.

If narrative compression is genuinely a physical constraint on information propagation—as the "mountain" classification suggests—then improving epistemic hygiene requires understanding and working with this constraint, not against it. The most rigorous formal framework accomplishes nothing if it cannot cross disciplinary boundaries.

If the empirical/social substrate split is real, then treating all epistemic failures as errors in reasoning about reality—rather than recognizing when you're in a strategic contest—produces predictable institutional failures. The most sophisticated methodology cannot fix a problem where the other party's "error" is actually their optimal strategy.

And if the taxonomy itself exhibits the structural properties it describes—appearing as coordination from some perspectives but extraction from others—then the framework's own success depends on narrative compression it analyzes but cannot fully control.

The pattern holds: epistemic warnings that compress into portable narratives survive. Those that resist compression remain discipline-local despite equal validity. The question is whether we can make this constraint work for epistemic progress rather than against it.
