Architecture Doctrine
Linux is not a compromise. Every sovereign machine with a defined sovereign function runs Ubuntu LTS ? not as a concession, but as the correct choice for the job. Ubuntu 26.04 LTS (Resolute Raccoon) is the current baseline across all Polaris OS configurations.
The Sovereign Gradient ? Collaboration Scale (v2.20)
How many components a concern resolves into is not a global constant; it is set by collaboration scale ? personal ? small firm ? large firm. This gradient is the corpus?s hidden parameter, named here as doctrine because it silently governs the structure already built. At personal scale (Preparedness), nothing precipitates: the discipline depends on nothing but Power (at Keystone-1, Power collapses to a good UPS), needs no central identity provider because there is no collective to authenticate, and carries its own data on the Backup Brain ? a one-legged stool with Power as the floor. Cross into multi-person collaboration and Identity and Data become load-bearing, because they must now be shared, access-controlled, and audited across people; that crossing is the moment a concern earns its own spec. Identity is not a pillar that was always present and finally noticed ? it precipitates out when the firm crosses from personal to collective (the UniFi case shows the same from the other side: identity is explicit on the wire there because UniFi solves a multi-user access problem). Power is the one scale-invariant ? under everything at every scale, which is why the continuity / runway basis keeps wanting to promote it to the diagonal. The gradient governs when Keystone is finally allowed to fork: not at -1 or -2, where its concerns share fate; watch at large-firm / Keystone-MAX scale, where custody-of-identity and custody-of-data may develop different operational signatures.
Two Boundaries ? Privacy and Privilege (v2.20)
There is not one boundary in the corpus but two, and they buy different things. The wire ? the Maxwell demarc ? buys privacy: cross inside it and metadata stops leaking, intrafirm collaboration becomes possible, control becomes local. The trust root ? Keystone ? buys privilege: a stricter classification that lives only on the root, gated by the sovereign session and walled off from the merely-private store by the NER tripwire. The two are different cuts. Sovereignty is therefore a property a thing has by what it is ? its classification ? not by where it sits: the wire is necessary for privacy but never sufficient for privilege. Nextcloud proves this from the inside (fully inside the wire, fully private, still not privileged sovereign data); the social server proves it from the outside (outside the wire, never sovereign). The classes nest ? public ? private ? privileged ? each inner ring more protected and more fused to the root. What Polaris keeps sovereign is the privileged tier and the methods: classification, not location.
Data as a Classification Axis ? Fusion to the Root (v2.20)
?Data? is not a single component and was never a single leg to fork; it is a classification axis whose tiers precipitate to different homes. Live custody distributes by classification; backup lineage centralizes at the root. Fusion-to-the-trust-root is monotonic in classification: privileged data is maximally fused ? live store, backup lineage, and identity all on Keystone, and it fails closed with the root (when Keystone is down, access to privileged matter is supposed to be gone ? correct behavior, not a weakness); private data is backup-fused only ? its live store sits off-root on the Sovereign Cloud Server and is required to degrade gracefully through an outage; non-sovereign data is unfused ? it may live in the cloud, governed only by the CLD-DNS exit-hinge. The privileged and private tiers thus carry opposite availability requirements, and that divergent contingency signature is exactly what licensed the Nextcloud split ? the shared-fate test (see the multi-basis refinement under Cross-Reference Doctrine) predicts the split already made. This monotonic binding of recovery to the root is the operational form of the sovereign gradient. Resolved, not to be reopened before large-firm scale: Identity does not fork from Keystone ? it shares fate with the privileged tier (same trust tier, custody model, recovery root, backup lineage, power tier, enclave). The backup routine is the committed, expensive thing; identity is cheap to carry on top of it, and the routine is never run twice. Lightening Keystone by forking auth would move the trivial leg and leave the heavy one in place.

Certification Model
Every spec is written with pass/fail criteria and measurable thresholds. Self-builders use the published specs. Certification is a paid engagement. Ongoing integrator relationships are recurring revenue.
Cross-Reference Doctrine
Every device has a job. Every spec is a node in a network. No component is specified more than once. Drift is eliminated at the spoke-tightening pass. The spoke-tightening pass happens after all six new Draft specs have received their first engineering reviews ? not before.
Multi-basis refinement (registered v2.19; made operative v2.20). The corpus is one spec-space readable in more than one basis, and the bases are of two kinds doing two different jobs. A partitioning basis sorts every component into exactly one cell ? the mission basis (Preparedness / Sovereign / Cloud) and the technical basis (Power / Compute / Communications, the three-legged stool) are partitioning bases, and a spec that lands in two cells tests for drift. A scoring basis assigns every component a coordinate rather than a cell ? the continuity / runway basis (time-to-failure and shed order, the basis in which Power becomes the diagonal and the industry?s neglect of it is exposed as a basis artifact) and the exposure basis (what a spec bets on about the world ? invariant / fragile / convex, the SAIA-box-vs-SAIA-pattern split) are scoring bases, and an empty region tests for a coverage gap. The two kinds must not be confused: a drift test run on a scoring basis returns nonsense. Spoke-tightening seats every component spec in one cell of each partitioning basis. But the multi-basis check only flags a two-cell spec; the shared-fate test decides what the flag means, and there are three outcomes, not two. (1) Shared fate ? the modes carry the same trust tier, custody model, recovery root, backup lineage, power tier, and enclave ? is not a mixture at all but a correctly-fused axis; leave it (Keystone identity + privileged data ? fusing them is coherence, and a split would manufacture a divergence reality does not contain). (2) Divergent fate ? the modes fail, scale, recover, or get-trusted differently, so binding them forces a compromise on at least one ? is genuine drift; resolve it into modes (Keystone?s collaboration plane wanted to not fate-share with the trust root, so the Sovereign Cloud Server split out; the privileged-vs-private opposite-availability requirement is the signature that licensed it). (3) Divergent fate that must nonetheless bind under stress, because binding-under-load is the whole point of the component, is a genuine capstone; keep it and certify the binding (CLD-HYBRID?s fail-closed-under-load; CLD-PLATFORM, MON-SOVEREIGN float above the grid by design and are not drift). One check, one test, three outcomes ? the governor that keeps spoke-tightening from either missing a real mixture or shattering a coherent axis into atoms. A claim that survives more than one basis is basis-independent, which is what a certification ultimately warrants. The picture sharpens as the corpus is walked through each basis in turn.
