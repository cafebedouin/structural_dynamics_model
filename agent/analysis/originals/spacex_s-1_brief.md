Space Exploration Technologies Corp. — IPO registration (Form S-1, filed 2026-05-20)

MAIN IDEA (the contested commitment): SpaceX is raising public capital (Class A common
stock) while concentrating voting control in its founder. Elon Musk — holder of a majority
of high-vote Class B shares, and serving as CEO, CTO, and Chairman — will control the board
and the outcome of every matter requiring shareholder approval. The company will be a Nasdaq
"controlled company," exempt from the independent-board requirements that otherwise protect
minority shareholders. Class A public investors supply capital and bear economic risk but
have, in the filing's own words, a "limited or precluded" ability to influence corporate
matters. The shared commitment under contest: that public investment in a founder-controlled
SpaceX is legitimate and warranted. Real constituencies read that commitment in structurally
incompatible ways.

READINGS:

- FOUNDER-STEWARDSHIP (the issuer's framing): concentrated, durable control is the enabling
  condition of an ambitious long-horizon mission (make life multiplanetary); the dual-class
  structure insulates the vision from short-term market pressure. Control is coordination in
  service of the mission. Held by management and mission-aligned long-term investors.

- MINORITY-EXTRACTION: Class A holders provide the capital and absorb the risk while receiving
  near-zero governance rights; the controlled-company exemptions strip the minority protections
  that securities regulation otherwise mandates. The structure transfers governance value from
  public shareholders to the founder. Held by proxy advisors (ISS / Glass Lewis tradition) and
  minority-shareholder advocates.

- DISCLOSURE-CONSENT: the structure is fully and explicitly disclosed in the S-1; investors buy
  Class A with eyes open; legitimacy rests on informed consent under the Securities Act, not on
  control parity. Held by securities-law formalists, the underwriters, and the SEC disclosure
  regime.

THE CONTEST: no single framework holds both "concentrated founder control legitimately serves
all shareholders" and "minority shareholders are entitled to governance proportional to the
capital and risk they bear." The readings emit structurally different constraints — what counts
as a violation of shareholder interest, what the board owes minority holders, and whether the
dual-class structure is coordination (mission stewardship) or extraction
(disenfranchisement-for-capital).
