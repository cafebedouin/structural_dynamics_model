# A Taxonomy of Epistemic Parables

## The Problem with Lists

**Situation.** Science, policy, and philosophy have accumulated a rich inventory of cautionary stories — parables that warn against specific reasoning failures. Wald's bullet holes (survivorship bias), Feynman's cargo cults (ritual without mechanism), Goodhart's Law (metrics devouring their purpose), Chesterton's fence (dismantling without understanding). These circulate as independent warnings, deployed when someone recognizes the pattern.

**Complication.** Flat lists of "cognitive biases" and "logical fallacies" mix mechanisms, symptoms, and metaphors without distinguishing what kind of failure is actually occurring. This mixing is structurally understandable — the parables emerged independently across disciplines, each responding to a local problem, and no organizing principle existed to connect them. However, the consequence is that the same underlying error gets rediscovered and renamed in every field. Survivorship bias in statistics, publication bias in science, and selection effects in economics describe the same structural failure wearing different disciplinary costumes. Without a taxonomy organized by *mechanism*, each field must learn the lesson independently.

**Question.** What structure emerges when epistemic parables are organized by their underlying failure mode rather than their discipline of origin?

**Answer.** The parables cluster into families defined by distinct error operators. These families further separate into two substrates — empirical (failures in reasoning about reality) and social (failures in reasoning about what game is being played). The families that lack clean parables are the ones that get reinvented most often, because narrative compression is itself the transmission technology for epistemic warnings.

## The Empirical Families

### Family 1a: Perceptual Filter

**The error:** The instrument limits detection, and the absence of evidence is silently converted into evidence of absence.

**Flagship:** Eddington's Fish Net. A marine biologist surveys the ocean with a net of six-inch mesh, examines the catch, and concludes no sea creature is smaller than six inches.

**Members:** The Streetlight Effect (searching where the light is, not where the keys fell). Kaplan's Law of the Instrument ("give a boy a hammer and everything needs pounding"). The instrument defines the ontology — but a different instrument would define a different one.

**Distinguishing feature:** Remediable. The error lives in the tool, not the observer. Swap the net, bring a flashlight, put down the hammer. The fix is conceptually straightforward even when practically difficult.

### Family 1b: Cognitive Boundary

**The error:** The observer's entire framework is the instrument, and no tool-swap can fix it because the observer cannot step outside the framework to recognize its limits.

**Flagship:** The Fish and the Turtle. A fish asks a turtle to describe land. The fish's questions — "Is it rippling? Buoyant? Wet?" — decompose land-beauty into water-properties, which destroys the information. The fish is not using the wrong tool. The fish's ontology *is* the tool.

**Members:** Kuhn's incommensurability between paradigms. The difficulty of explaining color to someone blind from birth. Any situation where the observer's vocabulary has no slot for the phenomenon being described.

**Distinguishing feature:** Not remediable by evidence or argument. Requires paradigm shift, contemplative practice, or a sufficiently disorienting experience that cracks the framework. The boundary between Family 1a and 1b is the boundary between problems that yield to more data and problems that require a different kind of observer.

### Family 2: Inventory Is Not Explanation

**The error:** A complete catalog of components is confused with a working theory of the system.

**Flagship:** Lazebnik's Radio. A biologist, tasked with fixing a broken radio, removes components one by one. Remove this transistor: horrible screeching. Conclusion: "the horrible screeching transistor." A complete parts list that explains nothing about how the radio plays music.

**Members:** Borges's 1:1 map (perfectly accurate, perfectly useless). The blind men and the elephant (six complete local descriptions that don't compose into understanding). Pauli's "not even wrong" (a description so vague it cannot fail — the terminal case of inventory posing as explanation, where the catalog doesn't even have the structure to be falsified).

**Distinguishing feature:** The error is not insufficient data. The error is the wrong *kind* of representation. More components in the catalog will never produce a circuit diagram.

### Family 3a: Metric Capture

**The error:** A proxy measure becomes the goal, and optimizing the proxy degrades the thing it was supposed to measure.

**Flagship:** Goodhart's Law — "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

**Members:** The McNamara Fallacy (measuring what is easy and ignoring what matters — body counts in Vietnam as a proxy for strategic progress). The Soviet nail factory (given tonnage quotas, produces one giant nail; given unit quotas, produces millions of useless tacks). Standardized testing optimized at the expense of learning.

**Distinguishing feature:** Differs from Family 3b in that the system doesn't fight back *adaptively* — the metric simply drifts from its referent through optimization pressure. The actors often don't realize the spirit has been killed.

### Family 3b: Adaptive Response

**The error:** An intervention in a reflexive or adaptive system triggers reorganization that produces the opposite of the intended outcome.

**Flagship:** The Cobra Effect. British colonial administrators in Delhi offered a bounty for dead cobras. People started breeding cobras. When the program was canceled, breeders released their stock, and the cobra population increased.

**Members:** Campbell's Law (the policy version). Seeing Like a State (James Scott's book-length demonstration that administrative simplification destroys the informal structures that made systems function). Any situation where the system reorganizes around the intervention.

**Distinguishing feature:** Differs from Family 3a in that the system *actively responds* to the intervention. The cobra breeders are strategic actors, not metrics drifting from referents.

### Family 4: Pattern Is Not Signal

**The error:** Discovered patterns are treated as if they were predicted ones. Structure is hallucinated in noise, or the target is painted after the bullet hole appears.

**Flagship:** The ISIS-2 Astrological Signs. A major aspirin trial ran subgroup analysis showing aspirin was beneficial for every astrological sign except Gemini and Libra. Published deliberately to demonstrate that slicing data enough ways produces "significant" patterns in noise.

**Members:** Russell's Chicken (inductively concludes the farmer is benevolent, right up until Christmas). The Texas Sharpshooter (paint the target around the bullet hole). Post hoc analysis presented as hypothesis-driven research.

**Distinguishing feature:** The error is temporal — treating after-the-fact discovery as though it had the evidential weight of before-the-fact prediction. The pattern is real in the data. The signal is not.

### Family 5: Form Mimics Function

**The error:** The institutional or procedural shape of a functional process is reproduced without the substance that made it work. The ritual is mistaken for the mechanism.

**Flagship:** Feynman's Cargo Cults. South Pacific islanders who had seen military airstrips built fake runways, control towers, and coconut-shell headphones, waiting for planes to land. The form was reproduced perfectly. The mechanism was absent entirely.

**Members:** The replication crisis (entire fields with the institutional shape of science — peer review, methods sections, significance tests — but without the substance). Compliance theater masquerading as safety. Innovation theater in corporate settings.

**Distinguishing feature:** The mimicry is often sincere, not cynical. The cargo cult builders genuinely expected the planes. The error is sociological — mistaking process for outcome — rather than purely epistemic.

### Family 6: Selection Distorts the Sample

**The error:** Reasoning from a filtered sample as though it were the population. The filtering may be invisible to the observer.

**Flagship:** Wald's Bullet Holes. Analysts recommend armoring the damaged areas of returning bombers. Wald identifies the error: the sample is conditioned on survival. The planes with bullet holes in the blank spaces never returned.

**Members:** Berkson's paradox (the "why are all the attractive people I date unintelligent" structure). Gell-Mann Amnesia (reading a newspaper article about a known field, noticing it is wrong, then turning the page and treating the next article as authoritative). The anthropic principle (observing a universe compatible with observers because a universe without observers has no one to observe it). Publication bias, social media virality bias.

**Distinguishing feature:** The structure in the data is real, not hallucinated (distinguishing Family 6 from Family 4). However, the structure was generated by the selection process, not by the phenomenon under study. Family 4 hallucinates structure. Family 6 encounters real structure that is misleadingly generated.

### Family 7: Don't Dismantle What You Don't Understand

**The error:** Removing a structure without understanding why it exists. The structure may be load-bearing in ways that are invisible to the reformer.

**Flagship:** Chesterton's Fence. Before removing a fence, first understand why someone built it. If the reason for the fence cannot be articulated, the fence should not be removed.

**Members:** The Lindy Effect (things that have survived a long time are likely to survive longer — survival as evidence of hidden function). Much of Taleb's corpus lives at the intersection of Family 3b and Family 7.

**Distinguishing feature:** This family is normative where the others are descriptive. Families 1-6 describe how reasoning goes wrong. Family 7 prescribes a heuristic: epistemic humility before intervention. The lone-parable family — Chesterton's fence is so productive it constitutes its own cluster.

### Incubating: The Composition Fallacy

**The error:** Properties that hold at one level of aggregation do not survive the transition to another. Local improvements do not compose into global improvements.

**Candidates:** The square-cube law (a 50-foot human needs different bones, not bigger ones). Braess's paradox (adding a road to a congested network increases total journey time). The successful pilot that fails at national rollout. Qualitative phase transitions hidden inside quantitative increases.

**Status:** Four disciplines, a shared mechanism, and genuine daylight from Family 2 (which says the description is the wrong *kind*; this says the description is the right kind at the wrong *scale*). However, no single flagship parable has achieved the cultural compression of Wald or Chesterton. The family may need to incubate until someone mints the right story.

## The Social Families

The empirical families share an implicit assumption: the participants are trying to understand reality and failing. Stress-testing the taxonomy against parables from oral traditions, fiction, and philosophy of argument reveals a second substrate — failures that operate on social terrain rather than empirical terrain.

### Family 8: Arbitrage of Intent

**The error:** The gap between a rule's formal specification and its substantive purpose is deliberately exploited by an agent who understands both.

**Flagship:** Murakami's Vegetarian Cat. A cat corners a rat. The rat begs for mercy. The cat says, "Don't worry — I'm a vegetarian." The rat relaxes. The cat pounces. The dying rat asks, "But you said you're a vegetarian?" The cat replies, "True. I'm going to take you home and trade you for lettuce."

**Members:** Malicious compliance (following orders to the letter in ways designed to cause harm). Regulatory arbitrage (exploiting the gap between economic substance and legal treatment). The Bushman Money Magic story (Jackal's cascading fraud, where each failed scam generates the attack surface for the next one — the victim's attempt to recover creates the new exploit).

**Distinguishing feature:** Differs from Family 3a (Goodhart) in agency and awareness. Metric capture is systemic drift — actors often don't realize the spirit has been killed. Intent arbitrage is adversarial exploitation — the cat knows exactly what "vegetarian" is supposed to mean and navigates the gap deliberately. Differs from Family 5 (cargo cults) because the mimicry is strategic, not sincere.

### Family 9: Game Misidentification

**The error:** Treating an interaction as epistemic (about evidence and truth) when it is actually positional (about dominance, loyalty, or status). The epistemic framing is either the cover story for a social process or the genuine but mistaken assumption of one participant.

**Flagship:** The Donkey Parable. A donkey and a tiger argue about the color of grass. The donkey says blue; the tiger says green. They go to the lion king for judgment. The lion rules the grass is blue and sentences the tiger to a year of silence — not for being wrong about the grass, but for wasting wisdom arguing with a fool. The tiger's error was not about botany. The tiger's error was engaging an epistemic contest when the donkey was running a dominance game.

**Members:** The Banks/Hume mechanism — reason serves the passions, so argument is almost never about the ostensible subject. Winning an argument attacks the justification while leaving the preference intact. The Desert Fathers parable (remediation — the disciple trained to recognize insults as DH0 regardless of verbal sophistication, thereby inoculating against frame confusion). Graham's Disagreement Hierarchy (diagnostic tool — classifying the DH level of an opponent reveals whether an epistemic contest is actually occurring).

**Distinguishing feature:** The other families assume all participants are trying to understand reality and failing at different stages. This family covers the case where at least one participant is not trying to understand reality at all, and the error belongs to whoever assumes otherwise.

### The Boundary Condition: The Churn

One parable resists placement in any family. Amos Burton's description of "the churn" in *The Expanse* — "when the rules of the game change... the jungle tears itself down and builds itself into something new" — is not an epistemic error. It describes the substrate on which all the families operate: the fact that the rules of the game change, and when they do, survival is orthogonal to understanding.

The Churn is the boundary condition of the entire taxonomy. Correct reasoning across all families does not guarantee survival when the environment restructures faster than any model can track. The taxonomy is a diagnostic manual for reasoning failures *within* a game. The Churn is the reminder that games end.

## The Two-Substrate Insight

The empirical families (1a through 7, plus the incubating Composition Fallacy) all describe failures in reasoning about how the world works. The social families (8 and 9) describe failures in reasoning about what game is being played and what the rules allow.

The deepest errors — the ones that cause the most damage in practice — occur when participants believe they are on the empirical substrate when they are actually on the social one. The Tiger thinks the dispute is about grass color. The policy analyst thinks the meeting is about evidence. The scientist thinks the peer review process is about truth. The empirical framing becomes the cover story for a social process that operates on its own logic.

This separation explains a persistent puzzle: why do well-documented epistemic errors persist despite widespread awareness? The answer is that the social substrate makes the empirical errors load-bearing. The reductionist knockout approach in biology (Family 2) persists not because biologists are unaware of Lazebnik's critique but because the entire incentive structure — grants, publications, tenure, tooling — is built on parts-list science. The capability that produces the error is the same capability that produces the field's successes. The strength and the ceiling share the same root.

Therefore, the two substrates are not independent halves. The empirical error is the visible symptom. The social constraint is the architecture that makes the error persistent. The families that resist correction are the families where the epistemic failure is coupled to institutional success.

## The Meta-Finding: Why Parables Matter

The families that have clean parables — Wald's bullet holes, Feynman's cargo cults, Chesterton's fence — become culturally legible. The families that lack clean parables — the legibility trap, performativity, expertise collapse, the Composition Fallacy — get reinvented independently in every field.

This is itself a Family 1a phenomenon. Narrative is the instrument. Compressed, visual, one-move stories are the mesh of the net. Epistemic warnings that fit through the mesh propagate. Those that don't remain invisible — not because they are less real, but because the transmission technology cannot carry them.

Therefore, the parables are not decorative. They are the transmission layer. A formal framework can classify errors with precision. A parable can make someone *recognize* the error in three sentences. Neither replaces the other. The formal system provides rigor; the parable provides portability. The errors that lack both — that have neither the formal machinery to classify them nor the narrative compression to transmit them — are the errors that cause the most persistent damage, precisely because they cannot be seen or shared.

### Open Questions (Ω)

Ω: **Composition Promotion** — Does the Composition Fallacy have enough distinct parables and sufficient daylight from Family 2 to justify promotion to a full family?

Ω: **Social Family Completeness** — Do the social families (8, 9) exhaust the space of social-substrate errors, or do additional families exist? The Overton window (constraints on what can be expressed) and performativity (models reshaping reality) may constitute distinct social families rather than subcases.

Ω: **Boundary Porosity** — Several parables span families (Wald spans 1a and 6; Russell's Chicken spans 4 and 6; Seeing Like a State spans 3b and 7). These overlaps may indicate shared deep structure or may indicate that the family boundaries need further refinement.

Ω: **Parable Mining** — Three gap families lack flagship parables. Can existing stories be identified or new ones minted that achieve the narrative compression necessary for cultural transmission?

Ω: **DR Integration** — A formal constraint-classification framework (Deferential Realism) maps every family onto specific failure modes and provides mechanism-level explanation for error persistence. The relationship between the portable, parable-level taxonomy and the formal machinery warrants separate treatment.
