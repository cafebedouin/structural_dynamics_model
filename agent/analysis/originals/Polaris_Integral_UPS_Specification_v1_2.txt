0.  Is This the Right Architecture?
For most machines in stable home or office environments, a retail AC UPS (~$150) combined with a standard ATX power supply (~$150) is the correct solution. It plugs in, works immediately, and costs $400 total. Build that instead.
The Integral UPS is the right architecture when one or more of the following apply:
? Zero transfer gap is a hard requirement — the 4–20ms relay switching time of a conventional UPS causes crashes under heavy GPU load or on systems with minimal PSU hold-up capacitance.
? Solar integration is required — eliminating the AC inverter stack and running natively on DC from a PV array via the Smooth Operator tier.
? Grid noise isolation matters — sensitive workloads (telemetry, audio, radio) benefit from the complete elimination of AC noise from the power path.
? Auditable failure modes are required — no black-box UPS firmware, no proprietary inverter logic; every component verifiable by inspection.
? Deployment is remote or off-grid — locations with unreliable grid power, no convenient UPS servicing path, or a requirement for solar cold-start recovery without any grid presence.
If none of the above apply to your deployment, stop here and buy a retail UPS. If one or more apply, continue.
I.  Overview
The Polaris Integral UPS is a purpose-built DC power architecture that replaces the conventional combination of an ATX power supply and an external UPS with a unified, battery-primary power delivery system. The motherboard and all connected hardware run exclusively on clean DC power drawn from a lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) battery bank. The grid is the charging source, not the power source.

Conventional UPS systems detect a power failure, switch a relay, and transfer the load to battery — a process that introduces a transfer gap, generates conducted noise, and depends on firmware and mechanical components to function correctly under stress. The Integral UPS eliminates the transfer mechanism entirely. There is no relay, no firmware, no transfer gap. When the grid fails, the power delivery does not notice — though an optoisolated signal is sent to the motherboard so the operating system can respond appropriately.

Two tiers of the Integral UPS are defined, differing in bus regulation sophistication, galvanic isolation characteristics, solar compatibility, and cost. Both tiers share the same core philosophy: the PC never sees AC power, and the battery is the only thing between the hardware and a grid event.

III.  Hammerhead   —   Direct Battery Topology

The Hammerhead is the straightforward tier. A 48V LFP battery sits permanently on the load path. The MeanWell AC/DC supply keeps it charged. The HDPLEX DC-ATX converter draws from the battery continuously. When the grid is present, the MeanWell operates in CV mode at the 54V setpoint, maintaining the battery at approximately 70% SOC. When the grid fails, nothing changes from the hardware’s perspective — the battery was already the supply.

The Hammerhead is blunt by design. It does not regulate the bus voltage — the HDPLEX input swings with the battery’s state of charge across the LFP discharge curve. The HDPLEX’s wide DC input range (12–60V) tolerates this swing without issue. What the Hammerhead gains from its simplicity is meaningful: fewer components, lower cost, easier assembly, easier diagnosis, and the full current delivery capability of the battery available on demand for bursty workloads.

MeanWell Voltage and Current Commissioning

The HLG-240H-54A has two internal potentiometers accessible via screwdriver ports on the chassis:

• Vo (voltage adjust) — sets the output voltage, which determines battery float SOC. Target: 54V ? 70% SOC. Lower is gentler on the battery; higher gives more runtime. Typical reference points: 55.2V ? 90%; 54.4V ? 85%; 54.0V ? 70–75%.
• Io (current adjust) — sets the maximum output current. Trim to match or stay below the battery BMS maximum charge current rating. Prevents MeanWell from overwhelming the BMS on recovery after deep discharge.

?  Commission the Vo and Io pots with the battery disconnected. Power up the MeanWell, confirm output voltage with a calibrated DMM, then set Io. Connect battery only after both pots are set and verified.

IV.  Smooth Operator   —   Regulated Bus Topology

The Smooth Operator is the refined tier. A Victron SmartSolar MPPT charge controller sits between the MeanWell AC/DC supply and the system bus, providing a regulated 41.5V load output that powers the HDPLEX under normal grid-connected operation. The 36V LFP battery charges via a separate, current-limited charge output and sits idle at approximately 85% SOC — it contributes nothing to the load while the grid or sun is present.

When the grid fails, the MeanWell collapses and the Victron load output sags. A passive Schottky diode, forward-biased instantaneously by the voltage differential, connects the battery to the system bus with zero transfer gap and no relay. The HDPLEX input remains stable. The transition is transparent to the hardware.

The Smooth Operator uses a blocking diode on the MeanWell output to enable passive solar priority. When solar array voltage exceeds the MeanWell output (~52V), the blocking diode reverse-biases and the MeanWell idles on standby, consuming minimal AC power to keep its control loops ready. When solar voltage drops below the MeanWell output (cloud cover, sunset), the blocking diode forward-biases and the MeanWell seamlessly resumes feeding the Victron. No switching, no firmware, no transfer gap — pure physics determines which source is active at any moment.

The Victron MPPT is designed for photovoltaic installations where the PV array presents as a large antenna for lightning-induced transients. Its input stage is hardened for these environments and provides robust filtering of grid-borne and solar-induced conducted transients — meaningfully more than a direct MeanWell connection alone. The MeanWell’s transformer-based AC/DC stage provides galvanic isolation from mains on both tiers. Solar input, when present, introduces a direct DC path from an outdoor conductor; this is addressed in the solar deployment note below.

?  Solar deployment note. The Smooth Operator tier with solar is the correct choice for deployments where extended grid independence is a design requirement — not as a primary power source, but as a guarantee that the machine is available precisely when the grid is not. A locally-hosted model with stored documentation runs without internet, without utility power, and without a data center. The sun comes up; the machine boots; you solve the problem in front of you. Solar input introduces a direct DC path from an outdoor conductor into the system — the MeanWell’s galvanic isolation from mains does not extend to the solar array. This is a real and unmitigated risk. Standard external surge protection on the solar input is strongly recommended and is outside the scope of this specification. What this system provides is a capable, resilient machine that runs on sunlight. Architect accordingly.

V.  Analog Power Sensor
Both tiers use an identical custom passive analog sensor — the Analog Power Sensor (APS) — to provide the motherboard with a binary grid-present / grid-absent signal. The sensor is a single passive device with no microcontroller and no firmware. It is verifiable by inspection against the published schematic.

The APS is intentionally identical across both tiers. On the Smooth Operator, the APS tap point is upstream of the blocking diode — it reads MeanWell output state directly. This is essential: if the APS were downstream of the blocking diode, solar voltage would hold the optocoupler forward-biased and the GPIO would report AC present even with the grid completely dead. Placing the tap upstream of the diode ensures the GPIO faithfully reflects MeanWell availability, independent of solar conditions.

The Smooth Operator’s BMS bootstrap function is provided by a separate kickstart resistor assembly at the Victron solar/battery node — it is not part of the APS enclosure.

APS Form Factor (Both Tiers)

Sealed polycarbonate enclosure (Hammond 1591 series or equivalent). Two pigtails through individually-sized dedicated cable glands:
• MeanWell sample pigtail — 10 or 14 AWG. 15A MC4 fuse holder captive outside enclosure. Dedicated heavy cable gland.

?  The Smooth Operator blocking diode must never be housed inside the APS enclosure. The APS is a signal device. The blocking diode dissipates 2.5–3.5W continuously under grid charging load — that heat will bake the PC817 optocoupler, desolder connections, and deform the polycarbonate enclosure. ?  Last-ditch surge protection. The APS GPIO signal path is separated from mains by the MeanWell’s AC/DC isolation barrier and the PC817 optocoupler. Both provide meaningful protection against normal conducted transients. For deployments where additional hardening is warranted, add a TVS diode (e.g. P6KE6.8A or equivalent, rated to motherboard GPIO voltage) across the GPIO input pin to ground on the motherboard side of the PC817, and a 1k? series resistor on the phototransistor output leg inside the APS enclosure. This is an integrator-discretion hardening step. Note: the Victron MPPT topology is not relevant to APS exposure — the APS taps the MeanWell output directly and the Victron is downstream of that tap point entirely.

VIII.  Safety Notes
?  This system operates at 36–58V DC depending on tier and battery state. These voltages are not immediately lethal to a healthy adult under typical conditions, but can deliver significant energy in a fault condition. All work must be performed with the system de-energized unless explicitly required for calibration.

?  The battery transfer Schottky diode cathode tab (Smooth Operator) is live at system bus voltage whenever the battery is connected. It must be treated as a live conductor at all times. Never contact the TO-247 metal tab with bare hands or tools while the battery is connected.

?  Smooth Operator solar input (Victron MPPT PV terminals) operates at up to 95.2V DC open-circuit under the specified 4S panel configuration. This voltage is potentially lethal. Solar wiring must use appropriately rated cable and connectors. Never disconnect or connect solar leads under load. Verify panel string Voc does not exceed 100V (Victron SmartSolar MPPT 100/20 maximum input) before energizing.

?  LiFePO4 batteries are thermally stable relative to other lithium chemistries but must not be short-circuited, overcharged, punctured, or exposed to temperatures above 60°C. All charge current must be controlled by the MeanWell Io pot (Hammerhead) or Victron MPPT (Smooth Operator) within BMS-specified limits.

?  All fuse positions require 10×38mm cylindrical PV DC-rated fuses in 60V DC MC4 inline holders. The system bus voltage exceeds the 32V DC maximum rating of automotive blade fuses and MAXI marine blade holders. Using underrated fuse hardware is a fire risk.
