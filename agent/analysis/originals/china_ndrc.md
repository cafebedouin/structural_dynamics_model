# Press Conference on the Economy — Fourth Session of the 14th National People's Congress

*On March 6, the Fourth Session of the 14th National People's Congress held a press conference on the theme of the economy. In attendance were Zheng Shanjie, Director of the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC); Lan Fo'an, Minister of Finance; Wang Wentao, Minister of Commerce; Pan Gongsheng, Governor of the People's Bank of China; and Wu Qing, Chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission. The following is the transcript of NDRC Director Zheng Shanjie's answers to questions from Chinese and foreign reporters.*

---

## Questions and Answers

### Russia Today (RT):

I'd like to ask Director Zheng. The Government Work Report summed up last year's development achievements and set this year's expected macroeconomic growth target at 4.5%–5%, while striving for an even better result in practice. How will China achieve this expected growth target? Thank you.

**Zheng Shanjie, Director of the NDRC:**

Thank you for your question, and thank you to all our media friends for your interest in and support of development and reform work. The achievements of last year's economic work have already been comprehensively summarized in the Central Economic Work Conference and the Government Work Report. The expected growth target you ask about — and which many people are paying close attention to — was put forward by the Party Central Committee and the State Council in a steady and prudent manner, after comprehensive assessment, systematic analysis, and rigorous deliberation. We have a solid foundation for achieving this target, which is reflected in at least three areas: aggregate scale, innovation capacity, and risk response. First, in terms of aggregate scale, last year the economy exceeded 140 trillion yuan, reaching a new level; the 14th Five-Year Plan concluded successfully, delivering a "report card" that exceeded expectations on all sides. Second, in terms of innovation capacity, our innovation achievements and major breakthroughs have drawn worldwide attention — as everyone can see for themselves — which shows that our development momentum is stronger, and these innovations also benefit the lives of the people. Third, our institutional strengths have been brought into full play, allowing us to respond firmly, flexibly, and effectively to risks and challenges from all directions. These enormous scale and volume, the vitality and breakthroughs in innovation, and our institutional and systemic advantages give us the confidence and the assurance to deal with risks, challenges, and market fluctuations from all quarters and to achieve our set and expected targets.

At the same time, we are clear-eyed about the fact that there are still many difficulties and problems in the process of economic and social development. This year is the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan, and how we begin bears on the whole. To achieve the expected targets, we must conscientiously and solidly implement the decisions and arrangements of the Central Economic Work Conference and the work plans of the Government Work Report, focusing our efforts on four areas.

**First, stepping up efforts to improve the effectiveness of macroeconomic regulation.** We will adhere to seeking progress while maintaining stability, improving quality and efficiency, emphasizing both intensity and coordination. "Intensity" means continuing to implement more proactive and effective macro policies, and increasing the strength of counter-cyclical and cross-cyclical adjustment. "Coordination" means leveraging the combined effect of existing and incremental policies and the superimposed effect of macro policy and reform measures — strengthening coordination across fiscal, monetary, industrial, investment, employment, consumption, price, regional, and other policies, so as to deliver a well-executed policy "combination punch." This year's GDP increment is projected to exceed 6 trillion yuan — equivalent to the full-year GDP of a developed economy — providing strong support for stabilizing employment, improving livelihoods, and preventing risks. At the same time, we are keenly aware that a market economy is in essence a rule-of-law economy. To conduct macro regulation well under new conditions, we must both "let things stay vibrant" and "manage them well," using the rule of law to genuinely define the boundaries between the market and the government.

**Second, stepping up efforts to build a strong domestic market.** China's volume of physical consumption already ranks first in the world. This year's work centers on two areas: consumption and investment. On consumption, we will continue to implement the "two new" policies [large-scale equipment renewals and consumer-goods trade-ins], and, together with the Ministry of Commerce and the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, deeply implement a special action to boost consumption, rolling out new measures such as an action to stabilize and expand employment with higher quality and an action to expand the capacity and quality of the service sector, while arranging more policies and funding to better unleash consumption potential. On investment, we will coordinate resources and forces to continue advancing work such as the "two priorities" construction [implementing major national strategies and building security capacity in key areas]. In terms of the actors involved, we will further increase the scale of government investment, stimulate the vitality of private investment, and push state-owned and private enterprises to work together. In terms of where investment goes, we will advance the 109 major projects and undertakings of the 15th Five-Year Plan and carry out a batch of actions to expand effective investment. For example, we will advance the "six networks" and the construction of key sectors to continually improve everyone's production conditions and living environment. The "six networks" are the water network, power grid, computing-power network, new-type communications network, urban underground pipeline network, and logistics network; the key sectors include integrated and multi-modal transportation facilities, as well as infrastructure and public-service facilities for consumption, the low-altitude economy, "AI Plus," education, healthcare, and the like. We preliminarily estimate that investment in these areas this year will exceed 7 trillion yuan.

**Third, stepping up efforts to strengthen the modern industrial system.** We will coordinate the advancement of sci-tech innovation and industrial innovation, and the deep integration of advanced manufacturing and modern services, accelerating the renewal of old growth drivers and the growth of new ones. On the one hand, we will optimize and upgrade the fundamentals; on the other, we will cultivate and strengthen new pillars. Optimizing and upgrading the fundamentals means implementing a series of actions for key industries to improve quality and upgrade, cut costs, and reduce carbon — arranging 200 billion yuan of ultra-long-term special treasury bonds to support equipment renewal, and pushing manufacturing to move faster from large to strong, from "leading in scale" to "leading in strength." At the same time, after the Two Sessions we will convene a national service-sector conference, and, together with the Ministry of Commerce, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and other departments, roll out a series of measures to expand capacity and quality, fully tapping the potential for high-quality development in the service sector; during the 15th Five-Year Plan period, the service sector is projected to surpass 100 trillion yuan in scale. Cultivating and strengthening new pillars means implementing an industrial innovation project and stepping up support for emerging and future industries. For example, the BeiDou system may seem far away, but it is in fact right at hand — including the phone navigation we use every day, as well as emergency rescue, ocean-going operations, and so on, all of which rely on BeiDou's services. We will continue to implement the BeiDou large-scale application project, pushing the BeiDou industry to exceed 1 trillion yuan in scale within five years. As another example, we will deepen the "AI Plus" action, empowering thousands of industries and serving thousands of households; by the end of the 15th Five-Year Plan, the scale of AI-related industries will grow to over 10 trillion yuan. To step up support for innovation, last year we established a national venture-capital guidance fund; this year, together with the Ministry of Finance, the People's Bank of China, and other departments, we will set up an additional national-level merger-and-acquisition fund, further smoothing the exit channels for venture capital and raising the efficiency of venture-capital turnover — this is expected to guide and leverage over 1 trillion yuan of funds of various kinds.

**Fourth, stepping up efforts to clear the chokepoints and bottlenecks in economic and social development.** We will deepen reform and improve the legal system, ensuring in accordance with laws and regulations that the public and enterprises genuinely feel "three fewers": first, fewer and fewer barriers; second, fewer and fewer unreasonable restrictions; and third, through combining targeted and broad measures and applying systematic policies, fewer and fewer problems.

### Xinhua News Agency:

Director Zheng Shanjie, the outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan has now been unveiled, and we see many new measures and highlights in it. How should we better understand and grasp these new key elements? Thank you.

**Zheng Shanjie, Director of the NDRC:**

Thank you for your question. The draft outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan does indeed contain many new measures and highlights. To implement the spirit of the Party Central Committee's Recommendations, the State Council organized the drafting of the draft outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan, and the NDRC, together with relevant parties, conducted solid drafting work; over these past few days, the deputies and committee members have been reviewing and discussing the draft outline. In my understanding, advancing development under the 15th Five-Year Plan requires "three grasps."

**First, grasping the historical task of linking past and future.** Both the Recommendations for the 15th Five-Year Plan and the draft outline mention two crucial phrases: "consolidating the foundation" and "exerting comprehensive effort." This is a very important formulation: we must both handle well those matters that have a foundational bearing on development and security — making the foundations of development steadier and resilience stronger — and focus on the strategically contested areas of the modernization process and future development, making the first move and seizing the initiative. For example, we must look ahead to address the impact of important fundamental variables such as population. China's population has, overall, shifted from incremental development to a stage of contractionary development and high-quality development, exhibiting features such as fewer children, an aging society, and divergence in regional population growth and decline; we need to optimize the allocation of public resources accordingly. Taking education as an example, the school-age populations for China's three stages — junior high, senior high, and higher education — will peak respectively this year, in 2029, and in 2032. To respond to these three wave-like peaks, the draft outline makes the construction of ordinary senior high schools and the expansion of high-quality undergraduate education key tasks, addressing and resolving this real-world problem in a targeted way. As another example, we must shore up the weak links in basic research and original innovation. China's innovation-driven development keeps making new breakthroughs, and by 2035 we will have built a leading country in education, science and technology, and talent — but at present our original-innovation capacity still has weak links. On this, the draft outline lays out arrangements for strengthening frontier sci-tech research and for advancing the development of education, science and technology, and talent in an integrated way.

**Second, grasping the distinctive theme of high-quality development.** The 15th Five-Year Plan makes promoting high-quality development its theme, in continuity with the 14th Five-Year Plan, and this also reflects establishing and practicing a correct view of performance and achievement. In practice, this means adhering to economic development as the central task: on the one hand, fully unleashing the potential for economic growth and promoting reasonable growth in quantity. The draft outline proposes keeping GDP growth over these five years within a reasonable range, without a specific quantitative target — but it also includes one further sentence: to lay the foundation for doubling per-capita GDP by 2035 compared with 2020, reaching the level of a moderately developed country. This formulation is both qualitative and carries quantitative implications. On the other hand, it means promoting the optimization and upgrading of the economic structure, and advancing effective improvement in quality. The draft outline makes the steady improvement of total factor productivity an important goal and lays out arrangements for the deep integration of sci-tech innovation and industrial innovation — all of which are key directions for improving quality and efficiency. At the same time, it emphasizes coordinating development and security, effectively preventing and defusing risks of all kinds to ensure that Chinese-style modernization advances steadily and far.

**Third, grasping the value orientation of putting the people first.** A five-year plan is both "state business" and "family business." The draft outline lists 20 main indicators, with those concerning people's wellbeing accounting for the highest share — 7 indicators set across employment, income, education, healthcare, elderly care, childcare, and the life expectancy that everyone is personally concerned about, guiding the upgrading of livelihood protections from "having" to "being good." At the same time, the 109 major projects and undertakings we have arranged emphasize the close integration of investing in physical things and investing in people, making the resolution of the people's most pressing difficulties and concerns a priority. In areas such as care for "the old and the young," primary-level medical services, and vocational-skills training, we will implement a batch of livelihood projects that people can perceive and benefit from, getting a number of practical livelihood matters done in a real and effective way.

In addition, there is an important item on this year's Two Sessions agenda: deliberation on the draft National Development Planning Law. This law concentrates the successful experience of guiding economic and social development through five-year plans under the leadership of the Communist Party of China, and it will provide strong rule-of-law guarantees for formulating and implementing plans.

### Shenzhen Radio & Television, *First Scene*:

Director Zheng Shanjie, over the past year, high-tech industries such as artificial intelligence and robotics have shown strong development momentum and left a deep impression on everyone. Going forward, what policy support does the NDRC have for the development of these industries? Thank you.

**Zheng Shanjie, Director of the NDRC:**

Thank you for your question. We deeply feel the phenomena and situation you describe — there are plenty of examples and data on this. For instance, a short video went viral recently: when a cargo ship on the Yangtze River passed the Nanjing section, a drone delivered takeout food to it — fast, steady, and convenient. It was a vivid scene. All sorts of new scenarios will keep emerging, driving new technologies to land faster and new industries to develop faster. In 2025, China's high-tech manufacturing, with a share of 17%, contributed 26% of industrial growth — the result of the combined effects of our institutional advantage, market advantage, industrial advantage, and talent advantage. We can be certain — and we will see more clearly — that during the 15th Five-Year Plan there will be a batch of high-growth industries gathering strength to surge ahead. Through creating increments and tapping the potential of existing stock, we will form several markets at the trillion-yuan scale or even larger, pushing the industrial system from large to strong and from quantitative change to qualitative change, becoming a new engine of high-quality development.

On the increment side specifically, let me focus on emerging industries and future industries. The capital markets are very interested in this, and we very much hope these emerging and future industries can provide support for the capital markets. We will focus on building six major emerging pillar industries and six major future industries. The six major emerging pillar industries include integrated circuits, aerospace, biopharmaceuticals, the low-altitude economy, new-type energy storage, and intelligent robots. At this year's Spring Festival Gala, our robots were not just showing off — there was a lot of technical substance to them as well. Robots are now developing in two directions: first, increasingly resembling humans, which will have broad application scenarios; and second, increasingly not resembling humans — depending on the needs of industrial development, they may end up with six fingers, eight fingers, or grow five or six arms. By preliminary calculation, the output value related to these six industries was already approaching 6 trillion yuan in 2025, and is expected to double or more by 2030, expanding to over 10 trillion yuan. The six major future industries include quantum technology, biomanufacturing, green hydrogen and nuclear fusion energy, brain-computer interfaces, embodied intelligence, and the just-emerging 6G. These industries are at the "eve" of technological breakthroughs; today's future industries may well be tomorrow's emerging pillar industries. I believe more and more pleasant surprises will be revealed to everyone. On the existing-stock side, we will continue to make traditional industries bigger and stronger. By pushing key industries to improve quality and upgrade, we expect that over the next five years, traditional industries will add a vast market of over 10 trillion yuan — likewise an important source of development momentum. Next, for high-growth industries, we will focus prominently on three areas of work.

**First, prominent long-term layout.** This long-term layout looks far ahead and is considered in connection with building Chinese-style modernization. In fields such as integrated circuits, satellite internet, domestically produced large aircraft, and the national integrated computing-power network, we will build a batch of long-chain, large-volume major projects — with investment scales reaching the hundred-billion or even trillion-yuan level — forging a batch of "national heavy weapons" that lay foundations and benefit the long term.

**Second, prominent factor guarantees.** Last year, here, also on March 6, I introduced to everyone the national venture-capital guidance fund. Together with the Ministry of Finance, the National Financial Regulatory Administration, the China Securities Regulatory Commission, and other departments, and working forcefully with localities such as Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong, the national venture-capital guidance fund and three regional funds are now up and running, benefiting a large batch of start-up enterprises across many fields. This year, as I just mentioned, we will set up a national-level M&A fund to help resolve the "difficulty of exit" for venture capital; at the same time, we will support carrying out mergers, acquisitions, and restructuring to help resolve the problem of "involutionary" competition.

**Third, prominent reform coordination.** We will pay attention to both the "hard inputs" of project funding and the "soft construction" of systems and mechanisms. For example, we will promote making intangible assets such as software development and data processing "bookable" and "monetizable," better reflecting the value of intellectual work and forming positive incentives. As another example, scenarios are a key link from technological breakthrough to industrial application; we will increase the intensity of reform and implement an action for the large-scale application and demonstration of new technologies, new products, and new scenarios. This year, at the national level, we will roll out around 10 comprehensive open scenarios in key fields, and in fields such as modern agriculture and elderly-care services, identify around 100 landmark scenario projects with a leading and driving role, to boost innovative industrial development.

### *The Beijing News*:

My question is for Director Zheng of the NDRC. Last year's press conference on the achievements of the 14th Five-Year Plan introduced the achievements of 102 major projects, and things like China's space station, the large aircraft, and the "Three-North" Shelterbelt Program have become "calling cards" for China that are widely discussed by the international community. I'd like to ask, Director Zheng, what is your view? What major projects will there be during the 15th Five-Year Plan, and how will they be advanced and implemented going forward?

**Zheng Shanjie, Director of the NDRC:**

Thank you for your question. From the First Five-Year Plan, drawn up in the 1950s, to the 14th Five-Year Plan we have just completed, batch after batch of major projects and undertakings have been implemented in succession, turning the goals we set into reality one by one. This draft outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan puts forward 109 major projects and undertakings — including both major projects that "reach the heavens, plumb the seas, and span thousands of miles," and livelihood projects bearing on everyone's food, clothing, housing, and transport. Specifically, there are six categories: 28 leading the development of new quality productive forces, 23 building a modern infrastructure system, 9 promoting integrated urban-rural development, 25 safeguarding and improving people's wellbeing, 18 promoting the green and low-carbon transition, and 6 ensuring security in key areas. We believe that implementing these major projects and undertakings — which closely integrate investing in physical things with investing in people — will make our comprehensive national strength stronger, our social safety net steadier, and residents' lives better. Overall, this can be summed up as "three batches."

**First, from the standpoint of long-term layout, implementing a batch of strategic projects.** We will continue to advance a series of energy mega-projects with investments of over a trillion yuan each — such as the lower Yarlung Zangbo hydropower project, the new-energy bases in deserts, the Gobi, and barren lands, and offshore wind-power bases — to safeguard the country's modernization drive and the people's happy lives. We will implement "dual-carbon" projects such as energy conservation and carbon reduction in key sectors and fields; these are very important, requiring both increases and reductions. We will also advance a series of major transportation projects — such as the new Three Gorges waterway passage and the new corridor for the southern section of the Beijing-Guangzhou high-speed railway — basically completing the "eight vertical and eight horizontal" high-speed rail trunk corridors and the national expressway network, and fully connecting the backbone highway corridors linking China's land border regions, thereby both lowering social logistics costs and supporting the public's convenient travel, better integrating investing in physical things with investing in people. For instance, at present we already have the world's largest high-speed rail network — about 50,000 kilometers, accounting for roughly 70% of the world's total high-speed rail mileage — allowing us to travel within a single day from the ice and snow of the Northeast to the spring-like warmth of Lingnan. There is also the Daliangshan slow train, the warm-hearted "market-day train" for the local folks, and the green-skinned trains of the Spring Festival travel rush, the "reunion train" for returning home to visit family. There is both a fast and a slow, and between this fast and this slow lies a solemn commitment to full-coverage service and leaving no one behind in development. In other words, whatever the people hope for and whatever development needs, that is what we strive to build.

**Second, from the standpoint of future development, advancing a batch of leading future-industry projects.** As I introduced earlier, we will lay out a series of new industries and new tracks. During the 15th Five-Year Plan we will also advance a series of major projects such as ultra-large-scale AI intelligent-computing clusters, satellite internet, and controlled nuclear fusion — letting more sci-tech achievements turn faster into useful products and services that make our lives more convenient, and letting new industries and new tracks grow faster into new productive forces, new growth points, and new employment opportunities, achieving global leading or parallel-running positions in more fields.

**Third, from the standpoint of the real needs of thousands of households, implementing a batch of livelihood projects with warmth and a real sense of gain.** Addressing the social and livelihood issues that everyone is broadly concerned about, in fields such as education, healthcare, elderly and childcare, and care for children in difficult circumstances and left-behind children, we will implement a batch of major systematic projects and clusters of undertakings that people can perceive and benefit from, raising the share of government investment in livelihood-related areas, continually improving the level of public services, making everyone's lives more convenient and comfortable, and providing more security when they encounter urgent or difficult matters. On this, we will work persistently over the long haul, continually putting in effort and seeking real results.

The vitality of a plan lies in its execution. During the 15th Five-Year Plan, major projects and undertakings will be advanced in an interlocking manner, providing strong guarantees for the plan's execution. We will, according to five categories — completed and built, continuing to advance, newly started, in preliminary-work stage, and under planning and feasibility study — apply differentiated policies to speed up construction, implementing these projects and undertakings step by step to support the realization of the plan's goals and tasks.

### Zhejiang Daily, *Tide News*:

Hello, Director Zheng! I'd like to share my own experience. I used to always worry that my parents are getting older and that cooking is inconvenient for them and they aren't eating well. When I went home for the New Year, I saw that the community canteen downstairs was convenient, affordable, and healthy, and right next to it were a nursery, a gym, a community clinic, and a university for the elderly — all very popular. However, the coverage of these services is still limited at present. During the 15th Five-Year Plan, how will these livelihood services be expanded?

**Zheng Shanjie, Director of the NDRC:**

I recall that you also asked me about livelihood matters last year — this is a very important issue. In his New Year's message this year, the General Secretary said that when every "small family" is bustling and warm, the big family of China thrives and flourishes. On this, we will do our best within our means to deliver well the livelihood services that everyone hopes for. The focus is on caring for two groups of people and doing well in three services.

The two groups of people are "the old" and "the young" — especially "the older elderly" and "the youngest children."

For elderly groups with higher needs for care, such as the advanced-age and those with physical or cognitive impairments, we will be demand-oriented and strive to provide more professional and convenient elderly-care services. For example, to meet the care needs of the elderly with physical or cognitive impairments, we will support equipment renewal in elderly-care institutions, renovate and upgrade 2,000 public elderly-care institutions, and strive to raise the proportion of nursing-type beds in elderly-care institutions to over 73% — there is still considerable social demand in this area. As another example, to meet home-based elderly-care needs, we will shore up the shortfall in elderly-care service facilities in old residential communities, promote community services such as meal assistance, medical assistance, and emergency assistance, and strive to raise the coverage of community elderly-care service institutions and facilities to over 70%. At the same time, we will include the installation of elevators in old residential communities within the scope of "two new" support — this too is a new policy and scope of support set up in response to everyone's calls and requests.

For infants and toddlers aged 0–3, we will strive to provide childrearing families with more inclusive childcare services that are close to home, affordable, and high-quality. On the one hand, we will support every prefecture-level city in building at least one comprehensive childcare service center, exercising functions such as service leadership, personnel training, standard-setting, and family guidance, establishing visible service benchmarks. On the other hand, we will support localities in developing integrated childcare-and-preschool services, fully utilizing street and community facilities to weave a dense, within-reach service network.

The three services are healthcare, education, and employment.

On medical treatment and seeking care, we will deeply implement a project to strengthen the foundations of healthcare, building around 1,000 closely integrated county-level medical communities, so that more people consult for common illnesses at the primary level, manage chronic diseases at the primary level, and receive everyday health services at the primary level. On rehabilitation, together with the National Health Commission we will launch a project to expand and upgrade rehabilitation and nursing care — guided and supported by tertiary hospitals, with secondary hospitals as the main body, primary-level medical and health institutions as the bottom layer of the network, and rehabilitation and nursing specialty hospitals participating together — so that more people enjoy high-quality rehabilitation and nursing services.

On education, as I introduced earlier, the school-age population will peak in wave-like fashion in the coming years; this is also a new challenge for us, and from the standpoint of resource allocation it is very difficult. We will tailor precise policies for different educational stages. Compulsory education must promote balance, implementing a project to standardize the construction of urban and rural compulsory-education schools. Ordinary senior high schools must expand supply, building and renovating/expanding 1,000 ordinary senior high schools and adding over 2 million places. Higher education must improve quality and upgrade, supporting "Double First-Class" universities in expanding undergraduate enrollment by over 100,000, and strengthening 200 application-oriented undergraduate institutions. Vocational education must develop its own distinctive features, building 500 high-level industry-education integration training bases, so that more graduates master the "marketable skill" the market needs.

On employment, we will give equal weight to expanding the volume and providing protection. On the one hand, we will do everything possible to expand the increment. During the 15th Five-Year Plan, China's manufacturing and service sectors will have over 40 trillion yuan of growth space, expected to create over 10 million jobs each year. We will also pay attention to AI's impact on employment, improve the skills-training system, and give full play to AI's role in creating new jobs and empowering traditional ones. On the other hand, we will strengthen protection through multiple measures. For example, we will support population-inflow areas in advancing the coverage of 81 basic public services for the permanent resident population, reducing the worries of new urban residents. As another example, we will raise the participation rates of flexibly employed people, migrant workers, and those in new forms of employment, reducing the worries of groups such as food-delivery riders and courier workers.
