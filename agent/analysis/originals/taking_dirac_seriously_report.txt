TAKING DIRAC SERIOUSLY
A Speculative Magnetic-Matter Sector and Its Cosmological, Galactic, Planetary, and Solar Signatures
Luke Graysmith
Conversation-derived research prospectus  |  July 2026
Status of the document
   This report is a disciplined reconstruction of a speculative hypothesis, not a claim of discovery. Established results, conditional deductions, and conjectural applications are labeled separately. The purpose is to make the idea precise enough that a modern theorist can decide quickly whether it is inconsistent, calculable, or unexpectedly fertile.
Abstract
Dirac quantization implies that a minimally charged magnetic monopole is not a weakly coupled magnetic analogue of the electron: in common conventions its magnetic fine-structure constant is alpha_m = 1/(4 alpha) approximately 34.3. This report asks what follows if that observation is elevated from a statement about isolated monopoles to a speculative matter sector containing magnetically charged leptons and color-carrying baryons, sharing the ordinary photon and, by hypothesis, the same SU(3) color interaction as ordinary quarks. The immediate consequence is a nonperturbative binding problem. Neutral magnetic composites could form early, hide their net monopole charge, and aggregate into compact droplets whose cross section per unit mass decreases with size. Small fragments could couple to ordinary nuclei through shared QCD at contact, while large droplets could behave gravitationally like cold macroscopic dark matter. The hypothesis is then mapped onto early black-hole seeding and JWST little red dots, galaxy formation, compact halo populations, anomalous mass concentrations in asteroid Psyche, and central components in the Sun. The same breadth that makes the idea attractive also makes it highly constrained: a viable theory must solve electric-magnetic quantum-field-theory consistency, fractional magnetic charge quantization, new colored degrees of freedom, mixed-sector nuclear binding, fission and passivation, Big Bang nucleosynthesis and CMB limits, free-monopole bounds, compact-object searches, and solar/planetary survival. The report closes with a concrete calculation program and a short list of falsifiable observational signatures.
Keywords: magnetic monopoles; Dirac quantization; composite dark matter; macroscopic dark matter; early black-hole seeds; little red dots; Psyche; helioseismology


1. Executive framing
   The conjecture can be stated in one sentence: the universe may contain a matter sector whose particles carry magnetic rather than electric charge, whose baryons still carry ordinary QCD color, and whose surviving matter asymmetry rapidly neutralized into compact composites that later behaved partly as cold dark matter and partly as an efficient source of early compact seeds.
   This is not the standard notion of a mirror sector. A conventional mirror Standard Model has a separate photon and usually a separate color gauge group. The present hypothesis is more radical and more testable: there is one observable photon, both electric and magnetic charges couple to it, and ordinary and magnetic quarks share the same gluon field. Those choices create the proposed phenomenology, but they also create the hardest consistency requirements.

Central discipline
   The hypothesis should be judged not by the number of phenomena it can narrate, but by whether one parameter set simultaneously survives cosmology, laboratory searches, stellar structure, planetary survival, and compact-object constraints.2. Dirac quantization and the magnetic coupling
   Dirac showed that the unobservability of the string attached to a point monopole requires electric and magnetic charges to lie on a quantized lattice [1]. In rationalized natural units a common convention is
e g = 2 pi n,        alpha_e = e^2/(4 pi),        alpha_m = g^2/(4 pi).
   With the alternative Gaussian normalization used earlier in the conversation, the same invariant statement is
alpha_e alpha_m = n^2/4.
   For n = 1 and alpha_e approximately 1/137.036,
alpha_m approximately 34.26.
   The number is the first engine of the proposal. It means that a minimally charged magnetic constituent is not a perturbative copy of an electron. Any Coulombic atomic calculation that substitutes alpha_m for alpha immediately enters a supercritical, relativistic regime. A formal proton-electron mass analogue would have a nonrelativistic length scale a_m = hbar/(mu c alpha_m) approximately 1.1 x 10^-14 m and a formal binding energy near 3 x 10^8 eV, far above the assumed lepton rest energy. The contradiction is informative: the size and binding of a magnetic neutral must be set by finite monopole cores, compositeness, pair creation, confinement, or another ultraviolet scale, not by the Schrödinger hydrogen problem.
   A theory containing both electric and magnetic charges is also technically subtle. Locality, Lorentz covariance, charge quantization, and coupling renormalization are commonly handled with patched gauge potentials, line operators, or two-potential formalisms such as Zwanziger's [3,4]. Fractional quark charges make the global structure of the Standard Model gauge group relevant; the allowed monopole charge lattice depends on the quotient of SU(3) x SU(2) x U(1), not merely on the electron charge [5]. A serious version of the hypothesis must specify that global structure before assigning fractional magnetic charges to magnetic quarks.
3. Minimal speculative particle content
   For discussion, define a magnetic baryon B_m with net magnetic charge +g and a magnetic lepton L_m with charge -g, together with antiparticles. Let magnetic quarks carry color under the same SU(3)_c as ordinary quarks, while their electromagnetic quantum numbers are magnetic. This is only an effective inventory; anomaly cancellation, electroweak representations, masses, and the ultraviolet origin of the monopoles remain unspecified.
B_m(+g) + L_m(-g) -> A_m(0) + photons.
   A primordial asymmetry can leave magnetic matter without a net cosmic magnetic charge if the surviving charge asymmetries balance: Delta n_Bm approximately Delta n_Lm. Pair annihilation removes the symmetric component; radiative recombination and many-body encounters then place the remainder into neutral composites. Because the photon is shared, this sector is not automatically thermally dark. Before neutralization it contributes to the electromagnetic plasma; afterward its coupling depends on size, multipoles, polarizability, and nuclear contact.
   The shared-color assumption is both powerful and dangerous. If magnetic quarks are additional light colored flavors, they alter QCD running, hadron spectroscopy, early-universe thermodynamics, and collider production. A viable model probably requires them to be heavy, topological, confined in nonstandard states, or otherwise inaccessible in ordinary high-energy collisions. This is not a small correction; it is one of the first calculations a particle theorist should attack.
4. Neutral atoms, few-body clusters, and droplets
   The phrase magnetic atom should not be allowed to smuggle in ordinary atomic intuition. At alpha_m much greater than 1, the constituent motion is relativistic and the vacuum can respond strongly. The existence, spin, parity, radius, and permanent multipole moments of the lowest neutral state must be solved nonperturbatively. A rotationally invariant isolated ground state need not have a permanent dipole even if its constituents have opposite magnetic charge; it can instead have transition moments and a large or small induced polarizability.
   If several neutrals bind, two limiting geometries are plausible. In a molecular regime the positive magnetic baryonic cores remain distinct and magnetic leptons occupy delocalized shared orbitals. In a merged-core regime the baryons form a common QCD droplet and the leptons form a relativistic screening distribution. Classical point charges on a spherical surface would adopt antipodal, triangular, tetrahedral, and higher polyhedral correlation patterns for small particle number, but the exact quantum state can remain rotationally symmetric in its one-body density.
   For large particle number, a snowflake is more naturally a kinetic aggregate than a ground state. Isotropic attraction, surface tension, particle mobility, and radiation tend to anneal a branching object into a compact droplet. The relevant bulk energy can be parameterized in liquid-drop form,
E(N) = a_v N + a_s N^(2/3) + E_shell(N) + E_screen(N) + E_grav(N).
   The sign of a_v determines whether bulk magnetic nuclear matter is self-bound. The surface term favors coalescence. Imperfect magnetic screening, fermion pressure, shell closures, or an ordinary-matter mantle can favor fission. Gravity is not necessarily the force holding microscopic or planetary-size pieces together; QCD and degeneracy can self-bind them. Gravity becomes decisive only when the object is sufficiently massive that its compactness 2GM/(Rc^2) is appreciable.

5. Collisions, sticking, fission, and the mass spectrum
   A neutral-neutral encounter has three logically separate stages: reaching short range, forming a transient complex, and losing enough energy to remain bound. The first stage can have a small cross section if the neutral is compact and well screened. The second can be violent because the constituents are strongly coupled. The third requires photons, fragment emission, a third body, or access to many internal degrees of freedom.
   For two isolated few-body neutrals, a newly formed dimer complex is born above its breakup threshold. It sticks only if radiation or another inelastic channel outpaces redissociation. For large droplets, the situation changes: surface deformation, neck formation, internal particle-hole excitations, and fragmentation provide many sinks for relative kinetic energy. Low-speed droplet collisions can therefore have a high trapping probability even when atom-atom radiative association is inefficient.
   Fission is not a side issue. If a droplet becomes unstable after absorbing additional magnetic or ordinary baryons, it can split into daughters, increasing the total exposed area. At fixed total mass divided into N_f equal fragments, the geometric area scales as A_tot proportional to N_f^(1/3). Fragmentation can therefore turn a gravitationally modest population into a chemically or nuclearly active aerosol. Conversely, repeated mergers reduce area per unit mass and make the surviving population increasingly CDM-like.
Key emergent variable
   The cosmology is controlled by a mass spectrum, not a single ball mass. Small fragments dominate contact area and ordinary-matter coupling; large fragments dominate gravitational granularity, lensing, dynamical friction, and compact-seed formation.6. Interaction with ordinary matter: electromagnetic exterior, QCD interior
   At distances larger than the screening length, a neutral and symmetric ball has no monopole field. Permanent dipole or quadrupole fields depend on its quantum state and surface structure; induced polarization may be more important. Thus an ordinary atom can miss a small ball almost completely unless it crosses the projected area or encounters an exposed magnetic defect.
   At nuclear contact, the shared-color assumption changes everything. Ordinary protons and neutrons and magnetic baryons are different baryon species coupled by the same gluon field. The closest established analogy is hypernuclear physics, where a distinguishable baryon can bind inside an ordinary nucleus without being Pauli blocked by nucleons [6]. An ordinary nucleus that physically overlaps magnetic nuclear matter should undergo strong scattering, deformation, spallation, capture, or hybridization on femtometer scales. It should not traverse a micron-thick nuclear-density ball intact.
   The outcome is governed by a mixed-sector separation energy,
S_X(A,Z) = E(A,Z) + E(X) - E(A,Z;X).
   If S_X is positive and the compound has channels to redistribute energy, an ordinary nucleus can be retained as hybrid nuclear matter. If it is negative, the surface can reflect or fragment the nucleus. If capture energy exceeds a heavy nucleus's fission barrier, uranium can fission after a direct hit; whether the magnetic neutral emerges again or remains in a daughter fragment determines whether the process is catalytic or one-shot.
   A small ball may self-limit. Absorbed protons add ordinary electric charge unless ordinary electrons follow. A mixed mantle can passivate the surface, alter the magnetic screening length, or destabilize the droplet to fission. These effects provide a physically plausible route by which Earth survives even if rare balls are captured: contact can be strong without implying unlimited conversion of ordinary matter.
7. Early-universe history and cold-dark-matter behavior
   The proposed chronology begins after inflation with a plasma containing magnetic matter and antimatter. Once the relevant species become nonrelativistic, pair annihilation removes the symmetric abundance. A balanced baryon-lepton asymmetry leaves nearly zero net magnetic charge but nonzero mass. Strong radiative recombination and coagulation then shift the abundance into neutral composites.
   This sequence must be demonstrated with coupled Boltzmann and coagulation equations. The shared photon bath makes Big Bang nucleosynthesis, the photon temperature, spectral distortions, and the CMB sensitive to the timing of annihilation and neutralization. Existing macro-dark-matter analyses already constrain baryon capture and momentum exchange in terms of sigma/M [7,8]. The proposal can evade free-monopole searches only if the abundance of unpaired magnetic charge is extraordinarily small and the composites remain neutral under collisions and fission.
   After neutralization, the large-ball tail can be effectively cold and collisionless because sigma/M decreases as M^(-1/3). During halo formation these objects undergo violent relaxation and occupy a spheroidal distribution rather than cooling into a thin disk. Small fragments, by contrast, can be dragged by dense gas, captured by stars, or processed in planetary bodies. Very massive clumps can sink by dynamical friction. The natural result is a three-way partition:
• a virialized halo of weakly interacting compact balls, observationally resembling granular CDM rather than WIMPs;
• a partially dissipated or captured component associated with stars, dense gas, and a possible thick disk;
• a central cusp and compact seeds fed by low-angular-momentum objects, mergers, and stellar delivery.
   Whether the halo is acceptable depends on the full mass function. Microscopic composites face particle and multiple-scatter constraints; kilogram-to-asteroid masses face macro-transit, geological, stellar-capture, and occultation searches; planetary-to-stellar masses face microlensing and dynamical constraints. A viable spectrum may occupy several windows or constitute only a subcomponent of total dark matter.
8. Galaxy formation and little red dots
   In this picture, magnetic matter does not replace ordinary baryons in galaxy formation. It changes the initial conditions. Ordinary gas provides shocks, radiation, chemistry, stars, and the observed spectrum. Magnetic balls provide a compact, partly collisionless mass component plus a route to unusually early central seeds.
   The attractive application to JWST little red dots is therefore narrow: the hypothesis could help explain why a substantial compact central mass exists early, not why the source is red. LRDs are a heterogeneous and actively debated population of compact high-redshift sources, many with V-shaped continua and broad Balmer features; interpretations include accreting black holes in dense envelopes, compact star formation, and mixed scenarios [9,10]. The magnetic sector would supply seed mass; ordinary gas around the seed would still have to reproduce the Balmer lines, reprocessed continuum, X-ray weakness, host properties, and redshift evolution.
   Once a magnetic droplet exceeds its maximum supported mass, it collapses into a black hole. Continued infall no longer requires the formation of a stable material composite. A seed can then grow by swallowing magnetic balls, ordinary gas, stars, and other black holes. The proposed chain is
magnetic asymmetry -> neutral composites -> compact droplets -> first black holes -> massive early seeds -> ordinary-gas LRD phase.
   This is potentially useful because a horizon changes the aggregation problem: before collapse, each merger must dispose of binding energy and avoid fission; after collapse, matter crossing the capture region is retained. But the claim remains quantitative. The model must predict a seed mass function, formation redshift, abundance, spatial bias, and merger history that match LRD demographics without overproducing black holes, gravitational waves, or compact-object lensing.
9. Planetary accumulation and the Psyche test
   A metal-rich differentiated body is an appealing archive because ordinary nuclei that intersect a magnetic fragment encounter QCD at contact, while the body preserves an integrated mass anomaly over billions of years. Psyche is especially relevant because the mission is designed to map its internal mass distribution rather than merely photograph its surface.
   The official gravity investigation expects GM precision better than 0.001 km^3 s^-2 and a spherical-harmonic gravity field to at least degree and order 10; inclusion of the lowest orbit may reach degree 12, corresponding to roughly 30 km spatial resolution. Bouguer anomalies will be formed by subtracting topographic gravity, and the gravity-shape relation can constrain the moment-of-inertia factor and radial mass distribution [11]. NASA currently expects arrival in July 2029 and prime operations beginning in August 2029 [12].
   The cleanest signatures are not isolated micron tracks. They are:
• an anomalously low moment-of-inertia factor, indicating more central mass than ordinary metal/porosity models allow;
• positive off-center Bouguer anomalies without corresponding topography, composition, or impact structure;
• a center-of-mass/center-of-figure offset or gravity harmonics that require implausibly dense inclusions;
• a population of mascons whose inferred density is far above iron-nickel material.
   Present-day halo rain is unlikely to build a detectable reservoir by itself. Using a local dark-matter density of 0.3 GeV cm^-3, speed 220 km s^-1, radius 113 km, and 4.5 Gyr, Psyche geometrically intercepts only about 7 x 10^11 kg even if capture is perfect. A detectable anomaly would more plausibly be primordial, inherited during planetesimal formation, or supplied by a rare massive clump. This turns Psyche into a test of early Solar-System segregation rather than a simple current-flux detector.
10. The Sun: gravity, nuclear contact, and current anomalies
   A central solar concentration has two qualitatively distinct effects. Its total mass changes hydrostatic structure and oscillation modes. Its exposed surface area changes nuclear processing because ordinary ions that reach magnetic nuclear matter encounter shared QCD.
   For a nonreactive compact core, recent solar-evolution calculations find that acoustic helioseismology can probe central masses down to about 10^-5 solar masses, and future secure g-mode measurements could reach around 10^-7 solar masses. Such a core produces a dense baryonic cusp, altered settling, off-center pp burning, and enhanced central CNO activity [15]. A reactive or fragmented population can matter at far smaller total mass because its rate scales approximately with total area, ion density, velocity, and sticking probability rather than with gravity alone.
dot M_process approximately rho_c v A_tot S,        L_X approximately dot M_process Q.
   This intersects several imperfectly modeled solar properties, but not all in the same way. The solar-abundance problem is a mismatch among low photospheric metallicity, helioseismic sound speed, convection-zone depth, surface helium, opacity, and neutrino constraints [13]. A compact core can modify the innermost sound-speed and composition profile, but it cannot directly fix the opacity discrepancy near the base of the convection zone. The Sun's severe lithium depletion may be sensitive to nonstandard early mixing or selective nuclear processing, but a magnetic explanation would have to destroy lithium without similarly overdestroying beryllium. The near-uniform rotation of much of the radiative interior and the tachocline remain transport problems; a distributed fragment population could exchange angular momentum, but would also transport heat and composition.
   Solar neutrinos are the sharpest calorimeter. Borexino has measured CNO neutrinos and finds results more compatible with high-metallicity standard solar models than the canonical low-metallicity alternative, though uncertainties remain [14]. If magnetic reactions supply photon luminosity without standard pp-chain neutrinos, ordinary fusion must fall; if reactions instead cool the core or create beta processes, the neutrino spectrum changes differently. A viable model must reproduce the measured pp, Be-7, B-8, pep, and CNO fluxes together with luminosity and helioseismology.
   The existence of the Sun, Earth, Moon, and old metal-rich bodies imposes an equally important survival condition. If mixed-sector absorption is barrierless and exothermic at MeV per nucleon, even a small captured seed could be dangerous. Therefore the same microphysics invoked to create astrophysical effects must also provide passivation, fission-limited growth, endothermicity, low capture probability, or an exceptionally low flux. This is not an embarrassment; it is a direct constraint on S_X(A,Z) and on the droplet equation of state.
11. What could already kill the idea?
1.  No consistent quantum field theory. A model with one photon and both electric and magnetic matter must specify locality, line operators, charge lattices, running couplings, electroweak symmetry breaking, and the global Standard Model gauge group.
2.  Colored-sector contradiction. Additional shared-color quarks may be excluded by QCD running, hadron spectra, cosmology, or collider production unless their realization is nonstandard or very heavy.
3.  No stable neutral state. The alpha_m approximately 34 bound-state problem may collapse, pair-produce, annihilate, or possess no long-lived baryon-lepton neutral.
4.  Catastrophic mixed-QCD chemistry. If ordinary nuclei are deeply and irreversibly absorbed, planetary and stellar survival can exclude an appreciable flux.
5.  CMB/BBN thermal history. A shared photon bath and early strongly coupled plasma can alter expansion, entropy transfer, light-element yields, recombination, and the matter power spectrum.
6.  Wrong dark-matter phenomenology. The mass spectrum may violate baryon-drag, self-interaction, microlensing, compact-object, seismic, or stellar-capture constraints.
7.  No seed advantage. Coagulation, fission, and angular-momentum barriers may prevent early collapse, leaving the model unable to make LRD-relevant black-hole seeds.
   Any one of these failures is sufficient. The proposal becomes scientifically interesting only if it is narrow enough to be wrong.
12. A calculation program worth attempting
1.  Field-theory specification: choose the gauge group and global quotient; define magnetic quarks and leptons; enforce Schwinger-Zwanziger quantization and anomaly cancellation; determine whether the objects are pointlike or solitonic.
2.  Two-body spectrum: compute the lowest magnetic baryon-lepton state with a finite core using lattice methods, Hamiltonian truncation, Dyson-Schwinger equations, or a controlled dual model. Output masses, radii, spin/parity, multipoles, annihilation widths, and polarizabilities.
3.  Mixed QCD: calculate magnetic-baryon/nucleon scattering and separation energies in light nuclei. Hypernuclear effective field theory and lattice QCD provide the methodological template, but the new baryon representation must be explicit.
4.  Many-body matter: construct an equation of state and liquid-drop functional; locate saturation density, shell closures, screening lengths, fission barriers, maximum stable mass, and the transition to black holes.
5.  Cosmological kinetics: solve annihilation, recombination, coagulation, fission, photon coupling, and asymmetry evolution through BBN and recombination. Predict the present mass function and free-charge fraction.
6.  Structure formation: simulate a multi-mass population with size-dependent baryon drag and self-interaction. Predict halo shape, disk fraction, central cusp, seed abundance, and gravitational-wave merger history.
7.  Targeted observations: pre-register predictions for Psyche gravity residuals, solar g modes and CNO ratios, macro transit morphology, LRD host/seed relations, and lensing/occultation signatures.

13. Bottom line
   Taking Dirac seriously does not by itself produce a parallel Standard Model. It produces a mandatory warning: the magnetic coupling associated with the minimal charge is nonperturbatively large. The conjecture explored here asks whether nature could turn that difficulty into a matter-building mechanism. If magnetically charged color baryons and leptons exist, if their net charge asymmetry balances, if a stable neutral spectrum survives supercritical dynamics, and if mixed QCD is strong but self-limiting, then one mass spectrum could in principle span compact dark matter, early black-hole seeding, planetary accumulation, and stellar-core effects.
   The breadth is intellectually attractive but evidentially neutral. At present the idea is a research program, not an explanation. Its strongest feature is that it can be attacked from several independent directions: quantum field theory, lattice QCD, nuclear structure, BBN/CMB calculations, N-body simulations, JWST demographics, Psyche gravity, and helioseismology. A successful model must make those domains agree without separate tuning.
Invitation to a physicist with morbid curiosity
   The shortest useful contribution would be a proof that the minimal particle content is inconsistent. The next most useful would be a controlled bound-state or mixed-QCD calculation. Either result would be progress. In the unlikely event that the hypothesis survives and predicts a new observation, negotiations concerning the entirely unofficial FIFA World Physics Prize may commence.References
