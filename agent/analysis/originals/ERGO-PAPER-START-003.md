## 0 · Title and Abstract

**Title (adopted 22 July 2026, unchanged):** *What a Constitution Needs from Its Substrate: Neutral Time, Public Randomness, Money That Cannot Vote, and the Price of Delegated Trust.*

**Abstract (trim executed this pass per adopted decision; ~330 words; the reference-design clause added per the internalization rule):**

> Communities that govern themselves through blockchain infrastructure inherit constitutional properties from that substrate, whether they choose them or not. This paper argues that the most consequential of these is the least discussed as such: the hardware cost of verifying the ledger without trusting anyone. Where independent verification demands datacenter-class resources, intermediation is not a failure of governance but its stable equilibrium — self-verification is priced off the equilibrium path, and the system reconstructs, in infrastructure, the very delegated trust that constitutional design exists to prevent. Verification at member cost is therefore not an operations detail but a constitutional precondition. We specify a reference institutional design — tamper-evident records without registries, sortition and coercion-resistant balloting, enforcement by pre-pledged collateral — and examine the three roles it asks a ledger to serve: a neutral source of time, a public source of randomness, and a custodian of pledged surety. Each role is subjected to its own adversarial analysis — reorganization economics, beacon-grinding, collateral capture — with the cost of delegating trust priced in each. From the three roles we generalize: the relation between money and governance is itself a constitutional variable with exactly three settings — fused, exiled, or held in constitutionalized adjacency — and history attests the capture path of the first two: fusion converts wealth into law directly; exile delegates the money power into discretion that captures the law from outside. A substrate can serve the third setting only if wealth held on the chain confers no weight over the chain. The fusion setting we treat categorically: proof-of-stake consensus cannot serve constitutional substrates — its bootstrap requires a social coordinator, its consensus pre-formats governance as shareholder voting, and its founding allocation compounds into a permanent electorate. The properties we require are stated falsifiably and chain-agnostically, and assessed against systems as shipped, not as roadmapped; the extended-UTXO model with native composable zero-knowledge proofs emerges as the most complete instance, its remaining gaps named as open problems. Drawing on the commons-governance tradition, the economics of costly verification, and the documented failure modes of blockchain-mediated governance, the paper asks what any institution inherits from the substrate beneath it — and what it cannot afford to delegate.

## 1 · Thesis (carried forward, condensed)

**1.1** The hardware cost of independently verifying a ledger is a *constitutional* property, not an operations detail. **1.2** Where verification is priced off the path, delegated intermediation is the stable equilibrium, and the system rebuilds in infrastructure the trusted offices constitutional design exists to prevent — now anchored to the costly-verification literature per §5.1 rather than asserted. **1.3** The three roles (time, randomness, surety) are the precondition's instances; the coupling theorem is their generalization. **1.4** eUTXO's structural non-interference (no shared mutable state; one organ's activity never inflates another's verification burden) supplies the hosting half of the §6.5 iff-claim. **1.5** The design tax, named and conceded: non-Turing-complete contract languages force multi-stage protocol design — pay in drafting effort once, up front, instead of in hardware forever, compounding; cite the multi-stage eUTXO formalization.

## 10 · Anticipated Objections (draft into the paper before reviewers do; now ten)

1. *"The ecosystem is small."* Split smallness (cured by adoption) from untestedness (cured by operation; the reference design is specified, unimplemented, and says so). The architecture is smallness-tolerant by design: content-free anchors are target-poor; failure degrades toward delay; deep history hardens monotonically under the rollback ceiling; the sole enforcement instrument is membership's future. Ecosystem maturity is a convenience property; verification cost is a constitutional one — the field traded these incorrectly.
2. *"Multi-stage protocols are harder to write."* Conceded as the design tax, with the formalization cite.
3. *"The statelessness roadmap will fix this."* On the merits at writing time; shipped versus roadmap.
4. *"This is chain maximalism."* Model-level properties; any chain shipping them qualifies; Cardano partially qualifies today; the subsidiarity exhibit (M-27) — chain where strangers, checked-not-believed paper where faces — shows the design treating the chain as scarce and scoped, not totemic.
5. *"Bitcoin suffices."* For anchoring alone, yes — conceded plainly; hidden-subset threshold recovery, contract-checkable clearing, and non-enumerable anchor scripts answer the rest, and a two-stack seam architecture fails the fewest-trusted-components rule.
6. *"Storage rent can eat records / the beacon is grindable / the SPV wallet isn't finished."* All conceded first, in our own text, with mechanics and mitigations. The daylight is the argument.
7. *"This is goldbug/anti-Fed polemic."* No: the exile branch criticizes a constitutional placement, not an institution; it is stated at the model level, paired with the lender-of-last-resort achievement, and the design's own pre-written crisis valve concedes crisis response is necessary — the disagreement is pre-written rule versus held discretion.
8. *"Non-convertibility is unenforceable — wealth always finds a path."* Concede the strong form; answer with mechanism: no delegation primitive to accumulate, receipt-freeness making purchased ballots undeliverable, the flat non-scaling bond with its recorded fence — then concede the residual honestly: the organized still out-attend and out-propose; wealth buys attendance, organization, and proposals, never ballots; the lot redistributes attention without creating anything wealth can buy.
9. *"Your bond, surety, and beacon economics are money in governance — the wall is already breached."* The design never claimed zero contact; it claims enumerated, capped, audited contact — a border with named crossings — and the distinction (fund judgments, price attacks, never buy voice) is testable at each crossing.
10. *"Proof-of-work's energy cost is indefensible / regulatorily doomed."* (Adopted addition.) The expenditure is not overhead on the constitutional argument; it is the mechanism: the thermodynamic cost is what makes consensus power a flow re-auctioned every block rather than a stock that compounds — remove it and the franchise becomes purchasable and permanent, which is the fusion setting. Address magnitude honestly (the security budget is priced, visible, and borne by consenters), engage the comparative-footprint literature at the model level, and treat regulatory fragility as a named gap in the same register as the others rather than waving it off.
