## II. The Victim Set Expansion

The research context describes Geedge's system as analyzing "telecommunications, social media and location data" to "generate profiles of Chinese citizens and then use AI to highlight who may pose a political risk." The engine's classification reveals what this means structurally: the victim set is not "dissidents" (people who have acted) but **"algorithmically_flagged_citizens"** (people predicted to act).

This is a categorical shift in who bears costs. Traditional reactive surveillance targets people after they express dissent. The victim set is defined by observed behavior: you wrote the essay, attended the protest, shared the banned document. Predictive surveillance targets people before they act. The victim set is defined by algorithmic classification: the model assigned you a risk score above threshold based on your telecommunications patterns, location history, and social media activity.

The scope effect analysis (§1.3 of the engine report) quantifies this shift through the Chi metric:

The Chi metric is ε × f(d) × σ(S)—extractiveness scaled by directionality (distance from enforcement) and scope modifier (spatial scale). For the moderate observer (biographical time horizon, constrained exit options, national scope), Chi = 0.863. This is the lived experience of a Chinese citizen who cannot leave the country, whose life trajectory is shaped by whether the algorithm flags them. For the institutional observer (immediate time horizon, arbitrage options, national scope), Chi = -0.033. This is the experience of a Public Security Bureau official whose career advances when the system "works"—when predictions lead to interventions that prevent incidents that would have embarrassed the bureau.

**The institutional observer sees negative extraction because they are a beneficiary, not a victim.** The engine's beneficiary list includes "state_security_apparatus" and "surveillance_technology_vendors." The victim list includes "algorithmically_flagged_citizens." This is not a coordination problem where everyone shares costs of a collective action problem. This is asymmetric benefit flow: one group gains (security apparatus, vendors) while another group loses (flagged citizens), and the constraint is designed to maintain that asymmetry.

The victim set expansion is quantified in the drift analysis. The engine detected **extraction_accumulation** drift at critical severity: "Evidence: evidence(extraction_delta,0,10,0.52,0.78)." Extractiveness rose from 0.52 to 0.78 over the modeled timeline (2024-2034). This is not implementation noise. This is the constraint becoming more extractive as it matures. The research context provides the mechanism: "In the first months of 2024, Geedge's researchers were working to develop behavioral profiles... trying to predict what citizens might do next and with whom."

As the models improve, the prediction threshold lowers. Early systems flag people after they exhibit multiple risk indicators. Mature systems flag people based on weaker signals—your friend's friend attended a protest, your location history shows you near a sensitive site, your reading patterns correlate with known dissidents even though you've never expressed dissent yourself.
