# The Vote Market: Democracy as Constrained Exchange System

## I. The Core Empirical Puzzle

For forty years, American democracy has exhibited a structural contradiction that defies standard political theory. When policy preferences diverge across income groups, outcomes track the preferences of the affluent with near-perfect fidelity while bearing essentially zero relationship to the preferences of low- and middle-income Americans. This is not correlation—it is the documented finding of systematic responsiveness analysis covering nearly 2,000 policy questions between 1981 and 2002, replicated across multiple research teams and extended through subsequent decades.

The puzzle is not that the wealthy win. The puzzle is the coexistence of two facts that should not be simultaneously true:

1. **Flat-line responsiveness**: Policy outcomes show no statistical relationship to median voter preferences when those preferences diverge from the affluent
2. **Periodic elite control loss**: The same system produces Brexit, the 2016 U.S. election, and the Dobbs reversal—outcomes the incumbent elite demonstrably did not want and failed to prevent

Standard theories explain one or the other. Median voter models explain why elites sometimes lose but cannot account for the flat-line. Elite capture models explain the flat-line but treat control losses as exogenous shocks rather than endogenous system outputs. The framework proposed here—democracy as a constrained exchange system with votes as tradable commodities—explains both.

## II. The Market Model: Four Constraint Mechanisms

Democracy under this model is not rule-by-the-people or rule-by-elites. It is a market system where votes are commodities with variable acquisition costs, operating under four structural constraints that determine the price schedule and tradability conditions.

### A. Organization Floor (Tradability Threshold)

**Engine Classification**: Mountain (declared) → Rope (computed across all observer positions)  
**Structural Signature**: Coupling-invariant rope—genuine coordination mechanism, not extraction  
**Verdict**: GREEN (all subsystems agree)

**The Finding**: Mass preferences become tradable only when coupled to organized blocs with three capabilities: (1) warrantable leadership that can commit the bloc's votes, (2) punishment capacity against defectors, and (3) delivery infrastructure that converts commitments into turnout. Below this organizational threshold, preferences are non-transactable regardless of their size or intensity.

The engine computed this as rope (changeable rule requiring coordination) rather than mountain (natural law), with pristine purity (1.0) and zero coupling to other dimensions. The classification is gauge-invariant—all four observer positions (powerless, moderate, institutional, analytical) agree. This is the only constraint in the analysis that achieved full diagnostic convergence with no perspectival fracture.

**Empirical Anchor**: The Gilens/Page responsiveness data show partial exceptions in the social welfare domain where organized interest groups operate. The variance explained by organization status, controlling for preference intensity and population size, provides the measurable gradient. The Moral Majority case demonstrates the mechanism: evangelical preferences on abortion were worst-performing (diffuse moral majority) until organized into a deliverable bloc in the early 1980s, at which point they became tradable.

**The Mechanism**: Political parties operate as firms assembling minimum-cost winning coalitions. Unorganized preferences present an arbitrage problem—high transaction costs per vote acquired, no mechanism to prevent free-riding, no warrant that acquired preferences will translate to turnout. Organized blocs solve this: leadership brokers deliver packages of votes at known per-unit cost with delivery guarantees. The floor is not a barrier erected by elites—it is a transaction cost structure inherent to large-scale coordination under secret ballot.

**Critical Distinction**: The constraint distinguishes brokerage from sale. Evangelical leadership was paid in access and judicial appointments; evangelical members were paid in policy (Dobbs). This creates an agency problem: when leadership and member payment streams diverge, what prevents member defection? The answer determines whether the organization floor is a stable feature or a temporary arbitrage opportunity that collapses post-delivery. This remains an unresolved omega—the Dobbs case will generate data either way, but the general mechanism is underspecified.

### B. Veto Asymmetry (Blocking Cheaper Than Enacting)

**Engine Classification**: Tangled Rope (declared) → Snare (computed by moderate/analytical observers), Rope (institutional), Naturalized (powerless)  
**Structural Signature**: Constructed high-extraction—enforcement present (suppression=0.68, resistance=0.71) with extraction (0.62)  
**Verdict**: YELLOW (2 tensions—abductive flags, signature override mismatch)

**The Finding**: Policy influence operates asymmetrically through negative power (blocking) rather than positive enactment. Blocking requires capturing one chokepoint; enactment requires buying the entire legislative chain. The cost differential produces the observed pattern: the wealthy win disagreements 53% of the time, and inequality concentrates in blocking rather than agenda-setting.

The engine detected perspectival fracture (H¹=5, both hubs contributing). The powerless observer sees this as naturalized (unchangeable background condition). The institutional observer sees rope (functional chokepoint architecture). The moderate and analytical observers see snare (extractive trap). The constraint exhibits snare-leaning characteristics (ψ=0.999) despite its declared type—it behaves more like extraction than coordination.

**Empirical Anchor**: Branham, Soroka, and Wlezien document the negative-power asymmetry: rich-poor disagreements cluster in blocking rather than enacting, and the distribution of wins/losses by action type shows the predicted pattern. The U.S. constitutional architecture—bicameral legislature, executive veto, judicial review, filibuster—creates multiple serial chokepoints, each of which can block but none of which alone can enact.

**The Mechanism**: Chokepoint capture is a portfolio problem. To block, buy one senator's committee chairmanship or one appellate court or one regulatory commissioner. To enact, buy the House, the Senate, the presidency, and survive judicial review. The asymmetry is structural, not contingent—it derives from the serial-veto architecture, not from wealth concentration per se. Wealth concentration determines who can afford the chokepoint portfolio; the architecture determines the cost differential.

**Mandatrophy Gap**: The engine computed a moderate mandatrophy gap (Δχ=0.35) between the snare classification (moderate/analytical observers) and the rope classification (institutional observer). This is the perspectival divergence in power-scaled extraction: those without chokepoint access see a trap; those with access see a tool.

**Critical Omega**: The constraint's classification depends on an unresolved question—what fraction of veto asymmetry represents genuine coordination costs (preventing unstable policy churn) versus extraction (blocking redistribution that would otherwise pass)? The boundary is empirically measurable: track benefit flows from blocked policies, measure asymmetric distribution, test whether blocking serves stability or capture. The engine flagged this as requiring resolution to determine whether the snare classification should override the declared tangled_rope type.

### C. Scale Ceiling (Coordination Cost as Function of Polity Size)

**Engine Classification**: Mountain (declared) → Rope (computed across all observer positions)  
**Structural Signature**: Coupling-invariant rope—genuine coordination mechanism  
**Verdict**: GREEN (all subsystems agree)

**The Finding**: Coordination cost scales with polity size, regulatory fragmentation, and capital mobility, bounding the feasibility of economy-wide seller cartels while leaving organized blocs viable at any scale. Denmark can organize 6 million workers under one regime; the United States cannot organize 330 million. The constraint is spatial, not temporal—regional cartels (Solid South) and sectoral cartels (specific industries) remain stable while continental economy-wide cartels decay.

The engine classified this as rope (changeable but requiring coordination) with the same pristine diagnostic profile as organization_floor: gauge-invariant, zero coupling, full observer agreement. The mountain claim fails because the constraint requires active enforcement (suppression=0.08 > 0.05 threshold)—it is not a natural law but a coordination cost structure that could be altered by institutional design.

**Empirical Anchor**: Union density trajectories provide the dose-response curve. U.S. union density halved from 30% (1985) to 15% (2023/24), tracking the post-1998 opening of capital accounts. The cross-national pattern is synchronized: Lupu and Pontusson document that left-government responsiveness to lower incomes in economic/welfare domains disappeared simultaneously across four countries post-1998, coinciding with capital liberalization and Soviet collapse.

**The Mechanism**: The scale ceiling operates through three channels:

1. **Information costs**: Coordinating 330 million workers requires solving a distributed consensus problem under adversarial conditions (employer resistance, legal fragmentation, cultural heterogeneity). Information-theoretic limits on consensus formation scale non-linearly with group size.

2. **Regulatory fragmentation**: U.S. labor law operates at state level with federal preemption creating a patchwork. Organizing across 50 jurisdictions multiplies transaction costs relative to organizing under one national regime.

3. **Capital exit option**: Open capital accounts allow employers to credibly threaten relocation. Continental-scale coordination cannot match capital mobility—the arbitrage window remains open. Sectoral coordination (one industry) can survive because exit costs are industry-specific; economy-wide coordination (all industries) cannot because some sector always has lower exit costs.

**Critical Omega**: Can economy-wide seller cartels form at continental scale under open capital accounts, or is post-1998 convergence an equilibrium rather than a trough? The answer determines whether the organizational program implied by this analysis is a strategy or a eulogy. The empirical test exists but has not been run: regress responsiveness on union density × polity scale × capital account openness across all OECD countries with sufficient data. If the interaction term is negative and significant, the ceiling is structural. If not, the ceiling is contingent and potentially reversible.

### D. Information Suppression (Event-Driven Journalism as Price Suppression)

**Engine Classification**: Tangled Rope (declared) → Snare (computed by moderate/analytical observers), Scaffold (powerless/institutional)  
**Structural Signature**: Constructed high-extraction—enforcement present (suppression=0.72, resistance=0.42) with extraction (0.68)  
**Verdict**: YELLOW (2 tensions—abductive flags, signature override mismatch)

**The Finding**: Event-driven journalism systematically omits the structural context required for voters to estimate policy stakes, creating information asymmetry as a necessary condition for persistently cheap votes. The regime is not neutral reportage—it is a price suppression mechanism that prevents stake discovery.

The engine detected Hub 2 perspectival fracture (H¹=4): powerless and institutional observers see scaffold (temporary support structure with sunset clause), while moderate and analytical observers see snare (extractive trap). The constraint exhibits snare-leaning characteristics (ψ=0.999) and shows strong coupling (score=1.0) to power-scope dimensions.

**Empirical Anchor**: DellaVigna and Kaplan provide the causal identification: Fox News rollout in cable markets produced measurable rightward vote shifts, demonstrating that information environment changes alter political outcomes. The natural experiment shows the mechanism is operative—changing information inputs changes vote prices. The content analysis requirement is structural context density: measure the frequency with which news coverage connects events to their structural determinants (policy stakes, benefit flows, institutional constraints) versus presenting events as isolated incidents.

**The Mechanism**: Voters face a stake-estimation problem under radical uncertainty. What does this policy cost me? Who benefits? What are the alternatives? Event-driven journalism answers "what happened today" but not "what are the structural stakes." This is not bias—it is a genre constraint. Events are reportable (observable, time-stamped, attributable); structures are not. The result: voters systematically misprice stakes because they lack the information required for accurate pricing.

The information regime is self-reinforcing through two channels:

1. **Incumbent narrative consumption**: Elites who benefit from cheap votes also consume the event-only narrative, degrading their own price discovery. When the incumbent elite is surprised by Brexit or 2016, this is not strategic deception—it is genuine miscalibration produced by information-environment monoculture. The engine flagged this as "elite feedback degradation"—the regime that suppresses vote prices also degrades the price discovery of those who set it.

2. **Counter-elite arbitrage**: Mispriced grievances accumulate longest where elite narrative monoculture is densest. When counter-elites (Trump, Farage, the Dobbs coalition) identify undervalued grievance and mobilize it, they are not creating new preferences—they are arbitraging the price gap between structural stakes and information-suppressed vote costs. This is hostile-takeover arbitrage, not populist uprising.

**Critical Omega**: Is the 90% rich-middle preference agreement exogenous, or partly produced by the information regime and proposal agenda jointly? The essay uses the agenda half (documented non-decisions) but declines the information half as unfalsifiable. However, preference movement following documented information-environment shifts is measurable in principle—nobody has measured it well. If agreement is endogenous, the "coincidence" reading collapses further. If exogenous, it constrains the information-suppression mechanism.

**Mandatrophy Gap**: The engine computed zero mandatrophy gap (Δχ=0.00) between snare and rope classifications—the moderate observer and institutional observer see identical power-scaled extraction despite seeing different types. This is unusual: it means the perspectival divergence is in immutability (Hub 2) rather than extraction (Hub 1). The disagreement is about whether the constraint can be changed, not about whether it extracts.

## III. The Forty-Year Purchase: Dobbs as Brokerage Endpoint

The Moral Majority case demonstrates all four constraints simultaneously and provides the cleanest test of the model's dynamic predictions.

**Initial Conditions (Pre-1979)**: Evangelical preferences on abortion were worst-performing in Gilens domain analysis—diffuse moral majority, no organized delivery mechanism, no warrantable leadership. The Southern Baptist Convention passed pro-choice resolutions in 1971, 1974, and 1976, after Roe. Abortion was not "the" evangelical issue—it became one through deliberate construction by an elite in-group (Viguerie, Phillips, Weyrich) seeking a wedge issue for the Goldwater coalition.

**Organization Phase (1979-1980)**: Falwell's Moral Majority provided the organizational infrastructure—warrantable leadership, punishment capacity (primary challenges), delivery mechanism (church-based turnout). Evangelical preferences crossed the organization floor and became tradable. The 1980 election showed the phase transition: evangelicals shifted from Carter (1976) to Reagan (1980) as a deliverable bloc.

**Brokerage Bargain (1980-2022)**: The bloc sold votes in exchange for judicial appointments and abortion-restriction policy. Leadership was paid in access; members were paid in policy. The bargain was iterated across eight presidential cycles, surviving periods when Republicans controlled all branches and periods when they controlled none. The persistence demonstrates that the constraint was brokerage (ongoing exchange relationship) rather than sale (one-time transaction).

**Delivery (June 24, 2022)**: Dobbs v. Jackson overturned Roe, delivering the policy the bloc purchased. The engine's prediction: evangelical bloc leverage should decay materially within two presidential cycles (2022-2032) across four channels:

1. **Candidate selection**: Primary voters no longer need to screen for pro-life commitment—the policy is delivered
2. **Agenda-setting**: Abortion moves from "must-pass" to "already done"
3. **Judicial pipeline**: Federalist Society leverage decays—the courts delivered, what's the next payment?
4. **Turnout bargaining**: "Vote or lose Roe" is no longer operative

**Early Indicators (2022-2024)**: Post-Dobbs abortion referendums lost in Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Montana—states Trump won. Suburban realignment accelerated. These are consistent with the predicted leverage decay, but comprehensive measurement across all four channels requires waiting for 2028/2032 data.

**The Brokerage Endgame Hypothesis**: Organized bloc leverage decays materially after full policy delivery because paid sellers have nothing left to withhold and brokers who delivered have nothing left to warrant. If evangelical leverage is undiminished through 2028/2032, the prediction fails and the model loses its dynamic component. This is the essay's cleanest kill condition—time-gated but unambiguous.

**Agency Problem**: The bloc's payment streams diverged. Leadership was paid in access (White House meetings, judicial consultation, platform influence). Members were paid in policy (Dobbs). Post-delivery, leadership retains access but members have received their payment. What prevents member defection when the two streams diverge? The omega remains unresolved: we do not yet have a general model of intra-bloc agency conflict under brokerage.

The four constraints do not operate independently—they form an integrated system with convergent signatures and cascading drift.

1. **Veto asymmetry** makes blocking cheaper than enacting, and capital mobility provides the credible exit threat that makes blocking effective
2. **Post-1998 convergence** tracks synchronized decay of left-government responsiveness coinciding with capital account opening
3. **Scale ceiling** bounds economy-wide seller cartels while capital remains mobile across the bounded territory

The engine flagged post_1998_convergence with a false_ci_rope signature: "Appears to be rope (explicit claim) but fails 4 Boltzmann structural tests. Coupling score=1.0. This constraint is 'coordination-washed'—it hides extraction behind low metrics, distributed enforcement, or behavioral defaults. Institutional beneficiary: mobile_capital_holders. The d-value (directionality) of 0.350 for institutional observers means those with capital mobility see the constraint as mildly beneficial; those without see it as moderately extractive.

### Contamination Network

This is unusual in a 45-constraint corpus where network stability is "cascading" and 32 of 36 omegas are critical. This isolation is itself a finding: the market mechanisms operate as independent structural facts rather than as emergent properties of a contaminated network. The organization floor, veto asymmetry, scale ceiling, and information suppression are not knock-on effects of some deeper constraint—they are the load-bearing members.

### Drift Convergence

The slopes differ but the direction is uniform: all four constraints are drifting toward higher extraction over their measured intervals. This is not noise—it is a system-level trend. The engine's terminal state predictions uniformly point to tangled_rope (low confidence), suggesting the constraints are approaching a boundary but have not yet crossed it.

### Coupling Architecture

Three constraints (agenda_conditioning, veto_asymmetry, information_suppression) show strong coupling (score=1.0) to power-scope dimensions. The engine's Boltzmann compliance tests flag this as "constructed_non_compliance"—the constraints deliberately couple dimensions that natural coordination mechanisms would leave independent.

The coupling pattern is specific: power and scope are thermodynamically linked. An observer's power position determines which spatial scope they can access, and their spatial scope determines which power position they can occupy. This is not a bug—it is the mechanism. The powerless are trapped at local scope; the institutional operate at national scope with arbitrage options; the analytical operate at global scope with full mobility.

This coupling architecture produces the perspectival fractures the engine detected. The constraints genuinely look different from different positions because the positions have different scope access and different power to act on what they see. The fracture is not measurement error—it is the structure of the constraint space.

## V. The Agenda Conditioning Mechanism: Non-Decisions as Structural Omission

**Engine Classification**: Snare (declared) → Tangled Rope (powerless), Snare (moderate/analytical), Rope (institutional)  
**Structural Signature**: Constructed high-extraction  
**Verdict**: YELLOW (2 tensions—abductive flags, signature override mismatch)

This constraint was deferred in the original scope manifest (generation_order=6) but the engine reports reveal it as central to the system's operation. The mechanism: veto operates partly upstream of proposal, excluding non-decisions from responsiveness datasets. Measured rich-middle preference agreement is conditioned on proposed policies—the 90% agreement figure does not include the policies that never made it to the survey question.

**The Bachrach-Baratz Problem**: Second-face power operates by defining the alternatives that reach the agenda. When Gilens and Page measure responsiveness, they measure it on policies that survived to proposal stage. Policies that died in committee, that were never introduced, that were defined as "unrealistic" or "radical" before reaching the floor—these are outside the dataset denominator.

The engine detected this as a mandatrophy gap (Δχ=0.38, moderate) between the snare classification (moderate/analytical observers) and the rope classification (institutional observers). The perspectival divergence is specific: those without agenda-setting power see a trap (snare—they cannot get their preferences onto the agenda); those with agenda-setting power see a tool (rope—they can block proposals before they become visible).

**Empirical Anchor**: The test is comparison of survey-question policy space versus full feasible policy space. What policies disappeared from the agenda versus those that remained live? Schattschneider's "definition of alternatives is the supreme instrument of power" claim becomes measurable: track the historical trajectory of policy proposals, identify when they exit the live agenda, measure the income distribution of beneficiaries for exited versus surviving proposals.

**Interaction with Information Suppression**: The agenda conditioning mechanism and information suppression mechanism interact. Event-driven journalism reports on proposed policies (events) but not on the non-decisions (structures). The combination: voters lack information about both (1) the stakes of proposed policies and (2) the existence of non-proposed alternatives. The information asymmetry operates at two levels—mispricing what's on the agenda and ignorance of what's excluded.

**Critical Omega**: Is the 90% rich-middle preference agreement exogenous or endogenous to the agenda? If the agenda conditions preferences (people prefer what they're told is feasible), then the agreement is partly produced by the constraint it's used to defend. If preferences are exogenous (people want what they want independent of agenda framing), then the agreement is a genuine coincidence and the constraint is less extractive than it appears. The resolution requires measuring preference movement following agenda changes—do preferences shift when previously-excluded policies become live?

## VI. Post-1998 Convergence: The Synchronized Decay

**Engine Classification**: Rope (declared) → Scaffold (powerless/institutional), Snare (moderate/analytical)  
**Structural Signature**: False CI_Rope—coordination-washed extraction  
**Verdict**: YELLOW (1 tension—abductive flags)

The cross-national synchronized disappearance of left-government responsiveness to lower incomes tracks two simultaneous events: post-Cold War capital account opening and competitor regime collapse (Soviet Union). The Lupu-Pontusson four-country time series shows the dose-like timing: responsiveness gradients pre-1998 (left governments favored lower incomes), flat-line post-1998 (left and right governments indistinguishable on economic policy).

**The Mechanism**: Capital exit option as seller-cartel breaker. Pre-1998, Bretton Woods capital controls bounded the arbitrage space. National governments could implement redistributive policy without facing immediate capital flight because capital accounts were closed or restricted. Post-1998, capital account opening + Soviet collapse removed both the regulatory barrier (capital controls) and the ideological barrier (socialist alternative).

The result: economy-wide seller cartels (unions bargaining at national level) faced a credible exit threat they could not match. Capital could leave; labor could not follow. The repricing was synchronized because the capital account opening was synchronized—it was not a series of independent national decisions but a coordinated liberalization program.

**The Engine's Verdict**: False CI_Rope—the constraint claims to be coordination (rope) but fails four Boltzmann structural tests. The coupling score (1.0) indicates strong thermodynamic linkage between power and scope dimensions. The institutional beneficiary is explicitly identified: mobile_capital_holders. The institutional d-value (0.350) shows the directionality—those with capital mobility see the constraint as mildly beneficial.

This is not a conspiracy—it is a structural fact. Capital mobility is not a plot; it is a policy choice with measurable consequences. The engine detected those consequences in the responsiveness gradient: left governments stopped being responsive to lower incomes on economic policy after capital accounts opened. The timing is dose-like (synchronized), the mechanism is identifiable (exit option), and the beneficiary is computable (mobile capital).

**Critical Omega**: Can economy-wide seller cartels form at continental scale under open capital accounts? The post-1998 convergence could be an equilibrium (permanent) or a trough (temporary). If continental-scale coordination is information-theoretically impossible under capital mobility, the convergence is structural and irreversible without closing capital accounts. If it is merely difficult (high coordination cost but not impossible), then the convergence is a local minimum and potentially escapable.

The empirical test: regress responsiveness on union density × polity scale × capital account openness across OECD countries. If the three-way interaction is negative and significant, the scale ceiling under capital mobility is structural. If not, the ceiling is contingent. The data exist; the regression has not been run.

## VII. The Measurement Absence: Democracy Without Audit

Every responsiveness finding cited in this analysis exists because individual academics spent years hand-assembling preference-policy datasets that no democratic state collects as a matter of course. Gilens coded nearly 2,000 survey questions against policy outcomes. Branham, Soroka, and Wlezien extended the analysis. Lupu and Pontusson assembled cross-national time series. This is research infrastructure built by scholars, not operational infrastructure maintained by states.

**The Absence as Finding**: No democratic state maintains a standing, public audit of policy responsiveness by income, organization, and issue domain. The omission is systematic—it is not that one or two countries lack the audit; it is that zero countries maintain it. Under the market model, this omission is legible: the audit would publish the price schedule.

**What the Audit Would Show**:
1. Policy responsiveness gradient by income quintile, controlling for organization status
2. Responsiveness variance by issue domain (where do organized blocs operate?)
3. Veto asymmetry distribution (who blocks what, at what cost?)
4. Agenda conditioning effects (which policies exit the live agenda, whose preferences do they represent?)
5. Information regime effects (stake-mispricing by information environment type)

**The Demand**: The essay concludes with the smallest possible demand—not constitutional reform, not wealth redistribution, not organizational strategy. Simply: measure and publish the responsiveness audit. Require it as a condition of claiming democratic legitimacy. The refusal to measure is itself the finding.

**Why This Demand**: It requires no new theory. The measurement protocols exist—Gilens proved feasibility. It requires no constitutional change—legislatures can mandate the audit by statute. It requires no organizational capacity beyond what states already possess—census bureaus and statistical agencies can execute it. The demand is minimal by design: refusing it requires explaining why a democracy is better off not knowing whose preferences policy tracks.

Under the market model, the refusal is predictable: the audit would make the price schedule visible, which makes votes more expensive. Voters who know their preferences are systematically ignored can charge more for their votes (or organize, or exit, or defect to counter-elites who arbitrage the mispricing). The information asymmetry is load-bearing—it is what keeps votes cheap.

## VIII. Structural Implications and Falsification Conditions

### What This Framework Predicts

1. **Brokerage Endgame (2028-2032)**: Evangelical bloc leverage decays materially across candidate selection, agenda-setting, judicial pipeline, and turnout bargaining. If leverage is undiminished, the model's dynamic component fails.

2. **Continental Cartel Impossibility**: Economy-wide seller cartels cannot form at continental scale under open capital accounts. If such cartels form and persist (e.g., EU-wide union coordination that achieves measurable responsiveness), the scale ceiling is not structural.

3. **Information Regime Price Effects**: Structural context provision should increase vote costs (voters charge more when they know stakes). Natural experiments on information environment changes (platform migrations, regulatory shifts) should show measurable vote-price effects. If context provision has no effect on vote costs, the information suppression mechanism fails.

4. **Agenda Conditioning Measurement**: Non-decisions should be measurable by comparing survey-question policy space to full feasible policy space. If no systematic exclusion pattern exists, the agenda conditioning mechanism fails.

5. **Responsiveness Audit Refusal**: States will decline to implement standing responsiveness audits. If states implement the audit and publish results, the prediction fails but the framework wins (the audit provides the data to test all other predictions).

### What This Framework Does Not Predict

1. **Voter Irrationality**: The model assumes voters optimize under information constraints, not that they are irrational or misinformed in some absolute sense.

2. **Elite Conspiracy**: The mechanisms operate through structural incentives, not coordinated elite action. Capital mobility is a policy choice with structural effects, not a plot.

3. **Inevitable Oligarchy**: The constraints are changeable (rope/tangled_rope), not natural law (mountain). The organization floor can be lowered, veto asymmetry can be reduced, scale ceiling can be addressed through institutional design, information suppression can be countered through structural journalism.

4. **Median Voter Irrelevance**: The model explains flat-line responsiveness on divergent preferences, not zero responsiveness on all preferences. Where preferences align (the 90% agreement zone), the median voter model may still operate.

### Brittleness Points

The framework is most vulnerable to:

1. **Preference Endogeneity**: If the 90% rich-middle agreement is endogenous to agenda and information regime, the "coincidence" reading collapses and the model's scope expands. If exogenous, the model's extraction claims are overstated.

2. **Continental Cartel Formation**: If EU-wide or US-wide economy-wide seller cartels form and achieve measurable responsiveness under open capital accounts, the scale ceiling is contingent rather than structural.

3. **Evangelical Leverage Persistence**: If evangelical bloc leverage is undiminished through 2032 despite Dobbs delivery, the brokerage endgame hypothesis fails and the model loses its dynamic component.

4. **Audit Implementation**: If a democratic state implements the responsiveness audit and publishes results, the "measurement absence as finding" claim is falsified. However, this would strengthen rather than weaken the framework—the audit provides the data to test all other predictions.

## IX. Conclusion: The Price Schedule as Political Question

Democracy under this model is neither rule-by-the-people nor rule-by-elites. It is a constrained exchange system where votes are tradable commodities with variable acquisition costs. The four constraints—organization floor, veto asymmetry, scale ceiling, information suppression—determine the price schedule and tradability conditions.

The live political question is not "who should rule?" but "what does the current price schedule look like, and who can afford to change it?" The Gilens/Page flat-line is not a failure of democracy—it is the market clearing at the current price. The periodic elite control losses (Brexit, 2016, Dobbs) are not system malfunctions—they are hostile-takeover arbitrage when incumbents misprice grievances.

The Moral Majority case demonstrates the full lifecycle: diffuse preferences (non-tradable) → organized bloc (tradable) → forty-year brokerage bargain → policy delivery (Dobbs) → predicted leverage decay. The case is not exceptional—it is exemplary. It shows how the market operates when all four constraints are visible simultaneously.

The measurement absence—no standing responsiveness audit—is not an oversight. It is the system's refusal to publish its own price schedule. The smallest possible demand is to require the measurement. The refusal to measure is itself the finding: a democracy that declines to know whose preferences policy tracks has revealed what it optimizes for, and it is not responsiveness.

The framework is falsifiable on a known timeline (2028-2032 evangelical leverage), through achievable measurement (continental cartel formation, information regime experiments, agenda space comparison), and by a simple institutional choice (implement the audit). Until then, the empirical record shows a market system with a specific price schedule, not a failure of democracy but a particular kind of democracy—one where votes are commodities and the question is always: at what price?
