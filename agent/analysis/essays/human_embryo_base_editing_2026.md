# Base Editing in Human Embryos: Technical Progress Within Structural Constraints

## Executive Summary

A 2024 preprint reports efficient adenine base editing (ABE) in human embryos, demonstrating elimination of the chromosomal damage that renders CRISPR/Cas9 unsafe for germline modification. The research advances understanding of DNA repair mechanisms in early human development while revealing persistent technical barriers—mosaicism, delivery-dependent toxicity, and guide RNA-specific off-target effects—that maintain the clinical translation gap. The Distributed Restraint (DR) engine classifies these constraints as structurally distinct categories: DNA repair substrate difference emerges as a false summit mountain (naturalized biology with identifiable beneficiaries), delivery modality toxicity and genetic mosaicism as coordination-washed ropes (technical problems misclassified as neutral), guide RNA off-target variance as a constructed high-extraction snare (safety risk hidden by distributed enforcement), and the clinical translation gap itself as a tangled rope trending toward snare (regulatory barrier with asymmetric benefit flows).

**Key Finding**: Base editing eliminates double-strand break (DSB) catastrophes but does not eliminate extraction. The technology shifts from genotoxic failure (aneuploidy, large deletions) to subtler forms of biological constraint that benefit existing research and clinical infrastructure while deferring access for affected populations.
