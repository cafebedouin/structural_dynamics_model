# The Leiden Declaration and the Structural Extraction of Mathematical Practice

## I. Introduction: A Discipline Under Asymmetric Pressure

In June 2026, sixteen mathematicians from fifteen universities published the Leiden Declaration on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics, a policy statement addressing how AI technologies threaten core values of mathematical practice. Endorsed by the International Mathematical Union and signed by over 130 mathematicians including Fields medalist Peter Scholze, the declaration identifies five principal threats: proof integrity, attribution erosion, research autonomy capture, peer review collapse, and ethical complicity in dual-use applications.

This essay examines the Leiden Declaration through the lens of structural constraint analysis, using the Dimensional Reduction (DR) framework to classify the mechanisms the declaration describes. The analysis reveals a pattern the declaration's framers may not have fully articulated: what appears as a collection of separate technological disruptions is actually a coordinated extraction system. Four of the six analyzed constraints exhibit **constructed high-extraction signatures**—mechanisms that appear benign from institutional vantage points while functioning as extractive traps from individual perspectives. This is not a natural consequence of technological change. It is an engineered outcome.

The DR engine computed classifications for six constraints derived from the declaration's claims. In every case where the constraint involved asymmetric power relationships between mathematicians and technology companies, the engine detected **perspectival incoherence**—different observer positions see fundamentally different constraint types. Institutions see coordination problems (Rope) or aspirational reforms (Scaffold). Individual mathematicians see extractive traps (Snare) or irreversible degradation (Tangled Rope moving toward Snare). The analytical observer—positioned to see structural patterns across all perspectives—consistently classifies these constraints as Snare.

This is the signature of extraction masking. The declaration documents it. The engine measures it. This essay explicates it.

## II. Proof Verification Crisis: The Epistemic Substrate

**What the engine found:** AI-generated mathematical arguments are epistemically unreliable. Distinguishing correct proofs from plausible-but-wrong arguments requires formal verification—a computationally expensive, human-intensive process. This is a **natural law**, not a coordination problem and not an extraction mechanism. The difficulty is intrinsic to the mathematics, not constructed by interested parties.

The declaration correctly identifies this as foundational. Proof integrity is the epistemic substrate on which all other mathematical values depend. If you cannot reliably distinguish correct from incorrect arguments, attribution becomes meaningless (whose work are you crediting?), peer review becomes impossible (what are you reviewing?), and research autonomy becomes moot (autonomy to do what?).

But here the declaration makes a crucial analytical move that the engine reveals as incomplete: it treats the *existence* of the epistemic problem (Mountain) as equivalent to the *institutional response* to that problem. The engine separates these. The verification crisis is Mountain. What happens next—how institutions, funders, and technology companies respond to the crisis—is where extraction enters.

**For asymmetric collaboration terms (Snare):**
- Not: better bargaining (assumes symmetric power)
- But: **public computational infrastructure with priority access** (Rope)

- Not: ethical review boards (theater)
- But: **material consequence for participation** (enforcement)
- Mechanism: Researchers who contribute to general-purpose AI training for companies with defense/surveillance contracts face professional sanction; institutional ethics boards have authority to prohibit participation

**The common thread:** Every intervention requires **enforcement mechanisms** and **material consequences**. Voluntary norms, aspirational frameworks, and disclosure requirements are structurally inadequate for Snare-classified constraints.
