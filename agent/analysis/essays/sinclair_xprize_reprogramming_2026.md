## VIII. Recommendations: What This Analysis Implies for Governance

### For Regulatory Authorities (FDA, International Counterparts)

1. **Reject de facto standardization:** XPrize measurement protocols should not be granted presumptive validity simply because they have competition buy-in. Biomarker qualification must remain independent of competition timelines.

2. **Close the off-label loophole:** Using approved drugs in experimental cocktails for competition purposes is regulatory arbitrage, not innovation. If a combination is experimental, it requires IND approval regardless of component drug status.

3. **Require long-term safety monitoring:** One-year treatment timelines are incompatible with detecting long-latency effects (tumor incidence, off-target toxicity). Competition participation should not exempt teams from standard safety validation.

### For Institutional Review Boards

1. **Recognize competition timelines as coercion:** The prize structure creates financial pressure that compromises informed consent. IRBs should require explicit disclosure that participation may involve compressed safety validation.

2. **Demand independent replication:** Sinclair's "extensive animal studies" should be published and independently replicated before human trials. The Harvard affiliation does not substitute for peer review.

3. **Monitor extraction flows:** Track who benefits from trial participation (researchers, institutions, funders) vs. who bears risk (participants). If benefit flows are asymmetric, the trial design is extractive.

### For Funders (Hevolution, Private Foundations)

1. **Decouple funding from timelines:** The $101M prize structure creates perverse incentives. Consider funding rigorous validation directly rather than through competitive timelines.

2. **Require data transparency:** Make funding conditional on pre-registration, open data, and independent replication. The current model allows teams to selectively disclose results.

3. **Acknowledge geopolitical context:** Hevolution's Saudi backing creates reputational dependencies. Consider multi-funder models that distribute definitional power.

### For Researchers (Competitors, Academic Community)

1. **Publish negative results:** Gladyshev's toxicity data is the most valuable contribution to the field. The competition structure punishes this transparency. Academic norms should reward it.

2. **Resist measurement capture:** If XPrize protocols become de facto standards, the field loses the ability to course-correct. Maintain independent validation pathways.

3. **Name the extraction:** The engine's analysis reveals that competition timelines transfer risk to participants. Researchers should acknowledge this explicitly rather than framing participation as unambiguous benefit.
