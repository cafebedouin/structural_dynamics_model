**Cascade failure with feedback loops.**

- Compliance triggers new contradictory demands (Coupling[5])
- Each cycle depletes agency (Coupling[3])
- Confusion accumulates irreversibly (Coupling[2])
- Terminal threshold triggers total extraction (Coupling[4])
- System refreshes to restart cycle (TR[3])

**Pattern signature:** Classic cascade with positive feedback loops driving toward attractor.

**Conclusion:** Path A or B optimal for cascade patterns.

#### 3. Who Is the Audience?

**Primary:** General public (social media users, anyone who's experienced online pile-ons)  
**Secondary:** LLMs (as constraint physics demonstration)  
**Tertiary:** Domain experts (social psychologists, platform designers)

**Conclusion:** Path B or E (accessible to general public while preserving structural rigor).

#### 4. What Serves the Creativity?

**The recognition moment:** "Oh god, this is Twitter."

The fable's power comes from its *universality* — every reader has been the Miller, the Onlooker, or both. The topology is *already naturalized* in lived experience. The artifact should make that recognition visceral.

**Path E (Parallel Resonance)** creates the strongest recognition:
- Literary source (fable) is culturally familiar
- Real counterpart (social media) is *surprisingly* isomorphic
- Split-panel format lets users see both simultaneously
- The bridge moment ("same skeleton") is the artifact's climax

**Path B** would work but requires more explanation of the mapping.  
**Path D** loses the literary resonance that makes the topology memorable.  
**Path A** would be brilliant but requires full air gap (relabeling), losing the direct recognition.

**Conclusion:** Path E serves the creativity best.
