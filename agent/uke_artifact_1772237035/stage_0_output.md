### C[1]: Athletic Performance as Social Capital

*   **Description:** The system in which physical prowess, specifically in college football, is the primary currency for status, admiration, and romantic success. Success is measured by on-field performance (yards gained, touchdowns) and translates directly into social importance.
*   **ε (estimated):** 0.55 — **Evidence:** The system provides genuine rewards (social status, admiration, Louise's affection, a sense of purpose) but extracts significant physical labor, risk of injury (Diederich's broken neck), and subordination to a hierarchy (coaches, star players). It's a genuine hybrid of coordination and extraction.
*   **Supp (estimated):** 0.8 — **Evidence:** Enforced by coaches ("Dig, now, dig!"), the captain's authority, the roar of the 70,000-person crowd, and the explicit social rewards (girls watching practice, being "pointed out on the campus").
*   **Coord:** true — It successfully coordinates eleven men on a field to achieve a collective goal.
*   **Asymmetric:** true — Rewards are disproportionately distributed. The star player (Diederich) gets the headlines and carries the ball "three times out of four," while blockers like Darling do the dangerous, less-glorified work.

#### Per character/index:

*   **Young Darling (pre-Diederich):**
    *   I=(powerful, biographical, mobile, local) → χ = 0.55 × 0.6 × 0.8 = **0.264**
    *   **Type: Rope (⊞)**
    *   **Analysis:** During the eighty-yard run, he is at the apex of this system. His power allows him to experience it as a pure coordination mechanism that validates his existence and promises future success. The costs are negligible compared to the rewards.

*   **Young Darling (post-Diederich):**
    *   I=(moderate, biographical, constrained, local) → χ = 0.55 × 1.0 × 0.8 = **0.44**
    *   **Type: Tangled Rope (⊞⊠)**
    *   **Analysis:** His role shifts to blocker. He still receives benefits (campus status, Louise's pride), but he now primarily serves to enable another's success. The system both rewards and uses him; the extraction becomes more palpable. His feeling that it "had never been as satisfactory as it should have been" reflects this shift from Rope to Tangled Rope.

*   **Louise (as girlfriend):**
    *   I=(moderate, biographical, mobile, local) → χ = 0.55 × 1.0 × 0.8 = **0.44**
    *   **Type: Tangled Rope (⊞⊠)**
    *   **Analysis:** She is a beneficiary of the system's rewards through her association with Darling. She enjoys his status ("I'm in love with an important figure"). However, her position is entirely dependent on his performance and status, making it a hybrid of genuine pride and vicarious, extracted value.

*   **Diederich (the star):**
    *   I=(institutional, biographical, mobile, local) → χ = 0.55 × -0.2 × 0.8 = **-0.088**
    *   **Type: Rope (⊞)**
    *   **Analysis:** As the system's star performer, he is a net beneficiary. The entire structure is reoriented to extract value *for* him. He embodies the system's ideal, and it rewards him accordingly.

*   **Indexical variance:** High. The same football team is experienced as a pure vehicle for glory (Rope) by the star, a complex bargain of reward and sacrifice (Tangled Rope) by the supporting player, and a source of dependent status (Tangled Rope) by the partner.

### C[2]: The Marital Partnership

*   **Description:** The evolving relationship between Christian and Louise, which begins as a romantic union based on C[1] and transforms into a complex structure of economic and emotional dependency.
*   **ε (estimated):** 0.20 (early) → 0.65 (late) — **Evidence:** Initially, the relationship is one of mutual affection and low extraction. After the crash, it becomes a system where Louise provides all financial and intellectual support, while Darling provides... his presence. The emotional and financial labor becomes highly extractive for her; his dependency is highly extractive of his agency and self-worth.
*   **Supp (estimated):** 0.3 (early) → 0.7 (late) — **Evidence:** Initially supported by mutual affection. Later, it is maintained by Darling's fear of loneliness ("the hopelessness... would be too much to take") and Louise's sense of pity, kindness, or lingering affection. The cost of exit becomes very high for him.
*   **Coord:** true (early) → false (late) — It ceases to be a partnership of equals.
*   **Asymmetric:** false (early) → true (late) — The power dynamic completely inverts.

#### Per character/index:

    *   **Type: Rope (⊞)**
    *   **Analysis:** He is the "important figure," the center of the relationship. It functions as a reward and support system for him.

    *   **Type: Snare (⊠)**
    *   **Analysis:** He is economically and emotionally dependent, with no viable exit. The marriage that was once a Rope has become a trap where he is confronted daily with his own inadequacy. He cannot leave due to the perceived cost of loneliness.

*   **Louise (early marriage):**
    *   I=(moderate, biographical, mobile, local) → χ (ε=0.2) = 0.2 × 1.0 × 0.8 = **0.16**
    *   **Type: Rope (⊞)**
    *   **Analysis:** The marriage is a partnership that provides her with the "important figure" she adores and a shared social life.

*   **Louise (late marriage):**
    *   I=(powerful, biographical, constrained, local) → χ (ε=0.65) = 0.65 × 0.6 × 0.8 = **0.312**
    *   **Type: Tangled Rope (⊞⊠)**

It is a "Tangled Rope" because she maintains it out of a sense of duty or pity, gaining the coordination of companionship at the cost of supporting a dependent. Her "patient, kindly, remote boredom" is the classic experience of navigating a burdensome Tangled Rope you can't or won't cut.

*   **Indexical variance:** Extreme. This is the central tragedy. A system that begins as a Rope for both degrades over time into a Snare for him and a Tangled Rope for her. They inhabit the same apartment but live inside entirely different constraint structures.

### C[3]: The New York Intellectual & Cultural Sphere

*   **Description:** The social system Louise enters after the crash, where value is determined by knowledge of modern art (Klee, Picasso), literature (Ernest Dobson), and politics (Trotsky, Communists). Currency is conversation, abstract ideas, and cultural sophistication.
*   **ε (estimated):** 0.40 — **Evidence:** The system provides genuine coordination benefits: community, stimulating conversation, professional networks, and a sense of being at the center of things. The cost of entry is intellectual labor: reading, learning, and participating. For those who cannot or will not pay this cost, the system is exclusionary.
*   **Supp (estimated):** 0.6 — **Evidence:** Enforced through social signals and exclusion. Not knowing who Odets is, or preferring "pictures with horses in them," results in being left "on the edge of things." The system self-polices through shared language and assumptions.
*   **Coord:** true — It effectively creates a vibrant social and intellectual community.
*   **Asymmetric:** true — Those with more cultural capital (like Flaherty or Louise) have higher status and influence than newcomers or outsiders (like Darling).

#### Per character/index:

*   **Louise:**
    *   I=(analytical, biographical, mobile, local) → χ = 0.40 × 1.15 × 0.8 = **0.368**
    *   **Type: Rope (⊞)**
    *   **Analysis:** She masters the rules of this system and thrives. She "devoured books," moved "confidently," and soaked it all in "without fear, with constant wonder." For her, it is a pure coordination mechanism for growth and self-actualization.

*   **Darling:**
    *   I=(powerless, biographical, trapped, local) → χ = 0.40 × 1.5 × 0.8 = **0.48**
    *   **Type: Tangled Rope (⊞⊠)**
    *   **Analysis:** He is trapped in this world by his marriage to Louise. He experiences the system's coordination (the parties, the conversations) but only feels the extraction of his self-worth. His attempts to participate fail ("it was no use. He was bored"). It is a system whose benefits are inaccessible to him, leaving only the cost of feeling inadequate.

*   **Cathal Flaherty:**
    *   I=(powerful, biographical, mobile, local) → χ = 0.40 × 0.6 × 0.8 = **0.192**
    *   **Type: Rope (⊞)**
    *   **Analysis:** He is a central node in this network, a man of strong opinions whose "judgment she accepted." He navigates it effortlessly, shaping the conversation. The system works for and through him.

*   **Indexical variance:** High. The same dinner party is a vibrant Rope for Louise and Flaherty, but a painful, alienating Tangled Rope for Darling. His wife is speaking "a different, new language, a language nobody had ever taught him."

It functions as a **terminal attractor** for his identity. All subsequent events are measured against this "high point," defining his life as "a decline."
