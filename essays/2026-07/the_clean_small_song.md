# The Clean Small Song

You go down empty-handed or the telling is a lie. That was the first thing Weha put in me, before the breath work, before the shelves, before she ever let me past the first drop. A teller carries nothing up. Not one shell, not one fish, not the smallest bright thing you fall in love with at nine fathoms and want to keep. You take a reef by leaving it exactly as full as you found it, and you bring the whole of it up in your chest instead, where it can't weigh anything or be spent.

I go down at the grey hour, before the boats. The water is still holding the night's cool and my ears clear easy, one shelf, two, the light going from white to green to the deep green that isn't a color so much as a pressure behind the eyes. I don't pick a way down. That's the craft nobody outside it believes. You don't choose. You let the reef hand you the next thing — a gap in the staghorn, a lane of sand, the shadow of the second drop — and you follow whatever it offers, and you never once decide, and you never go the same way twice in a life.

And you hum. Not out loud, there's no breath to spare out loud. You hold a pitch in the chest and you let each thing you pass move it. The bright massed life pulls it up sharp. The bare sand lets it fall. A teller's whole art is that the pitch hunts wild at first — up, down, nothing settling, the reef too big and too new every breath — and then, dive on dive, season on season, the hunting tightens. The swings get small. The pitch stops chasing and starts sitting, and one day it sits so still you understand you are no longer learning the reef. You are singing it. The pitch has come to true, and true is just the sound the whole reef makes when you've been in enough of it that no new stretch can surprise the note.

That is the thing they don't have a word for on the beach. Coming to true. It isn't knowing where you are — a teller is always lost, that's the point, we never map. It's that after enough water your held note stops arguing with the reef and starts agreeing with it, and the agreement is the reef, more truly than any one dive ever was.

I've had reefs come to true under me. It takes a life, or most of one. And when it happens there's nothing like it. You surface and you're not carrying a place in your chest anymore, you're carrying its whole measure, its true weight, and you could sing it to a stranger and they'd know it without ever getting wet.

*

Weha told me: any teller, any route, same true. She swore it, and I've watched it hold. Two of us can drop off opposite boats, take a thousand different lanes, never cross once, and come up a season later humming the same reef. It doesn't matter where you start. It doesn't matter what you choose, because you're not really choosing — you're being handed the reef in whatever order it likes, and if you stay lost in it long enough, the order stops mattering. Everything gets passed through. The rare bright stretch gets its few breaths, the endless sand gets its many, and the note you come up with is the reef's own proportion, weighed by how much of each there is to be lost in.

That's why we carry nothing up. If you took, the reef would be thinner behind you than in front, and your note would be tracking your own robbery instead of the reef. A teller's empty hands are the whole guarantee. Leave it full, and what you learn is what's there. Start taking, and what you learn is only how fast you can empty a thing.

I believed that the way you believe the water is under you. It was under me for forty years.

*

This season the note won't sit.

I go down over the deep garden — the far shelf, the richest water I've ever been lost in, a stretch that used to pull my pitch up so sharp and hold it there so long that half a season's telling was just that one blaze of reef refusing to average out. I go down and the pitch falls where the blaze should be. Falls and hunts and won't settle, and I think, old woman, you've lost your ear.

So I dive it again. And again, more than I should, more than the breath in me wants, because a teller who can't make a stretch come to true doesn't have the stretch, she has a hole. And the more I tell it the worse it gets, and then one grey morning at the bottom of the deep garden I understand it isn't my ear. The note isn't hunting because I don't know the reef. It's hunting because the reef I'm telling is smaller than the reef in my chest, and the two won't agree, and there's no lane I can take to make them, because the difference isn't somewhere I haven't been.

The difference is gone. You can't get lost in what isn't there.

*

The share-house wanted the count on the ninth day, same as every year.

I'll set it down flat, the way it was said, because the way it was said matters. Three of them at the long table, the season's share to fix before the boats went out. A share is baskets. So many baskets off the reef, split by household, set by the teller's count of what the reef can lose and stay itself. High count, big share, full nets, children through the lean months. Low count, small share, and hunger you can measure.

They asked me for the reef. I told them the deep garden wouldn't come to true. The heavy one, the eldest, asked what that meant in baskets. I said it meant the far shelf had less in it than my telling, by a margin I couldn't sit a note on, and until it settled I couldn't give a share for that water I'd stand behind. He said the boats don't run on a note that won't settle. He said there were forty households and a count due, and asked me — not unkind, that's the part — to give him a reef he could feed people from. Sing us something usable, he meant. He didn't say it cruel. He said it like a man who'd watched a woman fall in love with a sound and forget it was supposed to be a number.

And here is the thing I couldn't put on the table, because there's no basket for it: a small reef comes to true faster than a big one. There's less in it to argue with the note. The far shelf was settling. It was settling beautiful. It just wasn't settling on the deep garden. It was settling on what the deep garden had become, which was quiet, and clean, and quick to agree with, because a quiet reef has nothing left to surprise you with.

I gave them a count. I'll set that down too. I split the difference — I sang them a reef between the one in my chest and the one under my hands, and I called it caution, and the share came out low but not low enough, and the boats went out, and I have thought every day since about how the honest number and the merciful number and the number that keeps the peace were three different reefs and only one of them was true.

*

I went to the pale after that. I hadn't, before. You don't tell the pale; there's nothing to get lost in.

It's the far edge of the deep garden, where the color goes. First the reef greys, then it whitens, then it's just the bones standing up out of the sand, clean as something scrubbed, and the fish that made the blaze are not there, and the fish that eat those fish are not there, and the small hidden lives under those are not there, and you cannot tell what isn't there. That's the whole of it. A teller's note wants something to move it, and the pale gives it nothing, and so over the pale the note doesn't hunt at all. It sits at once. First breath, dead still.

The pale comes to true faster than any reef I ever told. It comes to true in a single breath, because it has nothing in it to argue with the note. And it is the most beautiful settling in the world, that instant clean agreement, and it means there is nothing there, and a teller who came to this reef new — who had never been lost in the blaze, who had no other reef in her chest — would surface from the pale humming a small clean perfect note and swear on the water that she had told the whole reef true.

Because she would have.

*

Lo is eleven and it's her first season and she's a natural, which is a word that means she gets lost easy and comes up empty-handed and doesn't ask why we don't take. I've had her on the near shelves all season, the shallow safe reef by the boats, and last week I let her past the first drop, and yesterday I put her over the deep garden and let her tell it fresh.

She came up shining. Her note had sat in half a morning. Half a morning — it took me a season, at her age, on that same water, and it never fully sat, it blazed and fought me for years. She surfaced with a small clean perfect reef in her chest and she was so happy I felt it come off her like heat, and she said, it came to true so fast, Sao, I have the whole of it, and she hummed me the far shelf.

She had it. That's what I need you to understand. She wasn't wrong. She told the reef that's there, all of it, true, and it's a small clean quick thing to tell now, and she is the first teller in the line who will never know it was ever anything else. Her whole reef is my hole. The blaze I can't stop hearing, the note that won't sit in my chest, is to her not a wrong note or a sad one — it's not a note at all. It's a silence she has no name for and no ear to miss. I could sing her the deep garden that was and she would hear a stranger's tune, a thing I made up, an old woman's flourish over a reef that plainly comes to true without it.

In her telling there is no hole, because a telling that's come to true has no room for one. That's what coming to true is. And she'll teach it to whoever comes after her exactly that clean, and the pale will be the reef, and the reef will come to true faster every generation because there'll be less and less in it to argue with, and every teller down the line will surface happy and honest with the whole of what's there in her chest, and each one will be right.

*

I did one thing. I don't know that it was the right thing. I don't think it changes anything and I've stopped pretending to myself that it does.

I taught Lo the hole.

I took her down over the deep garden and I made her hold the note where the blaze used to be, and then I made her stop singing — a held rest, an empty place in the middle of a true reef, a silence you have to keep on purpose. I made her carry a thing that isn't there.

She hated it. Of course she did. To her it's a flaw, a wrong beat in a song that had come to true without it, and every time I made her hold it I watched her hands want to close it, smooth it over, let the clean note run through the gap the way water runs over the pale. She kept asking what goes there. Nothing goes there, I said. That's the telling. You keep the nothing so you know it's nothing and not the whole.

She'll close it. I know she will. The day I'm not on the boat with her she'll let the note run clean through, because a teller's whole art is making the reef agree with her chest, and a hole doesn't agree with anything, and hers has already come to true without it. The hole is one life deep. Mine. When it's spent, the pale is the reef, clean and quick and honest, and the true small song is all anybody carries up.

The boats went out on the count I sang them. The far shelf gives less every year and comes to true a little faster each time, and the share-house sets the share off the count, and the count keeps settling, and everyone who reads it reads a reef that has come to true.

The water takes the note out past the last drop, out where the boats don't run, out past where any of us can go down and get lost enough to tell what it does with what it keeps.
